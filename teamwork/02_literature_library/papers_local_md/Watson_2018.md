# Abiotic stress-induced chloroplast proteome remodelling: a mechanistic overview

**Authors:** Samuel J. Watson, Robert G. Sowden, R. Paul Jarvis
**Journal:** *Journal of Experimental Botany*, 69(11): 2773–2781 (2018)
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/ery053

## Abstract
The chloroplast houses photosynthesis in all green plants and is therefore of fundamental importance to the viability and productivity of plants, ecosystems, and agriculture. Chloroplasts are, however, extremely vulnerable to environmental stress, on account of the inherent volatility of oxygenic photosynthesis. To counteract this sensitivity, sophisticated systems of chloroplast stress acclimation have evolved, and many of these involve broad proteome changes. In this review, the authors provide an overview of the interlocking and mutually dependent mechanisms of abiotic stress-induced chloroplast proteome remodelling, covering nucleus-to-chloroplast signalling, chloroplast-to-nucleus retrograde signalling, chloroplast pre-protein import regulation, and organellar proteolysis.
