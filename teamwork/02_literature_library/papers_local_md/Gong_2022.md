Keywords:                                                   To bear harsh environmental threats, plants have developed complex protection mechanisms involving phyto­
ABA                                                         hormones, counting abscisic acid (ABA). The function of the F-Box family containing the Domain of Unknown
DUF295                                                      Function 295 (DUF295) has not yet been comprehensively characterized in Arabidopsis (Arabidopsis thaliana). In
Seed germination
                                                            this study, we evaluated the function of a putative member of the F-Box/DUF295 family in Arabidopsis, F-box/
Seedling growth
                                                            DUF295 Brassiceae specific 2 (FDB2). We found that FDB2 expression was suppressed by ABA and abiotic
Reactive oxygen species
                                                            stresses. FDB2 overexpression (OE) reduced ABA sensitivity during seed germination and seedling growth, but
                                                            enhanced ABA-sensitivity of seed germination and seedling growth in fdb2 mutants was scored. When treated
                                                            with ABA, expressions of ABI3, ABI4 and ABI5 showed decreased in OE lines but increased in fdb2 mutants. In
                                                            addition, ABA-induced FDB2 degradation exhibited sensitive to MG132, suggesting that FDB2 degradation by
                                                            ABA might be mediated by the ubiquitin-26S proteasome system. Moreover, ABA-induced significant over-
                                                            accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) at the root tips of fdb2 mutants was observed, this phenome­
                                                            non was correlated to reduced activities of a set of ROS scavengers in fdb2 mutants relative to Col-0. In summary,
                                                            our results suggest that Arabidopsis FDB2 is involved in ABA-mediated inhibition of seed germination, seedling
                                                            growth including modulation of ROS homeostasis in roots.
