## Abstract

Comparative genomics between closely related extremophytes provides an evolutionary window to decipher the genetic foundations of plant drought persistence while controlling for phylogenetic noise. In the ancient, infertile *campos rupestres* of eastern Brazil, the monocot family Velloziaceae has evolved contrasting water-use strategies: the obligate resurrection plant *Vellozia tubiflora* survives vegetative dehydration exceeding 90% water loss through quiescent anhydrobiosis, whereas its sympatric congener *Vellozia peripherica* maintains continuous foliar hydration and active carbon capture via dehydration avoidance. Here, we present a large-scale comparative genomic analysis of high-contiguity chromosomal assemblies (9 pseudomolecules each, $>95\%$ BUSCO embryophyta completeness; detailed assembly and structural annotation workflows are documented in Appendix A). Chromosome-scale scaffolding was grounded in cytogenetic evidence confirming $2n = 18$ ($x = 9$) as the definitive karyotypic standard for the genus (BRAZ et al., 2025). Orthology clustering across 96,809 proteins resolved 24,013 orthogroups, identifying 11,048 core families shared across species and 7,061 universal single-copy orthologs, alongside 18,690 pairwise orthologous pairs between the two *Vellozia* genomes. Whole-genome synteny mapping identified 297 highly conserved collinear blocks with zero inter-chromosomal translocations, uncovering an ancestral paleoduplication block between Chromosomes 1A and 5A reflecting an ancient whole-genome duplication event. Gene family evolutionary modeling (CAFE5, $\lambda = 0.07456$) across 16,032 families identified 412 statistically significant evolving families ($p \le 0.05$). Automated statistical overrepresentation analysis (Fisher's exact test with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across relational annotation databases) demonstrated distinct, statistically significant functional enrichments: *V. tubiflora* significantly enriched plastid protein import machinery (TOC/TIC translocons, 103 hits, 13.60-fold enrichment, $q = 5.92 \times 10^{-114}$), photosynthetic electron transport and NDH complexes (112 hits, 11.25-fold enrichment, $q = 2.59 \times 10^{-101}$), plastid ribosomal biogenesis (47 hits, 9.45-fold enrichment, $q = 3.08 \times 10^{-36}$), acetyl-CoA carboxylase lipid biosynthesis (23 hits, 13.87-fold enrichment, $q = 7.96 \times 10^{-26}$), and Photosystem II core stabilizers (24 hits, 9.25-fold enrichment, $q = 1.43 \times 10^{-18}$) to overcome the kinetic rehydration bottleneck. Conversely, *V. peripherica* significantly enriched LRR receptor-like kinases (110 hits, 5.57-fold enrichment, $q = 1.99 \times 10^{-55}$), U-box domain-containing E3 ubiquitin ligases (63 hits, 6.41-fold enrichment, $q = 7.45 \times 10^{-37}$), UDP-glycosyltransferases (78 hits, 4.97-fold enrichment, $q = 7.37 \times 10^{-35}$), Cytochrome P450 monooxygenases (88 hits, 3.26-fold enrichment, $q = 4.01 \times 10^{-23}$), and ubiquitination target receptors (32 hits, 7.49-fold enrichment, $q = 2.80 \times 10^{-22}$) to maintain homeostatic regulation under continuous foliar hydration. Genome-wide codon-based selection modeling ($Ka/Ks$) demonstrated predominant purifying selection across single-copy orthologs (mean $Ka/Ks = 0.3542$), with divergent selective constraints between metabolic pathways associated with quiescent anhydrobiosis and continuous homeostatic regulation. These findings demonstrate that divergent ecological strategies in ancient montane environments are driven by targeted functional gene family expansions and modulated selective constraints within a structurally stable chromosomal framework, establishing the macroevolutionary backdrop for candidate gene family investigations in subsequent chapters.

# 1. Introduction

The *campos rupestres* of central and eastern Brazil represent an ancient, tropical montane ecosystem recognized biogeographically as an Old Climatically-Buffered Infertile Landscape (OCBIL) that harbors approximately 15% of Brazil's vascular plant diversity within less than 1% of the national territory (SILVEIRA et al., 2015; FERNANDES et al., 2020; ALCANTARA et al., 2015). This exceptional floristic endemism and beta diversity have been shaped by severe, multidimensional edaphoclimatic selective filters (FERNANDES et al., 2018). Soils derived from quartzite and sandstone outcrops are shallow, acidic, and hyper-oligotrophic, displaying available phosphorus concentrations among the lowest recorded globally (TEODORO et al., 2019; LAMBERS et al., 2022; ABRAHÃO et al., 2018). In addition to nutrient poverty, the skeletal substrate possesses minimal water-holding capacity, subjecting the vegetation to acute seasonal water deficits, saturating solar irradiance, high vapor pressure deficits (VPD), pronounced diurnal thermal amplitudes, and persistent abrasive winds (FERNANDES et al., 2020).

Within this severe edaphoclimatic matrix, the monocotyledonous family Velloziaceae (order Pandanales) constitutes the dominant floristic element, reaching its global evolutionary epicenter and maximum morphological diversification across the quartzitic massifs of the Espinhaço Range (DE MELLO-SILVA, 2005; ALCANTARA et al., 2015; ALCANTARA; REE; MELLO-SILVA, 2018; WANGA et al., 2021). To persist in phosphorus-depleted soils, Velloziaceae evolved specialized "vellozioid" roots that exude massive amounts of carboxylates (primarily citrate and malate) to chemically weather rock matrices and solubilize tightly bound inorganic phosphorus (TEODORO et al., 2019; LAMBERS et al., 2022).

Co-occurring Velloziaceae species exhibit contrasting water-use strategies that reflect divergent evolutionary solutions to water scarcity (ALCANTARA et al., 2015; VIEIRA et al., 2022). Desiccation-tolerant (DT) species, commonly termed resurrection plants, survive vegetative water losses exceeding 90% of their total protoplasmic water content ($\Psi < -100\text{ MPa}$), entering a state of quiescent anhydrobiosis and rapidly restoring metabolic competence upon rehydration (BONDI; MARKS; VANBUREN, 2023; TEODORO et al., 2021; MARKS et al., 2024). In the *campos rupestres*, *Vellozia tubiflora* represents an archetypal resurrection specialist occupying exposed, rapidly draining rock outcrops. In contrast, sympatric desiccation-sensitive but drought-avoidant (evergreen) species, such as *Vellozia peripherica*, maintain functional foliar hydration throughout dry periods through tight stomatal regulation, deep root foraging in rock fissures, and osmotic adjustment (DE MELLO-SILVA, 2005; VIEIRA et al., 2022; VIEIRA et al., 2025).

These contrasting life-history strategies reflect a fundamental evolutionary trade-off between productivity and survival (TEODORO et al., 2021; FERNANDES et al., 2020; VOLAIRE et al., 2026). Desiccation tolerance guarantees survival through catastrophic droughts at the cost of negligible dry-season carbon gain, whereas dehydration avoidance maximizes carbon assimilation during moderate water deficits (Phase II drought resistance) but leaves plants vulnerable to lethal xylem cavitation if drought exceeds hydraulic safety margins (Phase III drought survival) (VOLAIRE et al., 2026).

Pioneering genomic investigations in resurrection plants, including the African monocot *Xerophyta viscosa* (COSTA et al., 2017), the resurrection grasses *Oropetium thomaeum* (VANBUREN et al., 2015) and *Sporobolus stapfianus* (OLIVER et al., 2011), the eudicot *Boea hygrometrica* (XIAO et al., 2015), and the lycophyte *Selaginella lepidophylla* (VANBUREN et al., 2018), have highlighted seed gene co-option, genome compactness, and duplicate gene retention. However, previous studies predominantly contrasted resurrection plants against distant, mesophytic crop models, confounding drought-specific adaptations with broad phylogenetic divergence.

Comparing phylogenetically paired congeneric sister lineages from the same habitat provides a powerful approach to isolate the genomic signatures of extreme drought adaptation while eliminating macroevolutionary background noise (MARKS et al., 2024; BONDI; MARKS; VANBUREN, 2023). Here, we utilize the congeneric pair *Vellozia tubiflora* (resurrection plant) and *Vellozia peripherica* (evergreen avoidant) to investigate how macroevolutionary synteny, gene family expansions, and selective pressures drive contrasting adaptations to drought in an ancient Neotropical landscape.

# 2. Materials and Methods

## 2.1 Genomic Datasets and Quality Assessment Summary

High-contiguity chromosomal pseudomolecule assemblies and comprehensive structural gene annotations for *Vellozia tubiflora* (resurrection specialist) and *Vellozia peripherica* (evergreen drought-avoidant congener) were established as standard comparative inputs for this chapter. Complete methodological details regarding tissue procurement, high-molecular-weight DNA extraction, Pacific Biosciences (PacBio) HiFi long-read sequencing, Hi-C chromosomal scaffolding, multi-tissue transcriptomic profiling, and structural annotation pipelines are documented in **Appendix A: Sequencing, Assembly, and Structural Annotation Methodology**.

Both genome assemblies achieved chromosome-scale scaffolding into 9 pseudomolecules (Chr1A-Chr9A), aligning with the base karyotype of the genus *Vellozia* ($2n = 18$). Cytogenetic validation using KNL1 kinetochore protein immunolocalization definitively resolved historical karyotypic discrepancies in the genus, establishing $2n = 18$ ($x = 9$) as the true basic chromosome number across *Vellozia* species (BRAZ et al., 2025). The physical identification of functional centromeres confirmed that small chromosome-like structures previously hypothesized to be satellites or B chromosomes are authentic small chromosomes, providing empirical cytogenetic verification for our 9 assembled pseudomolecules (BRAZ et al., 2025). Assembly completeness evaluated by Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO v5.4.3) against the Embryophyta ODB10 lineage dataset confirmed $>95\%$ completeness in both genomes, providing a solid foundation for downstream macroevolutionary comparative analyses.

## 2.2 Orthology Inference and Core Proteome Clustering (OrthoFinder)

To establish orthology relationships and reconstruct the evolutionary history of gene duplications, we analyzed the predicted proteomes of *Vellozia tubiflora* (34,449 predicted proteins), *Vellozia peripherica* (34,705 predicted proteins), and the model plant *Arabidopsis thaliana* (TAIR10/Araport11 canonical proteome, 27,655 proteins; EMMS; KELLY, 2019). Canonical protein filtering was conducted using custom Python scripts to retain the longest translation product per locus.

Orthogroup clustering was executed using OrthoFinder v2.5.5 (EMMS; KELLY, 2019). Sequence similarity searches were computed using DIAMOND v2.0.15 under an all-versus-all format ($E \le 10^{-3}$). Orthogroups were delineated using the Markov Cluster Algorithm (MCL, inflation parameter = 1.5). OrthoFinder inferred unrooted gene trees for all orthogroups containing at least four sequences using DendroBLAST, followed by rooted species tree inference via the Species Tree from All Genes (STAG) algorithm and root positioning using the Duplication-Loss-Coalescence (STRIDE) pipeline. Single-copy universal orthologs (1:1:1 orthologs across all three taxa) and species-specific orthogroups were extracted using custom Python scripts.

## 2.3 Syntenic Collinearity Mapping and Duplication Mode Classification (MCScanX / JCVI)

Macrosyntenic conservation, chromosomal collinearity blocks, and micro-collinear contiguity were characterized across the 9 homologous chromosome pairs using MCScanX and the Python version of MCScan (JCVI v1.2.4; WANG et al., 2012). Pairwise gene homology was established using BLASTP searches ($E \le 10^{-5}$, maximum matches = 5). Collinear blocks were identified requiring a minimum of 5 collinear gene pairs with a maximum intervening gap of 25 genes.

To evaluate ancestral whole-genome duplications and lineage-specific expansions, intragenomic self-synteny (paralogous collinearity) was computed within *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* independently. Collinear blocks were classified into Whole-Genome Duplication (WGD)/segmental duplications, tandem expansions (consecutive adjacent paralogs with 0 intervening genes), proximal duplications (paralogs separated by $<10$ genes), and dispersed duplications. Syntenic relationships were visualized using Circos and JCVI micro-synteny plotting modules.

## 2.4 Gene Family Modeling (CAFE5), Relational Database Construction, and Statistical Functional Enrichment

To model the evolutionary dynamics of gene family sizes across the phylogenetic topology, we utilized CAFE5 (Computational Analysis of Gene Family Evolution v5.0; MENDES et al., 2020). The ultrametric species tree was calibrated using r8s based on fossil-calibrated divergence time estimates between monocots and eudicots (145 Mya) and the divergence between the focal *Vellozia* lineages.

Orthogroup gene count matrices generated by OrthoFinder were filtered to remove orthogroups with extreme species-specific expansions ($>100$ genes in a single taxon) to avoid parameter estimation bias. CAFE5 was executed using the Base Birth-Death ($\lambda$) model with global error estimation (`-e`). The maximum likelihood global birth-death parameter ($\lambda$) was estimated across 56 optimization iterations. Significant expansions and contractions across individual phylogenetic branches were identified at a family-wide significance threshold of $p \le 0.05$ (calculated via the Viterbi algorithm).

To integrate genomic coordinates, transcript models, EggNOG/InterPro functional descriptors, OrthoFinder orthology clusters, and CAFE5 evolutionary trajectories into a unified querying framework, relational SQLite annotation databases (`vtub_annotation.db`, `vper_annotation.db`, and `vellozia_master_annotation.db`) were constructed. Statistical functional overrepresentation analysis was implemented in Python using two-sided Fisher's exact tests ($2 \times 2$ contingency tables) evaluating candidate expanding genes against the species-specific annotated genomic background ($N = 21,259$ in *V. tubiflora*; $N = 22,341$ in *V. peripherica*). Fold enrichment was computed as:

$$\text{Fold Enrichment} = \frac{a / N_{\text{target}}}{n / M_{\text{background}}}$$

where $a$ is the number of target genes with the functional annotation, $N_{\text{target}}$ is the total number of candidate genes, $n$ is the total number of annotated genes in the genome with the term, and $M_{\text{background}}$ is the total annotated background size. Multiple testing correction was applied across all tested functional terms using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure to calculate False Discovery Rate ($q$-values), establishing statistical significance at $q \le 0.05$.

## 2.5 Selection Pressure Estimation Across Single-Copy Orthologs (Ka/Ks)

To evaluate selective regimes acting on coding sequences across the two *Vellozia* genomes, codon substitution modeling was strictly performed across the **7,061 universal single-copy 1:1:1 orthologs** delineated by OrthoFinder. Each single-copy orthogroup represents a unique ancestral locus conserved as a single-copy gene in *V. tubiflora*, *V. peripherica*, and *Arabidopsis thaliana* without lineage-specific duplication or gene loss. Restricting pairwise codon substitution modeling to 1:1 orthologs is essential for two methodological reasons: (1) it eliminates phylogenetic and paralogy ambiguity, ensuring that every gene pair represents an unambiguous orthologous relationship; and (2) it ensures that all analyzed gene pairs share an identical evolutionary divergence time (the speciation event between *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica*), avoiding temporal confounding caused by recent lineage-specific paralogous duplications.

For each of the 7,061 orthogroups, pairwise protein sequence alignments were generated using MUSCLE and back-translated into codon-preserving nucleotide alignments via PAL2NAL v14. Non-synonymous substitution rates ($Ka$ or $dN$), synonymous substitution rates ($Ks$ or $dS$), and the selective ratio ($\omega = Ka/Ks$) were computed using the maximum likelihood `codeml` program in PAML v4.9 under pairwise mode (`runmode = -2, CodonFreq = 2`; YANG, 2007). Alignments exhibiting mutational saturation ($Ks > 2.0$) were removed. 

To determine how evolutionary selective constraints vary across physiological systems, the 7,061 single-copy orthologs were stratified by biological function based on InterPro domains and EggNOG functional classifications stored in `vellozia_master_annotation.db`. Because broad biological categories comprise multiple distinct subcomponents and paralogous lineages that were established prior to the diversification of monocots, each functional category aggregates multiple independent 1:1 orthogroups (e.g., 204 distinct 1:1 LRR receptor kinase orthogroups, 87 distinct 1:1 ABC transporter orthogroups, 23 distinct 1:1 chloroplastic translocon subunit orthogroups, 101 distinct 1:1 ribosomal protein orthogroups, and 24 distinct 1:1 histone/chromatin orthogroups). Category-specific mean selective constraints and standard deviations ($\text{Mean } \omega \pm \text{SD}$) were calculated across all constituent 1:1 orthologs to assess differential purifying constraints ($\omega < 1$) versus accelerated evolutionary rates ($\omega \to 1$).

## 3.2 Orthogroup Landscape: Conserved Orthology and Species-Specific Partitions

Orthology clustering across the 96,809 input proteins grouped **90,293 genes (93.3%) into 24,013 orthogroups**, leaving only 6,516 genes (6.7%) as unassigned species-specific singletons (Figure 1). The mean orthogroup size was 3.8 genes, with a median of 3 genes.

A core set of **11,048 orthogroups (46.0%) was universally shared across all three species**, containing **7,061 universal single-copy orthologs (1:1:1 orthologous genes)** that represent the deeply conserved monocot-eudicot genetic backbone. Pairwise ortholog inference established **18,690 orthologous relationships between *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica*** (17,752 one-way orthologs), confirming high genetic homology between the two congeneric species.

Lineage-specific clustering revealed notable differences in gene duplication retention: *Vellozia peripherica* harbored **1,189 species-specific orthogroups (3,526 genes)**, whereas *Vellozia tubiflora* contained **655 species-specific orthogroups (2,019 genes)**. In total, 4,334 gene duplication events were mapped across species tree nodes, reflecting active gene duplication and retention dynamics within Velloziaceae.

## 3.3 Syntenic Architecture, Paleoduplications, and Chromosomal Collinearity

Genome-wide synteny analysis between *Vellozia tubiflora* and *Vellozia peripherica* revealed a high degree of chromosomal collinearity and macrosyntenic stability (Figure 2). A total of **297 collinear syntenic blocks** were preserved across all 9 homologous chromosome pairs, with zero large-scale inter-chromosomal translocations.

Intragenomic self-synteny mapping identified evidence of an ancestral whole-genome paleoduplication event shared prior to the divergence of the two species. Specifically, a large conserved collinear block connects **Chromosome 1A and Chromosome 5A** in both genomes. This ancestral duplication block encompasses multiple duplicated functional pathways, confirming that a large-scale paleopolyploidy event preceded speciation within the genus *Vellozia*.

In *Vellozia tubiflora*, expanding gene families clustered prominently in functional modules dedicated to organellar protection, photoprotective electron transport, and post-drought plastid proteome reconstitution. The most pronounced expansion occurred within the chloroplast protein translocation machinery. Specifically, the chloroplast inner envelope protein-conducting channel (**OG0000016**, $+55$ genes, expanding from 6 ancestral copies to 61 in *V. tubiflora* versus 3 in *V. peripherica*, $p < 0.001$) and the intermediate translocation complex (**OG0000087**, $+22$ genes, expanding from 4 to 26 in *V. tubiflora* versus 2 in *V. peripherica*, $p < 0.001$) endowed *V. tubiflora* with 87 total chloroplastic translocon loci compared to only 5 in *V. peripherica* (103 expanding genes, 13.60-fold enrichment, $q = 5.92 \times 10^{-114}$, Table 2). This structural expansion was functionally coupled to expansions in the chloroplastic NAD(P)H dehydrogenase (NDH) complex (**OG0000036**, $+29$ genes, expanding from 7 to 36; **OG0000062**, $+18$ genes, expanding from 7 to 25; 112 expanding genes, 11.25-fold enrichment, $q = 2.59 \times 10^{-101}$), which drives cyclic electron transport around Photosystem I to maintain a transmembrane proton gradient during foliar dehydration. In parallel, *V. tubiflora* exhibited targeted expansions across Photosystem II reaction center core and antenna components (**OG0000069**, $+18$ genes; **OG0000119**, $+15$ genes; 24 expanding genes, 9.25-fold enrichment, $q = 1.43 \times 10^{-18}$), the Photosystem I assembly factor Ycf4 (**OG0000076**, $+16$ genes; 22 expanding genes, 13.87-fold enrichment, $q = 8.95 \times 10^{-25}$), and primary 16S rRNA-binding proteins required for plastid 30S ribosomal subunit biogenesis (**OG0000104**, $+18$ genes; **OG0000042**, $+17$ genes; 47 expanding genes, 9.45-fold enrichment, $q = 3.08 \times 10^{-36}$). Concurrently, lipid biosynthesis enzymes such as acetyl-CoA carboxylase (**OG0000072**, $+17$ genes; 23 expanding genes, 13.87-fold enrichment, $q = 7.96 \times 10^{-26}$) and organonitrogen metabolic pathways (**OG0000014**, $+51$ genes; **OG0000017**, $+42$ genes; 81 expanding genes, 13.06-fold enrichment, $q = 4.78 \times 10^{-85}$) expanded substantially to support membrane lipid remodeling and osmoprotectant vitrification during anhydrobiosis.

In *Vellozia peripherica*, expanded gene families reflected the cellular demands of maintaining continuous foliar hydration, real-time cell wall surveillance, and constitutive pathogen defense. Transmembrane Leucine-Rich Repeat receptor-like kinases (LRR-RLKs) underwent extensive lineage-specific proliferation across multiple orthogroups, including extracellular LRR-domain proteins (**OG0000012**, $+57$ genes, expanding from 8 ancestral copies to 65 in *V. peripherica* versus 13 in *V. tubiflora*, $p < 0.001$; 110 expanding genes, 5.57-fold enrichment, $q = 1.99 \times 10^{-55}$), general receptor-like kinases (**OG0000021**, $+29$ genes), and LRR receptor serine/threonine kinases (**OG0000025**, $+25$ genes; **OG0000060**, $+22$ genes; **OG0000019**, $+25$ genes; **OG0000029**, $+17$ genes). In parallel, intracellular disease resistance genes encoding NB-LRR sentinels expanded significantly (**OG0000050**, $+22$ genes; **OG0000049**, $+17$ genes; 25 expanding genes, 10.07-fold enrichment, $q = 2.76 \times 10^{-24}$), accompanied by aspartic peptidases of the A1 family (**OG0000052**, $+19$ genes). To maintain proteome integrity under persistent transpiration, *V. peripherica* expanded protein degradation machinery, including ubiquitin target receptors (**OG0000043**, $+21$ genes; 32 expanding genes, 7.49-fold enrichment, $q = 2.80 \times 10^{-22}$) and U-box domain-containing E3 ubiquitin ligases (**OG0000094**, $+16$ genes; 63 expanding genes, 6.41-fold enrichment, $q = 7.45 \times 10^{-37}$), alongside pentatricopeptide repeat (PPR) proteins involved in organellar post-transcriptional RNA editing (**OG0000131**, $+16$ genes).

### 3.5 Selection Pressures Across Single-Copy Orthologous Genes

Pairwise codon selection modeling across all **7,061 universal single-copy 1:1:1 orthologous genes** (each representing an independent, single-copy orthogroup) revealed a pervasive genome-wide pattern of strong purifying selection (mean $Ka = 0.0339$, mean $Ks = 0.0838$, mean $Ka/Ks = 0.3542$; Figure 4). Across all 7,061 orthologous pairs, 100% of loci exhibited $\omega < 1.0$, demonstrating that purifying selection acts globally to maintain basic cellular and structural integrity.

When the 7,061 single-copy orthologs were categorized by functional domains, selective constraints exhibited a pronounced biological gradient across physiological systems (Figure 4C). Essential housekeeping machinery operated under severe purifying constraint ($\omega < 0.20$), characterized by minimal non-synonymous variation in core histone and chromatin-remodeling proteins (24 distinct 1:1 orthogroups, mean $\omega = 0.04 \pm 0.02$), core ribosomal translation machinery (101 distinct 1:1 orthogroups, mean $\omega = 0.08 \pm 0.03$), chloroplastic TOC/TIC translocon subunits (23 distinct 1:1 orthogroups, mean $\omega = 0.16 \pm 0.05$), and Photosystem II core stabilizers (76 distinct 1:1 orthogroups, mean $\omega = 0.22 \pm 0.06$). Intermediate selective constraints ($0.25 < \omega < 0.45$) characterized single-copy components of Cyclic Electron Transport (NDH complexes, mean $\omega = 0.29 \pm 0.07$) and osmoprotectant biosynthesis / vitrification pathways (mean $\omega = 0.38 \pm 0.09$), evolving near the genome-wide baseline. Conversely, single-copy orthologs functioning at cellular interfaces and environmental perception layers exhibited markedly elevated non-synonymous substitution rates ($0.50 < \omega < 0.70$), including secondary metabolite ABC transporters (87 distinct 1:1 orthogroups, mean $\omega = 0.54 \pm 0.12$), cell wall loosening pectinases (mean $\omega = 0.61 \pm 0.14$), and transmembrane Leucine-Rich Repeat receptor kinases (LRR-RLKs; 204 distinct 1:1 orthogroups, mean $\omega = 0.68 \pm 0.15$).

# 4. Discussion and Conclusions

## 4.1 Karyotypic Architecture, Syntenic Stasis, and Duplication Mechanics in Vellozia

Genome-wide macrosynteny analysis established an extraordinarily high degree of chromosomal collinearity between *Vellozia tubiflora* and *Vellozia peripherica*, characterized by **297 conserved collinear blocks** across the 9 homologous chromosome pairs and a complete absence of inter-chromosomal translocations. This structural stasis is consistent with the karyotypic stability established for the genus *Vellozia* ($2n = 18, x = 9$), where functional centromere mapping through KNL1 kinetochore immunolocalization definitively demonstrated that the smaller chromosome pair represents authentic functional chromosomes rather than supernumerary elements or satellites (BRAZ et al., 2025). 

The remarkable chromosomal conservation observed in *Vellozia* contrasts sharply with the dynamic chromosomal fission, fusion, and extensive reshuffling documented in grass genomes (Poaceae), where recurrent polyploidization and non-homologous end joining drive rapid karyotypic divergence (*Oryza sativa*, *Zea mays*, *Sorghum bicolor*; PATERSON et al., 2004; WANG et al., 2012; MARKS et al., 2024). Instead, the genomic architecture of *Vellozia* mirrors the evolutionary stability observed in slow-evolving monocot lineages such as *Ananas comosus* (Bromeliaceae, Poales), which has maintained ancestral chromosomal blocks without whole-genome duplication since the ancient $\sigma$ and $\tau$ events (MING et al., 2015), and the basal orchid *Apostasia shenzhenica* (ZHANG et al., 2017).

Intragenomic self-synteny mapping uncovered a conserved, ancient collinear block connecting **Chromosome 1A and Chromosome 5A** in both *Vellozia* genomes. This syntenic paleoduplication reflects a shared whole-genome duplication (WGD) or large-scale segmental paleoduplication event that preceded the speciation and ecological divergence of *Vellozia*. In resurrection plants, ancient polyploidy has been repeatedly hypothesized to provide the initial genomic redundancy necessary for the co-option of seed-specific desiccation programs into vegetative tissues without compromising vegetative fitness (VANBUREN et al., 2015; COSTA et al., 2017; BONDI; MARKS; VANBUREN, 2023). 

From a landscape evolutionary perspective, this syntenic stasis aligns with the predictions of the Old Climatically-Buffered Infertile Landscape (OCBIL) theory (HOPPER, 2009; SILVEIRA et al., 2015). Ancient, climatically buffered montane massifs such as the Espinhaço Range experience reduced rates of catastrophic extinction and climatic turnover compared to younger, glaciated landscapes (FERNANDES et al., 2020; DE MELLO-SILVA, 2005). Under such prolonged ecological buffering, lineages frequently exhibit "karyotypic orthoselection" and structural stasis (JAMES, 1992; HOPPER, 2009), wherein adaptive evolution proceeds primarily through localized gene family copy-number expansions and fine-scale coding substitutions rather than macro-chromosomal rearrangements.

## 4.2 Evolutionary Trajectories of Gene Family Expansions/Contractions (CAFE) Driving Divergent Environmental Adaptation

Gene family evolutionary modeling via CAFE5 ($\lambda = 0.07456$ gains/losses per gene per million years, final $-\ln L = 31,073$) captured pronounced functional asymmetry in gene family expansions between *Vellozia tubiflora* (924 expanded families) and *Vellozia peripherica* (1,549 expanded families). These divergent genomic trajectories reflect the contrasting ecological demands of wide-ranging resurrection poikilohydry versus narrow-range evergreen drought avoidance in the *campos rupestres*.

### 4.2.1 Plastid Proteostasis and the Kinetic Rehydration Bottleneck in *Vellozia tubiflora*

*Vellozia tubiflora* is a widely distributed resurrection specialist capable of colonizing fully exposed, hyper-draining quartzitic pavements across South American savannas, enduring total foliar dehydration below 10% relative water content ($\Psi < -100\text{ MPa}$; ALCANTARA et al., 2015; TEODORO et al., 2021). During severe vegetative desiccation, poikilochlorophyllous resurrection plants undergo controlled degradation or dismantling of thylakoid membranes, light-harvesting complex II (LHCII) antennas, and photosynthetic reaction centers to prevent chlorophyll-mediated singlet oxygen and superoxide generation under saturating tropical solar irradiance (OUNG et al., 2021; COSTA et al., 2017).

However, this photoprotective dismantling establishes a severe physiological challenge upon re-watering: the **kinetic rehydration bottleneck** (WATSON; SOWDEN; JARVIS, 2018; SUN; JARVIS, 2023). In poikilochlorophyllous Velloziaceae (such as African *Xerophyta viscosa* [COSTA et al., 2017; FARRANT et al., 2007] and Brazilian *Barbacenia graminifolia* [VIEIRA et al., 2022]), photosynthetic competence requires 48 to 72 hours to re-establish due to the extensive requirement for organelle rebuilding. In ephemeral rock outcrop environments where rainwater drains within 12 to 48 hours, *V. tubiflora* must rapidly reconstruct its chloroplastic proteome and reassemble the photosynthetic apparatus to capitalize on transient moisture before substrate desiccation recurs (XIAO et al., 2015; MARKS et al., 2024).
Because over 95% of chloroplast proteins are encoded in the nuclear genome and translated in the cytosol as precursor polypeptides with N-terminal transit peptides, the rate of plastid recovery depends directly on the transport capacity of the chloroplast envelope translocon complexes (SUN; JARVIS, 2023; XING et al., 2025). The massive lineage-specific expansion of the chloroplast inner envelope protein-conducting channel (**OG0000016**, $+55$ genes, expanding from 6 ancestral copies to 61 in *Vtub* vs. 3 in *Vper*) and the intermediate translocation complex (**OG0000087**, $+22$ genes, expanding from 4 to 26 in *Vtub* vs. 2 in *Vper*) endows *V. tubiflora* with **87 total chloroplastic translocon loci**, compared to only 5 in *V. peripherica* (103 expanding genes, 13.60-fold enrichment, $q = 5.92 \times 10^{-114}$).

Recent high-resolution cryo-electron microscopy structures demonstrate that TOC (outer membrane) and TIC (inner membrane) channels assemble into continuous, coupled supercomplexes that span both envelope membranes (LIU et al., 2023; XING et al., 2025). In standard mesophytic models, abiotic stress induces oxidative damage and proteotoxic pore clogging of TOC/TIC channels, necessitating active clearance via the CHLORAD (Chloroplast-Associated Protein Degradation) ubiquitin-proteasome pathway (LING et al., 2021; SUN; JARVIS, 2023; NELLAEPALLI; LAU; JARVIS, 2023). In *V. tubiflora*, possessing an eighty-seven-locus translocon reservoir removes the kinetic bottleneck of protein translocation. This expanded channel architecture permits high-throughput concurrent import of Rubisco small subunits, chlorophyll a/b-binding proteins, stromal chaperones, and electron transport cofactors directly into the stroma within hours of re-watering (GAO et al., 2022; CHEN et al., 2023).

This expanded translocon infrastructure is functionally coupled to auxiliary plastid biogenesis and protection modules in *V. tubiflora*. Photoprotection during foliar drying and early rehydration is sustained by the expansion of chloroplastic NAD(P)H dehydrogenase complexes (**OG0000036**, $+29$ genes; **OG0000062**, $+18$ genes; 112 expanding genes, 11.25-fold enrichment, $q = 2.59 \times 10^{-101}$). These NDH complexes drive Cyclic Electron Transport (CET) around Photosystem I, establishing a transmembrane proton gradient ($\Delta\text{pH}$) that powers non-photochemical quenching (NPQ) and generates ATP without net NADPH accumulation, preventing over-reduction of the plastoquinone pool (OUNG et al., 2021; MARKS et al., 2024). Simultaneously, the structural core of the light-driven water-splitting machinery is safeguarded by expansions in Photosystem II reaction center PsbA/D1/D2 stabilizers (**OG0000069**, $+18$ genes; **OG0000119**, $+15$ genes; 24 expanding genes, 9.25-fold enrichment, $q = 1.43 \times 10^{-18}$) and the PSI assembly factor Ycf4 (**OG0000076**, $+16$ genes; 22 expanding genes, 13.87-fold enrichment, $q = 8.95 \times 10^{-25}$). Immediate translation of chloroplast-encoded subunits upon water influx is facilitated by nuclear genes encoding primary 16S rRNA-binding proteins (**OG0000104**, $+18$ genes; **OG0000042**, $+17$ genes; 47 expanding genes, 9.45-fold enrichment, $q = 3.08 \times 10^{-36}$). Complementing organellar proteostasis, expansions in acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC, **OG0000072**, $+17$ genes; 23 expanding genes, 13.87-fold enrichment, $q = 7.96 \times 10^{-26}$) and organonitrogen metabolism (**OG0000014**, $+51$ genes; **OG0000017**, $+42$ genes; 81 expanding genes, 13.06-fold enrichment, $q = 4.78 \times 10^{-85}$) support de novo membrane lipid repair and the accumulation of non-reducing oligosaccharides (RFOs) and amino acid derivatives (proline, GABA) that vitrify the cytoplasm during anhydrobiosis (TEODORO et al., 2021; VIEIRA et al., 2022). Finally, retrotransposon-derived transposases expanded markedly (**OG0000010**, $+43$ genes; **OG0000020**, $+33$ genes; **OG0000013**, $+30$ genes; 43 expanding genes, 6.70-fold enrichment, $q = 1.06 \times 10^{-24}$), mirroring observations in *Oropetium thomaeum* (VANBUREN et al., 2015) and *Selaginella lepidophylla* (VANBUREN et al., 2018), where dynamic mobile elements supply stress-responsive cis-regulatory promoter elements.

### 4.2.2 Mechanoperception, Cell Wall Surveillance, and Immune Sentinels in *Vellozia peripherica*

In contrast, *Vellozia peripherica* is a micro-endemic species restricted to shaded, moist quartzitic canyons and crevices of the Espinhaço Range (DE MELLO-SILVA, 2005; ALCANTARA et al., 2015). *V. peripherica* relies on dehydration avoidance (Phase II drought resistance; VOLAIRE et al., 2026), maintaining continuous foliar hydration, active stomatal conductance, and ongoing carbon assimilation throughout seasonal dry periods (VIEIRA et al., 2022, 2025). This evergreen habit requires continuous physiological surveillance and metabolic maintenance. Plasma membrane-bound LRR-RLKs (**OG0000012**, $+57$ genes; **OG0000021**, $+29$ genes; **OG0000025**, $+25$ genes; **OG0000060**, $+22$ genes; **OG0000029**, $+17$ genes; 110 expanding genes, 5.57-fold enrichment, $q = 1.99 \times 10^{-55}$) and arabinogalactan peptides (20 expanding genes, 8.75-fold enrichment, $q = 1.58 \times 10^{-16}$) function as primary sentinels monitoring cell wall integrity, pectin cross-linking, and transmembrane turgor pressure (COLIN et al., 2023; VERSLUES et al., 2023; VAHER et al., 2024). Under fluctuating diurnal transpiration and high VPD, evergreen leaves experience intense mechanical shear between the shrinking protoplast and rigid cell walls, requiring an expanded sensory kinase repertoire to coordinate wall remodeling and prevent hydraulic failure. Concurrently, while desiccation-tolerant species eliminate active pathogen hosts during dry periods by entering metabolic dormancy, evergreen plants maintain metabolically active, water-rich foliage that constitutes an attractive nutritional sink for herbivores and phytopathogens (FERNANDES et al., 2018, 2020). To safeguard foliar longevity, *V. peripherica* expanded canonical NB-LRR intracellular disease resistance sentinels (**OG0000050**, $+22$ genes; **OG0000049**, $+17$ genes; 25 expanding genes, 10.07-fold enrichment, $q = 2.76 \times 10^{-24}$) and aspartic peptidases of the A1 family (**OG0000052**, $+19$ genes). In addition, marked enrichment in Cytochrome P450 monooxygenases (88 expanding genes, 3.26-fold enrichment, $q = 4.01 \times 10^{-23}$) and UDP-glycosyltransferases (78 expanding genes, 4.97-fold enrichment, $q = 7.37 \times 10^{-35}$) underpins the continuous synthesis and glycosylation of protective secondary flavonoids and antioxidant phenylpropanoids under high UV irradiance. Cellular proteome homeostasis under water deficit is preserved by expanded ubiquitination target receptors (32 expanding genes, 7.49-fold enrichment, $q = 2.80 \times 10^{-22}$) and U-box domain-containing E3 ubiquitin ligases (**OG0000094**, $+16$ genes; 63 expanding genes, 6.41-fold enrichment, $q = 7.45 \times 10^{-37}$) that coordinate the selective degradation of damaged proteins, alongside pentatricopeptide repeat (PPR) proteins (**OG0000131**, $+16$ genes) that ensure continuous organellar post-transcriptional RNA editing.

These divergent genomic expansions demonstrate the physiological trade-off between productivity and survival (TEODORO et al., 2021; VOLAIRE et al., 2026): *V. tubiflora* invests in survival and repair machinery to endure complete metabolic dormancy, whereas *V. peripherica* expands sensory, regulatory, and defensive repertoires to sustain year-round vegetative productivity.

## 4.3 Selection Regimes (Ka/Ks) and Functional Divergence in Stress and Signaling Pathways

Pairwise codon substitution modeling across all **7,061 universal single-copy 1:1:1 orthologs** revealed a genome-wide distribution dominated by strong purifying selection (mean $Ka = 0.0339$, mean $Ks = 0.0838$, mean $Ka/Ks = 0.3542$, with 100% of ortholog pairs displaying $\omega < 1.0$). This pervasive purifying constraint reflects the deep evolutionary pressure preserving core transcriptional, translational, and primary metabolic networks across the two congeneric species.

Methodologically, restricting $Ka/Ks$ modeling to the 7,061 strict 1:1 orthogroups provides a critical baseline: it avoids the phylogenetic ambiguities, non-orthologous pairing, and temporal distortions inherent in calculating substitution rates across multi-copy, expanding paralogs. When these 1:1 orthologs are grouped into functional superfamilies based on conserved protein domains, an informative evolutionary hierarchy emerges. Core cellular components (such as chromatin histones [mean $\omega = 0.04 \pm 0.02$, 24 distinct 1:1 orthogroups], ribosomal structural proteins [mean $\omega = 0.08 \pm 0.03$, 101 distinct 1:1 orthogroups], and chloroplastic translocon channels [mean $\omega = 0.16 \pm 0.05$, 23 distinct 1:1 orthogroups]) exhibit extreme purifying constraint ($\omega < 0.20$), indicating that nearly all non-synonymous mutations in these essential macromolecular complexes are deleterious and purged by natural selection.

In sharp contrast, single-copy orthologs functioning at the environmental sensing and cellular boundary layers (such as secondary metabolite ABC transporters [mean $\omega = 0.54 \pm 0.12$, 87 distinct 1:1 orthogroups], cell wall remodeling pectinases [mean $\omega = 0.61 \pm 0.14$], and transmembrane Leucine-Rich Repeat receptor-like kinases [LRR-RLKs; mean $\omega = 0.68 \pm 0.15$, 204 distinct 1:1 orthogroups]) exhibit markedly elevated $Ka/Ks$ ratios. This elevated rate reflects relaxed functional constraint and positive diversifying selection acting on extracellular ligand-binding and regulatory domains.

This divergence in selective constraints between *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* reflects the fundamental contrast in their physiological states during water deficit (VOLAIRE et al., 2026; BONDI; MARKS; VANBUREN, 2023). When *V. tubiflora* enters anhydrobiosis, cellular metabolism ceases entirely, suspending active physiological demands on housekeeping pathways and allowing neutral sequence drift to accumulate in non-essential pathways during dormancy. Conversely, *V. peripherica* maintains active metabolism under water deficit, subjecting physiological regulators and sensory kinases to continuous purifying and adaptive selection to preserve turgor and prevent cellular collapse.

## 4.4 Synthesis, Bioinformatic Limitations, and the Bridge to High-Resolution Mechanosensory Channel Profiling

This large-scale comparative genomics investigation establishes that the profound ecological divergence between *Vellozia tubiflora* and *Vellozia peripherica* in the *campos rupestres* evolved within a remarkably stable macrosyntenic chromosomal architecture ($n = 9$, 297 collinear blocks). Ecological adaptation has been driven primarily by targeted gene family expansions and selective sequence modulation rather than gross chromosomal restructuring.

Several analytical avenues remain to extend these comparative insights. While self-synteny analysis clearly delineated the conserved Chr1A $\leftrightarrow$ Chr5A paleoduplication block, future computational investigations should implement whole-genome Gaussian mixture deconvolution on synonymous substitution rates ($Ks$) to resolve the exact geological timing ($T = Ks / 2r$) of this event relative to ancestral monocot whole-genome duplications ($\sigma$, $\tau$, $\rho$; PATERSON et al., 2004; MING et al., 2015). Additionally, integrating chromosome-level assemblies across Pandanales and outgroup monocots (*Apostasia*, *Dioscorea*, *Ananas*) will enable syntenic depth ratio profiling to reconstruct the ancestral Pandanales proto-karyotype.

Most critically, while whole-genome comparative genomics reveals downstream metabolic and receptor adaptations, it leaves open the fundamental question of early stress perception: how plant cells detect initial changes in membrane tension and osmotic stress within seconds of water deficit. Addressing this question requires transitioning from genome-wide macroevolutionary patterns to high-resolution structural, phylogenetic, transcriptional, and promoter dissection of specific mechanosensory channel families. This provides the direct foundation and motivation for **Chapter 2**, which is dedicated to the comprehensive evolutionary, structural, and expression characterization of the **OSCA (Hyperosmolality-Gated Calcium-Permeable Channel)** superfamily in *Vellozia tubiflora* and *Vellozia peripherica*.
