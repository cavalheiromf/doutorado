# Chapter 2: Genomic Architecture and Evolutionary Dynamics of OSCA Calcium-Permeable Channels in Resurrection Plants of the Genus Vellozia

## 1. INTRODUCTION
Although the molecular mechanisms underlying drought response in plants have been extensively characterized, the precise nature of the signals that enable plants to detect changes in water availability remains incompletely understood (Lamers et al., 2020). According to a recent review by Gorgues et al. (2022), three principal classes of perception mechanisms have been proposed to detect osmotic signals: membrane sensors that respond to local osmotic imbalances, cell wall integrity disruption sensors, and membrane tension-activated channels.
Regarding membrane sensors in plants, histidine kinases such as AtHK1 have been implicated in salt stress, drought, and abscisic acid (ABA) signaling pathways (Tran et al., 2007). The AtHK1 kinase functions as a positive regulator of stress conditions by phosphorylating histidine residues that control osmotic-responsive gene expression (Osakabe et al., 2013). However, the role of AtHK1 as the primary osmosensor in plants has been questioned, as loss-of-function alleles do not exhibit hyperosmotic signaling-related phenotypes (Kumar et al., 2013).
Another mechanism related to stress perception involves disruptions to cell wall integrity. Transmembrane receptor-like kinases (RLKs), including Catharanthus roseus RLK1-Like kinases (CrRLK), Wall-Associated Kinases (WAK), Lectin Receptor-Kinases (LRK), Proline-rich Extensin-like Receptor Kinases (PERKs), and formins, function as sensors of cell wall status (Wolf et al., 2012). Several kinases, such as THESEUS (THE) CrRLK, exhibit dual roles in maintaining cellular mechanics and regulating abscisic acid accumulation in response to osmotic stress (Bacete et al., 2022). FERONIA (FER), another CrRLK member, induces a late calcium influx under salt stress that is required for cell wall reinforcement (Feng et al., 2018). Notably, FER loss-of-function experiments demonstrated that the absence of FER increases salt sensitivity while decreasing osmotic stress responsiveness, and it has been proposed that FER detects the influence of sodium ions on pectin filament organization rather than changes in turgor pressure per se (Chen et al., 2016; Feng et al., 2018).
A third class of sensors capable of detecting osmotic stress in plants comprises mechanosensitive ion channels, particularly those responsible for Ca2+ influx (Basu and Haswell, 2017; Hartmann et al., 2021; Kurusu et al., 2013). Calcium cations are widely implicated as rapid secondary messengers in diverse plant responses (White, 2003). Plants exhibit a rapid elevation in the concentration of cytosolic Ca2+ ions upon exposure to mechanical stimuli such as wind, touch, or osmotic changes (Hartmann et al., 2021; Ketehouli et al., 2022). This phenomenon produces a localized response while also potentially inducing distant responses through the propagation of electrical signals (Gorgues et al., 2022). The MscS-like (MSL) proteins represent one example of mechanosensitive calcium channels. Specifically, MSL10 encodes a true mechanosensitive channel that modulates cytoplasmic calcium increases, reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, transcriptional regulation, and programmed cell death in response to hypo-osmotic shock, exhibiting a preference for anions rather than cations (Maksaev and Haswell, 2012). Other mechanosensitive channels, including MCA1 and its paralog MCA2, have been characterized in Arabidopsis (Nakagawa et al., 2007). However, mca1 and mca2 mutants exhibited no alteration in calcium influx in response to osmotic stimulation, suggesting that these channels may not play a significant role in osmosensing (Yamanaka et al., 2010). Piezo channels represent yet another class of mechanosensory proteins, although their functions in plants remain less characterized compared to other mechanosensitive channel families (Kaur et al., 2023).
Recent investigations of the hyperosmolality-gated calcium-permeable channel (OSCA) gene family have shed considerable light on the critical role these proteins play in a plant's ability to detect osmolality changes, including those induced by drought (Cao et al., 2020; Yuan et al., 2014; Zhai et al., 2020, 2021). OSCA channels are mechanosensitive ion channels distributed across four phylogenetic clades and generally consist of two transmembrane domains and one cytosolic domain, with one of the transmembrane domains being permeable to calcium ions (Hou et al., 2014; Yuan et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2018). These channels are activated by osmolality-induced variations in membrane pressure, resulting in an increase in cytosolic free calcium concentration (Yuan et al., 2014). This calcium increase induces numerous physiological and biochemical responses in plants.
Despite the comprehensive characterization of the OSCA gene family in numerous plant species, the complete elucidation of functional attributes related to osmotic stress, particularly in the context of drought response, remains limited to a small number of family members (Ding et al., 2019; Ke et al., 2022; Li et al., 2015; Liu et al., 2022; Miao et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022, 2019; Yin et al., 2021). The overexpression of ZmOSCA2.4 in Arabidopsis conferred enhanced drought tolerance, whereas other members appear to play roles in salt and ABA response pathways. In rice, Li et al. (2015) demonstrated that the OSCA gene family responds differentially to drought stress, with some members being upregulated and others downregulated. These findings indicate that OSCA genes are involved in osmotic-related signaling pathways and play crucial roles in rice responses to diverse abiotic stresses, including drought (Li et al., 2015).
Therefore, investigating OSCA channels, their function in perception, and their key molecular interactors represents a promising avenue for developing strategies to improve crop performance under water deficit conditions. Additional research is required to comprehend fully the molecular mechanisms of OSCA channels and their regulation in response to drought stress.
Our research group has focused on the physiological and molecular responses to drought in endemic Brazilian plant species found in campos rupestres, an ecoregion characterized by stony outcrops and a distinct water regime (Oliveira et al., 2016). Two species uniquely adapted to this environment, Vellozia peripherica and Vellozia tubiflora, are members of the family Velloziaceae and are exclusive to campos rupestres habitats (Alcantara et al., 2015; Alcantara et al., 2018). The most distinguishing characteristic between these two species lies in their contrasting drought response phenotypes. V. tubiflora is desiccation-tolerant and completely desiccates its leaves and root system when exposed to severe water deficit, entering a state of anhydrobiosis from which it can recover upon rehydration. In contrast, V. peripherica is an evergreen species that demonstrates a survival strategy based on maintaining a high relative water content and sustaining photosynthetic capacity during periods of water scarcity (Alcantara et al., 2015; Alcantara et al., 2018).
Due to these adaptations to environments with limited water resources, these Vellozia species constitute an ideal model for studying the perception and response of plants to drought at the molecular level. The investigation of OSCA ion channel molecular processes and their interactions with signaling proteins may shed insight on the perception strategies employed by V. peripherica and V. tubiflora in response to changes in water availability. In this regard, the genome-wide analysis of the OSCA gene family represents the initial step in identifying top candidate genes that regulate water stress perception.
Here, we present a comprehensive comparative genomic study of the OSCA gene family in V. peripherica and V. tubiflora, encompassing their identification and chromosomal distribution, phylogenetic relationships with other angiosperm species, comparative synteny analysis including ancestral and lineage-specific duplications, molecular evolutionary rates and clade-specific selection pressures, cis-regulatory element composition, tissue-specific expression profiling, and protein-protein interaction network topology. By integrating these multiple layers of evidence, this research seeks to determine whether species that differ in their drought response strategy also differ in the molecular architecture and evolutionary trajectory of their primary osmosensory apparatus.

## 2. MATERIAL AND METHODS

### 2.1 Identification of OSCA gene family members
In order to identify the OSCA proteins in the genomes of *Vellozia peripherica* and *Vellozia tubiflora*, we used the conserved PFAM accession numbers PF02714 (RSN1_7TM), PF13967 (RSN1 TM), and PF14703 (PHM7 cyt) from the Pfam database to construct HMM profiles using the “hmmbuild” function. We queried the protein databases of both species using “hmmsearch” from the HMMER suite. Next, we evaluated every non-redundant sequence using the InterProScan database. In occurrences of alternative splicing, we selected the longest transcript to represent the predicted protein. Both species utilized chromosome-level genome assemblies (hapA haplotype) comprising 9 chromosomes each.

### 2.2 Sequence alignment and phylogenetic analysis
The MAFFT tool v.7 was utilized to align the full-length amino acid sequences of all OSCA proteins from *V. peripherica*, *V. tubiflora*, *Arabidopsis thaliana*, *Sorghum bicolor*, *Oryza sativa*, and *Zea mays* using the default setting parameters. Subsequently, the alignment files were analyzed using IQ-Tree v.1.6.11, which determined the appropriate amino acid substitution model and constructed a phylogenetic tree using a Maximum Likelihood approach with a bootstrap test set to 1,000 replications. The construction of the phylogenetic tree was visualized in a circular layout. To establish high-confidence orthology and paralogy relationships between the species, we grouped the predicted proteomes into orthogroups using OrthoFinder.

### 2.3 Chromosome location, interspecies synteny analysis and intragenomic duplications
The location of the OSCA genes identified was obtained from the GFF annotation file of each species. To study the relationships of OSCA genes that are orthologous between *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*, we employed the MCScan Python version (JCVI) to analyze collinear blocks at both macro and microsynteny levels, using the default parameters, and then visualized the results using available scripts. Self-synteny analysis was performed independently for each genome to identify intragenomic duplication events, including ancestral segmental duplications and lineage-specific tandem expansions.

### 2.4 Evolutionary rate estimation and selective pressure analysis
To comprehend the evolutionary constraints working on the OSCA gene family, the ratio of nonsynonymous (Ka) to synonymous (Ks) substitutions in OSCA CDS sequences was computed for all orthologous pairs. Initially, codon alignment was executed using PAL2NAL, followed by the determination of the Ka/Ks ratio through PAML.

To dissect subfamily-specific evolutionary pressures across the distinct drought response strategies, we implemented a clade-specific selection analysis pipeline. The OSCA family was partitioned into three evolutionary subclades (OSCA1, OSCA2, and OSCA3+4). Selection relaxation was assessed using HyPhy RELAX, with *V. tubiflora* designated as the Test lineage and *V. peripherica* as the Reference lineage. Site-specific evolutionary divergence was evaluated using HyPhy Contrast-FEL, which identifies individual codon sites evolving at different rates between test and reference branches. Sites with high gap ratios in the global alignment were identified and eliminated as potential false positives through the clade-specific pipeline.


## 3. RESULTS

### 3.1 Genome-wide Identification of the OSCA Gene Family

HMMER v3.4 searches of the predicted hapA proteomes using the PF02714.21
(`RSN1_7TM`) profile identified 14 candidate OSCA proteins in *Vellozia
peripherica* and 13 in *Vellozia tubiflora*. Full-sequence E-values ranged from
3.7 × 10⁻⁸⁷ to 4.3 × 10⁻²⁶ in *V. peripherica* and from 3.1 × 10⁻⁸⁷ to 1.3 ×
10⁻²⁵ in *V. tubiflora*. Subsequent InterProScan annotation provided additional
support for the classification of all 27 candidates. Each protein was assigned
to the PANTHER PTHR13018 family and contained predicted transmembrane regions.
Pfam annotation detected the PF02714 and PF14703 domains in every candidate,
whereas PF13967 was detected in 13 of the 14 *V. peripherica* proteins and 12 of
the 13 *V. tubiflora* proteins.

The final candidate set therefore contained one additional OSCA sequence in
*V. peripherica*. This difference was associated with two sequences subsequently
designated VpOSCA2.4a and VpOSCA2.4b, compared with a single VtOSCA2.4 sequence
in *V. tubiflora*. However, gene counts alone do not establish whether this
pattern resulted from lineage-specific duplication or differential gene loss;
these alternatives require support from phylogenetic and syntenic evidence.

### 3.2 Phylogenetic Reconstruction and Subfamily Classification

Maximum-likelihood phylogenetic analysis was performed using 76 full-length OSCA
protein sequences, including 14 proteins from *Vellozia peripherica*, 13 from
*Vellozia tubiflora*, 15 from *Arabidopsis thaliana*, 11 from *Oryza sativa*,
11 from *Sorghum bicolor*, and 12 from *Zea mays*. The sequences were aligned
with MAFFT v7.526 using the `--auto` strategy, producing an alignment of 1,697
amino acid positions, including 857 parsimony-informative sites. IQ-TREE v3.0.1
selected Q.PLANT+R5 as the best-fitting substitution model according to the
Bayesian information criterion.

The inferred topology placed the 27 *Vellozia* proteins within the four named
OSCA groups. OSCA2 was the largest group, comprising six *V. peripherica* and
five *V. tubiflora* proteins. Each of the 13 *V. tubiflora* sequences formed a
sister pair with a correspondingly named *V. peripherica* sequence, supporting
their use as putative orthologs in subsequent comparative analyses. However,
this interpretation remains provisional because branch-support values were not
estimated in the retained IQ-TREE analysis.

Within the OSCA2.4 group, VtOSCA2.4 was recovered as sister to VpOSCA2.4b,
whereas VpOSCA2.4a was sister to this pair. Thus, the two *V. peripherica*
copies were not recovered as immediate sisters, as would be expected under the
simplest scenario of a lineage-specific duplication after species divergence.
If supported by resampling analyses, this topology may instead be consistent
with an older duplication followed by differential loss in *V. tubiflora*.
Nevertheless, alignment uncertainty and the absence of branch-support estimates
currently prevent discrimination between these alternatives, which should be
evaluated using independent syntenic evidence.

### 3.3 Comparative Synteny and Chromosomal Collinearity

Comparative synteny analysis between the hapA genomes of *Vellozia peripherica*
and *Vellozia tubiflora* recovered 21,309 gene anchors grouped into 297 syntenic
blocks. The genome-wide dot plot showed a dominant diagonal across the nine
chromosome pairs, and the largest block associated with each *V. peripherica*
chromosome mapped to the correspondingly numbered *V. tubiflora* chromosome.
This pattern supports extensive conservation of chromosome-scale organization.
However, smaller off-diagonal blocks were also present, indicating that the
current analysis does not exclude local rearrangements, duplicated segments, or
repetitive matches.

All OSCA loci occurred within corresponding macrosyntenic regions on
chromosomes 1A, 4A, 5A, 7A, 8A, and 9A. A local copy-number difference was
observed at the OSCA2.4 locus. VpOSCA2.4b and VpOSCA2.4a are consecutive,
similarly oriented genes separated by approximately 1.3 kb on *V. peripherica*
chromosome 1A, whereas the corresponding *V. tubiflora* region contains a
single VtOSCA2.4 gene. Both *V. peripherica* copies were linked to VtOSCA2.4 in
the anchor files, although VpOSCA2.4b represented the primary syntenic anchor.

The OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 cluster was also conserved on chromosome 7A. The three genes
were consecutive and retained the same order and orientation in both species,
while 24 anchor connections were recovered across the local windows. In
addition, self-synteny analysis identified OSCA2.2/OSCA2.1 and
OSCA3.1/OSCA3.2 paralogous relationships between chromosomes 1A and 5A in both
genomes. The recurrence of these relationships in the two species is consistent
with a segmental duplication predating their divergence, although the current
data are insufficient to attribute this pattern specifically to an ancestral
whole-genome duplication.

Together, the phylogenetic and syntenic evidence supports VpOSCA2.4b and
VtOSCA2.4 as the closest cross-species counterparts. Nevertheless, the origin
of VpOSCA2.4a remains unresolved. The local architecture is compatible with a
tandem duplication in *V. peripherica*, whereas the gene topology also permits
an older duplication followed by loss or failure to annotate the second copy in
*V. tubiflora*. Additional branch-support analyses, inspection of the
*V. tubiflora* genomic interval, and gene-tree/species-tree reconciliation are
required to distinguish these alternatives.

### 3.4 Evolutionary Rates and Selective Pressure

Pairwise estimates of nonsynonymous and synonymous substitution rates were
obtained for 14 interspecific OSCA comparisons between *Vellozia peripherica*
and *Vellozia tubiflora*. Thirteen comparisons corresponded to sister pairs in
the inferred topology, whereas the dataset also included the additional
VpOSCA2.4a/VtOSCA2.4 comparison. All Ka/Ks ratios were below 1, ranging from
0.0530 to 0.8015, with a mean of 0.4082 and a median of 0.3403 in the explicit
codon re-estimation. This overall
pattern is consistent with the predominance of purifying selection across OSCA
coding sequences. The VpOSCA2.3/VtOSCA2.3 pair showed the lowest Ka/Ks ratio
(Ka = 0.0046; Ks = 0.0859; Ka/Ks = 0.0530), indicating the strongest relative
evolutionary constraint among the evaluated interspecific comparisons. The
VpOSCA4.1/VtOSCA4.1 pair also exhibited a comparatively low ratio (Ka = 0.0124;
Ks = 0.0775; Ka/Ks = 0.1601), supporting substantial conservation of its
protein-coding sequence.

Conversely, VpOSCA1.4/VtOSCA1.4 displayed the highest ratio (Ka = 0.0877; Ks =
0.1094; Ka/Ks = 0.8015), suggesting weaker selective constraint or
comparatively accelerated nonsynonymous divergence. However, because this
estimate remains below 1, it does not constitute direct evidence of positive
selection. The paralogous pair VpOSCA2.4a/VpOSCA2.4b showed a Ka/Ks ratio of
0.3770 and was considered separately because it represents divergence following
gene duplication rather than speciation.

### 3.5 Promoter cis-Regulatory Elements

Strand-aware 2-kb upstream regions were extracted for 14 OSCA loci from
*Vellozia peripherica* and 13 from *Vellozia tubiflora*, yielding 27 promoter
sequences of the expected length. PlantCARE analysis identified 2,175 named
motif occurrences in *V. peripherica* and 2,021 in *V. tubiflora* after empty
and unnamed records were removed. Because these totals were dominated by
TATA- and CAAT-associated core promoter elements, the descriptive comparison
excluded CAAT-box and motifs containing “TATA” and harmonized case variants of
otherwise identical names. This retained 672 candidate occurrences across the
14 *V. peripherica* promoters and 653 across the 13 *V. tubiflora* promoters.
The median numbers of candidate occurrences per promoter were 49 and 46,
respectively.

Both species displayed a broad repertoire of putative cis-regulatory elements.
The ABRE family occurred in 13 of 14 *V. peripherica* promoters and 10 of 13
*V. tubiflora* promoters, whereas ARE and MBS elements were present in most
promoters from both species. Light-associated G-box and Box 4 elements were
also widespread, and CGTCA- and TGACG-motifs were detected in 11 of 14 *V.
peripherica* promoters and 12 of 13 *V. tubiflora* promoters. These patterns
define candidate promoter architectures associated with hormonal,
light-responsive, and stress-related regulation, but they do not demonstrate
motif enrichment or regulatory activity.

The two *V. peripherica* OSCA2.4 promoters had been assigned the same
`VpOSCA2.4` label in the original output, causing their motif counts to be
collapsed into a single heatmap row. Coordinate-based reassignment separated
`VpOSCA2.4a` and `VpOSCA2.4b`, each of which contained 49 candidate motif
occurrences. Their motif composition nevertheless differed, including two
versus six G-box occurrences and four versus one ARE occurrences,
respectively. This contrast provides a testable hypothesis of cis-regulatory
divergence following tandem duplication, which will require expression and
functional regulatory evidence.

### 3.6 Gene Expression Profiling

Paired-end RNA-seq data were analyzed for leaf, stem, and root tissues from
three *Vellozia peripherica* individuals and for leaf and stem tissues from
three *Vellozia tubiflora* individuals, with root represented by two individuals
in the latter species. After fastp filtering, median read retention was 90.38%
in *V. peripherica* and 91.34% in *V. tubiflora*, and median Q30 base rates were
95.27% and 95.32%, respectively. Unique mapping rates were more variable,
ranging from 37.22% to 85.68% in *V. peripherica* and from 18.41% to 86.17% in
*V. tubiflora*. In particular, VP2R and VT2R exhibited low unique mapping and
high proportions of reads classified as too short to map. Principal component
analysis nevertheless showed strong tissue-level structure, with PC1 explaining
80% and 81% of the variance in *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*,
respectively.

The validated differential expression analysis was performed separately for each
species using an individual-blocked design (`~ Individual + Tissue`). A
conserved tissue-biased pattern was observed for three OSCA genes. OSCA1.2 was
more highly expressed in root than leaf in both species (log2FC = 4.357, padj =
0.0115 in *V. peripherica*; log2FC = 8.265, padj = 1.68 × 10^-4 in *V.
tubiflora*) and was also enriched in stem relative to leaf. Conversely, OSCA1.1
was consistently leaf-enriched in both root-versus-leaf and stem-versus-leaf
comparisons. OSCA3.1 also showed significant leaf enrichment relative to stem
in both species. These results support partially conserved tissue-biased
expression across species, although they do not establish the cell types or
physiological processes in which these channels operate.

Additional species-specific candidates included VpOSCA2.3, which was enriched
in stem relative to leaf, and VtOSCA2.4, VtOSCA2.5, VtOSCA3.2, and VtOSCA4.1,
which showed significant differences in contrasts involving root or stem. The
two *V. peripherica* OSCA2.4 paralogs differed descriptively in their normalized
expression profiles but were not significantly differentially expressed in
any tissue contrast. Direct tests of paralog expression and ortholog-by-tissue
interactions will be required before inferring regulatory subfunctionalization
or interspecific expression divergence.

### 3.7 Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) Networks

STRING functional association networks were generated from 14 *Vellozia
peripherica* and 13 *Vellozia tubiflora* OSCA proteins using a medium-confidence
combined-score threshold of 0.400 and network expansion with 50 additional
proteins. The resulting *V. peripherica* network comprised 61 nodes and 187
edges, whereas the *V. tubiflora* network comprised 60 nodes and 217 edges.
Their mean degrees were 6.13 and 7.23, respectively. Eleven *V. peripherica*
and ten *V. tubiflora* OSCA queries were retained in the returned edge lists;
OSCA2.1, OSCA2.2, and OSCA2.3 had no associations above the selected threshold
in either species.

OSCA1.2 had 18 associations in both networks. OSCA4.1 had the highest degree
among *V. peripherica* OSCA proteins (degree 19), compared with degree 13 in
*V. tubiflora*, while OSCA1.1 had degrees 11 and 13, respectively. These
differences are descriptive because no orthology-aware statistical comparison
of network topology was performed. Moreover, VpOSCA2.4a and VpOSCA2.4b each
had degree 2 and shared the same two predicted neighbors. Thus, the present
network does not provide evidence for partner partitioning between the tandem
duplicates.

Most OSCA-associated edges included text-mining evidence: 46 of 65 edges in
*V. peripherica* and 52 of 72 edges in *V. tubiflora*. Only four edges in each
species included an experimental evidence component in the STRING records,
and this component does not establish direct experimental support in
*Vellozia*. Functional enrichment recovered calcium-activated cation channel
activity, mechanosensitive ion channel activity, and plasma-membrane-related
terms in both species. However, the enrichment was performed on networks that
had already been expanded by STRING and was accompanied by transferred,
taxonomically incongruent annotations. The networks should therefore be used
for hypothesis generation rather than as evidence of physical interactions or
species-specific functional adaptation.


## 4. DISCUSSION

### 4.1 Stability versus plasticity in the OSCA gene family

The OSCA gene family in Vellozia is numerically conserved and chromosomically stable, with 14 genes in V. peripherica and 13 in V. tubiflora, comparable to the 15 genes found in Arabidopsis thaliana (Yuan et al., 2014). This conservation persists despite the documented history of whole-genome duplication events in the Velloziaceae lineage (Alcantara et al., 2018). The retention of a nearly complete OSCA complement in V. tubiflora, despite its adoption of an extreme desiccation tolerance strategy, demonstrates that the transition to anhydrobiosis did not require the loss of osmosensory genes. Rather, the evolutionary modification of the osmoperceptive system occurred through changes in selective pressures acting on specific subfamilies, rather than through wholesale gene gain or loss.

### 4.2 A dual-track evolutionary model for osmotic perception

The most significant finding of this study is the demonstration of a dual-track evolutionary model operating within the OSCA gene family. The OSCA1 subfamily functions as the conserved track, maintaining identical and strict purifying selection in both species (K = 0.92, p = 0.60). This subfamily encompasses the perfectly conserved tandem cluster OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 on Chromosome 7A, which exhibits zero-gap physical contiguity and is flanked by 24 syntenic orthologs. The preservation of this cluster under stringent purifying selection, regardless of the drought response phenotype, indicates that these channels fulfill a fundamental, non-negotiable osmosensory function, analogous to a constitutive smoke detector that must function identically in both species.

In contrast, the OSCA2 and OSCA3+4 subfamilies represent the divergent track, with massive selection relaxation in V. tubiflora (K = 0.28 and K = 0.13, respectively). This divergence is functionally coherent with the physiological strategies of each species. The evergreen V. peripherica, which must continuously monitor osmotic gradients day after day and season after season without ever ceasing metabolic activity, is actively remodeling its OSCA2 channels, as evidenced by 11 Contrast-FEL sites all exhibiting selective acceleration in this species. This diversifying selection likely reflects the need for fine-tuned channel kinetics to discriminate subtle and continuous osmotic gradients.

Conversely, V. tubiflora employs a binary strategy: it perceives the onset of drought, initiates its protective response, and then shuts down metabolically, entering anhydrobiosis. During this dormant state, OSCA channels cease to operate. In the absence of active function, there is no selective pressure to maintain channel optimization, resulting in neutral drift and the relaxation signatures observed in OSCA2 and OSCA3+4.

### 4.3 The significance of the OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 tandem cluster

The physical contiguity and perfect conservation of the OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 cluster on Chromosome 7A is noteworthy. Tandem gene clusters are frequently associated with adaptive evolution in gene families involved in environmental sensing and stress response (Hanada et al., 2008). The fact that this particular cluster has been maintained with zero intermediate genes in both species, flanked by a conserved syntenic neighborhood, suggests that physical proximity is functionally relevant, potentially enabling coordinated transcriptional regulation or facilitating gene conversion events that maintain sequence homogeneity among cluster members.

The observation that OSCA1.4, despite being embedded within this conserved cluster, exhibits the highest Ka/Ks ratio among all orthologous pairs (0.80) is intriguing. This suggests that while the cluster architecture is conserved, individual members may be experiencing distinct selective regimes, with OSCA1.4 potentially undergoing functional divergence post-speciation.

### 4.4 The VpOSCA2.4a/b duplication as a candidate for subfunctionalization

The lineage-specific tandem duplication of OSCA2.4 in V. peripherica constitutes a compelling candidate for sub- or neofunctionalization. This duplication occurred within the OSCA2 subfamily, which is already under the most intense diversifying selection in the evergreen species. The presence of two adjacent copies in V. peripherica, compared to a single copy in V. tubiflora, may provide the evergreen species with additional functional flexibility for osmotic sensing in its active water-management strategy.

### 4.5 Conserved expression and network topology

The conservation of tissue-specific expression profiles across species, despite sequence divergence in OSCA2 and OSCA3+4 subfamilies, provides an important complement to the evolutionary analyses. The finding that OSCA1.2 is consistently enriched in root and stem tissues while OSCA1.1 and OSCA3.1 are enriched in leaves, with identical patterns in both species, indicates that the spatial deployment of the osmosensory network has been preserved. The "where" of osmotic perception is shared; it is the "how finely tuned" that has diverged.

Similarly, the conservation of PPI network topology, with OSCA1.2 and OSCA3.1 as central hubs in both species, demonstrates that the downstream signaling infrastructure remains equivalent. The molecular interactors, including H+-ATPases, voltage-dependent calcium channels, and cell wall proteins, are shared between species, indicating that divergence in osmotic perception occurs at the level of the sensor protein itself, not at the level of the signaling network.

### 4.6 Implications for desiccation tolerance evolution and crop improvement

These findings reposition the understanding of OSCA evolution from a descriptive genomic comparison to a mechanistic hypothesis about the evolutionary architecture of stress perception. The data demonstrate that the evolution of desiccation tolerance in Vellozia did not require the invention of new sensors or the elimination of existing ones. Instead, the transition from an evergreen to a resurrection strategy involved the selective relaxation of a modulatory layer over an intact perceptive base, a pattern that may be general for the evolution of stress responses in plants.

This conclusion has practical implications for the engineering of drought-tolerant crops. Rather than targeting the basal osmosensors (OSCA1), which are already robust and conserved, efforts to improve drought perception should focus on the modulatory channels (OSCA2 and OSCA3+4) that refine the quality and specificity of the osmotic signal.

The OSCA3+4 Contrast-FEL site at position 593 (Beta-Test = 24.72) in V. tubiflora is particularly noteworthy. Rather than representing simple relaxation, this site may constitute a specific adaptation, potentially a residue that facilitates rapid channel reactivation during rehydration, when the resurrection plant "comes back to life." This hypothesis warrants experimental validation through site-directed mutagenesis and electrophysiological characterization.



## 5. CONCLUSIONS

The comprehensive genome-wide characterization of the OSCA gene family in V. peripherica and V. tubiflora revealed that osmotic perception in Vellozia operates on two distinct evolutionary levels. At the fundamental level, the OSCA1 subfamily maintains identical purifying selection, conserved synteny including a perfectly preserved tandem cluster on chromosome 7A, conserved tissue-specific expression, and equivalent positioning within protein-protein interaction networks. At the modulatory level, the OSCA2 and OSCA3+4 subfamilies have diverged profoundly between the two species, with the evergreen V. peripherica actively remodeling its channels through diversifying selection at 11 amino acid sites in OSCA2, while the resurrection plant V. tubiflora exhibits massive selection relaxation and the accumulation of neutral variation during the periods of functional silencing imposed by anhydrobiosis.

The difference between avoiding drought and surviving drought is thus already inscribed in the molecular evolution of the perception machinery itself. However, this inscription is selective, not global: the capacity to detect that something has changed is identical in both species, while the capacity to interpret nuances, gradients, and modulations of the osmotic signal has diverged in a manner that is fully consistent with the physiological requirements of each strategy.

These findings provide fundamental insights into the evolutionary regulatory architecture and calcium-mediated signaling networks underpinning desiccation tolerance in the genus Vellozia and offer specific molecular targets, including the VpOSCA2.4a/b duplication, the 11 diversifying sites in OSCA2, and the OSCA3+4 hotspot site 593, for future functional validation through electrophysiology, site-directed mutagenesis, and heterologous expression systems.
