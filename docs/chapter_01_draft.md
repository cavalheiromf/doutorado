# CHAPTER 1

## Comparative Genomics and Evolutionary Dynamics of Gene Families in *Vellozia* (*Velloziaceae*)

---

## MATERIAL AND METHODS

### Genomic Datasets and Sequence Pre-processing

Chromosome-scale genome assemblies and associated structural annotations for the evergreen drought-avoidant *Vellozia peripherica* and the desiccation-tolerant resurrection species *Vellozia tubiflora* (both $2n = 18$, haplotype A, version 1) were obtained from institutional repositories. High-confidence predicted protein-coding sequences and deduced peptide datasets were used as the primary substrate for all comparative analyses. To establish a standardized evolutionary baseline and prevent artificial inflation of gene family sizes caused by alternative splicing isoforms, genomic datasets were systematically pre-processed to retain exclusively the canonical (longest) primary transcript per locus. Protein sequences containing premature stop codons or lacking canonical start/stop codons were filtered out. As an angiosperm outgroup for phylogenomic calibration and gene family dynamics, the canonical proteome of *Arabidopsis thaliana* (Araport11/TAIR10) was retrieved from Ensembl Plants and processed under identical quality-filtering criteria.

### Orthology Inference and Species Tree Reconstruction

Genome-wide orthology inference was conducted using OrthoFinder v2.5.5 [@Emms_2019]. All-versus-all sequence similarity searches across the proteomes of *V. peripherica*, *V. tubiflora*, and *A. thaliana* were performed using DIAMOND v2.1.8 [@Buchfink_2021] in high-sensitivity mode with an $E$-value threshold of $1 \times 10^{-5}$. Sequence graph partitioning and orthogroup clustering were achieved through the Markov Cluster (MCL) algorithm implemented with an inflation parameter of 1.5. 

For each identified orthogroup containing at least four sequences, multiple sequence alignments were generated using FAMSA v2.2.2 [@Deorowicz_2016], and individual maximum-likelihood gene trees were inferred using FastTree v2.1.11 [@Price_2010] under the Whelan and Goldman (WAG) substitution model with CAT approximation. Rooted species trees were reconstructed from the comprehensive set of gene tree topologies using the Species Tree from All Genes (STAG) algorithm and rooted via the Species Tree Root Inference from Duplication Events (STRIDE) pipeline. Orthologous relationships between gene pairs were categorized into one-to-one (1:1), one-to-many (1:m), and many-to-many (m:m) orthology classes, establishing the orthogroup abundance matrix for downstream evolutionary modeling.

### Gene Family Expansion and Contraction Analysis

Dynamic evolutionary changes in gene family sizes across the phylogenetic framework were analyzed using the Computational Analysis of gene Family Evolution tool (CAFE5) [@Mendes_2020]. The orthogroup count matrix generated by OrthoFinder was filtered to exclude non-informative clusters, including lineages with identical counts across all taxa, singletons, and excessively large gene families ($\ge 100$ genes) that could introduce severe stochastic noise in maximum-likelihood parameter estimation.

To satisfy the assumption of ultrametric divergence required by birth-death demographic models, the STAG-inferred species tree was time-calibrated using penalized likelihood implemented in treePL v1.0 [@Smith_2012], incorporating secondary node calibration constraints for the divergence of monocot and eudicot lineages. Global birth-death parameter estimation ($\lambda$, representing the uniform rate of gene duplication and loss per gene per million years) was calculated using a single-parameter homogeneous model. Branch-specific and family-specific $p$-values were computed through Monte Carlo simulations ($N = 10,000$ replicates), classifying orthogroups with $p < 0.05$ as exhibiting statistically significant expansion or contraction events. Functionally expanded orthogroups in *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* were subsequently subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway overrepresentation analyses applying the Benjamini–Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR $< 0.05$) correction.

### Genome-wide Synteny, Collinearity, and Polyploidy Evaluation

Chromosomal architecture, macro-synteny, and micro-collinearity between the nine pseudo-chromosomes of *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* were evaluated using the MCScan pipeline implemented in the Python-based JCVI library v1.6.5 [@Tang_2008]. Pairwise local sequence alignments were calculated using LAST v1650 with sequence scoring parameters optimized for angiosperm comparative genomics. Synteny blocks were defined requiring a minimum of five collinear gene anchors, allowing a maximum distance of 25 intervening non-collinear genes between adjacent anchor points. 

Syntenic anchor quality was filtered using a reciprocal best-hit $C$-score threshold $> 0.70$. Genomic rearrangements, including inversions, translocations, and segmental duplications, were visualized through macro-synteny dot-plots, dual-synteny ribbon layouts, and localized micro-synteny alignment panels. To evaluate the occurrence and retention of ancestral whole-genome duplication (WGD) events (such as the paleoduplication between chromosomes Chr1A and Chr5A), intra-genomic self-synteny searches were executed independently for each *Vellozia* assembly.

### Estimation of Evolutionary Rates and Natural Selection Pressures

To quantify the intensity and direction of selective pressures across orthologous gene pairs, genome-wide non-synonymous ($K_a$) and synonymous ($K_s$) nucleotide substitution rates were calculated. For each 1:1 orthologous pair identified between *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*, peptide sequences were aligned using MAFFT v7.520, and the corresponding coding sequence (CDS) alignments were guided by protein templates using PAL2NAL v14 [@Suyama_2006] to preserve reading frames and codon boundaries. Gaps and ambiguous codons were purged from the alignments.

Pairwise $K_a$, $K_s$, and selective constraint ratios ($\omega = K_a/K_s$) were estimated using the Yang–Nielsen method (YN00) implemented in KaKs_Calculator v3.0 [@Zhang_2006] and cross-validated with the PAML codeml v4.9 package [@Yang_2007]. Pairs with saturated synonymous substitutions ($K_s > 2.0$) or abnormal alignment artifacts ($K_s < 0.005$) were excluded from global distribution modeling. The distribution of $K_s$ values was plotted to characterize the divergence history between both *Vellozia* lineages and to detect potential multi-peak signatures associated with ancestral polyploidization events. Orthologs exhibiting $\omega > 1.0$ were identified as candidate loci under positive selection, whereas genes with $\omega < 0.1$ were designated as evolving under stringent purifying selection.
