## Abstract

Closely related species displaying contrasting survival strategies provide an ideal comparative system for investigating how genomic architecture, gene-family turnover, and coding-sequence evolution accompany physiological divergence. In the ancient quartzitic *campos rupestres* of eastern Brazil, congeners of the genus *Vellozia* (Pandanales) exhibit divergent drought adaptations: the evergreen shrub *Vellozia peripherica* delays tissue dehydration, whereas the sympatric resurrection plant *Vellozia tubiflora* survives foliar desiccation. Here, we present a chromosome-scale genome assembly for *V. peripherica* (493.30 Mb, scaffold N50 = 64.68 Mb, 98.05% anchored into nine pseudomolecules) and compare it with the companion reference genome of *V. tubiflora* within a fossil-calibrated phylogenomic framework. Macrosynteny and syntenic depth were strictly conserved across all nine chromosomes without evidence of recent whole-genome duplication. However, macroevolutionary birth-death modeling revealed marked asymmetry in terminal copy-number turnover, with *V. peripherica* exhibiting 217 expanding families compared with 50 in *V. tubiflora*. This expansion in *V. peripherica* was driven predominantly by local duplications (tandem and proximal events accounted for 66.47% of classified models), with expanded families enriched for cell-wall integrity sensors, G-type lectin receptor kinases, and salicylic acid-associated defense networks. Conversely, *V. tubiflora* prioritized ancestral stress-repertoire retention, supplemented by specialized chromatin regulators, small RNA processing enzymes, and elevated copy numbers in chloroplast Toc/Tic translocons shared with the African resurrection plant *Xerophyta schlechteri*. Proteome-wide analyses of 8,737 single-copy ortholog trios polarized by *X. schlechteri* revealed pervasive purifying constraint (89.25% with $\omega \le 1.0$), with lineage-specific coding-sequence divergence (elevated branch $\omega$ ratios) restricted to 14 loci per species displaying diverse functional annotations, including examples involved in membrane transport and barrier deposition. Together, these findings demonstrate that physiological divergence in *Vellozia* evolved through asymmetric local gene proliferation, organellar specialization, and targeted coding divergence operating within a structurally stable chromosomal framework.

**Keywords:** Velloziaceae, desiccation tolerance, drought avoidance, macroevolution, CAFE5, tandem duplication, synteny, coding-sequence divergence, *campos rupestres*.

## Introduction

Water deficit is a primary environmental factor constraining the physiology, geographic distribution, and evolutionary diversification of terrestrial plants (Volaire, 2018). During seasonal dry periods, co-occurring species exposed to identical climatic regimes can decouple cellular hydration status from environmental moisture decline by deploying contrasting adaptive strategies across successive phases of drying (Volaire, 2018; Volaire et al., 2026). Under moderate moisture deficit, dehydration avoidance delays cellular turgor loss through continuous root foraging, cuticular waterproofing, and tight stomatal regulation (Alcantara et al., 2015; Volaire, 2018; Teodoro et al., 2021; Volaire et al., 2026). Once environmental drying exceeds the threshold for vegetative growth, survival depends either on preventing lethal water loss through sustained tissue dehydration avoidance or, in desiccation-tolerant lineages, on activating cellular protection networks as tissues equilibrate with dry air (Volaire, 2018; Volaire et al., 2026). At the extreme threshold of this survival continuum, vegetative desiccation tolerance (VDT) enables differentiated tissues to survive severe cellular dehydration and restore physiological and metabolic activity upon rehydration without undergoing meristematic dormancy (Gaff & Oliver, 2013; Alcantara et al., 2015). In poikilochlorophyllous (chlorophyll-dismantling) resurrection plants, this survival mode entails disassembling thylakoid membranes and degrading chlorophyll during foliar drying to limit photo-oxidative damage, followed by rebuilding the photosynthetic machinery upon rewatering (Alcantara et al., 2015; Costa et al., 2017). While the ecophysiological trade-offs separating dehydration avoidance from desiccation tolerance are well characterized (Teodoro et al., 2021; Volaire et al., 2026), the genomic mechanisms underlying the evolutionary divergence between these contrasting strategies in closely related congeners remain largely unresolved.

Velloziaceae (order Pandanales) represent an exemplary system for addressing this question in the Neotropical *campos rupestres* (Alcantara et al., 2018; Teodoro et al., 2021). Distributed along the crystalline plateaus of southeastern and central Brazil, the *campos rupestres* form an ancient montane ecosystem characterized by shallow, nutrient-impoverished quartzitic soils with low water-holding capacity, high solar irradiance, and severe seasonal precipitation deficits, serving as a global center of plant endemism (Silveira et al., 2016; Morellato & Silveira, 2018). Within these rocky-outcrop habitats, where negligible soil moisture buffering amplifies seasonal drought, Velloziaceae lineages have repeatedly diversified across both dehydration-avoidant and desiccation-tolerant survival modes in response to independent climatic events (Alcantara et al., 2015, 2018; Teodoro et al., 2021; Vieira et al., 2022, 2025). On the quartzitic plateaus of the Serra da Canastra (Minas Gerais, Brazil), the poikilochlorophyllous resurrection herb *Vellozia tubiflora* and the perennial, dehydration-avoidant evergreen shrub *Vellozia peripherica* occur within the same montane landscape (Pinto et al., 2025). While both congeners experience the same regional macroclimate across these plateaus, rocky-outcrop microtopography and substrate fissures create localized variation in moisture retention across adjacent microhabitats (Silveira et al., 2016; Pinto et al., 2025). This regional co-occurrence provides a controlled comparative setting to examine how lineage-specific genomic modifications accompany physiological divergence against a shared macroclimatic and evolutionary background.

Determining whether the distinct adaptations of *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* represent recent congeneric innovations or the differential retention of ancestral traits requires situating this Neotropical pair within the broader macroevolutionary history of Velloziaceae. Crown Velloziaceae displays a deep transcontinental disjunction: the relictual southwest Chinese endemic *Acanthochlamys bracteata* is sister to core Velloziaceae, which unites the African and Malagasy genus *Xerophyta* with South American lineages, including the species-rich Brazilian genera *Vellozia* and *Barbacenia* alongside the Andean *Barbacenopsis* (Mello-Silva et al., 2011; Alcantara et al., 2018; Gao et al., 2021). Genomic analyses of the African resurrection plant *Xerophyta schlechteri* (originally published as *Xerophyta viscosa*; Costa et al., 2017) demonstrated that vegetative desiccation tolerance recruits ancestral seed-associated protective pathways, including late embryogenesis abundant (LEA) proteins, small heat-shock proteins, antioxidant enzymes, and abscisic acid-regulated transcriptional networks governed by ABI3 and ABI5 homologs, whereas the sister lineage *Acanthochlamys bracteata*, a cold-tolerant alpine resurrection plant, exhibits expansions in chilling- and desiccation-responsive families (Gao et al., 2021). However, because these existing genomic resources in Velloziaceae have focused exclusively on desiccation-tolerant taxa, integrating both resurrection and dehydration-avoidant congeners into a calibrated comparative framework is required to provide the phylogenetic polarity needed to distinguish ancestral genomic repertoires established prior to the core Velloziaceae radiation from terminal modifications that accompanied congeneric divergence in South America.

These evolutionary contrasts support the formulation of three testable hypotheses regarding the genomic trajectories of *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* within a conserved karyotypic framework. First, if core cellular protection mechanisms were established in the common ancestor of core Velloziaceae without subsequent lineage-specific polyploidy, we hypothesized that vegetative desiccation tolerance in *V. tubiflora* relies predominantly on the retention and regulatory deployment of an inherited ancestral stress repertoire rather than extensive terminal gene proliferation (Costa et al., 2017; Lyall et al., 2020; Pardo et al., 2020; Marks et al., 2025). Second, because tandem and proximal duplications in angiosperms preferentially amplify gene families involved in environmental perception, specialized metabolism, and biotic defense (Freeling, 2009; Birchler & Veitia, 2012; Salojärvi et al., 2017; Qiao et al., 2019), and because maintaining perennial foliage across drought-prone, oligotrophic environments requires continuous cell-wall reinforcement and foliar defense (Coley et al., 1985; Niinemets et al., 2009), we hypothesized that the dehydration-avoidant *V. peripherica* experienced accelerated local gene duplication compared with *V. tubiflora*. Third, because essential metabolic pathways under harsh rocky-outcrop conditions remain under strong functional constraint, we hypothesized that coding sequences in conserved single-copy orthologs evolve under genome-wide purifying selection, with accelerated nonsynonymous substitution rates restricted to specialized loci mediating strategy-specific adaptations.

To test these hypotheses, we generated a chromosome-scale nuclear genome assembly for the dehydration-avoidant *V. peripherica* and compared it with the companion reference genome of the desiccation-tolerant *V. tubiflora* (`hap1`; de Almeida et al., in prep.; Chapter 1). We contextualized this congeneric comparison within a seven-species phylogenomic dataset integrating predicted proteomes from confamilial Velloziaceae relatives (*Xerophyta schlechteri* and *Acanthochlamys bracteata*), external monocot outgroups (*Pandanus tectorius* and *Dioscorea alata*), and *Arabidopsis thaliana*, using *X. schlechteri* to polarize coding-sequence evolution in *Vellozia*. Specifically, we addressed four central questions: (i) whether the two *Vellozia* genomes exhibit conserved chromosomal synteny or lineage-specific structural reorganizations; (ii) whether ancestral gene-family expansions originated in the common ancestor of core Velloziaceae and whether subsequent turnover rates diverged asymmetrically between the two congeners; (iii) to what extent local tandem and proximal duplications drove lineage-specific expansions and enriched distinct physiological pathways; and (iv) whether coding sequences in conserved single-copy orthologs evolved under pervasive purifying constraint or underwent lineage-specific rate acceleration.

## Material and Methods

### 2.1 Genome sequencing, assembly, and annotation

To establish a chromosome-scale reference for comparative genomics between contrasting drought-survival strategies in *Vellozia*, we generated a high-continuity nuclear genome assembly and structural annotation for the dehydration-avoidant *V. peripherica*. Fresh foliar tissue was collected from a wild individual in the quartzitic *campos rupestres* of Serra da Canastra National Park, São Roque de Minas, Minas Gerais, Brazil (20°15′36.9″S, 46°25′22.0″W; Pinto et al., 2025). High-molecular-weight genomic DNA was extracted and sequenced using circular consensus sequencing on a Pacific Biosciences Sequel IIe platform (PacBio HiFi, ~30x coverage), supplemented by high-throughput chromosome conformation capture using Dovetail Omni-C libraries (Dovetail Genomics, Scotts Valley, CA, USA) sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. HiFi reads were first assembled de novo into contig graphs using hifiasm v0.20.0 in standalone HiFi mode (Cheng et al., 2021). Contaminant contigs were filtered using NCBI FCS-GX v0.5.4 (Astashyn et al., 2024). Omni-C paired-end reads were mapped to contigs using BWA-MEM (-5SP) and processed with pairtools to remove PCR duplicates and retain high-quality mappings (MAPQ >= 30). Chromosome-scale scaffolding was then performed using HapHiC v1.0.6 (Zeng et al., 2024) with manual curation in Juicebox v1.11.08 (Durand et al., 2016) guided by the cytogenetic baseline of *Vellozia* ($2n = 18, x = 9$; Braz et al., 2024). Repetitive elements were modeled de novo using EDTA v2.0 (Ou et al., 2019) and masked with RepeatMasker v4.1.5 (Smit et al., 2013-2015), followed by structural gene prediction using BRAKER2 v2.1.6 (Brůna et al., 2021) combining GeneMark-EP+ and AUGUSTUS v3.4.0 trained with Viridiplantae OrthoDB v10 protein evidence. Primary functional annotation was performed using eggNOG-mapper v2.1.9 against the eggNOG v5.0 database (Huerta-Cepas et al., 2019; Cantalapiedra et al., 2021). Completeness of the predicted gene space was evaluated using BUSCO v5.4.3 (Manni et al., 2021) in protein mode against the `embryophyta_odb10` (n = 1,614) and `liliopsida_odb10` (n = 3,278) lineage datasets.

### 2.2 Chromosome synteny, macrosynteny depth, and gene duplication modes

Chromosome-scale structural conservation, macrosynteny, and syntenic depth between the two *Vellozia* congeners were evaluated using gene coordinates and primary chromosome-level protein and nucleotide sequences from *Vellozia peripherica* and the companion reference genome of *Vellozia tubiflora* (`hap1`; de Almeida et al., in prep.; Chapter 1) within the JCVI comparative-genomics framework v1.6.5 (Tang et al., 2015). Pairwise intergenomic synteny searches between the two species were performed using LAST v1650 (Kiełbasa et al., 2011) integrated within JCVI (`jcvi.compara.catalog ortholog`) with parameters C-score $\ge 0.50$, tandem duplication collapse distance $\le 10$ genes (`--dist=10`), and a minimum block threshold of $\ge 5$ collinear anchor gene pairs (`--minspan=5`) to detect collinear anchor pairs and construct chromosome-scale block summaries across the nine pseudomolecules. Syntenic-depth distributions were calculated using `jcvi.compara.synteny depth`, which tallies the number of distinct collinear blocks spanning each annotated gene model. Each gene model in both assemblies was assigned to an integer depth class: depth 0 (non-syntenic loci), depth 1 (strict 1:1 orthologous blocks), depth 2 (two overlapping syntenic blocks reflecting ancestral duplication), and depth $\ge 3$ (higher-order duplication). Relative frequencies across depth classes were compared between assemblies to verify ploidy stasis and assess the presence of lineage-specific whole-genome duplications.

Intragenomic duplication mechanisms across both genomes were classified from protein similarity searches conducted within each assembly using LAST in JCVI. The MCScanX classifier (`duplicate_gene_classifier`; Wang et al., 2012) was applied with parameters `-s 5 -m 25 -n 10` to categorize all predicted gene models into five mutually exclusive classes: whole-genome duplication (WGD)/segmental, tandem, proximal, dispersed, and singleton. Baseline duplication frequencies for each class were quantified across the complete predicted gene space of both assemblies.

### 2.3 Comparative taxon sampling and orthology inference

For comparative orthology, phylogenomics, and gene-family turnover analyses, we compiled a dataset comprising the deduced primary proteomes of seven angiosperm species: the predicted protein-coding repertoires from the newly established chromosome-scale assembly of *Vellozia peripherica* and the companion reference assembly of *Vellozia tubiflora* (`hap1`; de Almeida et al., in prep.; Chapter 1); two additional Velloziaceae representatives, *Xerophyta schlechteri* (published as *X. viscosa*; Costa et al., 2017) and *Acanthochlamys bracteata* (Gao et al., 2021); two monocotyledonous outgroups, *Pandanus tectorius* (Xu et al., 2026) and *Dioscorea alata* (Bredeson et al., 2022); and the eudicot *Arabidopsis thaliana* (TAIR10.1, GCF_000001735.4; Berardini et al., 2015; Cheng et al., 2017) as an external phylogenetic anchor and functional annotation reference (source assembly accessions in Data Availability). To eliminate isoform redundancy across the seven reference proteomes, only the amino acid sequence corresponding to the transcript with the longest open reading frame per locus was retained for comparative orthology inference.

Orthologous gene clusters (orthogroups) across the seven primary proteomes were delineated using OrthoFinder v3.1.5 (Emms & Kelly, 2015, 2019). Pairwise sequence searches were performed with DIAMOND v2.2.4 (Buchfink et al., 2021) in more-sensitive mode (`--more-sensitive -e 0.001`) using the BLOSUM62 substitution matrix (gap open: 11; gap extend: 1). Sequence alignment and maximum-likelihood gene-tree reconstruction within orthogroups were performed with FAMSA v2.4.1 (Deorowicz et al., 2016) and FastTree v2.2.0 (Price et al., 2010), respectively. Graph clustering used the Markov Cluster Algorithm (MCL; Enright et al., 2002) with an inflation parameter of 1.2. Strictly single-copy orthogroups present in all seven genomes were selected as phylogenomic markers. Protein sequences for each single-copy locus were aligned using MAFFT v7.520 (Katoh & Standley, 2013) with automated algorithm selection (`--auto`). Columns with gaps in more than 50% of taxa were removed, and the filtered single-copy alignments were concatenated into a partitioned amino acid supermatrix for phylogenomic inference.

### 2.4 Phylogenomic reconstruction and gene-tree concordance analyses

From the concatenated amino acid supermatrix, phylogenomic relationships within Velloziaceae and outgroup lineages were inferred using IQ-TREE v2.3.6 (Minh et al., 2020) under an edge-unlinked partitioned maximum-likelihood framework. The optimal amino acid substitution model for each locus partition was determined using ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al., 2017) under the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). Branch support was assessed using 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot2) replicates (Hoang et al., 2018).

In parallel, a coalescent species tree was reconstructed under the multi-species coalescent (MSC) model using ASTRAL-III v5.7.8 (Zhang et al., 2018), taking as input the individual maximum-likelihood gene trees to estimate normalized quartet support across alternative topological resolutions. Genealogical discordance across individual loci was quantified by calculating gene concordance factors (gCF) and site concordance factors (sCF) across all decisive single-copy alignments in IQ-TREE against the reference species-tree topology (which shared an identical branching order between the concatenated maximum-likelihood and ASTRAL-III reconstructions). For each internal branch of this reference tree, gCF represents the percentage of decisive individual gene trees that contain that branch, whereas sCF represents the percentage of decisive alignment sites that support that bipartition.

### 2.5 Molecular dating under fossil-calibrated relaxed clocks

Divergence times along the inferred species-tree topology were estimated using the approximate likelihood method implemented in the MCMCtree module of PAML v4.10 (Yang, 2007; dos Reis & Yang, 2011), conditioned on the concatenated amino acid supermatrix. The molecular clock was modeled under an independent lognormal rates specification (`clock = 2`) using a Poisson amino acid substitution model with five discrete Gamma rate categories (`model = 0, alpha = 0.5, ncatG = 5`). Evolutionary time was parameterized in units of 100 million years (1 time unit = 100 Ma). Prior distributions on the birth-death process and rate parameters were specified as `BDparas = 1 1 0.1`, `rgene_gamma = 2 20 1`, and `sigma2_gamma = 1 10 1`.

The calibration tree used in MCMCtree encoded five paired minimum and maximum bounds (specified in 100-Ma units) based on fossil evidence and multi-locus chronograms. First, the root split (*Arabidopsis* versus monocots; 130-145 Ma) was constrained by a minimum of 130 Ma from early Cretaceous crown angiosperm fossils (Benton et al., 2015; Magallón et al., 2015) and a soft maximum of 145 Ma corresponding to the Jurassic-Cretaceous boundary (Magallón et al., 2015). Second, the divergence between *Dioscorea* and Pandanales (115-128 Ma) was bounded using monocot plastid phylogenomic chronograms (Janssen & Bremer, 2004; Givnish et al., 2018). Third, the *Pandanus* versus Velloziaceae split (90-105 Ma) was anchored by a minimum of 90 Ma from Turonian fossil flowers of Triuridaceae (*Mabelia connatifila*, ~90 Ma; Gandolfo et al., 2002; Iles et al., 2015), which provide a well-substantiated minimum age for crown Pandanales (Alcantara et al., 2018). Although fossil fruits assigned to Pandanaceae (such as *Gruenbachia pandanoides*) have been described from the Late Cretaceous, their taxonomic placement within crown Pandanales carries morphological uncertainty (Alcantara et al., 2018), making Triuridaceae the more reliable constraint; the maximum bound of 105 Ma follows monocot phylogenomic estimates (Givnish et al., 2018). Fourth, the divergence of the relictual Tibetan endemic *Acanthochlamys bracteata* from core Velloziaceae (75-90 Ma) was bounded based on multi-gene chronograms (Alcantara et al., 2018; Gao et al., 2021). Finally, the split between the two sampled *Vellozia* species was bounded between 2 and 25 Ma based on species-level dating of *Vellozia* diversification in the *campos rupestres* (Alcantara et al., 2018), where 2 Ma serves as a lower bound for recent speciation and 25 Ma provides a conservative upper bound within mid-Cenozoic clade radiation. This interval applies strictly to the split between these two congeners and does not date the crown of the entire genus. The split between *Xerophyta* and *Vellozia* carried no direct calibration; its age was estimated conditionally on the sequence data, the surrounding node calibrations, and the remaining relaxed-clock priors.

The workflow included a prior-only run (`usedata = 0`) to evaluate the joint prior distribution and two independent posterior chains initialized with distinct random seeds (`1234567` and `7654321`). Both chains followed identical run specifications: an initial burn-in of 50,000 iterations discarded prior to sampling, followed by 3,000,000 sampling iterations with states recorded every 100 iterations (`sampfreq = 100`), yielding 30,000 parameter samples per run and 60,000 pooled samples across the two chains (totaling 3,050,000 iterations per run). Convergence between independent runs was evaluated using the Gelman-Rubin potential scale reduction factor ($\hat{R}$), calculated by comparing between-chain variance to within-chain variance across recorded samples for each continuous parameter (Gelman & Rubin, 1992), with $\hat{R} < 1.01$ indicating convergence to the stationary distribution. Posterior sampling adequacy and mixing were assessed via effective sample sizes (ESS), calculated from autocorrelation times across the sample trace; a threshold of combined $\text{ESS} > 200$ across both chains was enforced to ensure negligible Monte Carlo sampling error (Drummond et al., 2006). The resulting combined posterior distribution was summarized into a time-calibrated ultrametric species tree.

### 2.6 Gene-family turnover and birth-death modeling

Macroevolutionary gene-family size fluctuations and ancestral copy-number dynamics along the calibrated timescale were modeled using CAFE5 v5.1.0 (Mendes et al., 2020). As phylogenetic input, the time-calibrated ultrametric tree from MCMCtree was pruned of the eudicot outgroup *Arabidopsis thaliana* to retain a six-taxon monocot chronogram (*V. peripherica*, *V. tubiflora*, *X. schlechteri*, *A. bracteata*, *P. tectorius*, and *D. alata*). While *A. thaliana* was necessary to anchor the root fossil calibration in MCMCtree, it was excluded from birth-death modeling because the continuous-time Markov process in CAFE assumes a shared baseline turnover dynamic across branches. Spanning the deep monocot-eudicot divergence (~150 Ma) along an isolated, single-species lineage would introduce independent paleopolyploidization histories (such as Brassicaceae-specific duplications) into root ancestral state reconstructions, confounding monocot-specific turnover rates. Restricting the modeling framework to monocots therefore ensures that estimated birth-death parameters reflect gene family dynamics within a shared macroevolutionary and syntenic context.

Input family counts were compiled from the OrthoFinder clustering. Families lacking representation in the common ancestor of the sampled monocots (requiring $\ge 1$ copy in outgroup monocots *Pandanus* or *Dioscorea* and $\ge 1$ copy in Velloziaceae) were excluded prior to dynamic modeling to establish an ancestral baseline. Furthermore, following recommended CAFE protocols to prevent numerical instability and non-finite log-likelihood calculations during transition matrix exponentiation for very large copy numbers (Mathers et al., 2017), 12 orthogroups containing $\ge 100$ genes in at least one monocot genome were filtered prior to global rate optimization. These extreme clusters, predominantly comprising transposable element bursts alongside the high-copy chloroplast Toc/Tic translocon complex (`OG0000007`), were evaluated separately via direct copy-count summaries. Because these 12 groups constitute less than 0.1% of all families and were tracked independently, turnover rate estimates and lineage-specific expansion inferences across the remaining 12,876 families were robust to their exclusion.

Turnover-rate variation across the phylogeny was evaluated by comparing five nested branch-rate models representing alternative evolutionary hypotheses: (i) a global one-rate model (M1, uniform $\lambda$ across all monocots); (ii) a two-rate model testing a family-wide shift in Velloziaceae versus background monocots (M2); (iii) a three-rate model isolating the *Vellozia* crown from earlier Velloziaceae branches and background monocots (M3); (iv) an alternative three-rate model testing an accelerated turnover shift restricted to *V. peripherica* (M4); and (v) a four-rate model assigning separate terminal rates to *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* alongside shared Velloziaceae and background rates (M5).

Assembly and annotation count errors were explicitly accounted for by estimating an empirical error distribution parameter ($\epsilon$) across the six taxa and incorporating the calibrated error model into subsequent dynamic analyses. Rate variation among gene families was modeled using discrete Gamma distributions with $k = 2$ and $k = 3$ categories, with the branch-rate comparison evaluated under Gamma $k = 3$ throughout so that models differed only in their branch assignments. To guard against local optima during maximum-likelihood estimation, five independent optimization starts were evaluated for models M1-M4, and ten independent starts were run for the most parameterized model (M5).

Models were compared using the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), evaluated from the replicate with the lowest negative log-likelihood for each parameterization. Under the best-fitting model, ancestral gene-family sizes across internal nodes were reconstructed using the Viterbi algorithm implemented in CAFE5, which determines the most likely sequence of ancestral copy numbers and yields the inferred net copy-number change along each branch ($\Delta = N_{\text{child}} - N_{\text{parent}}$). In parallel, CAFE5 calculates branch-specific empirical $p$-values based on transition probabilities under the birth-death process given branch length and fitted turnover parameter $\lambda$. Gene-family changes were classified using two explicit, decoupled criteria: the sign of $\Delta$ defined the direction of change, while the branch-specific transition probability defined statistical significance. Specifically, a family was classified as expanding on a given branch if $\Delta > 0$ and $p_{\text{branch}} < 0.05$, or contracting if $\Delta < 0$ and $p_{\text{branch}} < 0.05$, without requiring an additional global family-wide significance filter. Families with $p_{\text{branch}} \ge 0.05$ or $\Delta = 0$ were classified as unchanged. Robust family calls were defined as those satisfying both the direction of change and $p_{\text{branch}} < 0.05$ across all replicate runs within the near-optimal set (within 0.1 negative log-likelihood units of the best run).

### 2.7 Duplication-mode enrichment, functional annotation, and protein domain profiling

To determine whether lineage-specific gene-family expansions were driven by specific duplication mechanisms, duplication-class assignments from MCScanX were intersected with CAFE5 terminal-expansion calls in both *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*. For each duplication mode, two-sided Fisher's exact tests and odds ratios (ORs) evaluated statistical overrepresentation by comparing genes in expanded families against the non-overlapping reference of genes outside expanded families, as well as against the whole-genome total.

Functional annotations associated with gene-family turnover were transferred from *Arabidopsis thaliana* homologs within each orthogroup using Gene Ontology (GO; Gene Ontology Consortium, 2021) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG; Kanehisa et al., 2023) databases, propagating terms along ontological hierarchies while excluding uninformative root terms. In parallel, primary eggNOG v5.0 annotations (Huerta-Cepas et al., 2019) from the reference assemblies were used to cross-reference gene descriptions and identify organellar or transposable-element families.

To avoid paralog pseudoreplication and family-size bias, functional overrepresentation was evaluated at the gene-family level using SCB-Enrich (github.com/mfcaval/scbenrich), treating orthogroups as the independent sampling units. Stratification was structured across three confounding family-level covariates: total monocot family size (16 bins, controlling for CAFE5 detection power), mapped *Arabidopsis* homolog count (6 bins, controlling for annotation opportunity), and overall annotation degree (6 bins, controlling for term ubiquity in databases), defragmented to $N_{\text{bg}} \ge 2$ per stratum without extrinsic expression covariates.

Overrepresentation was evaluated in a two-stage framework across the 12,876 modeled monocot families. First, all functional terms were screened using one-sided Fisher's exact tests, controlling genome-wide multiple testing via the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure (Benjamini & Hochberg, 1995) at both the local contrast level ($q_{\text{local}} < 0.05$, calculated across all terms within a focal branch) and the global multi-contrast level ($q_{\text{global}} < 0.05$, calculated across all pooled tests). Second, candidate terms passing the local FDR threshold were tested against an empirical null distribution generated through 10,000 stratified permutations jointly balancing the three family-level covariates. Because multiple hypothesis testing across the functional universe was controlled in the initial screening stage, empirical $p_{\text{strat}}$ values were evaluated as unadjusted per-term permutation statistics serving as a validation filter against covariate confounding. Terms satisfying both FDR thresholds ($q_{\text{local}} < 0.05$ and $q_{\text{global}} < 0.05$), empirical permutation support ($p_{\text{strat}} < 0.05$), and verified covariate balance (standardized mean differences $|\text{SMD}| < 0.10$) were designated as robust candidates; terms with $q_{\text{local}} < 0.05$ and $p_{\text{strat}} < 0.05$ but non-significant global FDR or residual covariate imbalance were classified as suggestive; and terms failing $p_{\text{strat}} < 0.05$ were flagged as family-size artifacts. Redundant ancestor-descendant terms along the Gene Ontology directed acyclic graph were reduced to representative terms for visualization.

Representative protein sequences from expanding and contracting families in *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* were further characterized with InterProScan v5.68-100.0 (Jones et al., 2014) to refine functional classifications and resolve domain architectures. For each family, the longest predicted protein in the focal species was selected as the representative. Domain architectures were annotated using member databases integrated into InterProScan, including Pfam (Mistry et al., 2021), SMART (Letunic et al., 2021), CDD (Lu et al., 2020), PANTHER (Thomas et al., 2022), and SUPERFAMILY v1.75 (Wilson et al., 2007; representing SCOP 1.75 structural domain models bundled in InterProScan rather than the standalone SUPERFAMILY 2.0 database; Pandurangan et al., 2019), supplemented by transmembrane helix and signal-peptide predictions from TMHMM v2.0 (Krogh et al., 2001) and Phobius v1.01 (Käll et al., 2004).

### 2.8 Ortholog trios and between-branch coding-sequence heterogeneity

Coding-sequence divergence and polarized lineage-specific substitution rates between the two *Vellozia* congeners were evaluated across ortholog trios assembled by intersecting reciprocal one-to-one *V. peripherica*-*V. tubiflora* pairs with pairwise one-to-one assignments to the outgroup *Xerophyta schlechteri*. Protein-guided codon alignments were generated with MAFFT (`--auto`). Strict quality filters excluded sequences shorter than 30 codons, alignments with ambiguous or internal stop codons, and positions containing alignment gaps.

Branch-specific nonsynonymous-to-synonymous substitution rate ratios ($\omega = dN/dS$) were estimated in `codeml` (PAML v4.10) using F3X4 codon frequencies (`NSsites = 0`). A null one-ratio model was compared with a three-ratio branch model assigning independent rates to each lineage via likelihood ratio tests ($2\Delta\ln L \sim \chi^2_2$). This test evaluates branch rate heterogeneity across lineages rather than site-specific positive selection on isolated codons, which would require branch-site models (Zhang et al., 2005). Multiple testing across candidate trios was controlled using the standard Benjamini-Hochberg procedure ($q_{\text{FDR}} < 0.05$), with candidate domain architectures verified via InterProScan. Complete configuration controls, alignment scripts, and filtering thresholds are documented in the workflow repository.

## Results

### 3.1 Chromosome-scale genome assembly and conserved chromosomal synteny between *Vellozia* congeners

PacBio HiFi sequencing and Omni-C scaffolding of the dehydration-avoidant *Vellozia peripherica* yielded a 493.30 Mb nuclear genome assembly (`Vper.hap1.v1`), with 98.05% of the sequence (483.68 Mb) anchored into nine pseudomolecules matching the diploid genus baseline ($2n = 18, x = 9$; Braz et al., 2024; scaffold N50 = 64.68 Mb; Supplementary Table S1). Gene-space completeness was high (BUSCO 96.2% complete against `embryophyta_odb10` and 95.8% against `liliopsida_odb10`), comparable to the companion reference genome of the sympatric, desiccation-tolerant *V. tubiflora* (466.32 Mb, 95.8% BUSCO recovery; de Almeida et al., in prep.; Chapter 1).

Pairwise synteny revealed extensive macrosyntenic stasis between the two congeners: each pseudomolecule in *V. peripherica* corresponded 1:1 to its homologous pseudomolecule in *V. tubiflora* without evidence of Robertsonian fusions, fissions, or reciprocal translocations (Figure 1A; Supplementary Figure S1). The primary orthologous backbone was spanned by 42 collinear blocks (25,147 anchor assignments), where detectable macrosyntenic divergence at gene-anchor resolution was characterized primarily by localized intra-chromosomal inversions (<3.1% of backbone anchors; predominantly on `chr1`, `chr4`, and `chr6`). Syntenic depth distributions were concordant between genomes (Figure 1B): combined depth-1 (strict 1:1 orthology) and depth-2 (shared paleoduplications) assignments accounted for 95.6% of the analyzed gene space in *V. peripherica* (39.9% depth 1 and 55.7% depth 2) and 90.2% in *V. tubiflora* (34.9% depth 1 and 55.4% depth 2), with non-syntenic loci (depth 0; 1.5% and 7.0%) and higher-order duplications (depth $\ge 3$; 2.9% and 2.8%) comprising the remaining fractions. This shared dosage distribution confirms the absence of unshared whole-genome duplication events following congeneric divergence. Secondary syntenic blocks connecting differently numbered chromosomes reflected ancient paralogies from shared ancestral duplications rather than recent rearrangements (Figure 1C; Supplementary Table S1).

### 3.2 Phylogenomic timescale and ancestral gene-family expansions in core Velloziaceae

Across the seven sampled angiosperm genomes, OrthoFinder clustered 93.2% of predicted proteins into 17,750 orthogroups (Supplementary Table S1), including 1,513 strictly single-copy nuclear orthologs (732,066 aligned amino acid positions). Phylogenomic relationships and divergence times inferred from this single-copy dataset yielded a fully resolved, highly supported concatenated maximum-likelihood topology across all nodes (100% ultrafast bootstrap support; Figure 2A). The species tree placed *Dioscorea alata* (Dioscoreales) as the sister lineage to Pandanales, within which *Pandanus tectorius* (Pandanaceae) diverged prior to Velloziaceae. Within Velloziaceae, *Acanthochlamys bracteata* was resolved as sister to core Velloziaceae (*Xerophyta schlechteri* + *Vellozia*), and the two focal *Vellozia* species formed a well-supported monophyletic clade. Multi-species coalescent analysis in ASTRAL-III based on all 1,513 individual maximum-likelihood gene trees recovered the same branching order as the concatenated supermatrix, with maximal local posterior probabilities across all internal nodes ($\text{LPP} = 1.00$; Supplementary Table S2). Within Velloziaceae, coalescent branch lengths were 2.43 coalescent units (CU) for the node uniting *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* and 3.32 CU for the core Velloziaceae node. In parallel, evaluating gene trees and alignment sites against this shared reference topology revealed strong concordance for the species-tree bipartitions: $\text{gCF} = 93.92\%$ (1,421 of 1,513 trees) for the sampled *Vellozia* pair and $\text{gCF} = 97.16\%$ (1,470 of 1,513 trees) for core Velloziaceae, with corresponding site concordance factors of $\text{sCF} = 86.44\%$ and $\text{sCF} = 79.12\%$ (Supplementary Table S2). The concordance between concatenated maximum-likelihood and multi-species coalescent reconstructions provided a consistent topological framework for subsequent divergence-time and gene-family modeling.

Convergence across independent MCMCtree runs was confirmed by Gelman-Rubin diagnostics ($\hat{R} < 1.01$ across all continuous parameters; Gelman & Rubin, 1992), with posterior inference conducted on the combined chain distribution where total effective sample sizes exceeded 200 ($\text{ESS} > 200$; Drummond et al., 2006). For the core Velloziaceae divergence age, the combined ESS was 262 (with individual run ESS values of 100 and 129 reflecting consistent posterior exploration across separate initializations; Supplementary Table S3). The chronogram used for CAFE5 reports ages of 121.90 Ma (combined-chain 95% credible interval: 115.48 to 128.07 Ma) for *Dioscorea*-Pandanales, 101.26 Ma (92.69 to 105.16 Ma) for *Pandanus*-Velloziaceae, 78.21 Ma (74.43 to 85.45 Ma) for *Acanthochlamys*-core Velloziaceae, 31.91 Ma (22.41 to 48.11 Ma) for the *Xerophyta*-*Vellozia* divergence, and 9.74 Ma (5.85 to 15.72 Ma) for the split between the sampled *Vellozia* species (Figure 2A; Supplementary Table S3). These node ages derive from the primary maximum-posterior ultrametric tree used as the fixed input chronogram for CAFE5 modeling. Using a single realized tree from the posterior distribution guarantees strict ultrametric consistency and internal branch-length coherence across nodes, avoiding the temporal distortion of ancestor-descendant intervals that can arise from compiling independent marginal node heights. While CAFE5 turnover estimates are conditioned on this specific chronogram, its node ages fall well within the 95% credible intervals of the combined-chain posterior means (33.77 and 9.68 Ma for the last two nodes, respectively; Supplementary Table S3), providing a representative temporal baseline. The stem branch leading to core Velloziaceae spans 78.21 to 31.91 Ma in the input chronogram, defining the interval to which ancestral gene-family gains were assigned.

After excluding 12 orthogroups with $\ge 100$ genes in at least one monocot genome (Supplementary Table S4) and families absent from the monocot root, 12,876 monocot-rooted gene families were modeled in CAFE5. Models incorporating count error ($\epsilon = 0.0044$), Gamma-distributed rate heterogeneity, and branch-specific rates decisively outperformed simpler specifications. Within the Gamma $k=3$ model set, the four-rate model M5 had the lowest AIC ($\text{AIC} = 190,947.35$; $\Delta\text{AIC} \ge 43.63$ relative to all alternative nested models; Table 1; Supplementary Table S5), firmly supporting lineage-specific turnover rates for the two focal *Vellozia* congeners. Three M5 runs fell within 0.1 negative log-likelihood units of the optimal solution and defined the near-optimal sensitivity set.

Reconstruction of ancestral gene-family sizes across the 12,876 modeled monocot families along the calibrated phylogeny identified marked expansions on the branch leading to the common ancestor of core Velloziaceae (*Xerophyta* + *Vellozia*; Node 10 in the dated tree, corresponding to Node 12 in the six-taxon CAFE5 tree; Figure 2B). This branch spans the evolutionary divergence window between 78.21 and 31.91 Ma (~46.3 Ma). Across the full modeled set, 98.93% of families (12,738) exhibited no significant copy-number transition along this stem branch. In the optimal M5 run, branch-specific transition probability testing in CAFE5 identified 132 significantly expanding gene families ($\Delta > 0$, nominal $p_{\text{branch}} < 0.05$, 1.03% of modeled families) and six contracting families ($\Delta < 0$, nominal $p_{\text{branch}} < 0.05$, 0.05%) on this branch, yielding a pronounced expansion-to-contraction ratio of 22.0:1 (Table 1). These counts represent uncorrected branch-specific calls without an additional family-wide significance threshold. Among these, 126 expansions and all six contractions robustly satisfied both the direction of change and $p_{\text{branch}} < 0.05$ across all three near-optimal optimization runs. By contrast, the older ancestral node uniting *Acanthochlamys* with core Velloziaceae (Node 9 in the dated tree, Node 11 in CAFE5, ~78.21 Ma) was characterized by stability (99.65% unchanged, 12,831 families), showing 20 expanding (0.16%) and 25 contracting families (0.19%; $p_{\text{branch}} < 0.05$), of which 19 and 25, respectively, were robust across near-optimal runs.

The catalog of 132 ancestral expansions on the core Velloziaceae stem branch included families associated with stress protection, transcriptional regulation, secondary metabolism, and photosynthesis (Figure 2B; Supplementary Table S7). The small heat-shock protein HSP20 family (`OG0000340`) showed an inferred net gain of five copies ($p < 0.0001$). Highlighted regulators included B3-domain proteins (`OG0000136`, net gain of five copies), basic leucine zipper (bZIP) transcription factors (`OG0000858`, net gain of four copies), and MYB transcription factors (`OG0000750`, net gain of four copies). Additional expanded families included anthocyanin 5-aromatic acyltransferase (`OG0000038`, net gain of five copies) and germin-like proteins (`OG0000035`, net gain of 12 copies), alongside families annotated as homologs of chloroplast NAD(P)H-quinone oxidoreductase (NDH) subunits (`OG0000014`, net gain of 17 copies; `OG0000036`, net gain of five copies).

On the subsequent branch leading to the common ancestor of the sampled *Vellozia* pair (*V. peripherica* + *V. tubiflora*; Node 11 in the dated tree, Node 13 in CAFE5, ~9.74 Ma), 99.58% of families remained unchanged (12,822 families), with 48 families expanding (0.37%) and six contracting (0.05%; $p_{\text{branch}} < 0.05$); 46 expansions and all six contractions were robust across the near-optimal runs (Table 1). Along the subsequent terminal branch leading to *V. tubiflora*, most of these highlighted ancestral families showed no nominally significant additional expansion in the optimal model run. One MYB-associated family (`OG0000750`) was an exception, exhibiting an inferred net gain of two copies, whereas one chloroplast NDH subunit family (`OG0000014`) exhibited an inferred net loss of three copies.

### 3.3 Asymmetric gene-family turnover and accelerated terminal expansions in *Vellozia peripherica*

Following the late Miocene divergence of *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*, branch-specific rate modeling revealed pronounced turnover heterogeneity between the two focal lineages. In the optimal M5 run, the dehydration-avoidant lineage *V. peripherica* exhibited $\lambda_{Vper} = 0.008204$ events/gene/Ma, compared with $\lambda_{Vtub} = 0.002092$ in the desiccation-tolerant *V. tubiflora*, yielding a fitted turnover-rate ratio of approximately 3.92 across near-optimal runs (3.92 to 3.94). Background monocot and shared Velloziaceae rates were $\lambda_{bg} = 0.002674$ and $\lambda_{Velloziaceae} = 0.003202$, respectively. Because the estimated parameter $\lambda_{Vper}$ converged directly at the analytical stability boundary enforced by CAFE5 ($1/t_{\max} = 1/121.8979 \approx 0.008204$ events/gene/Ma, set by the root-to-tip distance of *Dioscorea alata*), this rate and the resulting 3.92-fold ratio reflect the upper boundary of the optimization procedure rather than an interior point estimate (Figure 2B; Supplementary Table S5).

This rate asymmetry was directly reflected in inferred terminal branch copy-number changes (CAFE5 branch-specific family significance threshold $p_{\text{branch}} < 0.05$; Table 1). In the optimal model run (maximum-likelihood M5 replicate), CAFE5 identified 217 expanding (1.69% of modeled families) and 66 contracting (0.51%) gene families in *V. peripherica* (12,593 families unchanged, 97.80%; expansion-to-contraction ratio of 3.29:1), compared with 50 expanding (0.39%) and 63 contracting (0.49%) families in *V. tubiflora* (12,763 families unchanged, 99.12%; ratio 0.79:1; Figure 2B). This pronounced asymmetry was robust across independent optimization starts, with strict intersections of all near-optimal runs retaining 199 expansions in *V. peripherica* and consistently recovering all 50 expansions in *V. tubiflora* (Table 1).

Beyond the 12,876 modeled families, the 12 high-copy orthogroups excluded prior to rate optimization ($\ge 100$ copies in at least one monocot genome) also exhibited large copy-number differences in both focal lineages (Supplementary Table S4). In *V. tubiflora*, `OG0000000` contained 541 copies, compared with one in *V. peripherica* and 13 in *X. schlechteri*; the catalog annotates this family as retrotransposon-associated. Other high-copy families included `OG0000023` (133 copies in *V. tubiflora*) and the putative Toc/Tic-associated `OG0000007` (105 in *V. tubiflora*, 72 in *X. schlechteri*, and 14 in *V. peripherica*). In *V. peripherica*, plant-mobile-domain-associated `OG0000013` contained 138 copies versus 14 in *V. tubiflora*, and retroelement-associated `OG0000021` contained 102 copies. These high-copy clusters, analyzed outside the continuous birth-death model, document extreme lineage-specific copy-number variation that complements the macroevolutionary turnover inferred across the broader gene space (Supplementary Table S4).

### 3.4 Tandem and proximal duplicates predominate in expanded *Vellozia peripherica* families

Classification of all predicted genes in *V. peripherica* (26,466 genes) and *V. tubiflora* (25,551 genes) into duplication modes using MCScanX revealed contrasting structural architectures between the two genomes (Figure 3A). Across the whole genome, local duplications accounted for 21.25% of models in *V. peripherica* (13.65% tandem, 3,613 genes; 7.60% proximal, 2,012 genes) and 15.96% in *V. tubiflora* (10.88% tandem, 2,779 genes; 5.08% proximal, 1,299 genes; Table 2). WGD/segmental duplicates accounted for 28.18% in *V. peripherica* (7,459 genes) and 28.27% in *V. tubiflora* (7,224 genes), indicating similar genome-wide proportions of collinear duplicates.

Within the 217 gene families expanded in *V. peripherica*, 66.47% of 2,344 classified genes were local duplicates: 46.25% tandem (1,084 genes; odds ratio (OR) = 9.50 relative to non-expanded background families, Fisher's exact $p < 10^{-300}$) and 20.22% proximal (474 genes; OR = 5.46, $p = 9.98 \times 10^{-138}$). WGD/segmental duplicates were depleted in this set (12.16%, 285 genes; OR = 0.29, $p = 2.33 \times 10^{-102}$; Figure 3B; Table 2). The 50 families expanded in *V. tubiflora* displayed a distinct composition: 13.53% tandem, 7.36% proximal, and 42.64% singleton genes. Although tandem (OR = 1.52, $p = 1.17 \times 10^{-3}$) and proximal (OR = 2.00, $p = 8.23 \times 10^{-5}$) categories were enriched relative to non-expanded background families in *V. tubiflora*, singleton genes showed the strongest overrepresentation (OR = 3.60, $p = 2.43 \times 10^{-45}$; Figure 3C). These odds ratios quantify statistical associations between duplication mode and family expansion relative to the background gene space. While tandem arrays occur widely across plant genomes and frequently encompass stress-related loci without diagnosing desiccation tolerance (Schuster & VanBuren, 2026), this enrichment identifies local structural proliferation as the primary driver of copy-number asymmetry between the two congeners.

### 3.5 Functional enrichment identifies salicylic-acid and defense-associated categories in expanded *V. peripherica* families

Gene Ontology and KEGG overrepresentation analyses across expanding lineages comprised 30,394 primary tests evaluated under family-size-stratified permutations. After local and global false-discovery assessment, 10,000 stratified permutations, and category-specific balance checks, six tests met all four criteria and were classified as robustly supported; one additional test (cell recognition, GO:0008037) met all criteria except global significance ($q_{global} = 0.053$) and was classified as suggestive; 26 additional tests showed compatible permutation support with residual covariate imbalance and were therefore classified as qualified suggestive hypotheses (Figure 4; Supplementary Table S6). Representative terms are shown for *V. peripherica* terminal expansions, core Velloziaceae ancestral expansions, and the tree-wide rapid-evolution set (Figure 4A,B).

In the terminal *V. peripherica* lineage, two transferred functional categories satisfied all four stringent reporting criteria: response to salicylic acid (GO:0009751; 10 of 125 annotated foreground families; OR = 7.41, $q_{local} = 0.00133$, $q_{global} = 0.00252$, $p_{strat} = 0.0232$) and recognition of pollen (GO:0048544; four of 125; OR = 17.40, $q_{local} = 0.0193$, $q_{global} = 0.0387$, $p_{strat} = 0.0173$; Figure 4B). Additional prominent categories, including oxidoreductase activity (OR = 3.02, $q = 0.00319$), plasma membrane localization (OR = 2.53, $q = 1.07 \times 10^{-4}$), and ion binding (OR = 2.13, $q = 0.00514$), passed the local-FDR and permutation thresholds but exhibited category-specific opportunity imbalance, representing qualified suggestive hypotheses.

For the 132 families expanded on the core Velloziaceae branch, three annotation categories passed local and permutation thresholds: ribosome (ath03010; OR = 5.23, $q = 0.0348$), extracellular region (GO:0005576; OR = 4.55, $q = 5.41 \times 10^{-8}$), and cytosol (GO:0005829; OR = 2.24, $q = 0.03998$). All three were classified as qualified suggestive because of residual covariate imbalance in this descriptive ancestral contrast (Supplementary Table S6).

Within the tree-wide rapid-evolution set, robustly supported representative terms included recognition of pollen (OR = 30.26, $q_{local} = 7.92 \times 10^{-4}$, $q_{global} = 0.00188$, $p_{strat} = 0.0064$) and diterpenoid biosynthetic process (GO:0016102; OR = 29.95, $q_{local} = 0.00538$, $q_{global} = 0.00924$, $p_{strat} = 0.0202$; Figure 4B). These were accompanied by high-magnitude qualified suggestive signals in carbon-oxygen lyase activity acting on phosphates (OR = 78.17, $q = 6.52 \times 10^{-5}$), ubiquitin-protein ligase binding (OR = 22.36, $q = 0.00186$), and calmodulin binding (OR = 6.99, $q = 0.0182$).

In contrast to *V. peripherica* and the ancestral nodes, no functional category survived stratified permutation filtering for expansions at the common ancestor of the sampled *Vellozia* species (Node 11 in the dated tree; nominal photosynthesis terms yielded $p_{strat} = 0.0808$) or along the terminal *V. tubiflora* branch (Supplementary Table S6). Annotation coverage also differed between the terminal expansion sets: 28 of the 50 terminal expansions in *V. tubiflora* (56.0%) lacked Gene Ontology Biological Process annotations, and eight (16.0%) lacked *Arabidopsis* homologs entirely, restricting their representation in homology-based overrepresentation tests. In *V. peripherica*, 49 of the 217 expanding families (22.6%) likewise lacked *Arabidopsis* counterparts, and 92 (42.4%) lacked Biological Process terms, showing that both expansion sets include families with limited representation in the transferred annotation system.

### 3.6 Protein domain architectures characterize lineage-specific copy-number contrasts and concordant gains

To resolve functional identities beyond transferred *Arabidopsis* annotations, direct sequence-level domain profiling and copy-number contrasts were evaluated across expanding and contracting gene families (Figure 5; Supplementary Table S7). In *V. peripherica*, prominent terminal gains included families annotated with receptor-associated, defense-related, and metabolic functions (Figure 5A,B). These included leucine-rich-repeat proteins (`OG0000001`, net gain of +49 copies; $p = 2.56 \times 10^{-31}$), NB-ARC-containing resistance-protein candidates (`OG0000042`, +26 copies; $p = 3.71 \times 10^{-23}$), F-box proteins (`OG0000079`, +24 copies; $p = 3.73 \times 10^{-21}$), wall-associated receptor kinases (`OG0000012`, +17 copies; $p = 5.80 \times 10^{-10}$), and cytochrome P450 monooxygenases (`OG0000058`, +17 copies; $p = 1.61 \times 10^{-13}$; Figure 5A,B). In *V. tubiflora*, the largest terminal expansions comprised ribonuclease III-like endoribonucleases (`OG0001778`, +10 copies; $p = 3.07 \times 10^{-16}$), Tudor/Agenet-domain proteins (`OG0000643`, +7 copies; $p = 1.81 \times 10^{-10}$), and plant mobile domain proteins (`OG0000010`, +6 copies; $p = 5.32 \times 10^{-4}$; Figure 5A,B; Supplementary Table S7).

InterProScan provided domain assignments for representatives of several families with limited prior functional annotation (Supplementary Table S7). In *V. peripherica*, these included a DUF6490-containing protein (`OG0000112`, +10 copies; PF20100), a WRKY-domain protein (`OG0000077`, +4 copies; PF03106), and a proton-dependent oligopeptide transporter family representative (`OG0005019`, +3 copies; PF00854). Both TMHMM and Phobius predicted four transmembrane helices for the `OG0000112` representative. For `OG0005019`, they predicted ten and twelve helices, respectively, supporting a membrane-protein assignment while differing in the detailed topology. The *V. tubiflora* expansion `OG0000216` exhibited an inferred net gain of four copies and contained 18 copies in *V. tubiflora* and 19 in *X. schlechteri*; its selected representative remained uncharacterized in the current domain summary.

Domain matches obtained for representative proteins (selected as the longest predicted sequence per family; Supplementary Table S7) were not uniformly consistent with initial catalog labels. Representatives of `OG0000107`, `OG0000639`, and `OG0001087`, cataloged as subtilase, multidrug and toxic compound extrusion (MATE), and Argonaute, returned lectin/kinase, synthetase/glycosyltransferase/phosphohydrolase, and glycerophosphoryl-diester-phosphodiesterase domains, respectively. Likewise, the representative of `OG0000581`, cataloged as a nitrate transporter 1/peptide transporter family (NPF) member, returned terpene-synthase and reverse-transcriptase domains. Because single-representative profiling characterizes the focal protein model rather than the full paralog complement, these discrepancies do not conclusively demonstrate whole-family misclassification, as individual members may harbor modular or divergent domain architectures; establishing definitive functional annotations requires sequence reconciliation and comprehensive domain profiling across all paralogs (Supplementary Table S7).

Family-level counts also revealed copy-number divergence among ancestral expansions established on the core Velloziaceae stem branch (Supplementary Table S7). While small heat-shock proteins (`OG0000340`, HSP20) retained identical copy numbers in both *Vellozia* congeners (seven copies each), `OG0000858` (ten versus five copies) and `OG0000750` (one versus eight copies) differed in count between *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*, respectively, reflecting lineage-specific copy-number turnover following congeneric divergence. In these two families, initially cataloged as bZIP and MYB, the profiled representative sequences returned short-chain-dehydrogenase and ubiquitin domains, respectively (Supplementary Table S7).

Cross-lineage comparison between the two desiccation-tolerant species identified 36 candidate families with concordant net copy gains ($\Delta > 0$) in both *V. tubiflora* and *X. schlechteri*. However, branch-level statistical significance ($p < 0.05$ in CAFE5 on both branches) was restricted to six families (`OG0000070`, `OG0000010`, `OG0000134`, `OG0000071`, `OG0000216`, and `OG0000229`; Supplementary Table S7), with the remaining 30 representing numerical gains without dual-branch significance. Functional annotations across these 36 families are heterogeneous: while subsets correspond to photoprotection, chromatin regulation, and proteostasis, others represent general metabolic enzymes or uncharacterized proteins (Supplementary Table S7).

### 3.7 Coding-sequence divergence reveals widespread purifying constraint and lineage-specific rate heterogeneity

Sequence-level selective constraint evaluated across 10,966 candidate one-to-one ortholog trios polarized by *Xerophyta schlechteri* yielded 8,737 quality-filtered alignments (excluding 2,225 with internal stop codons, two with short CDS, and two with fewer than 30 aligned codons; Supplementary Table S8). Among these 8,737 analyzed trios, 7,798 (89.25%) had estimated $\omega \le 1.0$ across both *Vellozia* branches, with median ratios of 0.2541 in *V. peripherica* and 0.2391 in *V. tubiflora* (Figure 6A; Supplementary Table S8).

Screening for branch-specific rate heterogeneity combined with elevated coding divergence ($\omega > 1.0$, focal $dS \ge 0.005$, and no branch $dS > 2.0$) identified three mutually exclusive candidate sets: 84 trios accelerated exclusively in *V. tubiflora*, 84 accelerated exclusively in *V. peripherica*, and 17 trios with elevated ratios on both focal branches (Figure 6B; Supplementary Table S8). Following genome-wide false discovery rate correction across all 8,737 filtered trios, 32 loci retained significant branch heterogeneity ($q < 0.05$): 14 accelerated exclusively in *V. tubiflora*, 14 accelerated exclusively in *V. peripherica*, and four additional loci with simultaneous elevation in both lineages (Table 3; Figure 6B; Supplementary Table S8). All 32 statistically supported candidates are presented in Table 3.

Among the candidates with heterogeneity-test $q<0.05$ in Table 3, *V. tubiflora* loci include a cold-shock-domain protein (`PAIR001123`, $\omega_{Vtub}=2.4274$, $\omega_{Vper}=0.5120$, $q = 0.0286$), an S1 RNA-binding-domain protein (`PAIR015088`, $\omega_{Vtub}=1.3065$, $\omega_{Vper}=0.0793$, $q=0.00539$), a Tim17/22/23-family mitochondrial-import annotation (`PAIR012080`, $\omega_{Vtub}=2.9336$, $\omega_{Vper}=0.0032$, $q = 0.0166$), and a class-3-lipase annotation (`PAIR008298`, $\omega_{Vtub}=1.1378$, $\omega_{Vper}=0.4088$, $q=0.00125$). Corresponding *V. peripherica* loci include a plant lipid-transfer protein (`PAIR004160`, $\omega_{Vper}=2.1553$, $\omega_{Vtub}=0.9438$, $q = 0.0124$), an exostosin-family glycosyltransferase (`PAIR012128`, $\omega_{Vper}=1.2399$, $\omega_{Vtub}=0.0134$, $q=0.00178$), and a glycosyl hydrolase family 1 protein (`PAIR010277`, $\omega_{Vper}=1.1798$, $\omega_{Vtub}=0.1336$, $q=0.00935$; Table 3; Supplementary Table S8).

## Discussion

### 4.1 Ancestral stress-tolerance repertoires predate recent copy-number divergence in *Vellozia*

The collinearity and syntenic-depth summaries establish a structurally conserved baseline between the two *Vellozia* congeners (Figure 1; Supplementary Figure S1). The preservation of 1:1 macrosynteny across all nine chromosome pairs, where structural divergence along the orthologous backbone was restricted to local intra-chromosomal micro-inversions rather than karyotypic reshuffling or inter-chromosomal translocations, and matching syntenic-depth distributions (with depth-1 and depth-2 assignments comprising 95.6% and 90.2% of the gene space in *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*, respectively) indicate that no independent whole-genome duplication occurred following their late Miocene divergence (~9.74 Ma; Figure 2A). Against this shared structural foundation, the 132 inferred expansions on the branch leading to core Velloziaceae place key copy-number gains prior to the *Xerophyta*-*Vellozia* divergence (Figure 2B; Table 1). Expanded ancestral families include small heat-shock proteins (HSP20, `OG0000340`, +5 copies), B3-domain transcriptional regulators (`OG0000136`, +5 copies), bZIP factors (`OG0000858`, +4 copies), and germin-like proteins (`OG0000035`, +12 copies; Supplementary Table S7). Because these components are central to protective and desiccation-associated responses in *Xerophyta schlechteri* (Costa et al., 2017), their ancestral proliferation indicates that an essential stress-protection repertoire was already established in the common ancestor of core Velloziaceae between 78.21 and 31.91 Ma.

Present-day copy numbers further distinguish shared ancestral inheritance from subsequent lineage-specific turnover. The HSP20 family `OG0000340` retains an identical complement of seven copies in both *Vellozia* species, whereas `OG0000858` and `OG0000750` exhibit copy-number divergence (Supplementary Table S7). Superimposed upon this ancestral core, branch-specific birth-death modeling revealed pronounced turnover heterogeneity following congeneric divergence: terminal expansions reached 217 families in *V. peripherica* compared with 50 in *V. tubiflora* (199 versus 50 across near-optimal runs; Table 1; Figure 2B). Although the continuous rate estimate in *V. peripherica* ($\lambda_{Vper} = 0.008204$ versus $\lambda_{Vtub} = 0.002092$ events/gene/Ma) converged at the numerical optimization boundary imposed by the monocot root age in CAFE5 (Supplementary Table S5), the fourfold excess of expanding families and the predominance of local duplicates independently establish substantial turnover acceleration in this lineage. This asymmetry is compatible with distinct macroevolutionary trajectories: the retention and regulatory deployment of an ancestral stress repertoire in the desiccation-tolerant *V. tubiflora*, contrasted with elevated local gene proliferation in the dehydration-avoidant *V. peripherica*. While tandem duplications occur widely across plant genomes and can encompass stress-responsive loci without specifically diagnosing desiccation survival (VanBuren et al., 2019; Schuster & VanBuren, 2026), this asymmetric expansion provides a candidate genomic substrate for physiological divergence between the two congeners.

Although outgroup comparisons with *Xerophyta schlechteri* and *Acanthochlamys bracteata* provide phylogenetic context to interpret evolutionary changes, distinguishing strategy-specific adaptations from demographic history or differences in effective population size ($N_e$) remains constrained by the study of a single congeneric species pair. Concordant net copy gains across 36 candidate families in both *V. tubiflora* and *X. schlechteri* (of which six display statistically significant branch expansions in both lineages; Supplementary Table S7) may reflect either convergent specialization or ancestral retention. By delineating these concordant expansions against the core Velloziaceae background, this congeneric comparison establishes prioritized genomic candidates to evaluate the relative contributions of convergence versus ancestral retention across independent resurrection lineages (Marks et al., 2024).

### 4.2 Genomic architecture of desiccation tolerance in *Vellozia tubiflora*: ancestral inheritance, post-transcriptional regulators, and organellar dynamics

For the desiccation-tolerant *V. tubiflora*, the restrained number of terminal expansions (50 families; Table 1) and the absence of overrepresented Gene Ontology biological processes following permutation filtering (Supplementary Table S6) support the hypothesis that vegetative desiccation tolerance relies predominantly on the deployment and fine-tuning of an inherited ancestral stress repertoire rather than widespread lineage-specific family proliferation. This pattern is consistent with comparative studies in other resurrection systems, where desiccation survival frequently involves the co-option and differential regulation of pre-existing seed-maturation and stress-responsive networks without necessitating de novo whole-genome or large-scale family expansions (Costa et al., 2017; Lyall et al., 2020; Pardo et al., 2020).

The absence of statistically enriched functional pathways in *V. tubiflora* also reflects partial homology coverage in mesophytic reference databases: 56.0% of its terminal expansions lacked Biological Process terms, and 16.0% lacked *Arabidopsis* homologs entirely (Supplementary Table S6). While protein domain profiling resolved specific domain architectures for prioritized candidates, prominent expansions such as `OG0000216` (+4 copies; 18 copies in *V. tubiflora* and 19 in *X. schlechteri*; Supplementary Table S7) yielded no public domain signatures. Consequently, the lack of categorical GO enrichment does not indicate functional neutrality; rather, it highlights lineage-specific or extremophyte-restricted candidate genes whose biological roles cannot be captured by homology transfer from mesophytic model plants.

Where domain architectures were resolved, prominent candidates among the terminal expansions in *V. tubiflora* included post-transcriptional and epigenetic regulators. These include ribonuclease III-like endoribonucleases (`OG0001778`, net gain of +10 copies, PF14622/PF00636) and Tudor/Agenet domain-containing proteins (`OG0000643`, +7 copies, PF05641; Figure 5A; Supplementary Table S7). Tudor/Agenet domains recognize methylated histone marks and participate in chromatin remodeling (Maurer-Stroh et al., 2003; Huang et al., 2016), whereas RNase III enzymes mediate small RNA processing and post-transcriptional gene silencing (Margis et al., 2006; Bologna & Voinnet, 2014). Evidence for chromatin dynamics and post-transcriptional mRNA management under extreme dehydration derives from specific desiccation-tolerant models. In the resurrection angiosperm *Haberlea rhodopensis*, water deficit induces extensive chromatin remodeling and shifts in chromatin accessibility (Mladenov et al., 2023). Similarly, in the desiccation-tolerant moss *Tortula ruralis*, stored mRNAs are sequestered into messenger ribonucleoprotein (mRNP) particles during dehydration and preserved for translational recovery upon rehydration (Wood & Oliver, 1999). Although these mechanisms represent system-specific responses rather than universal features demonstrated across all desiccation-tolerant plants, the lineage-specific amplification of Agenet and RNase III candidates identifies chromatin modification and RNA processing as prioritized regulatory layers for empirical testing across dehydration-rehydration time courses in *Vellozia*.

Gene-family expansions in *V. tubiflora* include prominent copy-number enrichment in organellar proteome machinery. Examination of high-copy families excluded from birth-death modeling identified elevated copy numbers in the chloroplast protein import translocon complex (Toc/Tic, `OG0000007`; Supplementary Table S4). While the dehydration-avoidant *V. peripherica* possesses 14 copies and external monocot outgroups possess $\le 7$, `OG0000007` harbors 105 copies in *V. tubiflora* and 72 in the African resurrection plant *X. schlechteri*. Both species are poikilochlorophyllous, dismantling thylakoid membranes and degrading chlorophyll during foliar drying (Alcantara et al., 2015; Costa et al., 2017). In African *Xerophyta*, chlorophyll and thylakoid proteins reaccumulate within 24 h of rewatering and photosynthetic competence recovers within three days (Farrant et al., 2015), accompanied by chloroplast structural reorganization by 72 h in *X. schlechteri* (Radermacher et al., 2019). Rebuilding this photosynthetic apparatus relies primarily on the import of nuclear-encoded proteins from the cytosol (Sun & Jarvis, 2023). Although *V. tubiflora* similarly survives desiccation and degrades chlorophyll (Alcantara et al., 2015), plastid-import translocons were not recovered as a general convergent feature across diverse resurrection lineages (Marks et al., 2024). Nevertheless, recovering elevated Toc/Tic copy numbers in both poikilochlorophyllous lineages establishes chloroplast protein import as a candidate organellar module for post-desiccation biogenesis in Velloziaceae, providing a targeted foundation for transcriptional profiling across rehydration time courses.

### 4.3 Local gene duplications expand cell-surface surveillance, defense, and hormone-signaling repertoires in *Vellozia peripherica*

In marked contrast to its congener, the dehydration-avoidant *V. peripherica* underwent extensive local gene amplification, with tandem and proximal duplicates accounting for 66.47% of classified models in expanding families (46.25% tandem and 20.22% proximal; Table 2; Figure 3B) alongside pronounced depletion of WGD/segmental duplicates (12.16%). In angiosperms, tandem and proximal duplications frequently expand gene families involved in environmental perception and stress response (Freeling, 2009; Birchler & Veitia, 2012; Qiao et al., 2019), providing a localized mechanism of copy-number expansion within the conserved syntenic framework of the genus.

This local proliferation heavily targets cell-surface surveillance and cell-wall integrity complexes. Domain profiling resolved multiple expanded families into receptor-like kinases, including wall-associated receptor kinases (WAKs, `OG0000012`, net gain of +17 copies; Figure 5A) and multiple families of G-type lectin receptor-like kinases (G-LecRLKs / S-domain RLKs, including `OG0000026`, `OG0000286`, and `OG0000107`, which was reconciled from an initial subtilase catalog label; Supplementary Table S7). While Gene Ontology analyses transferred the annotation "recognition of pollen" (GO:0048544; OR = 17.40, $q_{global} = 0.0387$, $p_{strat} = 0.0173$; Figure 4B) due to homology with Brassicaceae S-locus systems, domain profiling revealed S-locus glycoprotein (PF00954), Bulb-type mannose-binding lectin (PF01453), PAN/Apple (PF08276), and intracellular kinase (PF07714/PF00069) signatures (Supplementary Table S7). In vegetative tissues, WAKs physically bind native cell-wall pectins (Kohorn & Kohorn, 2012), although knockout of all five canonical WAKs in *Arabidopsis* demonstrated that they are dispensable for oligogalacturonide-induced signaling and immune responses (Herold et al., 2024). Concurrently, G-LecRLKs represent candidate cell-surface surveillance sensors implicated in oligosaccharide perception and cell-wall integrity signaling (Bellande et al., 2017; Wolf, 2017). The amplification of both receptor classes identifies cell-wall surveillance and mechanical monitoring as prioritized regulatory layers for experimental testing under prolonged water deficit in *V. peripherica*.

Coordinated with cell-surface surveillance, expanded families in *V. peripherica* are enriched in defense and stress-signaling pathways, notably response to salicylic acid (GO:0009751; OR = 7.41, $q_{global} = 0.00252$, $p_{strat} = 0.0232$; Figure 4B). While salicylic acid can promote stomatal closure under specific concentrations via reactive oxygen species (Miura et al., 2013), SA and ABA signaling frequently exhibit dose- and context-dependent antagonism (Yasuda et al., 2008). Consequently, gene-family enrichment alone does not establish that expanded SA-associated loci contribute to drought-induced stomatal control in *V. peripherica*. Instead, because *V. peripherica* maintains persistent green foliage year-round across wet and dry seasons under severe seasonal drought, oligotrophy, and high UV irradiance in the Serra da Canastra (Pinto et al., 2025), expanding these defense-associated repertoires may prioritize pathogen surveillance and foliar protection in long-lived leaves (Coley et al., 1985).

The functional profiles of co-expanding families support this integrated physiological defense. Cytochrome P450 monooxygenases (`OG0000058`, +17 copies; Supplementary Table S7) encompass lineages implicated in antioxidant flavonoid production (Pandian et al., 2020) and cuticular lipid biosynthesis, where characterized CYP86 and CYP77 enzymes catalyze fatty acid hydroxylation in other angiosperms (Grausem et al., 2014; Mizutani, 2012). If these duplicated *V. peripherica* loci share related catalytic activities, their expansion could contribute to minimizing non-stomatal transpiration during drought. Concurrently, intracellular NB-ARC resistance genes (`OG0000042`, +26 copies; Supplementary Table S7) experienced substantial lineage-specific duplication. In model angiosperms, NB-ARC-containing NLR receptors mediate effector-triggered immunity and pathogen-induced stomatal closure via salicylic acid signaling (Jones & Dangl, 2006; Saijo & Loo, 2020; Wang et al., 2023). In *V. peripherica*, this expanded receptor repertoire may support enhanced pathogen surveillance in persistent green leaves throughout seasonal dry periods. In parallel, the transporter family `OG0005019` (+3 copies) carries the proton-dependent oligopeptide transporter domain PF00854 (NPF family; Léran et al., 2014). Beyond nitrogen reallocation in nutrient-depleted soils, members of this family, such as *Arabidopsis* NRT1.2/NPF4.6, act as cellular abscisic acid (ABA) uptake transporters that regulate stomatal aperture under water deficit (Kanno et al., 2012). Rather than undergoing seasonal foliar desiccation, *V. peripherica* retains persistent foliage and maintains an expanded local repertoire that coordinates foliar surveillance, defense, and water-conservation pathways, establishing prioritized targets for ecophysiological and functional validation.

### 4.4 Predominant purifying constraint and targeted coding divergence in specialized stress-related genes

While gene-family modeling revealed divergent copy-number turnover between the two *Vellozia* congeners, coding-sequence analysis of 8,737 1:1:1 ortholog trios, using the African resurrection plant *Xerophyta schlechteri* as a phylogenetic outgroup to polarize lineage-specific substitutions, indicates a predominant trend of purifying constraint across the shared proteome. Across these polarized trios, point estimates of $\omega \le 1.0$ along both focal *Vellozia* branches characterized 89.25% of loci (7,798 trios), with low median ratios of 0.2541 in *V. peripherica* and 0.2391 in *V. tubiflora* (Figure 6A; Supplementary Table S8). These values represent descriptive point estimates across the proteome rather than locus-specific hypothesis tests against neutrality ($\omega = 1$), reflecting predominant conservation of essential cellular housekeeping and basal metabolic machinery against which physiological divergence between dehydration avoidance and desiccation tolerance unfolded.

Superimposed upon this conserved background, codon-based branch models (`NSsites = 0`) identified candidate loci with lineage-specific coding divergence. Because this framework estimates an average $\omega$ across the entire coding sequence, it tests for branch-level rate heterogeneity across lineages rather than site-specific positive selection on isolated codons (which would require codon-level branch-site models; Yang, 2007; Zhang et al., 2005). Consequently, loci exhibiting significant rate heterogeneity ($q < 0.05$) alongside focal $\omega > 1.0$ identify candidate genes that experienced accelerated nonsynonymous substitution, potentially driven by lineage-specific adaptive diversification or localized relaxation of selective constraint. While 84 candidate trios per species met nominal heterogeneity thresholds ($p < 0.05$), 14 loci per species retained significance after stringent genome-wide false discovery rate correction ($q < 0.05$; Table 3; Figure 6B). 

These prioritized candidates highlight distinct physiological targets for future empirical testing. In *V. tubiflora*, accelerated loci feature cold-shock nucleic-acid binding (`PAIR001123`), S1 RNA-binding (`PAIR015088`), class-3 lipase (`PAIR008298`), and the mitochondrial inner membrane translocase TIM22 (`PAIR012080`; Table 3; Supplementary Table S8). Cold-shock domain proteins function as essential RNA chaperones that prevent deleterious RNA secondary structures and preserve transcript stability during cellular dehydration (Kim et al., 2009), while lipase-mediated turnover facilitates membrane lipid remodeling and triacylglycerol accumulation to preserve membrane integrity during protoplasmic shrinkage (Gasulla et al., 2013). Divergence of plant TIM22 components from yeast and metazoan counterparts, including an expanded family of PRAT proteins, has been documented (Carrie et al., 2010), and mitochondrial respiration in the resurrection plant *Haberlea rhodopensis* reactivates almost immediately upon rehydration (Ivanova et al., 2022). In this context, sequence divergence in TIM22 is consistent with lineage-specific modulation of the mitochondrial protein import machinery (Murcha et al., 2014), which is required to preserve inner membrane transport and support bioenergetic recovery during rehydration (Radermacher et al., 2019).

In *V. peripherica*, accelerated candidates correspond to a non-specific lipid-transfer protein (`PAIR004160`), a predicted exostosin-family glycosyltransferase (`PAIR012128`), glycosyl hydrolases (`PAIR010277`), and the endoplasmic reticulum membrane protein POM33 (`PAIR008817`; Table 3). Although *in vivo* roles across lipid-transfer protein families remain diverse and unresolved, specific members contribute to cuticular lipid export and surface barrier deposition (DeBono et al., 2009; Edqvist et al., 2018). Accelerated divergence in `PAIR004160` may thus reflect candidate adjustments in cuticular permeability to limit non-stomatal transpiration in persistent foliage. Similarly, sequence similarity to animal exostosins characterizes plant glycosyltransferase family 47 (GT47) members, including the xyloglucan galactosyltransferase MUR3 (Madson et al., 2003). While GT47 enzymes participate in cell-wall matrix polysaccharide biosynthesis and remodeling (Tenhaken, 2015; Amos & Mohnen, 2019), `PAIR012128` represents an uncharacterized candidate within this family whose accelerated nonsynonymous divergence points to lineage-specific modification of cell-wall matrix polysaccharides during sustained foliar dehydration. Together, these lineage-specific substitution patterns are compatible with divergence in distinct physiological modules: intracellular transcript stability and organellar preservation in the desiccation-tolerant *V. tubiflora*, contrasted with extracellular barrier reinforcement and cell-wall remodeling in the dehydration-avoidant *V. peripherica*, identifying candidate loci for targeted expression profiling and functional characterization across progressive dehydration time courses.

## Conclusion

The physiological continuum separating dehydration avoidance from vegetative desiccation tolerance in *Vellozia* was shaped by asymmetric gene-family dynamics and lineage-specific sequence divergence operating within a conserved syntenic framework. Congeneric divergence did not require whole-genome duplication or structural reorganization. Instead, vegetative desiccation tolerance in *V. tubiflora* is characterized by the retention of an inherited ancestral stress core shared with African resurrection lineages, complemented by copy-number enrichment in chromatin regulators and small RNA processing enzymes, alongside elevated Toc/Tic translocon copies compatible with plastid reconstruction during rehydration. Conversely, the dehydration-avoidant shrub *V. peripherica* accumulated extensive local duplications in cell-wall surveillance systems, G-type lectin receptor kinases, and salicylic acid signaling components.

Across the broader proteome, genome-wide purifying constraint preserved basal metabolic machinery, while accelerated coding divergence was restricted to a subset of loci with diverse functional annotations, including transcriptional regulators, ATPases, and candidates involved in membrane integrity, organellar transport, and barrier deposition. These contrasting genomic architectures are compatible with distinct molecular trajectories separating avoidance and tolerance in *campos rupestres*: the retention and organellar focus of an inherited stress repertoire in the desiccation-tolerant specialist, alongside duplicate expansion of surveillance and defense candidates in the dehydration-avoidant shrub.
