# Current CAFE5 methodology and results

Synchronized on 2026-10-05 from the verified current runs. The preceding document is preserved in the review archive.

## 2.5 Gene-family turnover and duplication architecture

Gene-family sizes were modeled with CAFE5 v5.1.0 (Mendes et al., 2020). Of the 17,750 orthogroups, 15 with at least 100 genes in a species were removed as a numerical safeguard, leaving 17,735 input families. The root-presence filter retained 12,276 families for model estimation and testing. The current Gamma analyses used the updated dated tree identified in Section 2.4.

A symmetric count-error model was estimated from replicated homogeneous-rate fits and then fixed in the Gamma analyses. Under the adopted parameterization, $\epsilon=0.00588995$ is the probability of each adjacent one-copy error away from the boundary, giving approximately $2\epsilon=0.0117799$ combined probability for a one-copy discrepancy. This models count uncertainty; it does not independently validate assemblies, annotations or orthogroup composition.

Five optimization runs were evaluated for each Gamma discretization (k=2, k=3 and k=4). Four k=2 runs were retained; replicate 04 reached an inferior optimum and recorded a family failure. All five k=3 runs were retained. Four k=4 runs were retained; replicate 01 reached an inferior optimum and recorded a family failure. The primary reconstruction used k=2 replicate 01. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied across all 12,276 tested families within each run. Tier A comprises the 196 families with q ≤ 0.05 in all four retained k=2 runs and all five k=3 runs; all 196 also passed this threshold in all four retained k=4 runs. Tier B comprises 58 additional families significant in all five k=3 runs but not in the k=2 consensus. These are repeated optimizations of the same dataset, not independent biological replicates. Family-level significance does not establish independently significant changes on individual branches; directional changes are Viterbi reconstructions.

Two distinct directional summaries were used. The sign of the reconstructed change describes all positive or negative changes within Tier A. The terminal-enrichment indicator additionally requires the focal species to have strictly more copies than the other sampled Velloziaceae. Thus, counts from the terminal-enrichment indicator are subsets of positive changes and should not be labeled as all expansions. Ancestral sign stability was assessed separately across retained k=3 runs.

Duplication architecture in the two *Vellozia* genomes was described using the archived MCScanX classifications (Wang et al., 2012) and JCVI collinearity analyses. MCScanX used the full intra-genomic homology input rather than the JCVI-filtered input, preserving local homologous hits. The documented parameters were `-k 50 -g -1 -s 5 -e 1e-5 -w 5 -m 25 -n 10`; classifications comprised WGD/segmental, tandem, proximal, dispersed and singleton categories. Category proportions use the complete annotated gene complement of each species as denominator. The combined WGD/segmental class describes collinearity and does not by itself date a whole-genome duplication. Genomic positions and inherited functional descriptions were used for candidate interpretation, not as independent tests of physiological function.


## 3.3 Family-level turnover and structural context of copy-number differences

The representative error-corrected k=2 reconstruction (replicate 01) had $\lambda=0.00362378$ per gene per Ma and shape parameter $\alpha=0.899434$, with $-\ln L\approx107,704$ (the reported likelihood is rounded to an integer). The 196-family Tier A consensus provides repeatable family-level evidence under the retained configurations. These estimates are conditional on the dated tree, count-error model and orthogroup definitions.

Within Tier A, the representative k=2 reconstruction contains 117 positive and 19 negative changes at the sampled *Xerophyta–Vellozia* ancestor (Node 12). Of the 117 positive changes, 104 retain a positive sign in all five k=3 runs, whereas 106 retain a positive sign in all four retained k=4 runs. These counts describe directional stability under each sensitivity model; they do not establish independently significant branch changes or the ancestral desiccation phenotype.

On the terminal branches, Tier A contains 86 positive and 64 negative changes in *V. peripherica*, and 56 positive and 80 negative changes in *V. tubiflora*. The more restrictive terminal-enrichment indicator retains 73 and 35 families, respectively, because it additionally requires the highest extant count among the sampled Velloziaceae. These subsets should not be substituted for all positive changes when describing branch totals or calculating a balance.

The annotated genomes differ in their local duplication classifications. *V. peripherica* contains 5,625 tandem or proximal genes (21.25% of 26,466 annotated genes), compared with 4,078 (15.96% of 25,551) in *V. tubiflora*. The difference is 1,547 genes. WGD/segmental classifications account for 7,459 genes (28.18%) and 7,224 (28.27%), respectively. These are descriptive proportions; similar WGD/segmental fractions do not independently establish an ancestral polyploidization event, and the local-duplicate difference does not estimate an adaptive duplication rate.

Several prioritized families combine higher *V. peripherica* counts with local arrays in the archived structural analysis. Examples include the provisionally DREB/CBF-like family OG0000306 (11 versus 3 copies) and the UDP-glycosyltransferase-associated family OG0000281 (14 versus 4). Their reported local clusters provide positional context for the copy-number differences, while biochemical specificity and roles during water loss remain to be established.

For the provisionally NDH-associated families OG0000014, OG0000156 and OG0000278, the v2 master table records 67, 18 and 6 genes in *V. peripherica*, totaling 91 annotated genes. The corresponding reconstructed terminal changes are +31, +8 and +3, totaling +42. The value 42 therefore refers to a sum of inferred changes, not the number of present-day anchored copies. Local arrays reported for these families motivate further investigation but do not demonstrate enhanced electron transport or coordinated physiological activity.

Other examples require reconciliation before functional interpretation. OG0000026 is a Tier A family with 21 *V. peripherica* copies and a reconstructed change of +4, rather than the previously stated F-box expansion of +51. For OG0000317 and OG0000332, the annotation discrepancy involves two sources: the Vellozia descriptions are pre-mRNA-splicing factor and glycine-rich RNA-binding protein, whereas the actual Arabidopsis members used in the enrichment mapping are the plastid reference sequences NP_051114.1 (ArthCp079/ndhA) and NP_051113.1 (ArthCp078/ndhI). The archived KEGG mapping assigns both to ath00190. This establishes the provenance of their NDH-associated pathway labels, not the function or integrity of every Vellozia model. In V. tubiflora, all 28 and 25 models, respectively, occur on unplaced contigs; in V. peripherica, all five OG0000317 models are unplaced, whereas OG0000332 has two chromosome-anchored and five unplaced models. These positions warrant checks for redundancy, fragmentation and organellar sequence representation but do not demonstrate artifacts. Neither contig placement nor chromosomal anchoring alone establishes copy authenticity or functionality. The annotation sources and gene positions are documented in [the reconciliation record](../../chapter_macroevolution/docs/reviews/annotation_provenance_2026-09-15.md).

### 3.3.1 Rate-heterogeneity sensitivity

The k=3 consensus contains 254 families, including the 196 Tier A families and 58 additional Tier B candidates. In the representative k=2 reconstruction, these 58 candidates include 31 positive changes at Node 12, 22 in *V. peripherica* and 11 in *V. tubiflora*. These overlapping subsets describe reconstructed directions rather than separately significant branch events. The representative k=3 and k=4 fits had reported −lnL values of approximately 107,175 and 106,856, with turnover scales of 0.00327108 and 0.00318130 per gene per Ma and shape parameters of 1.02510 and 1.01791, respectively.


## Functional enrichment update

The two-level enrichment workflow was recalculated using the current family table and locally archived GO and KEGG annotations. Exact Fisher tests used category-specific annotated universes, with local and primary-global Benjamini–Hochberg correction. The primary scenario used 10,000 stratified permutations with seed 20260904. Classification also required the covariate-balance gate. Among 7,269 primary inferential tests, one term was classified as confirmatory, 21 as suggestive with imbalanced annotation opportunity and 53 as permutation-discordant; 7,194 were not significant. These findings prioritize functional hypotheses and do not establish causal links to water-loss strategy.

Full results and category-specific denominators are in [the current enrichment report](functional_enrichment/functional_enrichment_summary.md). Redundancy was reduced for plotting only, preserving full test tables. The separate R simplification outputs are archived/intermediate and have not been rerun.
