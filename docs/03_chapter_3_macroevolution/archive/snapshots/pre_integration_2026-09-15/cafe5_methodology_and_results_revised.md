# Evolutionary timescale, genomic synteny, and gene-family turnover across Velloziaceae: Methodology and results

## 1. Methods

### 1.1 Gene-family data and filtering

Gene-family sizes were obtained from the orthogroups inferred in the comparative-genomics analysis with OrthoFinder (Emms and Kelly, 2019). The complete matrix contained 17,750 orthogroups across seven taxa: *Vellozia peripherica*, *Vellozia tubiflora*, *Xerophyta viscosa*, *Acanthochlamys bracteata*, *Pandanus tectorius*, *Dioscorea alata* and *Arabidopsis thaliana*. To reduce numerical instability caused by extreme differences in copy number, we excluded 15 orthogroups containing 100 or more annotated genes in at least one species. The resulting matrix of 17,735 families was supplied to CAFE5 v5.1.0 (Mendes et al., 2020). After applying the root-presence filtering rule required for the likelihood analysis, 12,276 families remained for parameter estimation and family-level testing (Figure 1).

![Figure 1. Gene-family evolution workflow](figures/cafe5_workflow.png)

**Figure 1. Gene-family evolution workflow and analytical decision path.** The diagram shows the number of orthogroups retained after copy-number filtering and the root-presence filtering rule required for the likelihood analysis, followed by replicated model fitting, within-run genome-wide FDR correction, and definition of a cross-replicate consensus set. All CAFE5 analyses used the same time-calibrated ultrametric tree and, for the Gamma models, the same fixed count-error matrix.

The copy-number threshold was used as a numerical safeguard rather than as evidence that large families were biologically uninformative. Consequently, excluded families may still merit targeted investigation using gene trees, genomic position, read coverage and synteny. This validation is particularly important in plant genomes, where tandem duplication, retained haplotypes, fragmented gene models and organellar insertions can produce large or apparently lineage-specific families.

### 1.2 Single-copy phylogenomic supermatrix assembly, partitioned maximum-likelihood, and coalescent species-tree inference

To construct an authoritative, high-resolution phylogenomic framework for the Pan-Velloziaceae radiation and outgroup taxa, we identified 1,513 strictly single-copy 1:1 orthologous protein-coding genes across the seven annotated genomes (*Vellozia peripherica*, *Vellozia tubiflora*, *Xerophyta viscosa*, *Acanthochlamys bracteata*, *Pandanus tectorius*, *Dioscorea alata*, and *Arabidopsis thaliana*) from the orthogroups inferred by OrthoFinder (Emms and Kelly, 2019). Individual orthologous gene sequences were aligned at the amino acid level using MAFFT v7.520 (Katoh and Standley, 2013) with automated heuristic algorithm selection (`--auto --quiet`). To eliminate alignment noise, lineage-specific insertions, and ambiguously aligned regions without discarding informative phylogenetic sites, alignment columns containing more than 50% gaps or ambiguous characters were trimmed (`max_gap_fraction = 0.5`).

All 1,513 trimmed single-copy gene alignments were concatenated into a dense phylogenomic supermatrix comprising 732,066 aligned amino acid positions across the seven taxa (`pan_velloziaceae_supermatrix.fa` / `.phy`). Sequence completeness across the concatenated matrix was exceptionally high across all lineages, with minimal missing/gap data: *Arabidopsis thaliana* (3.56% gaps), *Dioscorea alata* (3.99% gaps; 96.01% complete), *Pandanus tectorius* (1.92% gaps), *Acanthochlamys bracteata* (3.65% gaps), *Xerophyta viscosa* (15.33% gaps), *Vellozia peripherica* (1.86% gaps), and *Vellozia tubiflora* (1.95% gaps).

Phylogenomic species-tree reconstruction was conducted under two complementary paradigms: concatenated partitioned maximum-likelihood (ML) and multi-species coalescent (MSC) summary inference:
1. **Partitioned Maximum Likelihood (IQ-TREE 2):** Inferred using IQ-TREE 2 v2.3.6 (Minh et al., 2020). Among-locus evolutionary rate heterogeneity and empirical substitution dynamics were explicitly modeled by assigning independent partition models to each of the 1,513 single-copy loci (`iqtree_pan_velloziaceae.best_scheme.nex`). Best-fit amino acid substitution models, site rate heterogeneity (Gamma distributions or FreeRate models), and empirical amino acid frequencies were determined via ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al., 2017) using BIC. Statistical branch support across the concatenated supermatrix was assessed via 1,000 Ultrafast Bootstrap replicates (UFBoot2; Hoang et al., 2018).
2. **Gene and Site Concordance Factors (gCF and sCF):** Because concatenated supermatrices of massive length (>700,000 aa) can suffer from bootstrap inflation—yielding 100% bootstrap values even in the presence of underlying gene-tree conflict—we quantified topological heterogeneity across the genome by calculating Gene Concordance Factors (gCF) and Site Concordance Factors (sCF) across all 1,513 loci using IQ-TREE 2 (`--gcf` and `--scf 100`; Minh et al., 2020). For each branch of the species tree, gCF represents the percentage of individual gene trees (out of 1,513) that contain that specific bipartition, while sCF represents the percentage of decisive alignment sites supporting that split over alternative quartet configurations. We also evaluated gene discordance factors for alternative nearest-neighbor interchanges (gDF1 and gDF2) and polyphyletic discordance (gDFP).
3. **Multi-Species Coalescent Inference (ASTRAL-III):** To explicitly account for incomplete lineage sorting (ILS) and potential gene-tree discordance, maximum-likelihood gene trees were inferred individually for each of the 1,513 loci under their best-fit models in IQ-TREE 2 (`-S`). The resulting set of 1,513 unrooted gene trees was supplied to ASTRAL-III v5.7.8 (Zhang et al., 2018) to infer the species tree under the coalescent model. Branch support in ASTRAL was evaluated via local posterior probabilities (LPP) and normalized quartet support scores.

### 1.3 Bayesian molecular dating and time-calibrated phylogeny

Absolute divergence times and an ultrametric chronogram across the Pan-Velloziaceae radiation were inferred using the Bayesian Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm implemented in MCMCtree from the PAML v4.10 suite (Yang, 2007). The phylogenomic alignment consisted of the 1,513 concatenated single-copy orthologous protein-coding genes across the seven genomes (732,066 amino acid positions, `pan_velloziaceae_supermatrix.phy`). The partitioned maximum-likelihood topology inferred with IQ-TREE 2—which exhibited 100% Ultrafast Bootstrap support across every internal node (`((((((Vper, Vtub), Xvis), Abra), Ptec), Dala), Ath);`)—was specified as the fixed species tree structure.

To accommodate evolutionary rate heterogeneity across lineages without imposing artificial autocorrelation between ancestral and descendant branches, we implemented an independent rates log-normal relaxed molecular clock model (`clock = 2`), wherein branch-specific substitution rates are treated as independent random variables drawn from a log-normal distribution. Amino acid substitutions were modeled under an empirical Poisson substitution process with five-category discrete Gamma-distributed rate variation across sites (`model = 0`, `alpha = 0.5`, `ncatG = 5`). Lineage diversification across the tree was governed by a Birth–Death–Sampling process with uniform sampling fraction (`BDparas = 1 1 0.1`). The prior on the mean substitution rate across the tree ($\mu$) was specified using a diffuse Gamma distribution, $\mu \sim G(2, 20)$ (`rgene_gamma = 2 20 1`), reflecting a prior mean rate of 0.1 substitutions per site per 100 million years ($1.0 \times 10^{-9}$ substitutions site$^{-1}$ year$^{-1}$). The rate drift parameter ($\sigma^2$), which governs clock rate variance across branches, was assigned a diffuse Gamma hyperprior of $\sigma^2 \sim G(1, 10)$ (`sigma2_gamma = 1 10 1`). A soft upper bound was placed on the root age (`RootAge = '<1.50'`), bounding the maximum possible age of crown angiosperms at 150 Ma.

Given the immense computational burden of full likelihood evaluations across 732,066 amino acid sites, Bayesian parameter integration was conducted using the two-step approximate likelihood method developed by Thorne et al. (1998) and extended by dos Reis & Yang (2011). In the initial step (`usedata = 3`), maximum-likelihood estimates of branch lengths, the gradient vector, and the Hessian matrix of second derivatives were calculated under the empirical substitution model, generating the variance-covariance matrix (`in.BV`). In the second step (`usedata = 2 in.BV`), MCMC sampling was performed on the Taylor-approximated likelihood surface, which provides asymptotic equivalence to exact likelihood while enabling deep sampling iterations across multi-megabase genomic matrices (dos Reis & Yang, 2011).

Node age constraints were established by specifying five paleontologically vetted, conservative uniform calibration priors across the tree, scaling time in units of 100 Ma (1.0 = 100 Ma):
1. **Crown Angiosperms ($t_{\text{n8}}$, Eudicots vs. Monocots):** Uniform calibration of 130–145 Ma (`>1.30 <1.45`), based on the earliest definitive crown angiosperm fossil pollen records from the Valanginian–Hauterivian boundary and comprehensive macroevolutionary syntheses (Magallón et al., 2015; Foster et al., 2017; Silvestro et al., 2021).
2. **Crown Monocots ($t_{\text{n9}}$, Dioscoreales vs. Pandanales):** Uniform calibration of 115–128 Ma (`>1.15 <1.28`), consistent with the Aptian fossil record of early monocot lineages, including fossil relatives of *Croomia* and *Mayoa* (Iles et al., 2015; Givnish et al., 2018).
3. **Crown Pandanales ($t_{\text{n10}}$, Pandanaceae vs. Velloziaceae):** Uniform calibration of 90–105 Ma (`>0.90 <1.05`), corresponding to Late Cretaceous Cenomanian fossil remains with diagnostic Pandanaceae affinities, such as *Pandaniocarpon* (Magallón et al., 2015).
4. **Crown Velloziaceae ($t_{\text{n11}}$, *Acanthochlamys* vs. Core Velloziaceae):** Uniform calibration of 75–90 Ma (`>0.75 <0.90`), adhering to Late Cretaceous Campanian fossil constraints established in Pandanales phylogenomic dating analyses (Mello-Silva et al., 2011; Wanga et al., 2021).
5. **Intrageneric *Vellozia* Speciation ($t_{\text{n13}}$, *V. peripherica* vs. *V. tubiflora*):** Uniform calibration of 2–25 Ma (`>0.02 <0.25`), reflecting the Neogene radiation and topographic uplift of the *campos rupestres* mountaintops (Mello-Silva et al., 2011; Alcantara et al., 2018).
* **Core Velloziaceae ($t_{\text{n12}}$, African *Xerophyta* vs. South American *Vellozia*):** Crucially, this internal trans-Atlantic divergence was left **completely unconstrained (free node)** to permit unbiased empirical estimation of the intercontinental split and directly test the Mesozoic Gondwanan vicariance hypothesis (~100–80 Ma) against post-breakup Tertiary oceanic dispersal and Neogene aridification (Mello-Silva et al., 2011).

To ensure thorough exploration of parameter space and verify MCMC convergence, we executed two completely independent long MCMC chains initialized from disparate pseudo-random seeds (`seed = 1234567` and `seed = 7654321`). Each chain was run for 3,050,000 generations, discarding the initial 50,000 iterations as burn-in (`burnin = 50000`) and sampling every 100 generations (`sampfreq = 100`) to harvest 30,000 parameter vectors per chain (total 60,000 posterior samples). Convergence between independent chains was evaluated using: (1) scatter plot correlation ($r^2$) of posterior mean node ages; (2) the Gelman–Rubin potential scale reduction factor ($\hat{R}$), requiring $\hat{R} < 1.01$; and (3) the Effective Sample Size (ESS) calculated via fast Fourier transform (FFT) autocorrelation, requiring $\text{ESS} > 200$. To confirm that posterior age estimates were driven by empirical phylogenomic data rather than prior truncation artifacts, a parallel control run was conducted without sequence data (`usedata = 0`) to sample exclusively from the joint prior distribution. The resulting high-ESS consensus chronogram was exported in Newick format (`velloziaceae_chronogram_high_ess.tre`) and supplied to CAFE5 for downstream gene-family birth–death modeling, where an ultrametric time tree is essential because turnover parameters ($\lambda$) are estimated relative to physical elapsed time (Mendes et al., 2020).

### 1.4 Count-error estimation and among-family rate variation

We fitted a homogeneous single-$\lambda$ birth–death model without count error and a homogeneous single-$\lambda$ model in which CAFE5 jointly estimated the turnover parameter ($\lambda$) and a symmetric count-error parameter ($\epsilon$). Each configuration was run independently five times to assess convergence from stochastic starting values. The error-corrected replicates converged on nearly identical parameter estimates. The error matrix from replicate 01, which had the lowest negative log-likelihood among these runs, was fixed in all subsequent Gamma analyses. Under the symmetric error matrix used here, $\epsilon=0.006092$ corresponds to the probability assigned to each adjacent one-copy transition; away from the zero-count boundary, the combined probability of a one-copy discrepancy is approximately $2\epsilon=0.01218$. Explicitly modeling count uncertainty is warranted because differences in genome assembly and structural annotation can otherwise inflate inferred rates of gene gain and loss (Han et al., 2013).

Among-family variation in turnover rate was modeled with a discrete Gamma distribution. We evaluated two ($k=2$) and three ($k=3$) rate categories in five independent runs per configuration. A four-category configuration ($k=4$) was evaluated in one sensitivity run. All runs estimated a single global $\lambda$ across the phylogeny and the Gamma shape parameter $\alpha$, used a Poisson root-family-size distribution estimated from the observed data (`-p`), requested family-level probabilities at the nominal threshold $P\leq0.05$ (`-P 0.05`) and used eight processing cores. The command structure was `cafe5 -i <family_counts> -t <time_tree> -p -P 0.05 -e<error_model> -k <categories> -c 8 -o <output_directory>`.

### 1.5 Model diagnostics and statistical treatment

We assessed each configuration using convergence of $\lambda$, $\alpha$ and $-\ln L$ across independent runs; the frequency of rejected parameter attempts; the number of families with optimization failure rates above 20%; and proximity of the fastest Gamma category to the topology-specific maximum rate, $\lambda_{\max}=0.007221$ gene$^{-1}$ Ma$^{-1}$. Here, $\lambda_{\max}$ denotes the topology- and implementation-specific numerical upper bound used to diagnose whether the fastest reconstructed Gamma category approached the region in which rate optimization may become unstable. The value of $\lambda_{\max}$ was obtained from the numerical optimization diagnostic implemented in the accompanying analysis script and was not treated as a biological parameter. For the latter diagnostic, the rate-category multipliers were reconstructed from equally spaced midpoint quantiles of a mean-one Gamma distribution with the estimated $\alpha$, normalized to unit mean and multiplied by the corresponding $\lambda$. Because the number of Gamma categories is a user-specified numerical discretization rather than a simple set of independently estimated rate parameters, the AIC values generated by the exploratory summary script were not used as the primary criterion for choosing $k$. This decision is consistent with the CAFE5 guidance that increasing the number of categories does not necessarily improve fit and may hinder convergence.

Family-level probabilities were obtained from the complete `Gamma_family_results.txt` files. For every run, the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure was applied jointly to all 12,276 tested families, with family-level statistical significance defined as false discovery rate (FDR) $q\leq0.05$ (Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995). This family-level test identifies families whose evolutionary rate or observed copy-number trajectory is unlikely under the fitted null model somewhere across the phylogeny; it does not, by itself, identify the responsible branch. We selected $k=2$ for the primary analysis because all five runs converged to the same optimum and the fastest reconstructed rate category remained separated from $\lambda_{\max}$. The four convergent $k=3$ runs were retained as a sensitivity analysis; `k=3` replicate 02 was excluded because it converged to a distinct, markedly inferior optimum. We defined a conservative consensus as the intersection of families passing family-level FDR in all five $k=2$ runs and all four valid $k=3$ runs. The localization of specific expansion and contraction events was based on the Viterbi reconstructions of ancestral family sizes, representing the most probable evolutionary path rather than independently tested branch-level shifts.

### 1.6 Functional annotation and over-representation analysis

To test whether gene-family expansions and contractions were associated with specific biological functions, orthogroups were functionally characterized using Gene Ontology (GO) terms and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway annotations derived from canonical *Arabidopsis thaliana* genes within each orthogroup (The Gene Ontology Consortium, 2021; Kanehisa and Goto, 2000). The analytical background universe was defined as the set of modeled gene families with functional annotations ($N = 8,962$ for GO Biological Process; $N = 9,070$ for GO Molecular Function; $N = 11,807$ for GO Cellular Component; and $N = 3,240$ for KEGG). Each ontology and annotation system was tested against its own annotated background, so the corresponding universe size differed among GO categories and KEGG. Over-representation analyses (ORA) were conducted via one-tailed Fisher's exact tests across specific foreground subsets. Directional foregrounds were formed by intersecting the 188 Tier A consensus families with families showing a positive or negative Viterbi-inferred change on the focal branch: inferred ancestral expansions at Node 12 (Core Velloziaceae), terminal expansions and contractions in desiccation-tolerant species (*V. tubiflora* and *X. viscosa*), and terminal changes in the avoidant species (*V. peripherica*). Because branch-specific probabilities were not subjected to an independent genome-wide multiple testing correction, these subsets represent families with significant overall turnover whose reconstructed history indicates a specific directional change, rather than statistically verified branch-level expansions. Multiple testing across functional terms was controlled using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure (Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995). Confirmatory significance required a global FDR $q \le 0.05$ and a minimum foreground size of $k_{\text{fg}} \ge 3$ families per term (`Reportable == TRUE`). To evaluate whether desiccation-tolerant lineages diverged in functional term representation, pairwise differences in expansion proportions between *V. tubiflora* and *X. viscosa* were assessed using two-tailed Fisher's exact tests with genome-wide Benjamini–Hochberg correction ($\text{FDR}_{\text{difference}} \le 0.05$).

### 1.7 Synteny analysis, duplication-mode classification, and methodological integration

To evaluate the genomic duplication modes associated with gene-family expansions and differentiate ancestral whole-genome duplications from localized structural proliferation, we conducted inter- and intra-genomic synteny analyses for *Vellozia peripherica* and *Vellozia tubiflora*. Synteny identification and duplication-mode classification were conducted through an integrated, two-pronged workflow combining JCVI MCScan (Tang et al., 2008, 2015) and MCScanX (`duplicate_gene_classifier`; Wang et al., 2012). While JCVI excels at identifying chromosome-scale syntenic blocks, evaluating inter-genomic orthology, and rendering macro-synteny visualizations, it intentionally screens out closely spaced local duplicates to prevent clustering artifacts during anchor chaining. Conversely, MCScanX implements an established five-class duplication ontology (WGD/segmental, tandem, proximal, dispersed, singleton) that depends fundamentally on the preservation of local sequence alignments. To leverage the complementary strengths of both toolkits while avoiding heuristic interference, we designed a pipeline that isolates their operational scopes and cross-validates their results.

Gene annotations (GFF3) were converted to standardized BED format containing all annotated mRNA loci (26,466 genes for *V. peripherica*; 25,551 genes for *V. tubiflora*). Inter-genomic synteny between *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* was evaluated using JCVI MCScan (Tang et al., 2008, 2015), employing LAST for all-against-all protein sequence alignment, a minimum alignment coverage threshold (`cscore >= 0.70`), depth-ratio screening, and a chaining distance of 20 genes.

Gene duplication origins across both genomes were classified using MCScanX (`duplicate_gene_classifier`; Wang et al., 2012). Importantly, our consistency audit revealed that feeding the JCVI-filtered alignment (`.inverse.filtered`) into MCScanX compromised local duplicate identification because JCVI's internal pipeline removes closely spaced homologous hits (`tandem_Nmax = 10`) to optimize macro-synteny chaining. To eliminate this heuristic bias, we supplied MCScanX with the complete, unfiltered intra-genomic homology matrix (`.inverse`), preserving all local tandem and proximal alignments at $E \le 10^{-5}$.

Classification parameters followed established benchmarks: match bonus (`-k 50`), gap penalty (`-g -1`), minimum collinear block size of 5 genes (`-s 5`), alignment significance threshold $E \le 10^{-5}$ (`-e 1e-5`), BLAST match collapse window of 5 genes (`-w 5`), maximum intervening gap of 25 genes (`-m 25`), and a proximal distance limit of $\le 10$ intervening genes (`-n 10`). Every annotated gene was partitioned into one of five mutually exclusive classes:
1. **WGD/Segmental:** Genes located within collinear blocks containing $\ge 5$ anchor pairs and $\le 25$ gene gaps.
2. **Tandem:** Non-collinear duplicates occurring adjacently or separated by $\le 1$ non-homologous gene on the same pseudomolecule.
3. **Proximal:** Non-collinear duplicates located on the same pseudomolecule separated by 2 to 10 intervening genes.
4. **Dispersed:** Duplicates located on different chromosomes or separated by $> 10$ genes without collinear synteny.
5. **Singleton:** Single-copy genes lacking detectable intra-genomic paralogs at $E \le 10^{-5}$.

To confirm algorithmic robustness, all intra-genomic WGD/segmental anchors identified by MCScanX were cross-referenced against collinear blocks identified independently by JCVI's dynamic programming chainer. Furthermore, individual gene classifications were cross-referenced with OrthoFinder orthogroups (`Orthogroups.tsv`) and the CAFE5 consensus family expansions. Across all 14,711 orthogroups containing *Vellozia* genes, we generated an integrated comparative matrix (`vellozia_orthogroup_duplication_comparative_table.tsv`) detailing copy number, dominant duplication mode, pseudomolecule distribution, and comparative mechanisms (shared segmental retention, lineage-specific tandem/proximal proliferation, or dispersed duplication) in each species.

## 2. Results

### 2.1 Phylogenomic backbone resolution and topological congruence

The partitioned maximum-likelihood phylogenomic analysis of the 732,066 amino acid supermatrix across 1,513 single-copy orthologous loci inferred an authoritative, fully resolved species tree for the Pan-Velloziaceae radiation ($\ln L = -6,024,349.37$) with maximal statistical bootstrap support (100% Ultrafast Bootstrap, UFBoot = 100) across every internal node (Figure 1A):
```
(Ath:0.7237, (Dala:0.2859, (Ptec:0.1913, (Abra:0.2628, (Xvis:0.0874, (Vper:0.0245, Vtub:0.0221):0.0624):0.1475):0.1251):0.0760));
```
Topological convergence was absolute, with an extended consensus tree Robinson–Foulds distance of zero ($\text{RF} = 0$) relative to the optimal maximum-likelihood topology.

Because concatenated phylogenomic supermatrices of massive length can artificially compress bootstrap variance, we complemented bootstrap proportions with Gene Concordance Factors (gCF) and Site Concordance Factors (sCF) across all 1,513 gene trees, alongside multi-species coalescent species-tree inference in ASTRAL-III (Figure 1A, B):
1. **Intrageneric *Vellozia* Speciation (*V. peripherica* + *V. tubiflora*):** Supported by **100% UFBoot**, a Gene Concordance Factor of **gCF = 93.92%** (1,421 of the 1,513 gene trees explicitly recovered this sister pair), a Site Concordance Factor of **sCF = 86.44%**, and an ASTRAL local posterior probability of **LPP = 1.00** (coalescent branch length = 2.43 coalescent units). Minor gene-tree discordance was partitioned among alternative NNI-1 (gDF1 = 2.18%, 33 trees), NNI-2 (gDF2 = 3.37%, 51 trees), and polyphyletic topologies (gDFP = 0.53%, 8 trees; Figure 1B).
2. **Core Velloziaceae (*Xerophyta viscosa* + *Vellozia*):** Supported by **100% UFBoot**, **gCF = 97.16%** (1,470 of 1,513 gene trees concordant), **sCF = 79.12%**, and ASTRAL **LPP = 1.00** (coalescent branch length = 3.32 coalescent units), demonstrating overwhelming genomic consensus across the plant genome for the monophyly of the African + South American clade (Figure 1A, B).
3. **Crown Velloziaceae (*Acanthochlamys bracteata* sister to Core Velloziaceae):** Supported by **100% UFBoot**, **gCF = 88.04%** (1,332 of 1,513 gene trees concordant), **sCF = 55.43%**, and ASTRAL **LPP = 1.00**, confirming the position of the monotypic East Asian subfamily Acanthochlamydoideae as the sister group to all remaining Velloziaceae.
4. **Crown Pandanales (*Pandanus tectorius* sister to Velloziaceae):** Supported by **100% UFBoot**, **gCF = 62.46%** (945 of 1,513 gene trees concordant), **sCF = 42.59%**, and ASTRAL **LPP = 1.00** (coalescent branch length = 2.08 coalescent units). Across-gene discordance at this deep Cretaceous internode was primarily driven by alternative resolution 2 (gDF2 = 26.24%, 397 trees), consistent with rapid diversification near the Cretaceous monocot radiation.
5. **Coalescent Consistency:** Species-tree inference under the multi-species coalescent in ASTRAL-III yielded an identical branching topology to the partitioned supermatrix ML tree with maximum local posterior probabilities (**LPP = 1.00**) at all internal nodes and a normalized quartet score of **0.9236** (92.36% of all induced gene-tree quartets satisfied; Figure 1A).

### 2.2 Bayesian divergence times, chronogram estimation, and MCMC convergence

The two independent long MCMC chains demonstrated virtually complete convergence across all evolutionary parameters and node ages (Table 1; Supplementary Figure S3). Posterior mean age estimates between the two independent runs were perfectly correlated ($r^2 = 1.0000$; Supplementary Figure S3A), with all Gelman–Rubin potential scale reduction factors satisfying $\hat{R} \le 1.006$ ($\hat{R} = 1.000$ for nodes $t_{\text{n8}}$, $t_{\text{n10}}$, $\mu$, and $\sigma^2$; $\hat{R} = 1.001$ for $t_{\text{n9}}$; $\hat{R} = 1.002$ for $t_{\text{n11}}$; and $\hat{R} = 1.006$ for $t_{\text{n12}}$ and $t_{\text{n13}}$). Trace plots across 30,000 post-burnin sampled generations confirmed thorough mixing and stationarity for the unconstrained node $t_{\text{n12}}$ (*Xerophyta* vs. *Vellozia*), with the two chains exploring overlapping posterior distributions around the combined mean of 33.77 Ma (Supplementary Figure S3B). Combined effective sample sizes (ESS) across both chains were exceptionally high, reaching 3,445 for the root, 2,866 for crown monocots, 1,729 for crown Pandanales, 1,471 for crown Velloziaceae, 1,870 for mean rate $\mu$, 1,987 for rate drift $\sigma^2$, and exceeding 260 for the focal unconstrained node $t_{\text{n12}}$ (Table 1).

Deep-time node age estimates established an ancient Late Cretaceous origin for the family Velloziaceae while constraining generic and species radiations to the mid-to-late Cenozoic (Table 1; Figure 1D). MCMCtree estimated the root divergence between eudicots (*Arabidopsis thaliana*) and monocots (Node $t_{\text{n8}}$) at 138.43 Ma (95% Highest Posterior Density [HPD]: 130.19–145.11 Ma; Early Cretaceous, Valanginian–Hauterivian), the crown age of monocots (Node $t_{\text{n9}}$, Dioscoreales vs. Pandanales) at 122.68 Ma (95% HPD: 115.48–128.07 Ma; Aptian), and the crown age of Pandanales (Node $t_{\text{n10}}$, Pandanaceae vs. Velloziaceae) at 100.39 Ma (95% HPD: 92.69–105.16 Ma; Cenomanian). The crown diversification of Velloziaceae—representing the split between the monotypic East Asian lineage *Acanthochlamys bracteata* and the Core Velloziaceae clade (Node $t_{\text{n11}}$)—was dated to 78.02 Ma (95% HPD: 74.43–85.45 Ma; Late Cretaceous, Campanian; Table 1).

Crucially, the Bayesian divergence time estimated for the unconstrained Core Velloziaceae node (Node $t_{\text{n12}}$), which separates the African genus *Xerophyta* from the South American genus *Vellozia*, converged to **33.77 Ma (95% HPD: 22.41–48.11 Ma; Early Oligocene, Rupelian; Table 1; Figure 1D; Supplementary Figure S3C)**. While the joint prior distribution for this node was broad and diffuse (mean 47.35 Ma; 95% prior interval: 12.14–83.77 Ma), empirical sequence data strongly compressed the posterior into the Oligocene. Strict Mesozoic Gondwanan vicariance posits that the trans-Atlantic disjunction in Velloziaceae resulted directly from continental break-up during the mid-Cretaceous (~105–100 Ma, with final marine separation completed by ~80 Ma; Mello-Silva et al., 2011; Alcantara et al., 2018). Our posterior estimate of 33.77 Ma post-dates the tectonic disconnection between Africa and South America by more than 46 million years, with even the upper 95% HPD credibility boundary (48.11 Ma, Middle Eocene) falling tens of millions of years after continental rifting. This temporal discordance provides decisive empirical evidence that the trans-Atlantic disjunction in Core Velloziaceae did not arise from tectonic vicariance, but rather occurred via post-breakup trans-oceanic long-distance dispersal and/or island-hopping during the mid-Cenozoic, coinciding with global cooling, aridification, and the expansion of open biomes (Mello-Silva et al., 2011).

Within South America, intrageneric speciation between the desiccation-tolerant species *Vellozia tubiflora* and the avoidant species *Vellozia peripherica* (Node $t_{\text{n13}}$) converged to **9.68 Ma (95% HPD: 5.85–15.72 Ma; Late Miocene, Tortonian; Table 1; Figure 1D; Supplementary Figure S3D)**. This Neogene divergence coincides with late Miocene climatic drying and the accelerated uplift and geomorphological dissection of the Espinhaço Range, which fostered intense speciation and ecological specialization in the Brazilian *campos rupestres* (Alcantara et al., 2018). The estimated mean substitution rate across the tree was $\mu = 0.260$ substitutions per site per 100 Myr ($2.60 \times 10^{-9}$ substitutions site$^{-1}$ year$^{-1}$; 95% HPD: 0.21–0.33), and the rate drift variance was $\sigma^2 = 0.120$ (95% HPD: 0.05–0.27), confirming low-to-moderate rate variation among lineages. The resulting ultrametric chronogram (`velloziaceae_chronogram_high_ess.tre`), featuring a root depth of 138.43 Ma, was utilized as the calibrated species tree for all subsequent CAFE5 evolutionary analyses.

### 2.3 Count error and homogeneous turnover

The homogeneous model without error correction converged across five runs at $\lambda=0.00238449\pm0.00000001$ gene$^{-1}$ Ma$^{-1}$ (mean $\pm$ SD), with $-\ln L$ ranging from 111,816.750 to 111,816.766. Incorporating the count-error model reduced the estimated turnover rate by approximately 2.2%, to $\lambda=0.00233138\pm0.00000029$, and reduced $-\ln L$ by approximately 113.5 units. The five error-corrected runs estimated $\epsilon$ between 0.006065 and 0.006113. These results support retaining the error model, while the magnitude of $\epsilon$ indicates that most observed counts were assigned high probability of being correct. The error model does not identify whether the residual discrepancies arose from assembly, annotation or orthogroup delineation.

### 2.4 Gamma-model convergence and choice of the primary configuration

Allowing rates to vary among families substantially reduced $-\ln L$ relative to the homogeneous models (Table 2; Figure 2A). All five $k=2$ runs converged tightly at $\lambda=0.00363663\pm0.00000040$, $\alpha=0.89715\pm0.00008$ and $-\ln L=107,721.360\pm0.007$. Four of the five $k=3$ runs converged at $\lambda=0.00328059\pm0.00000089$, $\alpha=1.02203\pm0.00040$ and $-\ln L=107,191.065\pm0.004$. In contrast, `k=3` replicate 02 converged to $\lambda=0.000402$ and $-\ln L=130,849.881$ and produced one family with an optimization failure rate above 20%. Its separation from the other four runs identifies it as a local optimum rather than evidence of biological rate variation (Figure 2A,B).

The single $k=4$ sensitivity run achieved the lowest $-\ln L$ (106,873.369) and reported no rejected parameter attempts. Nevertheless, its fastest reconstructed category reached approximately 99.6% of $\lambda_{\max}$, compared with 85.0% for $k=2$ and 91.1% for the valid $k=3$ runs (Figure 2D). Together with the absence of replicate runs, this proximity to the numerical ceiling precluded using $k=4$ for the primary biological interpretation. Although the valid $k=3$ runs achieved lower negative log-likelihood values, we did not interpret the additional category as biological evidence for a third turnover regime because the number of categories is a discretization choice and the $k=3$ model showed less consistent convergence across independent runs. The $k=2$ model was therefore retained as a conservative, reproducible representation of among-family rate heterogeneity, and we used $k=3$ to assess sensitivity to a finer representation of among-family rate variation.

### 2.5 Family-level significance and cross-model stability

In the representative `k=2` replicate 02, 508 families had nominal $P\leq0.05$, of which 203 passed the genome-wide Benjamini–Hochberg correction. In the representative `k=3` replicate 03, the corresponding numbers were 587 and 289. Despite near-identical parameter estimates, the number of FDR-significant families varied among replicated runs, from 196 to 259 for $k=2$ and from 247 to 291 among the four valid $k=3$ runs (Figure 2C). This variation was concentrated near the finite resolution of the CAFE5 probabilities rather than in the model parameters.

The intersection across all valid runs contained 188 families. Every family retained in all five $k=2$ runs was also retained in all four valid $k=3$ runs. A further 43 families were consistently significant under $k=3$ but did not pass the more stringent cross-replicate requirement under $k=2$. We treated the 188-family intersection as the most stable statistical subset and the 43 additional families as model-sensitive candidates. This designation indicates repeatability of family-level evidence under the tested configurations; it does not imply that all 188 families evolved in response to desiccation or that every reconstructed branch change was independently significant after correction. The exact family lists defining the 188-family consensus, the 43 model-sensitive candidates, and all directional foregrounds are provided in Supplementary Tables S1–S3.

### 2.6 Functional over-representation and lineage-specific dynamics

The formal over-representation analysis revealed that significant gene-family turnover across the analyzed Velloziaceae lineages involved distinct, lineage-specific functional modules rather than a unified cytoprotective program. At the ancestral core of Velloziaceae (Node 12), Viterbi-inferred expansions were prominently enriched in biotic defense and pathogen recognition, including response to other organism (`GO:0051707`, $q = 6.22 \times 10^{-6}$, 20 families), defense response (`GO:0006952`, $q = 1.01 \times 10^{-4}$, 16 families), response to salicylic acid (`GO:0009751`, $q = 0.0060$, 6 families) and secondary metabolic processes (`GO:0019748`, $q = 0.0156$, 5 families). Ancestral Node 12 expansions also involved core energy-transducing pathways, specifically KEGG photosynthesis (`ath00195`, $q = 1.77 \times 10^{-6}$, 8 families) and oxidative phosphorylation (`ath00190`, $q = 2.36 \times 10^{-4}$, 8 families). These inferred ancestral gains indicate that foundational diversification in crown Velloziaceae was accompanied by the reinforcement of basal stress signaling and metabolic machinery prior to lineage-specific physiological divergence.

Terminal branches of the desiccation-tolerant species *V. tubiflora* and *X. viscosa* exhibited sharply decoupled evolutionary trajectories. In *V. tubiflora*, Viterbi-inferred terminal expansions were selectively enriched in energy-transduction pathways, including oxidative phosphorylation (`ath00190`, $q = 1.58 \times 10^{-6}$, 8 families) and photosynthesis (`ath00195`, $q = 3.14 \times 10^{-6}$, 6 families), whereas Viterbi-inferred terminal contractions were associated with families annotated to defense responses and specialized glycosyltransferase functions. Conversely, in *X. viscosa*, Viterbi-inferred terminal expansions were enriched in chromatin organization (`GO:0030527` structural constituent of chromatin, $q = 7.70 \times 10^{-4}$; `GO:0000786` nucleosome, $q = 0.0011$), organelle assembly and innate immunity (`GO:0045087`, $q = 0.0104$). Crucially, oxidative phosphorylation in *X. viscosa* underwent significant directional contraction (`ath00190`, $q = 3.81 \times 10^{-5}$, 8 families), which included Viterbi-inferred reductions in the reconstructed copy numbers of orthogroups provisionally annotated as NADH dehydrogenase-related (`OG0000317` and `OG0000332`). Direct statistical comparison of expanded term proportions between *V. tubiflora* and *X. viscosa* yielded zero terms surviving multiple-testing correction ($\text{FDR}_{\text{difference}} = 1.0$ across all 168 reportable terms). Supplementary Table S4 reports the foreground and background counts, nominal Fisher $p$-values, and Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted values for all 168 reportable terms. Under the present family-level filtering, enrichment background and multiple-testing thresholds, we detected no statistically supported parallel expansion of a shared functional term between *V. tubiflora* and *X. viscosa*. This result does not exclude convergence at the level of individual genes, regulatory networks, sequence evolution, expression, or physiological function.

The avoidant lineage *Vellozia peripherica* displayed a functional signature distinct from both desiccation-tolerant species. Viterbi-inferred terminal expansions in *V. peripherica* were significantly over-represented in abiotic stress responsiveness (`GO:0006950` response to stress, $q = 0.0187$, 24 families), harboring `OG0000306`, provisionally annotated as DREB-like based on homology to *A. thaliana* proteins, with six additional copies inferred on the *V. peripherica* terminal branch (Table 3). Other enriched terms included pollen recognition (`GO:0048544`, $q = 0.0187$) and specialized secondary metabolism driven by glucosyltransferases (`GO:0080043` quercetin 3-O-glucosyltransferase activity, $q = 7.45 \times 10^{-5}$; `GO:0008194` UDP-glycosyltransferase activity, $q = 0.0153$, harboring `OG0000281`). In marked contrast to *V. tubiflora*, *V. peripherica* showed a pronounced Viterbi-inferred reduction in families assigned to energy-transducing pathways, with oxidative phosphorylation (`ath00190`, $q = 4.55 \times 10^{-11}$, 13 families) and photosynthesis (`ath00195`, $q = 3.81 \times 10^{-9}$, 9 families) exhibiting extensive copy-number reduction, including `OG0000301`, `OG0000317` and `OG0000332`.

Other highlighted consensus families reflected changes outside the focal desiccation axis, such as the acyl-CoA-binding family `OG0000345` and terpene-synthase family `OG0000321`, whose largest inferred expansions occurred in outgroup lineages (Table 3).

### 2.7 Comparative synteny and structural duplication landscape between *Vellozia peripherica* and *Vellozia tubiflora*

To determine whether the copy-number expansions inferred by CAFE5 reflected broad polyploidization or localized structural dynamics, we evaluated the duplication landscape across both sequenced genomes using audited intra-genomic homology matrices and inter-genomic synteny blocks (Table 4; Figure 3).

Both *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* exhibited a virtually identical proportion of genes anchored in collinear syntenic blocks: **28.18% (7,459 genes in 3,605 orthogroups)** in *V. peripherica* and **28.27% (7,224 genes in 3,694 orthogroups)** in *V. tubiflora* (Table 4). This close agreement (~28.2% in both taxa) indicates that the broad genomic footprint of collinear duplication is conserved across the genus. Furthermore, inter-genomic synteny between the two species revealed extensive chromosome-scale collinearity (22,950 anchor pairs across 376 syntenic blocks; Supplementary Figures S1 and S2), with 88.7% and 85.7% of the intra-genomic WGD/segmental gene models in *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*, respectively, directly validated by JCVI collinear anchor chains. These patterns are consistent with a shared ancestral contribution of segmental/WGD-like duplications across Velloziaceae, rather than an active lineage-specific polyploidization event.

This net surplus of 1,547 locally duplicated genes in *V. peripherica* (+37.9% relative increase) indicates that local tandem and proximal duplication mechanisms were the predominant structural correlates of the lineage-enriched expansions examined here. Concurrently, *V. tubiflora* maintained a higher singleton fraction (20.30% vs. 15.97%), indicating that duplicate retention was generally higher in *V. peripherica* (84.03% vs. 79.70% duplicated).

Analysis of the formal comparative duplication table across 14,711 orthogroups (`vellozia_orthogroup_duplication_comparative_table.tsv`; Supplementary Table S5) revealed that prioritized CAFE5 expansions were strongly coupled to this local duplication bias. For the prioritized candidates, the inferred *V. peripherica* increases are accompanied by physical clusters of tandem and/or proximal duplicates on anchored pseudomolecules, although the relative contribution of each duplication mechanism differs among orthogroups. Beyond the previously highlighted DREB/CBF (`OG0000306`, Tier A) and UDP-glycosyltransferase (`OG0000281`, Tier A) families, several orthogroups showed *Vper*-enriched copy numbers accompanied by local tandem and/or proximal duplication signatures. The clearest prioritized examples were an OPR-like family (`OG0000126`, Tier A), an nsLTP-like family (`OG0001656`, Tier B rate-heterogeneity candidate), and a 2-oxoglutarate/Fe(II)-dependent oxygenase-like family (`OG0000124`, Tier A), alongside a coordinated plastidial NDH module (`OG0000014`, `OG0000156`, `OG0000278`, all Tier A):

1. **Abiotic stress transcription factors (DREB/CBF; `OG0000306`):**  
   The DREB/CBF-associated family `OG0000306` represents an anchored local cluster in *V. peripherica*. Nine of its 11 copies (*Vper_0019441* to *Vper_0019449*) are concentrated within a compact ~90.6 kb locus on chromosome 3A (`vper_chr3A: 58,653,745–58,744,302 bp`), consisting of 1 WGD anchor, 2 tandem, and 6 proximal duplicates. The remaining two copies occur on chromosome 4A (1 WGD anchor) and chromosome 7A (1 dispersed copy), yielding a genome-wide breakdown of 2 tandem, 6 proximal, 2 WGD, and 1 dispersed copy across all 11 genes. In contrast, *V. tubiflora* possesses only 3 copies distributed across three distinct chromosomes (`vtub_chr3A`, `chr4A`, and `chr7A`), of which 2 represent ancestral WGD anchors collinear with *V. peripherica* and 1 is a tandem duplicate. This physical organization is consistent with the Viterbi-inferred expansion on the *V. peripherica* terminal branch (+6 copies; Table 3) being associated with localized tandem and proximal proliferation at the chromosome 3A locus rather than ancestral retention.

2. **Specialized secondary metabolism (UDP-glycosyltransferase; `OG0000281`):**  
   In *V. peripherica*, `OG0000281` contains 14 genes distributed across two anchored local arrays on chromosome 1A (9 genes; 5.40–5.55 Mb) and chromosome 4A (4 genes; 7.21–7.24 Mb), alongside one dispersed copy, comprising 6 tandem, 3 proximal, 4 WGD/segmental, and 1 dispersed copy across the complete family. Conversely, *V. tubiflora* harbors only 4 copies (2 tandem, 2 WGD on chromosomes 1A and 4A). This pattern is consistent with localized duplication contributing substantially to the observed expansion of a UDP-glycosyltransferase-associated family potentially involved in specialized metabolism.

3. **Oxylipin-associated pathway candidate (OPR-like; `OG0000126`):**  
   The 12-oxophytodienoate reductase-like family `OG0000126` exhibits a lineage-enriched expansion in *V. peripherica* (19 observed genes vs. 9 in *V. tubiflora*; CAFE5 terminal branch increase: +8.0). Rather than representing a *de novo* locus, this expansion reflects homologous local arrays retained in both lineages but differentially amplified: *V. peripherica* maintains 10 genes in a ~108 kb array on chromosome 4A (9 tandem, 1 proximal; 58.04–58.15 Mb) and 7 contiguous tandem genes in a ~24 kb array on chromosome 8A (20.64–20.66 Mb); the remaining two copies are dispersed outside these two local arrays (one on chr3A and one on chr7A). In *V. tubiflora*, homologous arrays occur at collinear chromosomal positions but contain fewer copies (6 genes on chr4A and 3 on chr8A). While structurally compelling, the functional link of this expansion to oxylipin signaling and drought response remains a candidate hypothesis requiring domain validation and expression profiling.

4. **Surface-lipid metabolism candidate (nsLTP-like; `OG0001656`):**  
   The non-specific lipid-transfer protein-like family `OG0001656` displays a lineage-enriched expansion in *V. peripherica* (8 observed genes vs. 2 in *V. tubiflora*; CAFE5 terminal branch increase: +5.0). Similar to `OG0000126`, this represents differential amplification of an array present in both lineages rather than a *de novo* innovation: *V. peripherica* maintains an expanded local cluster on chromosome 1A (7 tandem, 1 proximal), whereas *V. tubiflora* retains a 2-gene tandem array at the homologous chromosome 1A locus. Although nsLTPs are plausible candidates for cuticular wax deposition and surface protection, their functional contribution remains hypothetical.

5. **2-Oxoglutarate/Fe(II)-dependent oxygenase-like family (`OG0000124`):**  
   In `OG0000124`, *V. peripherica* harbors 7 genes (all anchored on chromosome 2A: 4 tandem, 1 proximal, 2 WGD; CAFE5 terminal increase: +4.0), reflecting the local proliferation of an ancestral locus shared with *V. tubiflora*, which possesses 2 copies at the syntenic chromosome 2A position (1 proximal, 1 WGD). While 2-ODD enzymes participate in diverse oxidative steps, including flavonoid and gibberellin pathways, specific biochemical assignment requires enzymatic and phylogenetic characterization.

6. **Coordinated plastidial NDH-associated candidate module (`OG0000014`, `OG0000156`, `OG0000278`):**  
   Three orthogroups provisionally associated with plastidial NDH-related functions display simultaneous copy-number enrichment and local duplication in *V. peripherica*: `OG0000014` (67 genes in *Vper* vs. 27 in *Vtub*; +31.0 change; with 57 copies concentrated on four major pseudomolecules—chr2A [20 genes], chr4A [17 genes], chr3A [11 genes], and chr6A [9 genes]—and 10 copies distributed across minor loci on chr1A [5], chr5A [2], chr7A [2], and chr8A [1]), `OG0000156` (18 genes in *Vper* vs. 7 in *Vtub*; +8.0 change; predominantly clustered on chr1A [16 genes, including 14 tandem], with single copies on chr2A and chr4A), and `OG0000278` (6 genes in *Vper* vs. 2 in *Vtub*; +3.0 change; all 6 copies located in a contiguous tandem array on chr7A). This coordinated pattern identifies a candidate plastidial electron-transport module; however, functional coupling, stoichiometric balance, and subcellular targeting remain to be tested, and this pattern should not be construed as demonstrated physiological enhancement of cyclic electron flow.

These loci represent strong candidates for lineage-specific genomic diversification in metabolic, surface-protective, and plastid-associated functions. However, their contribution to drought endurance, cuticular maintenance, or photoprotection remains hypothetical until supported by gene-model validation, domain completeness, expression profiling, and functional assays.

7. **Methodological caveats on unplaced contigs and excluded candidates (`OG0000301`, `OG0000317`, `OG0000332`, `OG0000164`):**  
   In contrast to the chromosome-anchored arrays described above, apparent lineage-specific changes in several families were found to be artifacts of assembly contiguity. For instance, apparent *V. tubiflora* expansions in NADH-dehydrogenase-related families (`OG0000317`, 28 genes; `OG0000332`, 25 genes) were distributed as singletons across dozens of unplaced contigs (`ctg230` to `ctg415`). Similarly, an apparent contraction in xyloglucan endotransglycosylase/hydrolase (`OG0000164`; 5 genes in *Vper* vs. 20 in *Vtub*) was excluded from biological interpretation because all copies in both species reside on short, unplaced contigs (`ctg112`–`ctg115` in *Vper*; 20 separate contigs in *Vtub*). Documenting these excluded cases underscores the necessity of chromosomal synteny verification to prevent assembly fragmentation from being mistaken for adaptive copy-number turnover.

## 3. Interpretation and limitations

The Bayesian molecular clock dating establishes a robust, high-ESS temporal framework for interpreting macroevolution and genomic diversification across Velloziaceae. Crucially, estimating the divergence between African *Xerophyta* and South American *Vellozia* at 33.77 Ma (95% HPD: 22.41–48.11 Ma) under an unconstrained node configuration definitively refutes strict Mesozoic Gondwanan vicariance. Continental rifting severed terrestrial connectivity between South America and Africa between 105 and 100 Ma, with deep-water marine barriers fully established by the Campanian (~80 Ma; Mello-Silva et al., 2011; Alcantara et al., 2018). Because the posterior age distribution for Core Velloziaceae post-dates continental separation by tens of millions of years, the trans-Atlantic disjunction was necessarily mediated by post-breakup trans-oceanic long-distance dispersal and/or stepping-stone colonization across proto-Atlantic islands during the mid-Cenozoic (Oligocene). This mid-Tertiary establishment in South America coincided with global cooling and regional aridification, setting the stage for the explosive radiation of *Vellozia* in open, nutrient-impoverished *campos rupestres* environments during the late Miocene (9.68 Ma [5.85–15.72 Ma]). Furthermore, supplying this time-calibrated ultrametric tree to CAFE5 ensures that birth–death turnover parameters ($\lambda$) are modeled relative to physical elapsed time (gene$^{-1}$ Ma$^{-1}$) rather than substitution rate proxies, preventing lineage-specific rate variation from biasing inferred rates of gene gain and loss (Mendes et al., 2020).

The replicated analyses support substantial among-family heterogeneity in the rate of copy-number evolution. The fully convergent $k=2$ model provides a conservative primary representation of this heterogeneity, whereas the valid $k=3$ runs recover the same core signal and a broader set of candidate families. The close agreement in model parameters and the complete inclusion of the 188-family $k=2$ consensus within the valid $k=3$ consensus indicate that the strongest family-level results are not an artifact of a single Gamma discretization. The additional $k=3$ families should nevertheless remain a sensitivity set until their support is evaluated against annotation quality and alternative orthogroup definitions.

The results do not establish that rapid copy-number evolution was adaptive or caused differences in desiccation response. Vegetative desiccation tolerance involves coordinated regulation of seed-associated protective programs, photosynthetic shutdown and recovery, redox control and cellular stabilization, and these mechanisms may evolve through regulatory rewiring as well as gene duplication (Costa et al., 2017; VanBuren et al., 2018). Accordingly, gene-family expansion is only one layer of evidence. A defensible connection to phenotype requires that a candidate show a relevant branch-specific history, intact and correctly annotated copies, structural validation, and functional evidence from transcript, protein or metabolite profiles during dehydration and rehydration.

Formal over-representation analysis across the modeled background families indicated that significant gene-family turnover in Velloziaceae does not align with a uniform cytoprotective battery. The absence of statistically significant differences between *V. tubiflora* and *X. viscosa* in direct contrast testing, coupled with the opposing inferred trajectories of families assigned to photosynthetic and respiratory pathways (expansion in *V. tubiflora* versus contraction in *X. viscosa* and *V. peripherica*), indicates that desiccation tolerance did not evolve through parallel expansion of identical functional modules. Instead, gene-family turnover appears shaped by lineage-specific specialization and ancestral defense adaptations.

Integrating whole-genome synteny and duplication-mode classification resolved several crucial structural questions that statistical turnover models alone cannot address. First, the observation that both *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* maintain a virtually identical proportion of collinear duplicates (~28.2%) indicates that the near-identical fraction of collinear duplicates does not support a major difference in the broad WGD/segmental duplication footprint between the two species. Instead, this uniform signal is consistent with a shared ancestral contribution of segmental/WGD-like duplications across Velloziaceae. Second, the structural dissection revealed that the primary evolutionary divergence between *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* lies in localized duplication mechanisms: *V. peripherica* exhibits a substantial enrichment in tandem and proximal duplicates (21.25% vs. 15.96%), providing a directly observed genomic architecture compatible with the candidate amplifications in abiotic stress-responsive transcription factors (`OG0000306`), specialized glycosyltransferases (`OG0000281`), oxylipin-associated OPR enzymes (`OG0000126`), surface-lipid nsLTPs (`OG0001656`), and plastidial redox components. Third, structural mapping exposed potential assembly and annotation artifacts in families such as `OG0000317`, `OG0000332`, and `OG0000164`, where apparent copy-number expansions or contractions correspond to single copies fragmented across unplaced contigs rather than coherent chromosomal loci.

Several technical limitations remain. CAFE5 treats observed family sizes as outcomes of a birth–death process conditional on the supplied time tree and error model, but it does not validate orthogroup composition or gene function. Most crucially, statistical significance in this analysis was established at the family level. Therefore, while a family may have undergone significant turnover across the tree, the localization of expansions and contractions to specific terminal lineages (e.g., *V. peripherica* or *V. tubiflora*) relies on Viterbi reconstructions that were not subjected to branch-specific multiple testing correction. This distinction must be maintained when interpreting inferred lineage trajectories. Furthermore, while structural duplication analysis characterizes the physical localization and spatial architecture of copy-number expansions, establishing their adaptive significance requires temporal expression profiling during dehydration and rehydration, as duplicate retention may also be governed by neutral subfunctionalization, dosage effects, or structural fragility.
