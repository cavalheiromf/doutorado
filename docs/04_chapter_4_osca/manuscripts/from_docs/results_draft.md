## 3. RESULTS

### 3.1 Genome-wide Identification of the OSCA Gene Family in *Vellozia*
To establish a comprehensive catalog of the Hyperosmolality-Gated Calcium-Permeable Channel (OSCA) gene family in *Vellozia*, we performed a genome-wide survey using the chromosome-level assemblies of the evergreen *V. peripherica* (Vper) and the resurrection species *V. tubiflora* (Vtub). Utilizing conserved Pfam domain profiles (PF02714, PF13967, and PF14703) and structural validation via InterProScan, we identified a total of 27 non-redundant OSCA genes across the two species (Table 1).

Specifically, *V. peripherica* harbors 14 OSCA genes, whereas *V. tubiflora* possesses 13 OSCA genes. The identified genes were successfully mapped across the nine chromosomes of both genomes, revealing a widely conserved spatial distribution. This numerical stability demonstrates that the extreme desiccation-tolerance strategy of *V. tubiflora* did not result in a reduction or massive expansion of its primary osmosensory machinery, maintaining a virtually identical complement of calcium-permeable channels compared to the evergreen relative.

### 3.2 Phylogenetic Reconstruction and Subfamily Classification
To resolve the evolutionary relationships of the *Vellozia* OSCA genes and classify them within established angiosperm subfamilies, we performed a Maximum Likelihood (ML) phylogenetic reconstruction integrating OSCA proteins from *V. peripherica*, *V. tubiflora*, *Arabidopsis thaliana*, *Oryza sativa*, *Sorghum bicolor*, and *Zea mays* (Figure 1).

The phylogenetic tree confirmed the robust classification of all identified *Vellozia* OSCA proteins into four established monophyletic clades:
*   **OSCA1 Subfamily**: 5 genes in Vper and 5 genes in Vtub.
*   **OSCA2 Subfamily**: The most diverse and expanded clade, harboring 6 genes in Vper (including two copies of OSCA2.4) and 5 genes in Vtub.
*   **OSCA3 Subfamily**: 2 genes in Vper and 2 genes in Vtub.
*   **OSCA4 Subfamily**: 1 gene in Vper and 1 gene in Vtub.

Orthology relationships between the two species were strictly preserved (1:1 orthologous pairs) across almost all loci. A notable lineage-specific exception occurred in the OSCA2 clade: *V. peripherica* possesses a tandem duplication resulting in two adjacent paralogs (`VpOSCA2.4a` and `VpOSCA2.4b`), whereas *V. tubiflora* retains only a single ancestral copy (`VtOSCA2.4`). This localized expansion in the evergreen species suggests an active structural remodeling of Clade 2 channels, potentially to support continuous daily osmotic monitoring.

### 3.3 Comparative Synteny and Chromosomal Collinearity
Global comparative profiling revealed an extraordinary level of macro-collinearity between the two genomes, with 297 syntenic blocks successfully mapped. No major inter-chromosomal translocations or large-scale rearrangements were detected, confirming that the spatial architecture of their genomes has remained remarkably stable since their evolutionary divergence.

At the microsynteny level, we identified a perfectly conserved physical cluster on Chromosome 7A in both genomes. This cluster contains a physically contiguous tandem duplication of three genes (`OSCA1.3`, `OSCA1.4`, and `OSCA1.5`), displaying a physical gap of zero intermediate genes. The perfect preservation of this contiguous layout, flanked by 24 highly syntenic gene pairs, suggests that this tandem expansion occurred prior to speciation and is strictly maintained under structural or regulatory constraints.

Self-synteny analysis further revealed an ancestral segmental duplication linking Chromosome 1A and Chromosome 5A, simultaneously harboring duplicated members of the OSCA2 (OSCA2.1/2.2) and OSCA3 (OSCA3.1/3.2) subfamilies. This confirms that these clades were expanded through a shared ancient whole-genome duplication (WGD) event.

### 3.4 Evolutionary Dynamics and the "Dual-Track" Selection Model
The ratio of nonsynonymous to synonymous substitutions (Ka/Ks) revealed that the OSCA gene family in *Vellozia* is globally governed by robust purifying selection (Ka/Ks < 1.0, mean ~0.35). This indicates that the general structure of the mechanosensitive channels is maintained under severe functional constraint. For instance, the orthologous pair `VpOSCA2.3`/`VtOSCA2.3` exhibited the lowest ratio (Ka/Ks = 0.053), suggesting a highly specialized primary biological function. Conversely, the pair `VpOSCA1.4`/`VtOSCA1.4` exhibited a significantly elevated rate (0.80), pointing towards selective relaxation or accelerated functional divergence post-speciation.

To detect directional changes in natural selection intensity between the species, we implemented the HyPhy RELAX method, utilizing *V. peripherica* as the Reference and *V. tubiflora* as the Test lineage. The analysis revealed a highly heterogeneous evolutionary behavior, establishing a **"Dual-Track Evolution"** model for the OSCA family:
*   **The Conserved Track (OSCA1)**: The OSCA1 clade presented equivalent selection intensity in both species ($K = 0.92$, $p = 0.60$). As the primary basal osmosensor, this machinery remains non-malleable and is preserved under strong purifying selection regardless of the species' drought strategy (avoidance vs. tolerance).
*   **The Divergent Track (OSCA2 and OSCA3+4)**: The OSCA2 subfamily exhibited a massive, highly significant selective relaxation in *V. tubiflora* ($K = 0.28$, $p = 3.4 \times 10^{-5}$), with an equivalent signature in the OSCA3+4 clade ($K = 0.13$, $p = 0.006$). Ecophysiologically, during anhydrobiosis, the resurrection plant enters extreme desiccation and cellular dormancy. The prolonged absence of free water temporarily removes the selective pressure to maintain the mechanosensitive gating of these specific channels, allowing neutral mutations to accumulate via drift.

Site-specific resolution via HyPhy Contrast-FEL further elucidated this model. In the OSCA2 clade, 14 residues presented extreme adaptive acceleration exclusively in the evergreen *V. peripherica*, reflecting continuous evolutionary refinement to sustain its ultra-sensitive hydrostatic monitoring required for active drought avoidance. Conversely, in the OSCA3+4 clade, a single localized residue (position 593) exhibited massive adaptive acceleration in the resurrection species *V. tubiflora* ($\beta_{Test} = 24.72$, $p = 0.009$). Located in the transmembrane domain, this specific structural alteration is hypothesized to stabilize the OSCA protein during the desiccated state, functioning as a critical molecular trigger for rapid pore reactivation upon cellular rehydration.
