## Abstract

**Background:** OSCA/TMEM63 ion channels mediate primary osmotic and mechanical perception in plants. Their evolution in congeneric lineages with contrasting water-deficit adaptations remains unexplored. **Objective:** We tested whether OSCA channel repertoires diverged between desiccation-tolerant *Vellozia tubiflora* and evergreen *Vellozia peripherica*. **Methods:** Genomic comparisons evaluated gene duplications, macrosynteny, pairwise evolutionary rates, family-wide selection intensity, organ-level transcript abundance under the sampled field conditions, predicted functional networks, and promoter motif composition. We used HMMER/InterProScan searches, maximum-likelihood phylogenetics (IQ-TREE), syntenic collinearity (MCScan), and pairwise substitution rates (PAML YN00). We evaluated selection intensity across a full-family OSCA codon alignment (HyPhy RELAX), tissue RNA-seq profiles within each species (DESeq2), functional associations transferred by STRING v12, and upstream *cis*-element annotations (PlantCARE). **Results:** Fourteen loci were identified in *V. peripherica* and 13 in *V. tubiflora* across four canonical clades (OSCA1-4). Twenty-five of the 27 *Vellozia* proteins displayed the tripartite Pfam domain architecture, whereas *VpOSCA2.2* and *VtOSCA1.2* were short predicted models lacking PF13967. Macrosynteny revealed high collinearity, with two adjacent *OSCA2.4* loci in *V. peripherica* and one in *V. tubiflora*. HyPhy RELAX estimated a selection intensity parameter *K* = 0.7064 (*p* = 0.0003) for *V. tubiflora*-designated terminal branches relative to *V. peripherica*-designated terminal branches under the tested full-family codon model. Within each species, *OSCA1.2* transcripts were more abundant in roots and stems than in leaves. OSCA1.2 proteins also had high connectivity in STRING functional-association networks, including predicted links to carbohydrate-metabolism proteins. **Conclusion:** Broad conservation of the OSCA repertoire coexists with localized copy-number variation, a model-dependent difference in selection intensity, and organ-associated transcript patterns. These results establish a comparative baseline and identify candidates for testing whether OSCA divergence contributes to contrasting drought adaptations in *Vellozia*.

**Keywords:** Velloziaceae, mechanosensation, OSCA channels, desiccation tolerance, comparative synteny, purifying selection, tandem duplication, *campos rupestres*.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Water availability fluctuates across spatial and temporal scales (FENG et al., 2016). During dehydration, transpirational water loss diminishes cell volume and hydrostatic pressure, altering the physical interface between the plasma membrane and the cell wall while generating mechanical tension across the lipid bilayer within seconds of initial deficit (FRACHISSE et al., 2014; HASWELL; VERSLUES, 2015). These early mechanical perturbations precede downstream transcriptional cascades. At the cellular level, bilayer deformation triggers immediate biophysical responses, dominated by cytosolic calcium transients and ionic fluxes, that operate upstream of, and integrate with, broader physiological adaptations including phytohormone signaling, stomatal regulation, and osmotic adjustment (BASU; HASWELL, 2017; ZHU, 2016). Mechanosensitive ion channels form this primary sensory interface. In land plants, this primary sensory layer is mediated in part by mechanosensitive and osmosensitive ion channels that convert physical membrane tension into rapid ion conductances.

Hyperosmolality-gated calcium-permeable (OSCA) channels are principal plant mechanotransducers. In *Arabidopsis thaliana*, *OSCA1.1* mediates hyperosmolality-induced cytosolic calcium increases (YUAN et al., 2014), and broader electrophysiological surveys have established that multiple OSCA/TMEM63 family members operate as bona fide stretch-activated ion channels across eukaryotic lineages (MURTHY et al., 2018). Structural determinations of *Arabidopsis* *OSCA1.1* and *OSCA1.2* demonstrate that functional channels assemble as homodimers, with each eleven-transmembrane subunit coupling bilayer deformation to pore gating through interactions between cytosolic domains and lipid-interfacing elements (LIU; WANG; SUN, 2018; ZHANG et al., 2018). At the sequence level, this conserved architecture is defined by three canonical Pfam domain signatures (PF02714, PF13967, and PF14703), while phylogenetic surveys partition angiosperm repertoires into four ancient clades (OSCA1-4) with distinct activation thresholds, ion selectivities, and spatial expression profiles (YUAN et al., 2014; DING et al., 2019; YIN et al., 2021; KE et al., 2022).

Channel clades balance functional constraint with copy-number turnover (FLAGEL; WENDEL, 2009). Under this evolutionary framework, core mechanosensory channel orthologs mediating baseline cellular perception are expected to evolve under purifying selection, whereas gene duplication provides raw material for potential diversification through subfunctionalization, selective relaxation, or altered tissue allocation (LYNCH; FORCE, 2000; PANCHY; LEHTI-SHIU; SHIU, 2016). Retained paralogs may subsequently modulate channel density, tissue allocation, or responsiveness to environmental stimuli. Investigating how channel repertoires evolve across syntenic genomic contexts, selective regimes, and regulatory architectures provides an empirical framework to test whether extreme ecological divergence, such as vegetative desiccation tolerance, reshapes primary sensory systems or preserves deeply conserved mechanosensory constraints.

Neotropical Velloziaceae exemplify extreme divergence in drought survival. Species of Velloziaceae are prominent across the *campos rupestres*, nutrient-poor rocky outcrops of eastern Brazil where shallow soils, intense solar radiation, and a pronounced dry season impose recurrent water limitation (ALCANTARA et al., 2015). Across this harsh landscape, they have diversified into two primary dry-season strategies: dehydration avoidance, which limits water loss while maintaining physiological activity, and vegetative desiccation tolerance, which enables survival of extensive tissue dehydration and metabolic recovery after rehydration (ALCANTARA et al., 2015; DE FREITAS LAROCCA et al., 2022). Physiological comparisons further show that desiccation tolerance permits survival under extreme water loss but entails distinct costs and seasonal adjustments in carbon assimilation, growth, and metabolism (TEODORO et al., 2021; VIEIRA et al., 2025). Within *Vellozia*, *Vellozia tubiflora* is a desiccation-tolerant resurrection species, whereas *Vellozia peripherica* is an evergreen, non-desiccation-tolerant species that relies on dehydration avoidance. Previously established as a congeneric model of contrasting drought-adaptation strategies in seasonal microbiome analyses (PINTO et al., 2025), this species pair provides a well-defined comparative system to test whether mechanosensory channel repertoires vary with water-deficit strategy.

How does mechanosensory machinery evolve across contrasting drought strategies? On one hand, because both resurrection and evergreen plants must monitor cellular turgor and osmotic fluctuations during vegetative growth, baseline sensory demands could maintain the core OSCA repertoire under purifying selection across both lineages. On the other hand, vegetative desiccation tolerance imposes severe cellular shrinkage and profound mechanical strain on the protoplast during drying and rehydration, stresses that resurrection plants mitigate through diverse, species-specific cellular mechanisms such as wall folding, vacuolar fragmentation, or chemical vitrification (FARRANT et al., 2015; LYALL; GECHEV, 2020; VIEIRA et al., 2022). These distinct biomechanical demands could favor lineage-specific copy retention, shifts in selective constraint, or specialized transcriptional regulation in particular OSCA clades.

We evaluated these hypotheses across chromosome-scale assemblies. Specifically, we tested whether the divergence between dehydration avoidance and desiccation tolerance coincides with structural or regulatory differences in primary mechanosensory machinery by comparing the genome-wide OSCA gene-family repertoires of *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica*. Across both genomes, we assessed six complementary features: gene-copy history, macrosyntenic collinearity, family-wide and pairwise selective constraints, organ-level transcript abundance under native field conditions, predicted functional-association networks, and upstream *cis*-regulatory motif repertoires. By integrating these genomic and evolutionary layers, we characterized patterns of conservation and divergence in the OSCA family between resurrection and evergreen *Vellozia*.

## 2. MATERIAL AND METHODS

### 2.1 Genome resources, candidate identification, and sequence validation

Chromosome-scale haplotype-1 assemblies and predicted coding sequences (CDS) of *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* were translated into predicted proteomes using the standard eukaryotic genetic code in Biopython v1.83 (COCK et al., 2009), alongside the predicted proteome of the sister outgroup *Xerophyta schlechteri* (COSTA et al., 2017). OSCA candidates were identified across all three proteomes using HMMER v3.4 hmmsearch (EDDY, 2011) queried with the profile of the C-terminal pore-forming seven-transmembrane domain (RSN1_7TM, Pfam PF02714). Searches used default HMMER thresholds: $E \le 10$ for sequence and domain reporting, $E \le 0.01$ for sequence inclusion, and conditional $E \le 0.01$ for domain inclusion, without invoking the model-specific Pfam gathering cutoff. For loci with alternative isoforms, the protein encoded by the longest transcript was retained. Candidate sequences were subsequently evaluated for conserved domain composition using the PF13967 (cytoplasmic domain) and PF14703 (calcium-dependent channel domain) Pfam profiles and annotated with InterProScan v5.76-107.0 (JONES et al., 2014) to confirm family assignment to PANTHER PTHR13018. Candidates were retained when they contained PF02714 and PF14703 and were assigned to PTHR13018; PF13967 presence was recorded but was not required, allowing retention of the two shortened models described below. Membrane topology and physicochemical properties were then resolved. Transmembrane helix (TMH) topologies were independently predicted using both TMHMM v2.0 (KROGH et al., 2001) and Phobius v1.01 (KÄLL; KROGH; SONNHAMMER, 2004) integrated within InterProScan, and molecular mass and theoretical isoelectric point were computed with Biopython (COCK et al., 2009).

### 2.2 Phylogenetic reconstruction and clade assignment

To reconstruct evolutionary relationships and establish clade designations, a phylogenetic dataset was assembled combining the identified *Vellozia* and *X. schlechteri* OSCA protein sequences with 49 canonical angiosperm reference models from *Arabidopsis thaliana* (15), *Oryza sativa* (11), *Sorghum bicolor* (11), and *Zea mays* (12). Sequences were aligned with MAFFT v7 under automated strategy selection (--auto; KATOH; ROZEWICKI; YAMADA, 2019). Maximum-likelihood inference was conducted in IQ-TREE v3.0.1 (MINH et al., 2020) under the Q.PLANT+R5 substitution model selected by ModelFinder according to the Bayesian information criterion. Branch support was evaluated with 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates (HOANG et al., 2018). In accordance with established deep-paralogy rooting in angiosperm OSCA families (YUAN et al., 2014; KE et al., 2022), the tree was rooted between Clade OSCA1 and the remaining clades (OSCA2, OSCA3, and OSCA4). The resulting circular cladogram topology was formatted and visualized using Interactive Tree Of Life (iTOL) v5 (LETUNIC; BORK, 2021).

### 2.3 Chromosomal location and interspecific synteny

OSCA gene coordinates were obtained from the GFF3 annotation of each haplotype-1 assembly and converted into 0-based BED format with species-prefixed chromosome identifiers (vp_ and vt_). Longest coding sequences (CDS) per locus were extracted and paired with BED files to infer interspecific collinearity between *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* using the Python implementation of MCScan in JCVI v1.6.5 with LAST-backed parameters (TANG et al., 2008). The resulting anchor table was filtered to identify collinear blocks containing OSCA loci. Chromosome correspondence, gene order, and phylogenetic placement were cross-referenced to distinguish reciprocal 1:1 syntenic orthologs from interchromosomal duplications and candidate tandem rearrangements. Local gene order, orientation, and flanking collinear genes were further resolved by extracting microsyntenic windows around OSCA loci of interest, incorporating up to 15 upstream and 15 downstream flanking genes per locus. Macrosynteny and local collinearity were visualized using JCVI synteny modules.

### 2.4 Pairwise evolutionary rates and family-wide selection-intensity analysis

To quantify baseline selective pressures across orthologous and paralogous OSCA pairs, coding-sequence divergence was estimated for pairwise alignments of syntenic orthologs and lineage-specific paralogs. Pairwise protein alignments generated with MAFFT v7 were converted into codon alignments with their corresponding coding sequences using PAL2NAL v14 (SUYAMA; TORRENTS; BORK, 2006) with strict removal of gaps and mismatched codons (-output paml -nogap -nomismatch). Nonsynonymous (Ka), synonymous (Ks), and omega (Ka/Ks) substitution rates were estimated with the Yang-Nielsen method in PAML YN00 v4.10 (YANG; NIELSEN, 2000; YANG, 2007) under the standard genetic code (icode = 0, weighting = 0, commonf3x4 = 0).

Differences in selection intensity between species-designated terminal branches of the OSCA gene tree were evaluated using RELAX in HyPhy v2.5.99 (KOSAKOVSKY POND et al., 2020) under the MG94xREV codon substitution model (WERTHEIM et al., 2015). The analysis used all 27 *Vellozia* OSCA sequences and therefore represents a full-family gene-tree comparison rather than a set of exclusively one-to-one orthologs. Deduced protein sequences were aligned and converted into a multiple codon alignment using PAL2NAL v14, excluding alignment columns with gaps in >20% of sequences; the resulting alignment contained 639 filtered codons. The analysis was conditioned on the maximum-likelihood gene-tree topology connecting these loci. The 13 terminal branches leading to *V. tubiflora* sequences were designated as the Test set, the 14 terminal branches leading to *V. peripherica* sequences as the Reference set, and the 24 internal branches remained unclassified. Under this formulation, RELAX tests whether the distribution of nonsynonymous-to-synonymous rate ratios (ω) on Test branches is systematically contracted toward neutrality (ω = 1; *K* < 1) or expanded away from neutrality (*K* > 1) relative to Reference branches using a likelihood ratio test (2Δln *L* ~ $\chi^2_1$). Optimization sensitivity was assessed by repeating the fit with ten initial random starting points, and alignment sensitivity was evaluated by repeating the analysis after excluding only columns with gaps in >50% of sequences. Because the design compares terminal branches across a multigene family, the estimate is interpreted as a family-wide, model-dependent contrast rather than as an independent estimate for each ortholog or clade. Clade-level sensitivity fits were also evaluated. Exploratory clade-specific fits were evaluated for OSCA1, OSCA2, and OSCA3. The corrected OSCA3 analysis was restricted to *OSCA3.1* and *OSCA3.2* from each species and explicitly excluded the two OSCA4 loci that had been combined with OSCA3 in a superseded exploratory analysis. The four OSCA3 proteins were aligned locally with MUSCLE v3.8.1551, threaded to their coding sequences, and filtered at the same >20% gap threshold, retaining 733 codons. The two *V. tubiflora* terminal branches were designated as Test and the two *V. peripherica* terminal branches as Reference. Sensitivity fits used 10 and 25 optimization starting points and an alternative >50% gap threshold.

### 2.5 Plant sampling, RNA sequencing, and organ-level transcript abundance

One population of each focal species was sampled from *campos rupestres* on private property adjacent to Serra da Canastra National Park, Delfinópolis, Minas Gerais, Brazil: *V. peripherica* at 20°29'07.2"S, 46°30'49.8"W (1,002 m elevation) and *V. tubiflora* at 20°29'15.8"S, 46°32'05.3"W (950 m elevation), located approximately 2.3 km apart. All individuals analyzed in this study originated from these two populations.

To establish organ-level expression profiles under naturally occurring field conditions, leaf, stem, and root tissues were collected from three mature biological individuals per species, immediately flash-frozen in dry ice in the field, and stored at -80 °C. Because samples were harvested directly from native habitats, these transcriptomes capture vegetative expression under ambient *in situ* environmental conditions. Following total RNA extraction via a CTAB protocol (CHANG; PURYEAR; CAIRNEY, 1993), poly(A)-selected, paired-end Illumina libraries were sequenced at a target depth of approximately 30 million reads per library. Raw sequencing reads were inspected for quality with FastQC v0.12.1 (ANDREWS, 2010) and MultiQC v1.33 (EWELS et al., 2016). Quality trimming and adapter removal were performed using fastp v0.23.4 (CHEN et al., 2018) with automated paired-end adapter detection (--detect_adapter_for_pe). Reference annotations were converted from GFF3 to GTF format using gffread v0.12.7 (PERTEA; PERTEA, 2020), and reference indices were generated in STAR v2.7.11b (DOBIN et al., 2013) with scaled suffix-array indexing (--sjdbOverhang 99 --genomeSAindexNbases 13). Clean paired-end reads were mapped to the respective native haplotype-1 genome assemblies using STAR v2.7.11b (--outSAMtype BAM SortedByCoordinate --quantMode GeneCounts), and reverse-stranded gene-level counts were compiled directly from ReadsPerGene.out.tab matrices. The final dataset comprised nine libraries for *V. peripherica* and eight for *V. tubiflora* (owing to the exclusion of one failed root library, VT1R).

Organ-level transcript abundance was modeled with DESeq2 v1.40 (LOVE; HUBER; ANDERS, 2014). Because libraries were mapped to species-specific reference assemblies, each species was analyzed independently using a statistical design incorporating biological individual as a blocking factor to account for paired tissue sampling: ~ Individual + Tissue. This design evaluates organ-associated expression partitioning within each species; it does not evaluate direct cross-species quantitative differences. Genes with low cumulative counts across all libraries within a species (rowSums < 10) were filtered out prior to model fitting. Pairwise tissue contrasts (Root vs. Leaf, Stem vs. Leaf, and Root vs. Stem) were assessed via Wald tests, with significant differential abundance defined by a Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p-value < 0.05. Transcriptome-wide sample relationships were visualized through Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on variance-stabilizing transformed matrices (vst, blind = FALSE).

### 2.6 STRING functional-association networks and enrichment analysis

To infer candidate functional contexts of the OSCA channels, the deduced protein sequences of *Vellozia* OSCA channels were analyzed using the STRING v12 REST API (SZKLARCZYK et al., 2023). Predicted proteomes of *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* were mapped into STRING v12 under custom organism identifiers (STRG0A77QVI and STRG0A11TDW, respectively), which transfer functional associations from reference plant networks (principally *Arabidopsis thaliana*, NCBI taxon ID 3702, and *Oryza sativa*, NCBI taxon ID 4530) via STRING's automated orthology pipeline based on eggNOG orthologous groups and all-against-all sequence alignments. Queries were executed separately for each species using the canonical transcript identifiers of the identified OSCA loci. Networks were generated using the functional network mode (network_type = "functional"), where edges represent integrated functional associations (including co-expression, shared genomic context, automated text-mining, and orthology transfer) rather than strictly physical protein-protein interactions. We retrieved edges meeting a medium-confidence threshold (combined score $\ge 0.400$) and allowed the addition of up to 50 associated nodes per species network (add_nodes = 50) to represent the extended association context of the queried channels. The resulting species-specific functional-association networks were modeled as undirected graphs to extract key topological metrics, including node degree, connected components, and unweighted clustering coefficients, in NetworkX (HAGBERG; SCHULT; SWART, 2008). Finally, to identify biological pathways and Gene Ontology terms overrepresented across all connected network nodes, associated proteins were analyzed via the STRING functional annotation and enrichment endpoints, retaining terms with a false discovery rate below 0.05.

### 2.7 Upstream *cis*-element annotation

To characterize the *cis*-regulatory architecture of the OSCA family, transcript coordinates were retrieved from the genomic annotations of both *Vellozia* species. A 2,000-bp promoter region immediately upstream of each annotated transcript boundary was extracted in a strand-specific manner using BEDTools v2.30.0 (QUINLAN; HALL, 2010). Putative *cis*-regulatory elements within these regions were then identified by querying the PlantCARE database (LESCOT et al., 2002). To resolve database redundancies, orthographic variants of identical motif families (e.g., G-box, MYC, and MYB) were normalized, and overlapping coordinates for the same motif class were merged into single non-redundant occurrences. Curated motifs were classified into five functional categories following the framework of QU et al. (2019): core promoter elements (TATA-box and CAAT-box), light responsiveness, growth and development, phytohormone signaling (abscisic acid, methyl jasmonate, and salicylic acid), and abiotic stress response (drought, low temperature, anaerobic induction, and defense/wounding).

## 3. RESULTS

### 3.1 Four canonical OSCA clades and 27 channel loci are conserved across *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*

Profile searches and domain curation identified 27 OSCA loci across the two focal genomes, comprising 14 in *V. peripherica* and 13 in *V. tubiflora*, alongside eight loci identified in the sister outgroup *X. schlechteri* (Figure 1A; Supplementary Table S1). Both *Vellozia* species contained five OSCA1 loci, two OSCA3 loci, and one OSCA4 locus. The difference in total family size occurred within OSCA2, which included six loci in *V. peripherica* and five in *V. tubiflora*. Each candidate retained the PANTHER PTHR13018 assignment and the PF02714 and PF14703 domains.

Among the 27 *Vellozia* OSCA proteins, 25 also contained PF13967 and therefore showed the complete three-domain combination recovered for most of the family (Figure 1B,C; Supplementary Table S1). The exceptions were *VpOSCA2.2* and *VtOSCA1.2*. These sequences were 478 and 485 amino acids long, respectively, whereas most identified OSCA proteins ranged from 634 to 869 amino acids, and both lacked a saved PF13967 hit. Their reduced length was accompanied by fewer predicted transmembrane segments, with TMHMM returning six for *VpOSCA2.2* and five for *VtOSCA1.2*. Across the complete OSCA gene set, TMHMM counts ranged from five to 12, indicating that baseline predictions do not support a uniform count of eleven helices across all 27 gene models. Both shortened loci showed consistent RNA-seq read coverage across annotated exons, confirming baseline transcriptional activity. In the sister outgroup *X. schlechteri*, all eight identified loci retained the complete three-domain Pfam complement (PF13967, PF14703, and PF02714; Supplementary Table S1).

Predicted molecular masses ranged from 71.83 to 100.53 kDa across canonical full-length proteins, with the two shortened variants (*VpOSCA2.2* and *VtOSCA1.2*) yielding expectedly lower values of 54.42 and 55.12 kDa, respectively (Supplementary Table S1). Theoretical isoelectric points were consistently basic across 25 of the 27 channels (pI 8.23-9.48; mean = 9.05). The sole exception was the single-copy *OSCA4.1* channel, which exhibited conserved near-neutral isoelectric points in both species (6.55 in *V. peripherica* and 6.52 in *V. tubiflora*; Supplementary Table S1).

The maximum-likelihood tree placed the *Vellozia* sequences within the four established OSCA clades alongside the *X. schlechteri* outgroup and angiosperm references (Figure 1A). Across the 27 *Vellozia* proteins, 12 interspecific ortholog pairs resolved as reciprocally monophyletic one-to-one pairs (*OSCA1.1*, *OSCA1.2*, *OSCA1.3*, *OSCA1.4*, *OSCA1.5*, *OSCA2.1*, *OSCA2.2*, *OSCA2.3*, *OSCA2.5*, *OSCA3.1*, *OSCA3.2*, and *OSCA4.1*), with 11 pairs supported by ultrafast bootstrap values ≥ 99% and *OSCA1.4* nested within the chromosome 7 *OSCA1.3*/1.4/1.5 cluster (UFBoot = 100%). Within the remaining orthologous group (*OSCA2.4*), the two *V. peripherica* sequences and the single *V. tubiflora* sequence formed a monophyletic clade (UFBoot = 100%) sister to *XsOSCA2.4*. The topology resolved the canonical four-clade architecture rooted between Clade OSCA1 and Clades OSCA2-4.

### 3.2 Chromosome-scale collinearity coexists with a lineage-specific *OSCA2.4* tandem duplication on chromosome 1

Interspecific MCScan collinearity analysis revealed strong chromosome-scale positional conservation across orthologous chromosomes, identifying 12 focal collinear anchor rows across corresponding chromosomes 1, 4, 5, 7, 8, and 9 (Figure 2A). The OSCA loci associated with these anchors occupied conserved chromosomal blocks between *V. peripherica* and *V. tubiflora*, supporting the inference that much of the chromosomal distribution of the family predates the divergence of the two lineages.

Conserved microsynteny was particularly evident in the chromosome 7 tandem cluster comprising *OSCA1.3*, *OSCA1.4*, and *OSCA1.5*, which resolved as reciprocal one-to-one orthologs between the two genomes (Figure 2B). The surrounding local genomic block contained 24 collinear anchor pairs flanking the three tandem OSCA channels across intervals of 213.6 kb in *V. peripherica* and 188.6 kb in *V. tubiflora*, demonstrating strict positional conservation and structural stasis of this tandem array in both species.

The chromosome 1 comparison revealed the principal family-size difference between the two assemblies (Figure 2C). *Vellozia peripherica* contained two adjacent loci, *VpOSCA2.4b* and *VpOSCA2.4a*, ordered from lower to higher genomic coordinates and separated by 1,292 bp, whereas the orthologous *V. tubiflora* interval contained a single locus, *VtOSCA2.4*. Extensive collinearity among the flanking genes showed that the copy-number difference is localized rather than part of a large-scale chromosomal rearrangement. The two *V. peripherica* copies and *VtOSCA2.4* therefore constitute a co-orthologous group within this conserved interval. Together with the single *OSCA2.4* locus in the sister outgroup *X. schlechteri* (*XsOSCA2.4*) and the sister placement of the *V. peripherica* paralogs in the phylogenetic tree, this pattern supports a derived tandem duplication in *V. peripherica* under maximum parsimony (Figure 1A).

### 3.3 A family-wide difference in selection intensity coexists with pairwise ω values below unity

The full-family OSCA gene tree exhibited asymmetric selection intensity across lineages (Figure 3A; Supplementary Table S2). HyPhy RELAX returned a significantly lower selection-intensity parameter for the 13 *V. tubiflora* Test branches than for the 14 *V. peripherica* Reference branches (*K* = 0.7064, LRT = 12.8509, *p* = 0.000337). This asymmetry persisted when fitting the 639-codon alignment from ten initial random starting points (*K* = 0.7130, LRT = 11.2788, *p* = 0.000784) and when relaxing the gap threshold to 735 codons (*K* = 0.8105, LRT = 5.4587, *p* = 0.0195). Under the tested codon model, these results reflect a family-wide contraction of the fitted ω distribution toward neutrality on the *V. tubiflora* branches, although pooling paralogs across all four clades precludes attributing this relaxation to any single locus or clade.

By contrast, OSCA3 alone showed no clade-specific shift. Fitting the model exclusively to the OSCA3 clade, with OSCA4 excluded, yielded non-significant differences across all configurations (*K* = 0.2565, LRT = 3.1720, *p* = 0.0749; Figure 3A; Supplementary Table S2). Point estimates of *K* remained consistently below one across 1, 10, and 25 optimization starting points (*K* = 0.2565-0.3188, *p* = 0.0745-0.0752) as well as under the alternative 734-codon gap filter (*K* = 0.2899, *p* = 0.0675), indicating that while point estimates were suppressed, the dataset provides no statistical support for an OSCA3-specific shift at α = 0.05.

Pairwise coding-sequence comparisons confirmed widespread purifying selection. All 14 interspecific comparisons had ω < 1.00 (Figure 3B; Supplementary Table S3), comprising 12 reciprocal one-to-one ortholog pairs and the two comparisons between *VtOSCA2.4* and each *V. peripherica OSCA2.4* co-ortholog. Across these pairs, ω values ranged from 0.0530 for *OSCA2.3* to 0.8015 for *OSCA1.4* (mean = 0.4082, median = 0.3368), consistent with strong functional constraint on protein sequences despite the absence of locus-specific tests against neutral expectations. Similarly, the *VpOSCA2.4a*-*VpOSCA2.4b* intraspecific tandem pair exhibited ω = 0.3770, indicating that purifying selection also constrained the duplicated coding sequences within *V. peripherica*.

### 3.4 Reciprocal root-stem versus foliar expression polarities distinguish core Clade 1 and Clade 3 paralogs

Organ transcriptomes separated cleanly by vegetative tissue. In principal component analysis, RNA-seq libraries clustered strictly by vegetative organ (Supplementary Figure S1), revealing structured tissue-level expression profiles across the OSCA family (Figure 4; Supplementary Table S4). Within OSCA1, *OSCA1.2* and *OSCA1.1* established reciprocal root-stem versus foliar polarities. Specifically, *OSCA1.2* transcript abundance was markedly higher in roots than in leaves in both *V. peripherica* (log~2~FC = 4.3568, *p*~adj~ = 0.0115) and *V. tubiflora* (log~2~FC = 8.2653, *p*~adj~ = 0.000168), as well as in stems relative to leaves (log~2~FC = 5.3005, *p*~adj~ = 0.00141; and log~2~FC = 5.3692, *p*~adj~ = 0.00882, respectively). Conversely, *OSCA1.1* was depleted in subterranean and caulinary organs relative to foliage across both congeners, showing significantly lower abundance in roots (*V. peripherica*: log~2~FC = -1.3977, *p*~adj~ = 0.0252; *V. tubiflora*: log~2~FC = -1.1758, *p*~adj~ = 0.0474) and stems (*V. peripherica*: log~2~FC = -1.7833, *p*~adj~ = 0.00302; *V. tubiflora*: log~2~FC = -2.0638, *p*~adj~ = 2.69 × 10^-6^). Among the remaining Clade 1 channels, only *VtOSCA1.4* displayed significant tissue regulation, being enriched in stems over leaves (log~2~FC = 3.7440, *p*~adj~ = 0.0220).

Within OSCA2, significant organ contrasts were confined to individual species models (Figure 4; Supplementary Table S4). *VpOSCA2.3* showed elevated abundance in roots and stems relative to leaves in *V. peripherica* (root-leaf: log~2~FC = 4.2153, *p*~adj~ = 0.0402; stem-leaf: log~2~FC = 4.1253, *p*~adj~ = 0.0461), whereas neither contrast achieved significance in *V. tubiflora*. At the chromosome 1 locus, the single *VtOSCA2.4* gene was enriched in roots compared to leaves (log~2~FC = 0.8016, *p*~adj~ = 0.0255) and stems (log~2~FC = 1.0112, *p*~adj~ = 0.00623), while neither tandem paralog in *V. peripherica* (*VpOSCA2.4a* or *VpOSCA2.4b*) showed significant within-gene tissue variation. Additional species-specific contrasts in *V. tubiflora* included stem depletion of *VtOSCA2.2* relative to leaves (log~2~FC = -0.7879, *p*~adj~ = 0.0331) and root enrichment coupled with stem depletion for *VtOSCA2.5* (root-stem: log~2~FC = 1.0999, *p*~adj~ = 0.0290; stem-leaf: log~2~FC = -1.5526, *p*~adj~ = 1.10 × 10^-5^), although independent modeling precludes interpreting these single-species contrasts as demonstrated interspecific divergence without explicit species-by-tissue interaction testing.

Clade 3 channels also displayed distinct organ-associated patterns (Figure 4; Supplementary Table S4). *OSCA3.1* transcript abundance was significantly lower in stems than in leaves across both species (*V. peripherica*: log~2~FC = -1.6826, *p*~adj~ = 0.000757; *V. tubiflora*: log~2~FC = -2.9118, *p*~adj~ = 5.31 × 10^-5^), while its root-leaf depletion was statistically significant only in *V. peripherica* (log~2~FC = -1.5623, *p*~adj~ = 0.00200) despite an identical downward trend in *V. tubiflora* (log~2~FC = -1.3779, *p*~adj~ = 0.1733). In contrast, *VtOSCA3.2* exhibited marked root enrichment over leaves (log~2~FC = 4.4166, *p*~adj~ = 0.0103), whereas the corresponding contrast in *V. peripherica* did not reach the significance threshold.

For OSCA4, *VtOSCA4.1* abundance was higher in roots than in stems (log~2~FC = 1.8408, *p*~adj~ = 0.00338) and lower in stems than in leaves (log~2~FC = -1.4430, *p*~adj~ = 0.00341), whereas *VpOSCA4.1* showed no significant vegetative tissue contrasts (Figure 4; Supplementary Table S4). Thus, while *OSCA1.1*, *OSCA1.2*, and the stem-leaf depletion of *OSCA3.1* represent conserved family-wide spatial deployments, other single-species contrasts define prioritized targets for factorial validation rather than confirmed evolutionary shifts.

### 3.5 *OSCA1.2* and *OSCA4.1* are highly connected queries in predicted networks enriched for carbohydrate catabolism

STRING functional networks revealed conserved topological hubs across both congeners. The reconstructed network for *V. peripherica* comprised 61 nodes and 187 edges, whereas the *V. tubiflora* network comprised 60 nodes and 217 edges (Figure 5; Supplementary Table S4; pathway enrichment in Supplementary Table S5). Thus, despite nearly identical node counts, the *V. tubiflora* network contained 16.0% more edges and exhibited a higher mean node degree than the *V. peripherica* network (7.23 vs. 6.13). Mean unweighted clustering coefficients were similar between species (0.4718 in *V. tubiflora* and 0.4937 in *V. peripherica*), and both networks partitioned into two connected components: a major component containing 54 nodes in *V. tubiflora* and 55 in *V. peripherica*, alongside a separate six-node component.

Enrichment analyses identified carbohydrate catabolism as the predominant biological theme (Supplementary Table S5). Starch and sucrose metabolism (KEGG map00500) was the most significantly enriched pathway, comprising 23 genes in *V. tubiflora* (FDR = 3.57 × 10^-28^) and 22 in *V. peripherica* (FDR = 2.60 × 10^-26^). Enriched molecular functions included amylase activity (GO:0016160; FDR = 3.74 × 10^-16^ and 3.22 × 10^-16^, respectively), amylopectin maltohydrolase activity (GO:0102229; FDR = 3.61 × 10^-15^ and 4.14 × 10^-13^), β-amylase activity (GO:0016161; FDR ≤ 2.14 × 10^-10^), α-amylase activity (GO:0004556; FDR ≤ 6.93 × 10^-5^), and calcium-activated cation channel activity (GO:0005227; FDR = 8.80 × 10^-18^ and 5.64 × 10^-20^, respectively). Values in paired statements are reported for *V. tubiflora* followed by *V. peripherica*. Plasma membrane (GO:0005886) and plasmodesma (GO:0009506) were also enriched cellular-component categories in both networks (FDR < 0.009).

Individual OSCA channels occupied distinct network positions (Figure 5; Supplementary Table S4). Restricting the calculation specifically to the connected OSCA channels (10 of 13 in *V. tubiflora* and 11 of 14 in *V. peripherica*, excluding the three loci lacking associations in each species), the mean degree of the focal channels was 7.30 in *V. tubiflora* (5.62 across all 13 queried loci) and 6.09 in *V. peripherica* (4.79 across all 14 queried loci), closely tracking the whole-network mean node degrees of 7.23 and 6.13 across all interactors. Within OSCA1, *OSCA1.2* occupied the same predicted topological position in both networks, with a degree of 18 (comprising 16 predicted functional associations with carbohydrate-catabolizing enzymes and two adjacent channel associations in each network). *OSCA1.1* had degree 13 in *V. tubiflora* and 11 in *V. peripherica*, whereas *OSCA1.3*, *OSCA1.4*, and *OSCA1.5* were more peripheral, each displaying degree 3 in *V. tubiflora* and degree 1 in *V. peripherica*.

Differences were also observed among OSCA2 proteins (Figure 5A,C; Supplementary Table S4). At the chromosome 1 locus, the single-copy *VtOSCA2.4* had degree 5, connecting to the channel hub *VtOSCA4.1* (score = 0.564), a pentatricopeptide repeat (PPR) protein, a putative methyltransferase, and two DnaJ chaperone family proteins. In *V. peripherica*, the tandem paralogs *VpOSCA2.4a* and *VpOSCA2.4b* each had degree 2 and shared identical interactors: the orthologous hub *VpOSCA4.1* (score = 0.456) and a PPR protein (score = 0.484). *OSCA2.5* had degree 7 in *V. tubiflora* and 4 in *V. peripherica*, sharing predicted associations with ABCG-family transporters (including ABCG22; score = 0.802), alpha/beta-hydrolases, and DnaJ chaperones in both species, with *VtOSCA2.5* connecting additionally to an AP2/ERF transcription factor and a mitochondrial complex I LYR protein. By contrast, *OSCA2.1*, *OSCA2.2*, and *OSCA2.3* returned no associations in either species at the adopted confidence threshold.

The OSCA3 proteins displayed a distinct but conserved organization (Figure 5C; Supplementary Table S4). In both species, *OSCA3.1* and *OSCA3.2* segregated from the primary network into the separate six-node component, each exhibiting degree 4 and a clustering coefficient of 0.000. In this subnetwork, both channels connected to the same four organellar and membrane-associated partners without direct intra-clade channel-to-channel edges: a vacuolar sorting protein 39 (VPS39 / plant-type vacuole; score = 0.416), a photosystem-associated thylakoid protein (photosystem I reaction center subunit V in *V. peripherica* and a cytochrome *b*~5~ family protein in *V. tubiflora*), and two cellular-periphery defense-associated proteins (score = 0.550; experimental evidence score *e* = 0.425).

OSCA4 was a major topological hub in both species. It functioned as the primary hub in *V. peripherica* (degree 19; Figure 5A,C1) and among the most connected queries in *V. tubiflora* (degree 13; Figure 5A,C2; Supplementary Table S4). The two networks shared a conserved core of *OSCA4.1* interactors, including small GTPases of the Arf family (score = 0.603), ABCG transporters (including ABCG22; score = 0.802), homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase (score = 0.585), PhoX-domain proteins (score = 0.559), ankyrin repeat proteins (score = 0.428), PPR proteins (scores = 0.517-0.610), and direct edges connecting to the *OSCA2.4* channels. Species-specific associations for *VpOSCA4.1* included heat shock protein 70 (HSP70 family; score = 0.430), a DnaJ subfamily C chaperone (score = 0.647), and multi-antimicrobial extrusion (MATE) transporters, whereas *VtOSCA4.1* uniquely associated with an AP2/ERF transcription factor and a mitochondrial complex I LYR protein.

Text mining supported most network edges: 157 of 217 (72.35%) in *V. tubiflora* and 132 of 187 (70.59%) in *V. peripherica*. Curated-database evidence supported 119 and 109 edges, respectively, whereas five edges in each network had experimental support. Coexpression, co-occurrence, genomic neighbourhood, and gene fusion contributed smaller subsets.

### 3.6 Locus-specific differences in predicted hormone- and stress-responsive *cis*-elements occur among *OSCA2.4* promoters

Promoter architectures revealed extensive *cis*-element diversity across the family. PlantCARE annotation of the 2-kb upstream promoter regions across all 27 *Vellozia* OSCA loci identified non-overlapping *cis*-regulatory motifs across core promoter, growth and development, light responsiveness, phytohormone signaling, and abiotic stress categories, with similar overall category distributions between the two species (Figure 6; Supplementary Tables S6 and S7).

Excluding basal core promoter elements (TATA-box and CAAT-box; 1,380 total occurrences across all 27 promoters), growth- and development-associated transcription factor sites (MYB and MYC, alongside a single RY-element; 204 occurrences) and light-responsive elements (Box 4, G-box, and GT1-motif; 187 occurrences) were the most abundant categories, followed by phytohormone signaling (161 occurrences) and abiotic stress response (116 occurrences). Phytohormone-related elements included the ABA-responsive element (ABRE), MeJA-responsive elements (CGTCA-motif and TGACG-motif), and the salicylic acid-responsive TCA-element. Stress-related motifs included the drought-responsive MBS (MYB binding site involved in drought inducibility), the low-temperature-responsive LTR, the anaerobic induction element ARE, TC-rich repeats, and the WRKY-binding W box involved in defense and wounding responses (Supplementary Table S6).

Despite family-level conservation, individual promoters diverged markedly in motif composition (Figure 6; Supplementary Table S7). This contrast was evident at the *OSCA2.4* locus: *VpOSCA2.4a* contained two non-overlapping ABRE matches and two G-boxes, *VpOSCA2.4b* contained four ABREs, four G-boxes, and three defense-associated W boxes, whereas the single-copy *VtOSCA2.4* harbored six ABREs, six G-boxes, and one W box. The highest ABRE densities in *V. peripherica* occurred in *VpOSCA2.1* and *VpOSCA3.2* (six matches each, accompanied by six and seven G-boxes, respectively), whereas *VtOSCA2.1* had the highest count in *V. tubiflora* (seven ABREs and ten G-boxes), followed by *VtOSCA2.4* (six each). Abiotic stress motifs also showed locus-specific distributions: the low-temperature-responsive LTR motif was most abundant in *VpOSCA2.5* (six occurrences) and *VpOSCA1.2* (four), whereas drought-inducible MBS motifs were present in 10 of the 14 *V. peripherica* promoters and seven of the 13 *V. tubiflora* promoters.

## 4. DISCUSSION

### 4.1 Structural conservation and the functional status of truncated OSCA channel models

Both *Vellozia* species retain a conserved OSCA channel repertoire across all four canonical angiosperm clades. Shared family composition, extensive macrosyntenic collinearity, and pairwise ω point estimates below unity demonstrate that the primary mechanosensory complement was established prior to the divergence of these congeners. Although OSCA family membership establishes sequence similarity and orthology rather than demonstrated channel biophysics in *Vellozia*, electrophysiological recordings in model angiosperms confirm osmotic calcium signaling and mechanically activated currents (YUAN et al., 2014; MURTHY et al., 2018), defining clear conductance, ion-selectivity, and tension-gating hypotheses for direct testing in *Vellozia* OSCA channels.

The shortened architectures of *VpOSCA2.2* and *VtOSCA1.2* demonstrate that family-level conservation does not imply uniform protein structure across all loci. Both proteins retained PF02714, PF14703, and PTHR13018 but lacked a detectable PF13967 domain. Truncations of OSCA channels have also been documented in other monocots; for instance, in maize (*Zea mays*), *ZmOSCA1.1a* and *ZmOSCA3.1* harbor independent deletions that reduce the number of predicted transmembrane segments relative to canonical full-length paralogs (DING et al., 2019). In *Vellozia*, this reduction in predicted transmembrane passes (five to six helices compared to 7 to 12 predicted by TMHMM in full-length models) coincides with the absence of PF13967. Because structurally resolved OSCA channels form functional homodimers with eleven transmembrane helices per subunit, including the membrane-deforming TM0 (LIU; WANG; SUN, 2018; ZHANG et al., 2018), these predicted deletions could affect channel topology, oligomerization, or mechanosensory gating. While transcript-supported re-annotation and *in vivo* functional assays remain necessary to determine whether these shortened models represent specialized proteins, incomplete gene models, or annotation artifacts, their consistent RNA-seq read coverage indicates that structural variation coexists with the conserved core family framework.

In terms of gene copy number and family architecture, *V. tubiflora* shows no lineage-specific OSCA expansion relative to *V. peripherica*. The RELAX comparison nevertheless detected a significant family-wide difference between species-designated terminal branches (*K* = 0.7064, *p* = 0.0003), while pairwise ω estimates remained below unity. The OSCA3-only analysis yielded a lower point estimate of *K* but did not reach statistical significance, so the family-wide signal cannot be localized to OSCA3. Although this contrast is compatible with lower selection intensity across the *V. tubiflora*-designated branch set under the fitted model, it pools paralogs across clades and cannot isolate specific loci or determine whether cellular mitigation during severe dehydration, such as cell-wall folding and protoplast shrinkage (FARRANT et al., 2015), involves relaxed mechanosensory tuning. How this conserved repertoire is organized across the genome provides critical insight into the structural stability of the family.

### 4.2 Syntenic stasis and asymmetric local duplication at the *OSCA2.4* locus

Interspecific synteny demonstrates that chromosomal organization is largely static between the two congeners, confining copy-number variation to a single tandem locus on chromosome 1. On chromosome 7, the *OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5* tandem array occurred in conserved gene order within a local block containing 24 collinear flanking gene pairs and spanning more than 188 kb. Although high sequence similarity among adjacent paralogs can generate degenerate pairwise anchor matches, the concordance between phylogenetic topology, local gene order, and flanking collinearity supports the inference that this three-gene array predates the divergence of the focal *Vellozia* lineages.

The principal departure from this conserved organization occurred at *OSCA2.4* on chromosome 1, where *V. peripherica* contained two adjacent copies (*VpOSCA2.4a* and *VpOSCA2.4b*) and *V. tubiflora* contained one (*VtOSCA2.4*). Their chromosomal contiguity is consistent with a local tandem duplication event. Orthology mapping and domain annotation identified a single *OSCA2.4* locus in the sister outgroup *Xerophyta schlechteri* (*XsOSCA2.4* / MP002250; Supplementary Table S1). The single-copy states in *X. schlechteri* and *V. tubiflora*, together with the *OSCA2.4* gene-tree topology, support a derived duplication in *V. peripherica* under maximum parsimony. Both *V. peripherica* copies are therefore co-orthologous to *VtOSCA2.4*. Their sequence distances are asymmetric: *VpOSCA2.4b* and *VtOSCA2.4* have lower synonymous divergence (*K*~s~ = 0.0343) than *VpOSCA2.4a* and *VtOSCA2.4* (*K*~s~ = 0.1215; Supplementary Table S3), but this difference does not redefine the co-orthology relationship. The ω point estimate between the *V. peripherica* duplicates was 0.3770, and estimates for their comparisons with *VtOSCA2.4* were 0.2985 and 0.5155. While these subunitary values, intact open reading frames, and organ-level transcription do not by themselves establish active channel gating or exclude nascent subfunctionalization, the *OSCA2.4* locus emerges as a primary candidate for broader evolutionary sampling and functional validation across Velloziaceae. Whether sequence evolutionary rates mirror this structural stasis requires family-wide selection analysis.

### 4.3 Family-wide selection-intensity contrast and caveats of a two-species comparison

Pairwise coding sequences reflect strong purifying selection across all loci, yet lineage-level modeling reveals an asymmetric selective regime. All pairwise *K*~a~/*K*~s~ (ω) point estimates were below unity. The 14 interspecific comparisons, comprising 12 one-to-one ortholog pairs and two *OSCA2.4* co-ortholog comparisons, had a median of 0.3368, while the intraspecific tandem duplicate pair had ω = 0.3770 (Supplementary Table S3). This distribution is consistent with predominantly purifying selection across the OSCA gene set, but the pairwise analysis did not include confidence intervals or locus-specific tests against ω = 1. *OSCA1.4* had the highest estimate (ω = 0.8015) and therefore represents a candidate for testing comparatively weaker constraint. Predominantly subunitary ω estimates have also been reported for duplicated OSCA pairs in *Vigna radiata* (YIN et al., 2021) and *Liriodendron chinense* (KE et al., 2022).

RELAX detected a significant reduction in the fitted selection-intensity parameter for the *V. tubiflora*-designated terminal branches in the full-family gene tree (*K* = 0.7064, *p* = 0.0003). The direction and significance were stable to additional optimization starting points and to the alternative gap filter, although the magnitude of *K* varied from 0.7064 to 0.8105. Because phenomenological codon-model parameters may absorb unmodeled mutational dynamics, substitution saturation, gene-specific rate variation, or differences in effective population size (*N*~e~; LYNCH; CONERY, 2003; STRASBURG et al., 2011; JONES et al., 2018), this RELAX contrast represents a comparative family-wide signal that motivates broader multi-species sampling rather than definitive evidence that anhydrobiotic quiescence directly relaxed selective constraint. How this pervasive sequence conservation translates into the spatial deployment of channels across vegetative organs was assessed through native-habitat transcriptomes.

### 4.4 Concordant organ-associated expression patterns across contrasting drought-survival strategies

Field transcriptomes demonstrate that primary channel deployment is partitioned along conserved root-stem versus leaf axes across contrasting drought-survival strategies. Parallel to sequence-level constraints, OSCA deployment may be influenced by spatial regulation across plant organs (HARTMANN et al., 2021). The sampled transcriptomes revealed repeated spatial patterns in both species. *OSCA1.2* was significantly more abundant in roots and stems than in leaves, whereas *OSCA1.1* showed the opposite pattern, displaying higher transcript abundance in leaves than in roots and stems. These concordant within-species polarities demonstrate that primary organ partitioning has been conserved following lineage divergence. In contrast, other detected contrasts, such as stem enrichment of *VtOSCA1.4*, root enrichment of *VtOSCA2.4* and *VtOSCA3.2*, or stem depletion of *VtOSCA2.2*, reached statistical significance in only one of the two independently modeled species. Although differences in nominal significance between separate species models cannot establish interspecific divergence without factorial species-by-tissue testing, these organ-specific contrasts prioritize candidate loci for targeted physiological experiments.

Because these transcriptomes were generated from mature individuals sampled directly in their native *campos rupestres* habitats, they provide an authentic *in situ* reference for vegetative organ partitioning under ambient environmental conditions, rather than a laboratory-controlled, unstressed baseline. While resolving dehydration-induced transcriptional cascades and stomatal dynamics will require controlled drying time courses, these field-derived organ profiles establish that primary mechanosensory channel deployment is structured by conserved spatial compartmentalization, providing an essential foundation for testing how OSCA channels respond to fluctuating water availability across contrasting drought-survival strategies. Beyond organ partitioning, resolving the candidate functional associations of these channels illuminates the cellular processes in which they may participate.

### 4.5 Predicted functional associations prioritize candidates for experimental analysis

Predicted interaction networks partition the family into distinct functional modules, linking the most connected channels to carbohydrate mobilization and membrane trafficking. Topological analysis of the reconstructed STRING networks identified *OSCA1.2*, *OSCA4.1*, and *OSCA1.1* as highly connected query proteins, while the remaining clades occupied distinct predicted modules (Figure 5). Because these networks were transferred primarily from model-plant orthology, included up to 50 added interactors, and were supported predominantly by text mining, they should be interpreted as a hypothesis-generating framework rather than as *Vellozia*-specific evidence of physical interaction, pathway membership, or subcellular localization.

Among the most connected queries, *OSCA1.2* occupied the same predicted topological position in both species and was associated with 16 carbohydrate-metabolizing enzymes, principally α- and β-amylases. Although transferred network topology cannot establish direct enzymatic coordination in *Vellozia*, this conserved association, coupled with higher transcript abundance in roots and stems (Figure 4), prioritizes *OSCA1.2* as a candidate mechanosensor linking mechanical turgor changes to amylolytic starch breakdown during osmotic adjustment (SECCHI; ZWIENIECKI, 2011; THALMANN; SANTELIA, 2017; ZHAI et al., 2021; LI et al., 2022).

High predicted connectivity also characterized *OSCA4.1*, which formed the most connected OSCA query in *V. peripherica* (degree 19) and ranked among the most connected queries in *V. tubiflora* (degree 13). In maize, natural allelic variation at *ZmOSCA4.1* is genetically linked to seedling dehydration survival (DING et al., 2019). Both transferred *Vellozia* networks included associations with proteins annotated in membrane trafficking and cellular protection, including Arf-family small GTPases, ABCG-family transporters, homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase, and *OSCA2.4*. While differences in peripheral edges between species may reflect database coverage rather than biological divergence, these conserved topological ties prioritize *OSCA4.1* for experimental validation in membrane trafficking and cellular protection.

In contrast to the larger network component, *OSCA3.1* and *OSCA3.2* occurred in a separate six-node predicted subnetwork in both congeners (degree 4, clustering coefficient 0.000). Their transferred partners included the vacuolar sorting protein VPS39 and photosystem- or cytochrome-associated proteins. Although network modularity alone cannot establish subcellular localization or organellar physiology, the historical identification of the *Arabidopsis* OSCA3 ortholog (*AtOSCA3.1*) as early responsive to dehydration 4 (*ERD4*; KIYOSUE; YAMAGUCHI-SHINOZAKI; SHINOZAKI, 1994) motivates evaluating whether *Vellozia* OSCA3 channels participate in vacuolar remodeling or photosynthetic-membrane preservation during water deficit (FARRANT et al., 2015).

At the *OSCA2.4* locus, *VpOSCA2.4a* and *VpOSCA2.4b* shared the same two transferred associations, whereas *VtOSCA2.4* returned five associations, including DnaJ-family proteins (Figure 5). While the resolution and provenance of STRING edges are insufficient to determine whether the duplicated proteins retained identical interaction partners or whether regulatory divergence predominated over protein-level divergence, these patterns nominate candidate co-factors for targeted testing. Similarly, the predicted associations of *OSCA2.5* with ABCG transporters, alpha/beta-hydrolases, and an AP2/ERF transcription factor identify candidate relationships for validation rather than a demonstrated lipid-transport or stress-signaling module.

Topological peripheralization and the absence of predicted edges should not be interpreted as evidence of biological insignificance. The low transferred degree of tandemly duplicated *OSCA1.3*, *OSCA1.4*, and *OSCA1.5*, and the absence of returned associations for *OSCA2.1*, *OSCA2.2*, and *OSCA2.3*, may reflect incomplete one-to-one representation in model-species databases. The predicted networks are therefore most useful for prioritization: *OSCA1.2*, *OSCA4.1*, and the *OSCA2.4/2.5* loci emerge as candidates for localization, interaction, and channel-physiology experiments, while degree-zero loci remain unresolved rather than functionally excluded. Understanding how these prioritized channels are transcriptionally controlled directs attention to their upstream regulatory regions.

### 4.6 Predicted promoter-motif differences define hypotheses for regulatory testing

Upstream promoter architectures show substantial motif divergence despite structural stasis, prioritizing specific candidates for drought-response testing. Within each *Vellozia* species, predicted OSCA promoter regions differed in motif composition, paralleling locus-level variation reported in *Liriodendron chinense*, sunflower, alfalfa, and *Vigna radiata* (KE et al., 2022; SHAN et al., 2023; LI et al., 2023; YIN et al., 2021). Across both *Vellozia* genomes, frequently observed non-basal annotations included light-responsive (Box 4, G-box, GT1-motif), developmental (MYB, MYC), phytohormone-related (ABRE, CGTCA/TGACG motifs), and abiotic-stress-related (MBS, LTR, ARE, W box) classes. While computational motif occurrences do not prove transcription-factor binding, environmental responsiveness, or integration into functional regulons, their presence establishes testable hypotheses regarding stress inducibility.

The duplicated *OSCA2.4* locus illustrates this regulatory variation. Although the ω point estimate between *VpOSCA2.4a* and *VpOSCA2.4b* was 0.3770 and both proteins retained canonical domain architectures, their annotated 2-kb upstream regions differed in motif counts: *VpOSCA2.4a* contained two ABRE and two G-box matches; *VpOSCA2.4b* contained four ABREs, four G-boxes, and three W boxes; and *VtOSCA2.4* contained six ABREs and six G-boxes. While these differences are compatible with regulatory divergence after duplication, promoter-reporter assays, chromatin immunoprecipitation, and expression time courses are necessary to determine whether they drive functional subfunctionalization or differential inducibility.

High ABRE and G-box counts occurred in selected loci, including *VtOSCA2.1*, *VtOSCA2.4*, *VpOSCA2.1*, and *VpOSCA3.2*. Because ABA-mediated transcriptional cascades contribute to desiccation responses in resurrection plants (FARRANT et al., 2015; COSTA et al., 2017; LYALL; GECHEV, 2020), these loci are prime candidates for testing during controlled dehydration and rehydration, even though motif density alone cannot establish whether these promoters are poised for induction or active in mature tissues.

In the dehydration-avoidant *V. peripherica*, maintenance of tissue hydration is central to dry-season survival (ALCANTARA et al., 2015; TEODORO et al., 2021). The predicted motifs in *VpOSCA2.1*, *VpOSCA3.2*, and the duplicated *VpOSCA2.4a/b* promoters provide testable candidates for ABA- and water-deficit-responsive regulation, though cross-species comparisons must be validated empirically before assigning these motif configurations to dehydration avoidance in *V. peripherica* or desiccation preparation in *V. tubiflora*.

More broadly, vegetative desiccation tolerance in *V. tubiflora* coexists with a structurally conserved OSCA repertoire. The localized copy-number difference, organ-associated transcript patterns, transferred functional associations, and predicted promoter motifs define hypotheses about where regulatory or functional divergence may occur. Determining whether drought-strategy divergence depends on OSCA regulation, downstream signaling, or other physiological systems will require broader phylogenetic sampling and direct experiments across controlled dehydration and rehydration.

## 5. CONCLUSIONS

Comparative genomic analysis of *Vellozia peripherica* and *Vellozia tubiflora* revealed a predominantly conserved OSCA repertoire, with comparable family sizes, retention of all four canonical clades, and extensive chromosome-scale collinearity. Pairwise ω estimates below unity were consistent with broad coding-sequence constraint, while the full-family RELAX model detected lower selection intensity on *V. tubiflora*-designated terminal branches relative to *V. peripherica*-designated branches. Against this conserved background, chromosome 1 contained a localized copy-number difference consistent with a lineage-specific *OSCA2.4* tandem duplication in *V. peripherica*. These findings support broad chromosomal and sequence conservation accompanied by localized evolutionary differences between the two congeners.

The organ transcriptomes, predicted functional-association networks, and promoter-motif annotations establish a comparative baseline and identify candidates for experimental characterization. *OSCA1.2* showed concordant root- and stem-associated transcript abundance and predicted associations with carbohydrate-catabolizing proteins, whereas the *OSCA2.4* locus provides a model for testing the consequences of tandem duplication. Vegetative desiccation tolerance in *V. tubiflora* therefore coexists with a broadly conserved OSCA framework. Whether the localized genomic and regulatory differences contribute to contrasting water-deficit strategies remains to be tested through expanded taxonomic sampling, controlled dehydration-rehydration expression series, promoter assays, and channel physiology.

![](figures/Figure_3_OSCA_Evolutionary_Rates_and_Selection.png)

**Figure 3. Selection-intensity model and pairwise evolutionary rates.** (A) RELAX estimates of the selection-intensity parameter *K* for the full-family OSCA gene tree and exploratory clade-specific datasets, with *V. peripherica* terminal branches designated as Reference and *V. tubiflora* terminal branches as Test. The full-family fit was significant (*K* = 0.7064, *p* = 0.0003), whereas the OSCA1 (*K* = 0.9262, *p* = 0.6080), OSCA2 (*K* = 0.8191, *p* = 0.3446), and corrected OSCA3-only (*K* ≈ 0.26, *p* ≈ 0.075) fits were not significant. OSCA4 was excluded from the OSCA3 analysis and was not fitted separately. Under this model, *K* < 1 indicates contraction of the fitted ω distribution toward neutrality on Test relative to Reference branches, but only a significant likelihood-ratio test supports a difference in selection intensity. (B) Pairwise whole-gene coding-sequence divergence ratios (ω = *K*~a~/*K*~s~) for 12 one-to-one ortholog pairs, two *OSCA2.4* co-ortholog comparisons, and the *VpOSCA2.4a*-*VpOSCA2.4b* tandem paralogs calculated via YN00. All point estimates were below unity. Across the 14 interspecific comparisons, the median was 0.3368 and the range was 0.0530-0.8015; the tandem-paralog estimate was 0.3770. This pattern is consistent with predominantly purifying selection.

## SUPPLEMENTARY MATERIAL

Supplementary tables accompanying this chapter are provided in the companion workbook `Supplementary_Tables_OSCA.xlsx`:

**Table S1. Curated genomic coordinates, predicted protein physicochemical descriptors, and membrane topology of the OSCA mechanosensitive ion channel family in *Vellozia peripherica*, *Vellozia tubiflora*, and *Xerophyta schlechteri*.** Structural exon-intron boundaries and 1-based coordinates derived from chromosome-level haplotype-1 annotations. Molecular weights (MW) and theoretical isoelectric points (pI) were computed using Biopython. Canonical functional domains (PF02714, PF14703, PF13967) and PANTHER PTHR13018 signatures were verified using InterProScan, and transmembrane helices were modeled independently using TMHMM and Phobius.

**Table S2. Evaluation of selection intensity across species-designated terminal branches of the full-family OSCA gene tree using HyPhy RELAX.** Selection constraint was tested across 27 validated *Vellozia* OSCA loci (639 filtered codon sites) with *V. tubiflora* terminal branches designated as Test and *V. peripherica* branches as Reference under the MG94xREV codon substitution model. Sensitivity fits report 10 random starting points and alternative gap filtering (>50% removed; 735 codons), alongside exploratory clade-specific fits for OSCA1, OSCA2, and OSCA3.

**Table S3. Pairwise nonsynonymous and synonymous substitution rate estimates ($d_N$, $d_S$, and $\omega$) across 15 focal OSCA channel comparisons in *Vellozia*.** Pairwise codon alignments were evaluated under the Yang and Nielsen (2000) maximum-likelihood model in PAML yn00 for 12 one-to-one ortholog pairs, the two *VtOSCA2.4* co-ortholog comparisons, and the intraspecific tandem duplicate pair *VpOSCA2.4a* versus *VpOSCA2.4b*. All comparisons exhibit $\omega < 1.00$, confirming pervasive purifying selection across the channel family.

**Table S4. Vegetative organ transcript abundance, pairwise DESeq2 differential contrasts, and STRING topological metrics for the *Vellozia* OSCA family.** DESeq2 normalized counts and differential expression statistics across leaf, stem, and root tissues in adult field plants ($n = 3$ biological replicates; blocking by individual). STRING network topological metrics (degree, local clustering coefficients) were computed from predicted functional-association networks at medium confidence (combined score $\ge 0.400$).

**Table S5. Gene Ontology and KEGG pathway functional enrichment of predicted OSCA functional-association networks in *Vellozia peripherica* and *Vellozia tubiflora*.** Functional enrichment statistics retrieved via the STRING v12 API across Biological Process, Molecular Function, Cellular Component, and KEGG metabolic pathways for terms with false discovery rate (FDR) $< 0.05$ in at least one species, highlighting predominant enrichment in carbohydrate catabolism and ion transport.

**Table S6. Functional classification, consensus sequences, and responsive annotations of curated *cis*-regulatory elements in OSCA promoters.** Systematic classification framework detailing curated motif identifiers, IUPAC consensus sequences, functional response classes (Phytohormone Signaling, Abiotic Stress Response, Growth & Development, Light Responsiveness, and Core Promoter Elements), predicted biological responsiveness, and primary literature citations.

**Table S7. Curated non-redundant *cis*-acting regulatory element occurrence counts across 2.0-kb upstream promoters of *Vellozia* OSCA genes.** Motif counts across 2,000-bp regions upstream of transcription start sites from PlantCARE, normalized for case variants and merged across overlapping coordinate intervals of identical motif classes to eliminate palindromic and nested inflation.
