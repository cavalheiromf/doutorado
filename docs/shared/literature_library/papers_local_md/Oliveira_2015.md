# Mineral nutrition of campos rupestres plant species on contrasting nutrient-impoverished soil types

**Authors:** Rafael S. Oliveira, Hugo C. Galvão, Mariana C. R. de Campos, Caroline B. Eller, Sandy J. Pearse, Hans Lambers  
**Year:** 2015  
**Journal:** *New Phytologist*, 205(3), 1183–1194  
**DOI:** 10.1111/nph.13175  

## Abstract

Old, climatically buffered, infertile landscapes (OCBILs) in the tropics harbor high plant diversity and endemism, but the physiological mechanisms underlying plant survival on these severely nutrient-impoverished substrates remain poorly understood. We investigated soil chemical properties and foliar nutrient concentrations of 50 plant species occurring on contrasting soil types (quartzite-derived sandy soils and ironstone canga) in the hyper-diverse *campos rupestres* of the Espinhaço Range, south-eastern Brazil. 

Available soil phosphorus, measured by anion-exchange resin extraction, was exceptionally low across all sampled quartzitic sites, frequently falling below 1.0 mg P kg⁻¹ (typically 0.2–0.8 mg P kg⁻¹ resin P in pure quartzitic sands and rocky outcrops). This extreme phosphorus impoverishment acts as an intense evolutionary filter, favoring specialized non-mycorrhizal nutrient-acquisition strategies, including carboxylate-exuding and sand-binding root adaptations. Foliar N:P ratios revealed severe community-wide phosphorus limitation, demonstrating that prolonged geological weathering has driven unique ecophysiological specializations to acquire rock-bound nutrients in this ancient montane archipelago.
