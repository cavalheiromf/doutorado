                                                  pose a unified conceptual framework of plant adaptive strategies to drought
                                                  based on a revised terminology in order to enhance comparative studies.
                                                  Ecological strategies encapsulate plant adaptation to multidimensional variation in
                                                  resource variability but cannot account for the dynamic and short-term responses
                                                  to fluctuations in water availability. Conversely, several plant physiological strate-
                                                  gies have been identified along the mono-dimensional gradient of water availabil-
                                                  ity in a given environment. According to a revised terminology, dehydration
                                                  escape, dehydration avoidance, dehydration tolerance, dormancy, and desiccation
                                                  tolerance are clearly distinguishable. Their sequential expression is expressed as
                                                  water deficit increases while cavitation tolerance is proposed here to be a major
                                                  hydraulic strategy underpinning adaptive responses to drought of vascular plants.
                                                  This continuum of physiological strategies can be interpreted in the context of
                                                  the ecological trade-off between water-acquisition vs. water-conservation, since
                                                  growth maintenance is associated with fast water use under moderate drought
                                                  while plant survival after growth cessation is associated with slow water use
                                                  under severe drought. Consequently, the distinction between ‘drought resistance’
                                                  and ‘drought survival’, is emphasized as crucial to ensure a correct interpretation
                                                  of plant strategies since ‘knowing when not to grow’ does not confer ‘drought
                                                  resistance’ but may well enhance ‘drought survival’. This framework proposal
                                                  should improve cross-fertilization between disciplines to help tackle the increasing
                                                  worldwide challenges that drought poses to plant adaptation.

       BOX 1      GLOSSARY

             Anisohydry, low stomatal regulation enabling plants to tolerate rapid and extensive fluctuations in water potential
             Cavitation, xylem conduits becoming air-filled or embolized under negative pressure, that can no longer transport water in the
       plant (= hydraulic failure)
             Dehydration avoidance (= dehydration postponement), strategy allowing plants to maximize water uptake and/or minimize water
       loss to maintain hydration in leaves
             Dehydration escape, strategy allowing plants to complete or shorten the life (or reproductive) cycle before the onset of drought
             Dehydration tolerance, strategy allowing plants to tolerate low tissue level of dehydration (>30% water)
             Desiccation tolerance, strategy allowing plants to tolerate leaf moisture content equal to ambient atmospheric moisture content
       and revive upon rehydration after long periods
             Dormancy, endogenous controlled strategy that reduces or stops meristem activity to render it insensitive to growth promoting
       signals in order to enhance plant survival during seasons when environmental conditions are most life threatening
             Disturbance, environmental factor resulting in partial or total destruction of plant biomass
             Drought resistance, maintenance of leaf growth and plant productivity in response to what can be then defined as a ‘moderate
       drought’, i.e. resulting in the maintenance of growth for the considered species and population, and the maintenance of productivity
       notably for crops
             Drought survival, plant ability to survive after growth cessation due to a severe drought (for the considered species and popula-
       tion) or a disturbance caused by an extreme and prolonged drought
             Isohydry, plant water status maintained at a steady high state by strong stomatal regulation under water deficit
             Plant economics spectrum, strong relationships between multiple functional plant traits that determine resource fluxes in vascular
       plants
             Strategy, grouping of traits and responses among species and populations which cause them to exhibit ecological similarities such
       as a particular behavior during periods of water stress
             Stress, environmental factor restricting plant functioning
             Survival, at population scale, component of demographic processes of a population
             Trait, (= characteristic) any morphological, physiological or phenological measurement on individuals at any level (cellular to whole
       plant)
             Water stress, alteration of plant physiological functions underlying fitness (e.g. plant growth) away from a reference state (plant
