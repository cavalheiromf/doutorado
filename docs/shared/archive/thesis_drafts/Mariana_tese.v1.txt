Genomic Architecture, Evolutionary Dynamics, and Tissue-Specific Expression of OSCA Calcium-Permeable Channels in Two Vellozia Species with Contrasting Drought Response Strategies

Advisor: Prof. Dr. Paulo Arruda

ABSTRACT
The Brazilian campos rupestres, a biodiversity hotspot characterized by prolonged seasonal droughts, harbor plant species that have evolved remarkably distinct strategies to cope with water scarcity. Among the Velloziaceae, Vellozia tubiflora tolerates complete desiccation by entering anhydrobiosis, whereas Vellozia peripherica maintains evergreen foliage throughout extended dry periods. Whether these contrasting drought response strategies are reflected in differences at the level of osmotic perception remains an open question. To address this, we performed a comprehensive genome-wide characterization of the OSCA (Hyperosmolality-Gated Calcium-Permeable Channel) gene family in both species, leveraging chromosome-level genome assemblies. The analysis encompassed gene identification, phylogenetic reconstruction, comparative synteny, molecular evolutionary rate estimation, cis-regulatory element profiling, tissue-specific differential expression analysis, and protein-protein interaction network topology. We identified 14 OSCA genes in V. peripherica and 13 in V. tubiflora, distributed across four conserved clades (OSCA1-4), with a lineage-specific tandem duplication of OSCA2.4 exclusive to V. peripherica. Comparative synteny analysis revealed extraordinary chromosomal collinearity between the two genomes, including a perfectly conserved tandem cluster of OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 on chromosome 7A and a shared ancestral segmental duplication linking chromosomes 1A and 5A. Strikingly, clade-specific evolutionary selection analysis using HyPhy RELAX demonstrated that the OSCA1 subfamily is under identical and strict purifying selection in both species (K = 0.92, p = 0.60), whereas the OSCA2 and OSCA3+4 subfamilies exhibit highly significant selection relaxation in V. tubiflora (K = 0.28, p = 3.4 x 10-5 and K = 0.13, p = 0.006, respectively). Contrast-FEL analysis further revealed that all 14 divergent sites in the OSCA2 clade are selectively accelerated in V. peripherica, indicating active diversifying selection in the evergreen species. Differential expression analysis using a paired experimental design confirmed conserved tissue-specific expression profiles, with OSCA1.2 accumulating preferentially in root and stem tissues and OSCA1.1 and OSCA3.1 enriched in leaves. Protein-protein interaction network reconstruction positioned OSCA1.2 and OSCA3.1 as central hubs connected to H+-ATPases, voltage-dependent calcium channels, and cell wall integrity proteins. Our findings reveal a dual-track evolutionary model in which basal osmosensory perception (OSCA1) is invariantly conserved between the two species, while the modulatory periphery of the perceptive apparatus (OSCA2 and OSCA3+4) has diverged profoundly, reflecting the functional dichotomy between continuous active water monitoring in the evergreen species and metabolic shutdown during anhydrobiosis in the resurrection plant.
Keywords: OSCA gene family; mechanosensitive channels; calcium signaling; desiccation tolerance; Velloziaceae; comparative genomics; molecular evolution; campos rupestres.

1. INTRODUCTION
Although the molecular mechanisms underlying drought response in plants have been extensively characterized, the precise nature of the signals that enable plants to detect changes in water availability remains incompletely understood (Lamers et al., 2020). According to a recent review by Gorgues et al. (2022), three principal classes of perception mechanisms have been proposed to detect osmotic signals: membrane sensors that respond to local osmotic imbalances, cell wall integrity disruption sensors, and membrane tension-activated channels.
Regarding membrane sensors in plants, histidine kinases such as AtHK1 have been implicated in salt stress, drought, and abscisic acid (ABA) signaling pathways (Tran et al., 2007). The AtHK1 kinase functions as a positive regulator of stress conditions by phosphorylating histidine residues that control osmotic-responsive gene expression (Osakabe et al., 2013). However, the role of AtHK1 as the primary osmosensor in plants has been questioned, as loss-of-function alleles do not exhibit hyperosmotic signaling-related phenotypes (Kumar et al., 2013).
Another mechanism related to stress perception involves disruptions to cell wall integrity. Transmembrane receptor-like kinases (RLKs), including Catharanthus roseus RLK1-Like kinases (CrRLK), Wall-Associated Kinases (WAK), Lectin Receptor-Kinases (LRK), Proline-rich Extensin-like Receptor Kinases (PERKs), and formins, function as sensors of cell wall status (Wolf et al., 2012). Several kinases, such as THESEUS (THE) CrRLK, exhibit dual roles in maintaining cellular mechanics and regulating abscisic acid accumulation in response to osmotic stress (Bacete et al., 2022). FERONIA (FER), another CrRLK member, induces a late calcium influx under salt stress that is required for cell wall reinforcement (Feng et al., 2018). Notably, FER loss-of-function experiments demonstrated that the absence of FER increases salt sensitivity while decreasing osmotic stress responsiveness, and it has been proposed that FER detects the influence of sodium ions on pectin filament organization rather than changes in turgor pressure per se (Chen et al., 2016; Feng et al., 2018).
A third class of sensors capable of detecting osmotic stress in plants comprises mechanosensitive ion channels, particularly those responsible for Ca2+ influx (Basu and Haswell, 2017; Hartmann et al., 2021; Kurusu et al., 2013). Calcium cations are widely implicated as rapid secondary messengers in diverse plant responses (White, 2003). Plants exhibit a rapid elevation in the concentration of cytosolic Ca2+ ions upon exposure to mechanical stimuli such as wind, touch, or osmotic changes (Hartmann et al., 2021; Ketehouli et al., 2022). This phenomenon produces a localized response while also potentially inducing distant responses through the propagation of electrical signals (Gorgues et al., 2022). The MscS-like (MSL) proteins represent one example of mechanosensitive calcium channels. Specifically, MSL10 encodes a true mechanosensitive channel that modulates cytoplasmic calcium increases, reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, transcriptional regulation, and programmed cell death in response to hypo-osmotic shock, exhibiting a preference for anions rather than cations (Maksaev and Haswell, 2012). Other mechanosensitive channels, including MCA1 and its paralog MCA2, have been characterized in Arabidopsis (Nakagawa et al., 2007). However, mca1 and mca2 mutants exhibited no alteration in calcium influx in response to osmotic stimulation, suggesting that these channels may not play a significant role in osmosensing (Yamanaka et al., 2010). Piezo channels represent yet another class of mechanosensory proteins, although their functions in plants remain less characterized compared to other mechanosensitive channel families (Kaur et al., 2023).
Recent investigations of the hyperosmolality-gated calcium-permeable channel (OSCA) gene family have shed considerable light on the critical role these proteins play in a plant's ability to detect osmolality changes, including those induced by drought (Cao et al., 2020; Yuan et al., 2014; Zhai et al., 2020, 2021). OSCA channels are mechanosensitive ion channels distributed across four phylogenetic clades and generally consist of two transmembrane domains and one cytosolic domain, with one of the transmembrane domains being permeable to calcium ions (Hou et al., 2014; Yuan et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2018). These channels are activated by osmolality-induced variations in membrane pressure, resulting in an increase in cytosolic free calcium concentration (Yuan et al., 2014). This calcium increase induces numerous physiological and biochemical responses in plants.
Despite the comprehensive characterization of the OSCA gene family in numerous plant species, the complete elucidation of functional attributes related to osmotic stress, particularly in the context of drought response, remains limited to a small number of family members (Ding et al., 2019; Ke et al., 2022; Li et al., 2015; Liu et al., 2022; Miao et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022, 2019; Yin et al., 2021). The overexpression of ZmOSCA2.4 in Arabidopsis conferred enhanced drought tolerance, whereas other members appear to play roles in salt and ABA response pathways. In rice, Li et al. (2015) demonstrated that the OSCA gene family responds differentially to drought stress, with some members being upregulated and others downregulated. These findings indicate that OSCA genes are involved in osmotic-related signaling pathways and play crucial roles in rice responses to diverse abiotic stresses, including drought (Li et al., 2015).
Therefore, investigating OSCA channels, their function in perception, and their key molecular interactors represents a promising avenue for developing strategies to improve crop performance under water deficit conditions. Additional research is required to comprehend fully the molecular mechanisms of OSCA channels and their regulation in response to drought stress.
Our research group has focused on the physiological and molecular responses to drought in endemic Brazilian plant species found in campos rupestres, an ecoregion characterized by stony outcrops and a distinct water regime (Oliveira et al., 2016). Two species uniquely adapted to this environment, Vellozia peripherica and Vellozia tubiflora, are members of the family Velloziaceae and are exclusive to campos rupestres habitats (Alcantara et al., 2015; Alcantara et al., 2018). The most distinguishing characteristic between these two species lies in their contrasting drought response phenotypes. V. tubiflora is desiccation-tolerant and completely desiccates its leaves and root system when exposed to severe water deficit, entering a state of anhydrobiosis from which it can recover upon rehydration. In contrast, V. peripherica is an evergreen species that demonstrates a survival strategy based on maintaining a high relative water content and sustaining photosynthetic capacity during periods of water scarcity (Alcantara et al., 2015; Alcantara et al., 2018).
Due to these adaptations to environments with limited water resources, these Vellozia species constitute an ideal model for studying the perception and response of plants to drought at the molecular level. The investigation of OSCA ion channel molecular processes and their interactions with signaling proteins may shed insight on the perception strategies employed by V. peripherica and V. tubiflora in response to changes in water availability. In this regard, the genome-wide analysis of the OSCA gene family represents the initial step in identifying top candidate genes that regulate water stress perception.
Here, we present a comprehensive comparative genomic study of the OSCA gene family in V. peripherica and V. tubiflora, encompassing their identification and chromosomal distribution, phylogenetic relationships with other angiosperm species, comparative synteny analysis including ancestral and lineage-specific duplications, molecular evolutionary rates and clade-specific selection pressures, cis-regulatory element composition, tissue-specific expression profiling, and protein-protein interaction network topology. By integrating these multiple layers of evidence, this research seeks to determine whether species that differ in their drought response strategy also differ in the molecular architecture and evolutionary trajectory of their primary osmosensory apparatus.

2. MATERIAL AND METHODS
2.1 Identification of OSCA gene family members
To identify OSCA proteins in the genomes of V. peripherica and V. tubiflora, we used the conserved PFAM accession numbers PF02714 (RSN1_7TM), PF13967 (RSN1 TM), and PF14703 (PHM7 cyt) from the Pfam database to construct HMM profiles using the "hmmbuild" function and queried the protein databases of both species using "hmmsearch" from the HMMER suite. Subsequently, every non-redundant sequence was evaluated using the InterProScan database. In occurrences of alternative splicing, the longest transcript was selected to represent the predicted protein. Both species had chromosome-level genome assemblies (hapA haplotype) comprising 9 chromosomes each.
2.2 Sequence alignment and phylogenetic analysis
Full-length amino acid sequences of all OSCA proteins from V. peripherica, V. tubiflora, Arabidopsis thaliana, Sorghum bicolor, Oryza sativa, and Zea mays were aligned using the MAFFT tool v.7 (Katoh et al., 2018) with default parameters. The alignment files were analyzed using IQ-Tree v.1.6.11 (Nguyen et al., 2015), which determined the appropriate amino acid substitution model and constructed a phylogenetic tree using a Maximum Likelihood approach with 1,000 bootstrap replications. The phylogenetic tree was visualized using iTOL (Letunic and Bork, 2021).
2.3 Chromosome location, interspecies synteny analysis, and intragenomic duplications
The chromosomal positions of the identified OSCA genes were obtained from the GFF annotation files of each species. To analyze collinear blocks between V. peripherica and V. tubiflora, we employed the MCScan Python version (https://github.com/tanghaibao/jcvi/) with default parameters. The macrosynteny dot-plot and microsynteny views were generated using available MCScan/JCVI scripts. Self-synteny analysis was performed independently for each genome to identify intragenomic duplication events, including ancestral segmental duplications and lineage-specific tandem expansions.
2.4 Evolutionary rate estimation and selective pressure analysis
The ratio of nonsynonymous (Ka) to synonymous (Ks) substitutions in OSCA coding sequences was computed for all orthologous pairs. Codon alignment was performed using PAL2NAL (Suyama et al., 2006), followed by determination of the Ka/Ks ratio through PAML (Yang, 2007).
To dissect subfamily-specific evolutionary pressures, we implemented a clade-specific selection analysis pipeline. The OSCA family was partitioned into three evolutionary subclades: OSCA1 (10 genes), OSCA2 (11 genes), and OSCA3+4 (6 genes). For each clade, localized protein alignments were generated using MUSCLE, codon-level alignments were threaded to maintain strict triplet alignment, and the global Maximum Likelihood tree was mathematically pruned using Biopython to preserve topological relationships and branch lengths. This approach eliminated global alignment artifacts arising from gaps in hypervariable loops.
Selection relaxation was assessed using HyPhy RELAX (Wertheim et al., 2015), with V. tubiflora designated as the Test lineage and V. peripherica as the Reference lineage. Site-specific evolutionary divergence was evaluated using HyPhy Contrast-FEL, which identifies individual codon sites evolving at different rates between test and reference branches. Sites with high gap ratios (>20%) in the global alignment were identified and eliminated as potential false positives through the clade-specific pipeline.
2.5 Gene promoter region analysis
Gene promoter regions (2 kb upstream of the translation start site) were extracted from the annotation files of both species using BEDTools v.2.30.0 (Quinlan and Hall, 2010) with the "flank" parameter to obtain coordinates and "getfasta" to extract sequences. The promoter sequences were analyzed using the PlantCARE database (Lescot et al., 2002). Overlapping elements of the same kind were merged to avoid multiple counting. The identified elements were classified according to their functions into categories including "Light responsive", "Growth and development", "Anaerobic responsive", "Auxin responsive", "Gibberellin responsive", "Wounding/pathogen responsive", "Cold responsive", "Ethylene responsive", "Stress responsive", "ABA responsive", "Drought responsive", "Jasmonic acid responsive", and "Salicylic acid responsive" (Qu et al., 2019).
2.6 Experimental design and RNA-seq analysis
Tissue-specific gene expression was assessed from three individuals of each species. Three tissue types (leaf, stem, and root) were sampled from each individual, yielding 9 samples for V. peripherica (3 individuals x 3 tissues) and 8 samples for V. tubiflora (3 individuals x 3 tissues, with one root sample excluded due to quality control). All tissue samples were immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 degrees C for subsequent RNA extraction.
Total RNA was extracted using a CTAB protocol (Chang et al., 1993). RNA sequencing libraries were generated and sequenced on an Illumina platform, yielding paired-end reads. The reads were aligned to the annotated genome of their respective species using the STAR aligner v.2.7.10 (Dobin et al., 2013), following ENCODE guidelines for best practices in eukaryotic RNA sequencing (Hitz et al., 2016), including the additional "--quantMode GeneCounts" quantification option.
2.7 Differential expression analysis
Differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2 (Love et al., 2014) with a paired experimental design (formula: ~ Individual + Tissue) to control for inter-individual variability. Three pairwise tissue contrasts were evaluated for each species: Root vs. Leaf, Stem vs. Leaf, and Root vs. Stem. Genes with adjusted p-values (Benjamini-Hochberg) less than 0.05 were considered significantly differentially expressed.
2.8 Protein-protein interaction network analysis
The proteomes of V. peripherica and V. tubiflora were annotated using STRINGdb v.11.5 (Szklarczyk et al., 2021). Using the STRINGdb API, we retrieved interaction data and constructed comprehensive protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks for each species, with emphasis on interactions involving OSCA proteins. Interactions were transformed into undirected graphs using NetworkX, with proteins as nodes and known interactions as edges. Only interactions with a confidence score of at least 0.4 were retained for analysis. For enrichment analysis, biological process terms with FDR < 0.05 appearing in at least 10% of the input dataset were considered significant.

3. RESULTS
3.1 The OSCA gene family is numerically conserved in V. peripherica and V. tubiflora
We searched the V. peripherica (Vper) and V. tubiflora (Vtub) protein datasets for the PF02714 domain, which corresponds to the formation of calcium-permeable channels (Maity et al., 2019). Our analysis identified 14 genes in Vper and 13 genes in Vtub encoding OSCA proteins. These were grouped into four distinct clades, consistent with previous observations in other plant species (Ding et al., 2019; Li et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2019; Yin et al., 2021; Yuan et al., 2014). The number of OSCA family members in both Vellozia species is comparable to that observed in Arabidopsis thaliana (15 genes), suggesting that the family has remained evolutionarily stable without undergoing major expansions following the whole-genome duplication (WGD) events documented for the genus. Notably, the desiccation tolerance strategy exhibited by V. tubiflora did not result in the elimination of OSCA genes, as the resurrection plant retained a nearly identical complement of osmosensory channels as the evergreen species.
3.2 Phylogenetic analysis reveals conserved orthology with a lineage-specific duplication in V. peripherica
Phylogenetic reconstruction using Maximum Likelihood analysis of OSCA protein sequences from V. peripherica, V. tubiflora, A. thaliana, O. sativa, S. bicolor, and Z. mays confirmed the classification of all identified genes into four well-supported clades: OSCA1, OSCA2, OSCA3, and OSCA4. Clade 2 was the most diverse, containing 6 genes in V. peripherica and 5 in V. tubiflora. Orthology between the two Vellozia species was nearly perfect across all clades, with 1:1 orthologous pairs for almost every locus.
A critical exception was identified within Clade 2: V. peripherica possesses a lineage-specific duplication yielding two adjacent copies, VpOSCA2.4a and VpOSCA2.4b, whereas V. tubiflora retains only a single copy, VtOSCA2.4. This observation indicates that the evergreen species, which continuously monitors water availability on a daily and seasonal basis, invested in expanding its repertoire of a specific osmosensory channel. The nomenclature strategy adopted in this work was designed to emphasize the genetic correspondence between the two species under study, with letter notations assigned to genes that arose from duplications occurring after speciation.
3.3 Comparative synteny reveals extraordinary chromosomal conservation with targeted divergence
3.3.1 Macrosynteny and global collinearity
Genome-wide comparative synteny analysis between V. peripherica and V. tubiflora revealed an exceptionally high degree of macro-collinearity across their nine chromosomes, with a total of 297 syntenic blocks identified. The analysis demonstrated a conserved, one-to-one chromosome-level architectural relationship with no major inter-chromosomal translocations since the evolutionary divergence of the two species.
3.3.2 Microsynteny of the OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 tandem cluster
At the microsynteny level, a perfectly conserved syntenic block was identified on chromosome 7A containing a tandem duplication cluster of OSCA1.3, OSCA1.4, and OSCA1.5. This cluster exhibits perfect gene order conservation and physical contiguity with a gap of zero intermediate genes in both species. The local region is flanked by 24 highly syntenic orthologous gene pairs, confirming robust structural preservation. This perfect micro-collinearity indicates that the tandem expansion event predated the speciation of V. peripherica and V. tubiflora, and the cluster has been strictly maintained under strong purifying selection, potentially due to its crucial role in environmental sensing and stress adaptation.
3.3.3 Ancestral paleoduplication and lineage-specific tandem expansion
Self-synteny analysis revealed two major intragenomic duplication events. First, an ancient large-scale segmental duplication block connecting Chromosome 1A and Chromosome 5A was identified in both genomes, simultaneously harboring duplicated members of the OSCA2 subfamily (OSCA2.1 and OSCA2.2) and the OSCA3 subfamily (OSCA3.1 and OSCA3.2). This conserved paleoduplication confirms that these subfamilies were expanded through an ancestral whole-genome duplication event prior to the divergence of V. peripherica and V. tubiflora.
Second, a lineage-specific evolutionary divergence was detected on Chromosome 1A. V. peripherica possesses an adjacent tandem duplication of the OSCA2.4 gene, yielding two consecutive copies (VpOSCA2.4a and VpOSCA2.4b) with a gap of zero intermediate genes. In contrast, V. tubiflora retains only a single copy (VtOSCA2.4). This represents a recent localized expansion event in V. peripherica, indicating active reshaping of the osmosensory apparatus in the evergreen species.
3.4 Evolutionary rate analysis reveals a dual-track selection model
3.4.1 Global Ka/Ks analysis
Estimation of molecular evolutionary rates based on Ka/Ks ratios for 14 orthologous pairs revealed that all OSCA genes are under strong purifying selection (Ka/Ks < 1, with a mean of approximately 0.35). The OSCA2.3 pair exhibited the most extreme purifying selection in the family (Ka/Ks = 0.053), indicating an extraordinarily severe functional constraint preserving the primary sequence of this channel. Loci such as OSCA1.1 and OSCA3.1 also displayed strong purifying signatures (Ka/Ks = 0.33 and 0.34, respectively). In contrast, the OSCA1.4 pair exhibited an accelerated evolutionary rate (Ka/Ks = 0.80), suggesting potential divergence or evolutionary relaxation after speciation.
3.4.2 Clade-specific selection analysis (HyPhy RELAX)
To dissect whether the selective forces acting on OSCA channels differ between drought response strategies, we implemented a clade-specific evolutionary pipeline. The results revealed that evolutionary selection pressures are highly heterogeneous across OSCA subfamilies, demonstrating a striking dual-track pattern.
The OSCA1 subfamily (10 genes) showed no significant change in selection intensity between the two species (K = 0.92, p = 0.60). This demonstrates that the OSCA1 subfamily, which houses the physically contiguous tandem cluster OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5, is under severe and identical purifying constraints in both the resurrection and evergreen species, preserving baseline osmosensing function.
In striking contrast, the OSCA2 subfamily (11 genes) exhibited highly significant and drastic selection relaxation in V. tubiflora (K = 0.28, p = 3.4 x 10-5). Similarly, the OSCA3+4 subfamily (6 genes) showed extremely significant selection relaxation in V. tubiflora (K = 0.13, p = 0.006). Because V. tubiflora survives desiccation by entering an inactive metabolic state (anhydrobiosis), functional pressure on OSCA2 and OSCA3+4 channels is suspended during dormancy, allowing these subfamilies to accumulate genetic variation, while core baseline osmosensing (OSCA1) remains strictly protected.
3.4.3 Site-specific evolutionary divergence (Contrast-FEL)
Clade-specific partitioning and gap sanitization exposed and eliminated 5 false-positive divergent sites that contained high gap ratios (22% to 34%) in the global alignment. The remaining significant sites were mapped with high precision.
In the OSCA1 clade, 6 significant sites were identified (p <= 0.05), with 3 sites exhibiting elevated non-synonymous rates in V. peripherica and 2 in V. tubiflora, representing balanced, localized adaptive adjustments in both species.
In the OSCA2 clade, 14 significant sites were detected, and strikingly, all 14 sites exhibited extreme selective acceleration exclusively in V. peripherica (Beta-Ref >> 0, Beta-Test approximately 0). This pattern indicates active adaptive remodeling of OSCA2 channels in the evergreen species, potentially to fine-tune channel properties for continuous daily osmotic monitoring.
In the OSCA3+4 clade, one highly significant site was revealed at local position 593 (Beta-Test = 24.72, Beta-Ref = 0.018, p = 0.009), representing a robust evolutionary relaxation hotspot in V. tubiflora. This site may represent an adaptation specific to the resurrection phenotype, potentially facilitating rapid channel reactivation during rehydration.
3.5 Promoter regions are equivalently equipped with stress-responsive cis-regulatory elements
Analysis of the 2 kb upstream promoter regions of all OSCA genes in both species revealed a rich and diversified repertoire of cis-regulatory elements. A notable abundance of ABRE (abscisic acid-responsive), MBS (MYB-binding site involved in drought induction), LTR (low-temperature-responsive), and TC-rich repeat (defense and stress-responsive) motifs was observed across both species.
Furthermore, the diversified presence of light-responsive elements (such as G-box, Box 4, MRE) and oxidative stress-responsive elements (ARE) suggests that the transcriptional regulation of OSCA channels is intrinsically connected to the signaling networks coordinating adaptation to extreme drought, high solar irradiance, and thermal oscillations characteristic of the campos rupestres.
The regulatory potential for activating OSCA channels in response to abiotic stress appears to be equivalent in both species. This finding suggests that the difference between the two species does not reside in the capacity to activate transcription of OSCA genes, but rather in the protein that is produced when transcription is activated, as those proteins have diverged selectively in the OSCA2, OSCA3, and OSCA4 subfamilies.
3.6 Tissue-specific expression profiles are conserved between species
Differential expression analysis using a paired experimental design (controlling for individual plant variance) revealed a striking signature of evolutionary conservation and tissue-specific functional specialization within the OSCA gene family across both species.
OSCA1.2 exhibited a dramatic and highly conserved preference for root and stem tissues, with significant transcriptional accumulation compared to leaves (Log2FC = 4.40 in V. peripherica and Log2FC = 8.64 in V. tubiflora; padj < 0.05). This pronounced subterranean and vascular enrichment suggests a key ancestral role for these channels in osmotic perception and mechanosensation within transport and anchorage tissues.
Conversely, OSCA1.1 and OSCA3.1 displayed a robust and consistent leaf-enriched expression profile in both genomes. Notably, VtOSCA1.1 exhibited significant enrichment in leaves relative to stems (Log2FC = -2.07, padj = 1.2 x 10-6), indicating functional specialization geared toward the aerial photosynthetic tissues, likely involved in turgor regulation and stomatal signaling.
These divergent expression profiles among distinct OSCA family members, which remain remarkably conserved between the two species post-speciation, indicate that both species position the same sensors in the same tissues. OSCA1.2 monitors subterranean and vascular osmolarity; OSCA1.1 and OSCA3.1 monitor leaf turgor and stomatal aperture. The spatial deployment of the osmosensory apparatus is identical in both drought response strategies.
3.7 Protein-protein interaction networks reveal conserved hub topology
Protein-protein interaction network reconstruction using integrated STRINGdb data and topological analysis with NetworkX revealed the strategic positioning of the OSCA family within primary signal transduction and mechanosensory cascades. OSCA proteins exhibited strong neighborhood connectivity and functional co-expression with essential ion transport components, including H+-ATPases, voltage-dependent calcium channels, and cell wall proteins involved in mechanical stress signaling.
OSCA1.2 and OSCA3.1 emerged as central hubs in the PPI networks of both species, with connectivity values exceeding four times the average network degree. These hubs connect membrane mechanosensation to downstream biochemical signaling cascades, coupling physical plasma membrane deformation and turgor pressure changes to immediate calcium-mediated signal transduction.
Functional enrichment analyses confirmed a significant overrepresentation of biological processes associated with cytosolic calcium homeostasis, responses to osmotic stress, and cellular volume regulation in both species networks. The conservation of interaction partners and network topology between V. peripherica and V. tubiflora indicates that the molecular context in which OSCA channels operate is fundamentally the same, regardless of the species' drought response strategy.

4. DISCUSSION
4.1 Stability versus plasticity in the OSCA gene family
The OSCA gene family in Vellozia is numerically conserved and chromosomically stable, with 14 genes in V. peripherica and 13 in V. tubiflora, comparable to the 15 genes found in Arabidopsis thaliana (Yuan et al., 2014). This conservation persists despite the documented history of whole-genome duplication events in the Velloziaceae lineage (Alcantara et al., 2018). The retention of a nearly complete OSCA complement in V. tubiflora, despite its adoption of an extreme desiccation tolerance strategy, demonstrates that the transition to anhydrobiosis did not require the loss of osmosensory genes. Rather, the evolutionary modification of the osmoperceptive system occurred through changes in selective pressures acting on specific subfamilies, rather than through wholesale gene gain or loss.
4.2 A dual-track evolutionary model for osmotic perception
The most significant finding of this study is the demonstration of a dual-track evolutionary model operating within the OSCA gene family. The OSCA1 subfamily functions as the conserved track, maintaining identical and strict purifying selection in both species (K = 0.92, p = 0.60). This subfamily encompasses the perfectly conserved tandem cluster OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 on Chromosome 7A, which exhibits zero-gap physical contiguity and is flanked by 24 syntenic orthologs. The preservation of this cluster under stringent purifying selection, regardless of the drought response phenotype, indicates that these channels fulfill a fundamental, non-negotiable osmosensory function, analogous to a constitutive smoke detector that must function identically in both species.
In contrast, the OSCA2 and OSCA3+4 subfamilies represent the divergent track, with massive selection relaxation in V. tubiflora (K = 0.28 and K = 0.13, respectively). This divergence is functionally coherent with the physiological strategies of each species. The evergreen V. peripherica, which must continuously monitor osmotic gradients day after day and season after season without ever ceasing metabolic activity, is actively remodeling its OSCA2 channels, as evidenced by 14 Contrast-FEL sites all exhibiting selective acceleration in this species. This diversifying selection likely reflects the need for fine-tuned channel kinetics to discriminate subtle and continuous osmotic gradients.
Conversely, V. tubiflora employs a binary strategy: it perceives the onset of drought, initiates its protective response, and then shuts down metabolically, entering anhydrobiosis. During this dormant state, OSCA channels cease to operate. In the absence of active function, there is no selective pressure to maintain channel optimization, resulting in neutral drift and the relaxation signatures observed in OSCA2 and OSCA3+4.
4.3 The significance of the OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 tandem cluster
The physical contiguity and perfect conservation of the OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 cluster on Chromosome 7A is noteworthy. Tandem gene clusters are frequently associated with adaptive evolution in gene families involved in environmental sensing and stress response (Hanada et al., 2008). The fact that this particular cluster has been maintained with zero intermediate genes in both species, flanked by a conserved syntenic neighborhood, suggests that physical proximity is functionally relevant, potentially enabling coordinated transcriptional regulation or facilitating gene conversion events that maintain sequence homogeneity among cluster members.
The observation that OSCA1.4, despite being embedded within this conserved cluster, exhibits the highest Ka/Ks ratio among all orthologous pairs (0.80) is intriguing. This suggests that while the cluster architecture is conserved, individual members may be experiencing distinct selective regimes, with OSCA1.4 potentially undergoing functional divergence post-speciation.
4.4 The VpOSCA2.4a/b duplication as a candidate for subfunctionalization
The lineage-specific tandem duplication of OSCA2.4 in V. peripherica constitutes a compelling candidate for sub- or neofunctionalization. This duplication occurred within the OSCA2 subfamily, which is already under the most intense diversifying selection in the evergreen species. The presence of two adjacent copies in V. peripherica, compared to a single copy in V. tubiflora, may provide the evergreen species with additional functional flexibility for osmotic sensing in its active water-management strategy.
4.5 Conserved expression and network topology
The conservation of tissue-specific expression profiles across species, despite sequence divergence in OSCA2 and OSCA3+4 subfamilies, provides an important complement to the evolutionary analyses. The finding that OSCA1.2 is consistently enriched in root and stem tissues while OSCA1.1 and OSCA3.1 are enriched in leaves, with identical patterns in both species, indicates that the spatial deployment of the osmosensory network has been preserved. The "where" of osmotic perception is shared; it is the "how finely tuned" that has diverged.
Similarly, the conservation of PPI network topology, with OSCA1.2 and OSCA3.1 as central hubs in both species, demonstrates that the downstream signaling infrastructure remains equivalent. The molecular interactors, including H+-ATPases, voltage-dependent calcium channels, and cell wall proteins, are shared between species, indicating that divergence in osmotic perception occurs at the level of the sensor protein itself, not at the level of the signaling network.
4.6 Implications for desiccation tolerance evolution and crop improvement
These findings reposition the understanding of OSCA evolution from a descriptive genomic comparison to a mechanistic hypothesis about the evolutionary architecture of stress perception. The data demonstrate that the evolution of desiccation tolerance in Vellozia did not require the invention of new sensors or the elimination of existing ones. Instead, the transition from an evergreen to a resurrection strategy involved the selective relaxation of a modulatory layer over an intact perceptive base, a pattern that may be general for the evolution of stress responses in plants.
This conclusion has practical implications for the engineering of drought-tolerant crops. Rather than targeting the basal osmosensors (OSCA1), which are already robust and conserved, efforts to improve drought perception should focus on the modulatory channels (OSCA2 and OSCA3+4) that refine the quality and specificity of the osmotic signal.
The OSCA3+4 Contrast-FEL site at position 593 (Beta-Test = 24.72) in V. tubiflora is particularly noteworthy. Rather than representing simple relaxation, this site may constitute a specific adaptation, potentially a residue that facilitates rapid channel reactivation during rehydration, when the resurrection plant "comes back to life." This hypothesis warrants experimental validation through site-directed mutagenesis and electrophysiological characterization.
────────────────────────────────────────
5. CONCLUSIONS
The comprehensive genome-wide characterization of the OSCA gene family in V. peripherica and V. tubiflora revealed that osmotic perception in Vellozia operates on two distinct evolutionary levels. At the fundamental level, the OSCA1 subfamily maintains identical purifying selection, conserved synteny including a perfectly preserved tandem cluster on chromosome 7A, conserved tissue-specific expression, and equivalent positioning within protein-protein interaction networks. At the modulatory level, the OSCA2 and OSCA3+4 subfamilies have diverged profoundly between the two species, with the evergreen V. peripherica actively remodeling its channels through diversifying selection at 14 amino acid sites in OSCA2, while the resurrection plant V. tubiflora exhibits massive selection relaxation and the accumulation of neutral variation during the periods of functional silencing imposed by anhydrobiosis.
The difference between avoiding drought and surviving drought is thus already inscribed in the molecular evolution of the perception machinery itself. However, this inscription is selective, not global: the capacity to detect that something has changed is identical in both species, while the capacity to interpret nuances, gradients, and modulations of the osmotic signal has diverged in a manner that is fully consistent with the physiological requirements of each strategy.
These findings provide fundamental insights into the evolutionary regulatory architecture and calcium-mediated signaling networks underpinning desiccation tolerance in the genus Vellozia and offer specific molecular targets, including the VpOSCA2.4a/b duplication, the 14 diversifying sites in OSCA2, and the OSCA3+4 hotspot site 593, for future functional validation through electrophysiology, site-directed mutagenesis, and heterologous expression systems.
