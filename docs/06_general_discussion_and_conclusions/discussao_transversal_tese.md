# GENERAL DISCUSSION

## Genomic Conservation and Divergence Across Contrasting Water-Use Strategies in *Vellozia*

The comparison between *Vellozia tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* reveals contrasting patterns of genomic conservation and divergence across biological scales. Differences in gene-family turnover coexist with broadly conserved chromosomal organization and a similar repertoire of OSCA mechanosensory candidates. Within this shared repertoire, local copy-number differences, predicted protein architectures, and promoter variation identify specific targets for investigating the molecular basis of contrasting water-use strategies. The overarching contribution of this thesis lies in connecting these patterns, bridging the chromosome-scale genomic resources that make rigorous comparison possible with the evolutionary and functional questions that emerge from them.

This comparative inquiry is situated within the ecological diversity of the Brazilian *campos rupestres*, where nutrient-impoverished soils and seasonal water limitation occur across heterogeneous montane landscapes (SILVEIRA et al., 2016). Velloziaceae exhibit considerable morphological and physiological diversity within these environments, displaying both vegetative desiccation tolerance and strategies that maintain tissue hydration during drought (ALCANTARA et al., 2015; LAROCCA et al., 2022). In the Serra da Canastra region, *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* represent these contrasting strategies, respectively, with differences in seasonal leaf moisture documented in a four-species survey (PINTO et al., 2025). Their comparison provides an opportunity to examine how genomic architecture and coding divergence relate to water-use strategies within a shared regional climatic setting.

Across the genomic chapters, a foundational question emerges: how do inherited gene repertoires, subsequent changes in gene content, and variation within a sensory family contribute to the divergence between these species? Addressing this question requires distinguishing the genomic patterns recovered by the analyses from the physiological mechanisms they suggest. The computational training component contributes to this effort at a methodological level, examining how bioscientists develop the skills needed to analyze, document, and interpret complex biological data with rigor and reproducibility.

## Genomic resources and evolutionary context

Chromosome-scale resources make it possible to place individual genes within their broader evolutionary context. Earlier work on desiccation-tolerant Velloziaceae, including the draft genome published as *Xerophyta viscosa*, established a basis for investigating the genetic components of desiccation tolerance, although its fragmented assembly limited the resolution of chromosomal organization (COSTA et al., 2017). The haplotype-resolved *V. tubiflora* resource presented in Chapter 1 extends this foundation. Version 2 comprises two haplotypes, each organized into nine chromosome-length pseudomolecules, consistent with the chromosomal framework of the genus (2*n* = 18; BRAZ et al., 2025). Scaffold N50 values reached 49.8 and 50.0 megabases, with respective completeness estimates of 96.5% and 96.8% based on Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO). Its near telomere-to-telomere continuity provides a reference for investigating gene order, structural variation, and synteny across the family.

To maintain analytical consistency, the comparative analyses in Chapters 3 and 4 used version 1 assemblies of both species while version 2 of *V. peripherica* was being generated. These references established the necessary chromosomal framework for distinguishing broad macro-syntenic conservation from localized structural variation. Across the annotated gene sets, tandem and proximal duplicates accounted for 21.25% of loci in *V. peripherica* and 15.96% in *V. tubiflora*, highlighting distinct contributions of local duplication modes that shape plant gene family turnover under different selective regimes (BIRCHLER; VEITIA, 2012; QIAO et al., 2019). These proportions connect the physical organization of the references directly to the reconstruction of gene-family turnover models.

Phylogenomic dating places these comparative observations within a defined temporal framework calibrated by fossil and secondary nodes across monocots (BENTON et al., 2015; MELLO-SILVA et al., 2011). Reconstruction from 1,513 single-copy orthologs dated the separation of *Xerophyta* and *Vellozia* to approximately 31.91 million years ago (Ma), during the Oligocene, and the divergence between the focal *Vellozia* lineages to 9.74 Ma, with a 95% credible interval of 5.85-15.72 Ma, consistent with the timing of Miocene diversification documented across the *campos rupestres* flora (ALCANTARA et al., 2018). Although these divergence dates define the timing of lineage separation, the exact chronological emergence of distinct physiological strategies remains an independent question that requires broader taxonomic sampling to resolve.

Within this temporal framework, 132 gene families expanded at the common ancestor of core Velloziaceae, with 126 families reproducibly recovered across near-optimal runs. These ancestral expansions included gene families annotated for stress protection and transcriptional regulation, consistent with the hypothesis that vegetative desiccation tolerance evolved through the co-option and diversification of an ancestral seed-protection apparatus (COSTA et al., 2017; MARKS et al., 2024; OLIVER et al., 2020). Importantly, the stress-related families highlighted in Chapter 3 exhibited no significant additional copy-number expansion along the terminal branch leading to *V. tubiflora*. Rather than reflecting recent lineage-specific gene amplification, the retention of these inherited copies supports investigating how they are regulated and deployed during dehydration and recovery, although ancestral copy-number reconstruction identifies a genomic basis rather than establishing when physiological tolerance arose.

In contrast to this shared ancestral foundation, recent gene-family turnover diverged markedly between the two lineages. The macroevolutionary analysis identified 217 significant terminal expansions in *V. peripherica* (199 reproducible across near-optimal runs) compared with 50 expansions in *V. tubiflora* (all 50 reproducible across runs). Among duplicated genes in the expanded families of *V. peripherica*, 66.47% were classified as local duplicates (46.25% tandem and 20.22% proximal), whereas local duplicates constituted only 20.89% (13.53% tandem and 7.36% proximal) in the expanded families of *V. tubiflora*. This disparity connects the turnover models with the physical organization of the genome and shows how local duplication contributed to the observed differences in gene content, as local tandem proliferation often permits rapid divergence in defense and environmental perception pathways without generating whole-pathway stoichiometric dosage imbalances (BIRCHLER; VEITIA, 2012; COLEY et al., 1985; QIAO et al., 2019). The macroevolutionary comparison therefore reveals two distinct evolutionary trajectories: the shared preservation of an ancestrally expanded stress-protection repertoire and subsequent asymmetric, lineage-specific gene-family expansion driven by local events.

Coding-sequence divergence adds a further dimension to this interpretation. Across 8,737 single-copy ortholog trios polarized by *Xerophyta schlechteri*, 89.25% had nonsynonymous-to-synonymous substitution ratios (*ω*) below or equal to one across both focal branches, with low median ratios (0.2541 in *V. peripherica* and 0.2391 in *V. tubiflora*), consistent with the predominant action of purifying selection across functional coding sequences (YANG; BIELAWSKI, 2000). Accelerated nonsynonymous divergence was restricted to just 14 high-confidence candidate loci per species (*p* < 0.05). Thus, the pronounced differences in gene-family turnover and local duplication occurred against a background in which the broader shared proteome remained under widespread purifying constraint. The OSCA analysis extends this comparison to a sensory family in which a similar overall repertoire coexists with localized variation and a family-level difference in selection intensity.

## Conservation and variation in the OSCA repertoire

The OSCA analysis provides a focused view of the relationship between conservation and divergence. *Vellozia peripherica* and *V. tubiflora* contain 14 and 13 loci, respectively, numbers comparable to the 15 loci in *Arabidopsis thaliana* and 11 in *Oryza sativa* (LI et al., 2015; YUAN et al., 2014). Together with the conserved chromosomal contexts, these similar family sizes indicate that the contrast in water-use strategies is accompanied by broad conservation of the OSCA repertoire.

Within this conserved family, the chromosome-scale references resolved the syntenic *OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5* array on chromosome 7A, which spans more than 188 kilobases with no intervening genes and is flanked by 23 collinear anchors identified through microsynteny analysis (WANG et al., 2012). In contrast, chromosome 1A revealed a localized structural divergence: two adjacent *V. peripherica* copies, *VpOSCA2.4a* and *VpOSCA2.4b*, separated by 1,292 base pairs, occupied the syntenic position corresponding to the single *VtOSCA2.4* locus. The local variation at *OSCA2.4* is therefore informative because it identifies a discrete departure from an otherwise conserved physical gene arrangement.

Conservation of gene complement also coexists with a family-wide difference in evolutionary constraint. Selection-intensity modeling in a phylogenetic framework (RELAX; WERTHEIM et al., 2015) detected significantly lower selection intensity across the complete OSCA family along *V. tubiflora* branches relative to *V. peripherica* (*K* = 0.7064, *p* = 0.000337). In contrast, clade-specific tests revealed no significant shifts, with the OSCA1 clade exhibiting an estimate of  *K* = 0.9262 (*p* = 0.6081), which does not establish invariant selection nor can it be attributed specifically to *OSCA1.2*. Because clade-specific tests lacked statistical significance, this relaxation reflects a family-wide pattern. This family-level relaxation remains compatible with the predominance of genome-wide purifying selection, as the two analyses quantify distinct evolutionary processes: codon-level divergence across single-copy orthologs versus relative selection intensity across designated phylogenetic branches.

At the level of individual genes, basal transcript abundance and predicted functional associations highlight candidates for experimental investigation. *OSCA1.2* showed greater transcript abundance in roots and stems than in leaves in both species. In the predicted association network of each species reconstructed through STRING functional integration (SZKLARCZYK et al., 2023), OSCA1.2 had degree 18 and was associated with carbohydrate-metabolizing enzymes, including alpha- and beta-amylases. Although these computational networks derive from functional associations transferred through orthology rather than direct physical interaction assays in *Vellozia*, they identify a shared, tissue-associated metabolic context in which to explore links between mechanosensory signaling and carbohydrate metabolism, a pathway repeatedly implicated in osmolyte adjustment and energy management during desiccation (FARRANT et al., 2015; OLIVER et al., 2020).

The shortened *VtOSCA1.2* gene model makes this structural distinction particularly relevant. Topology predictions indicate that *VtOSCA1.2* lacks a detectable PF13967 domain and possesses only five to six transmembrane helices, compared with the canonical nine to eleven helices resolved in full-length OSCA/TMEM63 cryo-electron microscopy structures (LIU et al., 2018; MURTHY et al., 2018; ZHANG et al., 2018). Its conserved root- and stem-associated transcript pattern therefore co-occurs with a predicted structural divergence. If the shortened gene model is confirmed, it would provide a specific system for testing whether altered channel properties modify signaling during cellular shrinkage, although establishing that connection requires direct measurements of channel activity.

The *OSCA2.4* locus illustrates how structural duplication interacts with putative regulatory divergence. The tandemly duplicated *VpOSCA2.4a* and *VpOSCA2.4b* copies contain two and four predicted abscisic acid-responsive element (ABRE) motifs in their promoter regions, respectively, whereas the single *VtOSCA2.4* promoter contains six motifs, identified using curated plant cis-regulatory profiles (LESCOT et al., 2002; MIAO et al., 2022). Although motif frequencies provide candidates for testing promoter activity, motif presence alone does not establish transcriptional responsiveness, precluding any inference of preferential abscisic acid sensitivity in the avoidant species. Because both the outgroup *X. schlechteri* and *V. tubiflora* possess a single OSCA2.4 locus, and *VpOSCA2.4b* shares lower synonymous divergence with *VtOSCA2.4* than with *VpOSCA2.4a*, maximum parsimony supports a derived tandem duplication in *V. peripherica*, whose exact evolutionary timing awaits broader taxonomic sampling across Velloziaceae.

Functional associations also bridge the nuclear mechanosensory repertoire with organellar biology. In the predicted networks, OSCA3.1 and OSCA3.2 formed a distinct module whose predicted interactors included plastid- and vacuole-associated proteins, motivating comparative evaluation alongside complete plastid and mitochondrial assemblies, as environmental stress signaling frequently requires coordinated feedback between nuclear loci and organellar proteomes (SLOAN et al., 2018). While demonstrating direct organellar interactions or cytonuclear coevolution requires empirical localization and biochemical assays, these functional associations establish a computational bridge connecting the nuclear OSCA repertoire with the organellar analyses of the thesis.

## Physical osmosensing and chemical signaling across contrasting drought strategies

Comparing the macroevolutionary and sensory analyses highlights how distinct functional layers evolve across water-use strategies. In the drought-avoidant *V. peripherica*, local tandem and proximal duplications amplified cell-surface signaling families, including G-type lectin receptor kinases (G-LecRKs), wall-associated kinases (WAKs), and leucine-rich repeat receptor-like kinases (LRR-RLKs), which monitor cell-wall integrity and mediate extracellular cue perception during dehydration (BACETE et al., 2022; BELLANDE et al., 2017; WOLF et al., 2012), alongside abscisic acid and salicylic acid pathway components. In contrast, the desiccation-tolerant *V. tubiflora* preferentially retained ancestrally expanded stress-response families associated with organellar protection and chromatin regulation while showing few terminal expansions.

Whereas these receptor and signaling families underwent asymmetric turnover, the OSCA mechanosensitive channel repertoire remained largely conserved in copy number and chromosomal synteny between both species. Biophysically, OSCA channels gate in response to lateral lipid bilayer tension generated by turgor and volume shifts, initiating rapid cytosolic calcium influx that coordinates downstream physiological adjustments (BASU; HASWELL, 2017; THOR et al., 2020; YUAN et al., 2014; ZHANG et al., 2018). During extreme dehydration and cellular shrinkage in resurrection plants, unmodulated tension-gated calcium influx could risk cytosolic calcium overload (FARRANT et al., 2007). Whether the lower family-wide selection intensity estimated along *V. tubiflora* branches (*K* = 0.7064) and the shortened *VtOSCA1.2* model reflect adaptive dampening of mechanosensory sensitivity during anhydrobiosis or broader demographic and selective constraints remains a central hypothesis for experimental validation. Rather than acting as mutually exclusive systems, conserved mechanosensitive channels and diverging receptor kinase repertoires represent complementary sensory tiers that may be differentially tuned between dehydration avoidance and desiccation tolerance.

## Ecological interpretation and limits of inference

These candidate molecular mechanisms must be interpreted within the natural ecological settings of the focal species. *Vellozia tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* were sampled at distinct sites within the Serra da Canastra National Park, establishing a regional comparative context rather than identical microenvironments (PINTO et al., 2025). Across Velloziaceae, desiccation-tolerant species occur predominantly on exposed rocks, whereas non-tolerant species are more frequent in deeper sandy soils (ALCANTARA et al., 2015). Microhabitat differences and local edaphic water availability can therefore contribute to the genomic contrasts alongside the physiological strategies themselves.

Comparative physiological studies in related taxa illustrate the functional trade-offs that contextualize these genomic findings. Under mild drought, the desiccation-tolerant *V. nivea* exhibited lower vegetative productivity than the non-tolerant *V. intermedia*, yet survived severe water exclusion from which *V. intermedia* did not recover (TEODORO et al., 2021). Similarly, field investigations comparing *Barbacenia gentianoides* and *V. caruncularis* demonstrated contrasting survival mechanisms, with the former undergoing leaf senescence followed by resprouting and the latter preserving desiccated foliage through vegetative desiccation tolerance (VIEIRA et al., 2025). These contrasting responses illustrate diverse survival solutions in *campos rupestres*, while underscoring that physiological mechanisms observed in one species pair cannot be uncritically extrapolated to another without direct empirical testing.

Validating the predicted structural truncation in *VtOSCA1.2* and the regulatory divergence at *OSCA2.4* represents a logical next step in connecting genomic variation to water-use physiology. Although the basal transcriptomes analyzed here capture organ-associated expression profiles, they do not resolve dynamic transcriptional adjustments during acute dehydration or rehydration. Combining time-resolved transcriptomics, in vivo cytosolic calcium imaging, and electrophysiological patch-clamp assays will be necessary to resolve the causal links connecting gene regulation, channel gating, and whole-plant survival under osmotic perturbation (FRACHISSE et al., 2014; THOR et al., 2020; YUAN et al., 2014).

These evolutionary inferences are also bounded by the two-species comparative design, where trait differences cannot be separated from lineage divergence without phylogenetically replicated comparisons (FELSENSTEIN, 1985). A pairwise comparison cannot definitively disentangle adaptive traits tied to water-use strategies from lineage-specific demographic histories, such as differences in effective population size (*N*~e~; JONES et al., 2018). Sequencing additional *Vellozia* and *Barbacenia* genomes will reveal whether observed duplication patterns and selection dynamics recur across independently evolved tolerant and avoidant lineages. Complementing reference genomes with population-level resequencing will further clarify the interplay between natural selection, genetic drift, and demographic bottlenecks in shaping coding divergence.

Assembly versioning represents an additional methodological consideration that directly impacts comparative genomics in non-model organisms (VURUPUTOOR et al., 2023). Utilizing version 1 assemblies across Chapters 3 and 4 ensured methodological parity in assembly and annotation algorithms, even as Chapter 1 introduced the refined version 2 assembly for *V. tubiflora*. This analytical choice maintains internal comparability across the macroevolutionary and sensory chapters, while acknowledging that gene models and structural boundaries may be refined in subsequent releases. Re-evaluating these findings against future chromosome-scale assemblies of *V. peripherica* will allow the stability of inferred copy-number variants to be tested systematically. Explicitly documenting this analytical choice ensures provenance and transparency according to FAIR data principles (WILKINSON et al., 2016; WILSON et al., 2017), linking the genomic findings directly to the reproducible computational practices evaluated in the final chapter.

## Computational training and research practice

The production of genomic resources creates a corresponding need for the ability to inspect analytical choices, document procedures, and interpret computational results, addressing a persistent computational training gap documented across the international and regional life sciences community (BARONE et al., 2017; CRESTANA et al., 2026). Chapter 5 addresses these practices through introductory programming for bioscientists with little or no previous experience. The Brazilian Python Workshop for Biological Data combined domain-specific biological problems, live coding, digital notebooks, and structured evaluation based on established scientific computing pedagogy (WILSON et al., 2017; ZUVANOV et al., 2021), successfully adapting to a virtual format during the COVID-19 pandemic. This educational initiative connects directly with the genomic chapters by reinforcing the reproducible practices required to transform raw sequencing reads into reliable, interpretable biological models.

The virtual cohort encompassed 37 learners, and 32 of the 33 submitted individual notebooks showed good documentation and note-taking. The published study also formulated 19 recommendations for future training initiatives (ZUVANOV et al., 2021). These outcomes support the feasibility of teaching programming and computational documentation through biological problems. Although the long-term impact on research independence and workflow reproducibility requires ongoing assessment, the workshop provides a practical contribution to developing the skills on which data-intensive biological research depends.

# GENERAL CONCLUSIONS

This thesis investigated how genomic architecture, gene family dynamics, and osmosensory channel evolution differ between two *Vellozia* species with contrasting water-use strategies: desiccation tolerance in *V. tubiflora* and dehydration avoidance in *V. peripherica*. Integrating chromosome-scale resources, macroevolutionary models, sensory characterization, and computational training leads to the following conclusions:

The phased, near telomere-to-telomere reference assembly of *V. tubiflora* resolves the nuclear chromosomal architecture of the genus. High-continuity, haplotype-resolved assemblies are indispensable for establishing baseline gene orders and distinguishing genuine structural variants and duplication events from fragmentation artifacts in non-model Neotropical taxa.

At the whole-genome level, contrasting water-use strategies coexist with broad conservation of synteny and widespread purifying constraint across the shared proteome. Vegetative desiccation tolerance in *V. tubiflora* did not require widespread terminal gene-family expansions; instead, it relies on the evolutionary retention and physiological deployment of an ancestral stress-protection toolkit inherited from the common ancestor of core Velloziaceae.

Conversely, dehydration avoidance in *V. peripherica* is characterized by accelerated, lineage-specific gene family turnover driven predominantly by local tandem and proximal duplications. This asymmetric local expansion preferentially enlarged families of cell-surface receptor kinases and environmental perception pathways, consistent with an active strategy of physiological monitoring and transpirational avoidance.

The OSCA mechanosensitive channel family exhibits pronounced evolutionary stability across contrasting drought strategies. Both species retain the canonical clades and maintain microsyntenic collinearity across chromosomes. Evolutionary divergence within the family is restricted to localized structural events, notably a lineage-specific tandem duplication in *V. peripherica* and family-level relaxation of selection intensity in *V. tubiflora*, suggesting that adaptation to desiccation involves the selective modulation of membrane-tension sensitivity rather than wholesale remodeling of osmosensory repertoires.

Tissue-associated expression profiles and promoter analyses identify candidate regulatory links between mechanosensation and metabolic responses. Conserved transcript accumulation of *OSCA1.2* in vascular and underground tissues, alongside predicted associations with carbohydrate-metabolizing enzymes, and differential promoter motif compositions at *OSCA2.4* define prioritized targets for investigating the functional interface between physical osmosensing and cellular acclimation.

Interactive, biological problem-based instruction effectively builds computational literacy and reproducible documentation practices among bioscientists. Strengthening programming autonomy and transparent data management provides the methodological foundation necessary to interrogate high-throughput sequencing data and support open science in biodiversity genomics.

Overall, the divergence between vegetative desiccation tolerance and dehydration avoidance in *Vellozia* demonstrates that major physiological innovations can evolve through localized gene duplications, regulatory divergence, and selective tuning upon a conserved ancestral genomic foundation. Because these conclusions derive from a pairwise comparative framework and basal transcriptomes, verifying predicted structural variants, conducting time-resolved dehydration-rehydration experiments, and expanding taxon sampling across Velloziaceae represent the essential frontiers for determining how these molecular mechanisms operate during environmental drought.
