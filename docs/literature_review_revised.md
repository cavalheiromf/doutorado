## THESIS PROPOSAL SUMMARY
This literature review establishes the theoretical framework and methodological workflow to investigate the genomic and regulatory bases of contrasting drought adaptation strategies in Velloziaceae from the *campos rupestres*. We focus on the structural and functional characterization of the OSCA superfamily of mechanosensitive calcium channels as primary candidates for early osmotic stress perception. The thesis comprises two integrated chapters: Chapter 1 establishes the large-scale comparative genomic foundation of *Vellozia* species through high-contiguity chromosomal assemblies, synteny analysis, and gene family evolutionary dynamics; Chapter 2 examines the gene architecture, transcriptional expression profiles, cis-regulatory promoter composition, and protein-protein interaction networks of the OSCA family across contrasting drought regimes.

# 1. Ecological Context, Morphophysiological Diversity, and Evolutionary Trade-offs in Velloziaceae

The *campos rupestres* is a tropical montane ecosystem, classified as an Old Climatically-Buffered Infertile Landscape (OCBIL), containing approximately 15% of Brazil's vascular flora in under 1% of its territory \cite{Silveira_2015, Fernandes_2020, Alcantara_2015}. Severe edaphoclimatic filters drive this floristic richness and high beta diversity \cite{Fernandes_2018}. The soils are shallow, acidic, and oligotrophic, with available phosphorus concentrations among the lowest globally \cite{Teodoro_2019, Lambers_2022, Abrah_o_2018}. Furthermore, the quartzite substrate retains minimal water, exposing plants to acute soil water deficits, saturating irradiance, high vapor pressure deficits (VPD), pronounced thermal amplitudes, and persistent winds. Climate change models project that these selective pressures will intensify by increasing the frequency and severity of prolonged droughts in tropical mountains.

Velloziaceae (Pandanales) dominates this stringent environment, reaching its global center of diversity in the *campos rupestres* \cite{DE_MELLO_SILVA_2005, Alcantara_2015, Alcantara_2018}. These predominantly heliophytic monocots exhibit diverse vegetative growth forms, from arborescent shrubs to compact graminoid rosettes, typically featuring dense apical leaf rosettes on fibrous caudices \cite{Wanga_2021}. To acquire nutrients from phosphorus-impoverished substrates, Velloziaceae evolved specialized "vellozioid" roots that exude carboxylates to weather quartzite rocks and solubilize inorganic phosphorus \cite{Teodoro_2019, Lambers_2022, Abrah_o_2018}. Foliar morphoanatomical traits delay dehydration. For example, *Barbacenia graminifolia* possesses a single-layered adaxial epidermis with thickened cuticles, symmetrical mesophyll compaction, and reversible leaf rolling upon turgor loss, mitigating photo-oxidative damage \cite{Alves_2009, Teodoro_2021}. Palisade parenchyma cells also accumulate vacuolar mucilage enriched in acidic polysaccharides, which facilitates foliar water retention and accelerates rehydration \cite{Vieira_2022}.

Congeneric Velloziaceae species exhibit contrasting water-use strategies \cite{Alcantara_2015, Vieira_2022}. Desiccation-tolerant (DT) species, or resurrection plants, survive vegetative water losses exceeding 90% by entering anabiosis and restoring full metabolic competence upon rehydration \cite{Bondi_2023, Teodoro_2021, Marks_2024}. This vegetative anhydrobiosis is rare among angiosperms. DT lineages in the *campos rupestres* include *Vellozia tubiflora* and *Vellozia caruncularis*. Conversely, desiccation-sensitive but drought-avoidant (evergreen) species, such as *Vellozia peripherica* and *Barbacenia gentianoides*, maintain functional foliar hydration through strict stomatal regulation, osmotic adjustment, and deep root systems, sustaining baseline carbon assimilation during dry seasons \cite{DE_MELLO_SILVA_2005, Vieira_2022, Vieira_2025}. In avoidant species, exceeding the leaf turgor loss point ($\Psi_{TLP}$) represents an irreversible threshold triggering xylem cavitation and tissue mortality. In DT species, however, reaching $\Psi_{TLP}$ acts as an inductive cue that initiates cellular protection programs, including cell wall folding and metabolic arrest \cite{Bondi_2023, Teodoro_2021}.

This physiological niche divergence causes spatial segregation along soil depth and moisture gradients \cite{Alcantara_2015, Silveira_2015, Zappi_2017}. For example, *Vellozia caruncularis* colonizes exposed, rapidly draining rocky outcrops where water availability fluctuates violently, leveraging anhydrobiosis for survival \cite{Vieira_2025}. Conversely, *Barbacenia gentianoides* occupies deeper, organic matter-rich crevices, relying on continuous moisture and seasonal carbon remobilization. These contrasting life-history strategies illustrate a fundamental evolutionary trade-off between productivity and survival \cite{Teodoro_2021, Fernandes_2020, Fernandes_2018, Volaire_2026}. Desiccation tolerance ensures persistence through catastrophic droughts at the expense of negligible growth rates during dry spells \cite{Bondi_2023, Teodoro_2021}. Dehydration avoidance maximizes carbon assimilation under moderate water deficits, functioning as temporary "drought resistance," but exposes plants to catastrophic mortality if stress exceeds their hydraulic safety margins. This reflects a broader trade-off between early growth potential and long-term drought survival \cite{Alcantara_2015, Vieira_2022, Volaire_2026}.

# 2. Biophysical Bases of Drought Responses and the Comparative System in Velloziaceae

Understanding plant responses to water scarcity requires distinguishing between environmental water deficit, cellular osmotic stress, and terminal desiccation \cite{Thor_2020, Han_2022, Hartmann_2021}, as well as standardizing terminology for adaptation strategies \cite{Volaire_2017, Volaire_2026}. Evaluating drought adaptation requires separating "drought resistance"—traits focused on maintaining turgor and growth potential under moderate water deficits (e.g., dehydration avoidance)—from "drought survival"—mechanisms like extreme dehydration or desiccation tolerance required when severe water limitations halt growth entirely \cite{Volaire_2026}. While water deficit denotes macroscopic moisture depletion, osmotic stress represents its immediate biophysical manifestation: the drop in extracellular water potential establishes a thermodynamic gradient driving water efflux, increasing plasma membrane lateral tension and perturbing protoplast-cell wall biomechanics. This mechanical strain initiates signaling cascades within seconds, opening mechanosensitive ion channels that generate a transient wave of cytosolic calcium influx ($OICI_{cyt}$) \cite{Zhang_2018, Liu_2022, Zhu_2025, Kaur_2021}. Calcium decoders subsequently phosphorylate downstream targets and activate abscisic acid (ABA)-dependent and independent transcriptional networks, coordinating stomatal closure, osmotic adjustment, and heat shock responses \cite{Thor_2020, Han_2022, Kang_2023}. When protoplasmic water content falls below 10–20% ($\Psi < -100\text{ MPa}$), terminal cellular desiccation occurs, causing structural collapse and membrane phase transitions. Sensitive plants suffer irreversible lysis; resurrection plants execute coordinated biochemical programs that stabilize the desiccated protoplast in a vitrified, quiescent state \cite{Bondi_2023, Teodoro_2021}.

These divergent physiological strategies rely on distinct cellular and metabolic adaptations \cite{Bondi_2023, Vieira_2025}. Avoidant species maintain high foliar water potentials during drought through tight ABA- and jasmonate-mediated stomatal closure, osmotic adjustment, and extensive root foraging \cite{Teodoro_2019, Abrah_o_2018}. An evergreen habit sustains year-round carbon capture, but leaves these plants vulnerable to conduit cavitation if water potential drops below $\Psi_{TLP}$ \cite{Alcantara_2015, Vieira_2022}. Resurrection plants, in contrast, poikilohydrically equilibrate with atmospheric water potential. During dehydration, they convert starch reserves into non-reducing sugars (sucrose, trehalose, and raffinose family oligosaccharides), which substitute for water hydrogen bonds on macromolecular surfaces and induce cytoplasmic vitrification—forming an amorphous glassy matrix that prevents crystallization \cite{Vieira_2022, Bondi_2023}. Simultaneously, cell walls remodel with arabinose- and pectin-rich polymers, allowing controlled wall folding that contracts harmoniously with the shrinking protoplast \cite{Teodoro_2021}. Vacuoles fragment into multiple microvacuoles loaded with osmolytes to preserve subcellular compartmentalization. To minimize photo-oxidative damage, tolerant plants either degrade their chlorophylls or deploy robust non-photochemical quenching (NPQ/qE) and antioxidant enzymatic cascades to scavenge Reactive Oxygen Species \cite{Oung_2021, Thor_2020, Han_2022, Bondi_2023}.

The sympatric congeneric pair *Vellozia tubiflora* and *Vellozia peripherica* provides a natural comparative model to investigate the genomic and regulatory foundations of drought adaptation \cite{Alcantara_2015, Vieira_2022}. As an obligate resurrection plant (DT), *V. tubiflora* responds to acute water deficit through programmed foliar rolling, metabolic shutdown, and entry into anabiosis \cite{Bondi_2023, Teodoro_2021}. In contrast, the evergreen *V. peripherica* enforces strict stomatal regulation and foliar water retention to remain photosynthetically active, but lacks macromolecular desiccation protection and suffers irreversible tissue mortality if cellular water drops below $\Psi_{TLP}$. This physiological dichotomy drives fine-scale microhabitat segregation: *V. tubiflora* occupies exposed rock crevices prone to rapid drying, whereas *V. peripherica* colonizes deeper soil pockets \cite{Bondi_2023, Vieira_2025}. These adaptive differences indicate fundamental divergences in early osmotic perception and transcriptional regulation, making mechanosensitive channels of the OSCA family prime candidates for comparative investigation \cite{Thor_2020, Han_2022}.

Pairwise comparative models between phylogenetically paired desiccation-tolerant (DT) and desiccation-sensitive relatives isolate the mechanistic foundations of extreme drought adaptation while controlling for macroevolutionary phylogenetic noise \cite{Marks_2024, Bondi_2023}. Velloziaceae has already enabled foundational breakthroughs in resurrection plant biology: the chromosome-level genome of the African monocot *Xerophyta viscosa* demonstrated that vegetative desiccation tolerance evolved through the co-option of ancestral seed maturation networks \cite{Costa_2017}. In South American *campos rupestres*, ecophysiological comparisons between co-occurring *Vellozia* and *Barbacenia* species delineated contrasting hydraulic safety margins and seasonal carbon remobilization \cite{Teodoro_2021, Vieira_2022, Vieira_2025, Alcantara_2015}. These findings parallel comparative systems across the plant kingdom, including the resurrection grasses *Oropetium thomaeum* \cite{VanBuren_2015} and *Sporobolus stapfianus* \cite{Oliver_2011}, and the lycophyte *Selaginella lepidophylla* \cite{VanBuren_2018}. However, previous literature has predominantly contrasted DT plants with standard, mesophytic relatives. This study pioneers a comparative genomics framework contrasting a DT resurrection plant directly against a highly specialized drought-avoidant relative from the same extreme environment. Extending this proven comparative paradigm to *Vellozia tubiflora* (DT) and *Vellozia peripherica* (avoidant) provides an unprecedented model to bridge whole-genome evolutionary dynamics with discrete mechanosensory signaling cascades.

# 3. Comparative Genomics and Macroevolutionary Dynamics of Adaptation

Comparative genomics provides the framework to determine whether divergent ecological strategies correlate with broad chromosomal reorganizations, copy-number variations, or family-specific evolutionary rates \cite{Wu_2022, Li_2023}. The foundation relies on high-contiguity chromosomal assemblies evaluated by metrics such as BUSCO, followed by structural annotation supported by multi-tissue RNA-seq data \cite{Vuruputoor_2023}. Functional annotation incorporates Hidden Markov Models (HMM) to query conserved protein domain databases (Pfam, SMART, CDD). In the context of early osmotic signaling, the structural domain DUF221 (Pfam: PF02714) identifies members of the hyperosmolality-gated calcium-permeable OSCA family \cite{Zhang_2018, Liu_2022, Yuan_2014}. OSCA proteins display a modular tripartite architecture: an N-terminal RSN1_TM domain (PF13967) associated with membrane trafficking, a central PHM7_cyt cytosolic loop (PF14703) functioning as a mechanical scaffold, and the C-terminal DUF221 pore domain (PF02714) that conducts calcium fluxes under membrane lateral tension.

Homology relationships distinguish orthologs (genes diverged via speciation) from paralogs (genes generated via duplication) \cite{Wu_2022, Li_2023}. Software packages such as OrthoFinder group translated proteomes into shared orthogroups to quantify lineage-specific expansions and contractions. Synteny and collinearity analyses (via MCScanX and Circos) elucidate the mechanistic origin of duplicated loci—categorizing them into Whole-Genome/Segmental Duplications (WGD), tandem expansions, proximal, or dispersed duplications \cite{Bondi_2023, Marks_2024, YANG_2019, Tong_2021}.

Codon-based evolutionary models evaluate selective pressures on coding sequences through the ratio of non-synonymous ($Ka$) to synonymous ($Ks$) substitutions \cite{Wu_2022, Li_2023}. Values of $Ka/Ks < 1$ indicate purifying selection, $Ka/Ks = 1$ denotes neutral evolution, and $Ka/Ks > 1$ reveals positive selection \cite{Gu_2018, She_2022, Shan_2023, Zhu_2025}. Furthermore, synonymous substitution distributions ($Ks$) estimate the relative timing of duplication and speciation events ($T = Ks / 2r$). Post-duplication functional divergence is evaluated using tools such as DIVERGE, which classify amino acid shifts into Type I (shifts in site-specific evolutionary rates) and Type II (radical changes in physicochemical properties). Establishing this macroevolutionary landscape provides the necessary genetic backdrop to dissect the discrete molecular sensors that initiate stress perception.

# 4. Primary Osmosensing and the OSCA Mechanosensitive Calcium Channel Family

## 4.1 The Search for Primary Osmosensors in Plants
While downstream drought signaling pathways are well mapped, the primary biophysical mechanisms by which plant cells first sense water deficit remain an active area of investigation \cite{Zhang_2018, Liu_2022, Hartmann_2021}. Osmotic stress rapidly alters membrane-cell wall biomechanics through three interconnected pathways: plasma membrane sensors, cell wall integrity sentinels, and mechanosensitive ion channels. Early paradigms proposed the *Arabidopsis* histidine kinase AtHK1 as the primary osmosensor \cite{Thor_2020, Han_2022}; however, *athk1-null* mutants display normal cytosolic calcium signaling under hyperosmotic shock, indicating the presence of independent mechanosensory channels. As cell volume contracts under dehydration, tensile stress develops at the cell wall-membrane interface, where Receptor-Like Kinases (RLKs) monitor this biomechanical strain, modulating ABA accumulation and evoking late calcium transients to induce cell wall remodeling.

Mechanosensitive (MS) ion channels are the fastest sensors capable of converting lipid bilayer physical deformation directly into intracellular chemical signals \cite{Zhang_2018, Liu_2022, Kaur_2021}. Tension changes directly gate the channel pore, driving rapid $Ca^{2+}$ influx from the apoplast within seconds ($OICI_{cyt}$) \cite{Thor_2020, Han_2022}. Alternative plant mechanosensitive families fulfill specialized functions: MSL channels are predominantly anion-selective; MCA channels are calcium-permeable, but *mca1/mca2* mutants maintain normal hyperosmotic calcium spikes; and plant Piezo channels participate in root penetration mechanotransduction \cite{Hartmann_2021, Kaur_2021}. Consequently, the OSCA (*hyperosmolality-gated calcium-permeable channels*) family stands out as the only plant mechanosensor class whose direct gating by membrane lateral tension and rapid $Ca^{2+}$ conductance under hyperosmotic shock have been unequivocally demonstrated \cite{Zhang_2018, Liu_2022, Yuan_2014}. Thus, comparative analysis of OSCA channels between *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* provides an ideal platform to uncover the molecular foundations of early water sensing.

## 4.2 Molecular Architecture, Phylogeny, and Biophysical Gating of OSCA Channels
The founder locus OSCA1 (*AtOSCA1.1*) was isolated in *Arabidopsis thaliana* as the essential channel mediating hyperosmolality-induced $Ca^{2+}$ increase \cite{Yuan_2014, Han_2022}. Knockout *osca1* plants exhibit abolished root $OICI_{cyt}$ waves and defective stomatal closure, leading to accelerated dehydration \cite{Zhang_2018}. *AtOSCA1.2* was subsequently validated as an active paralog sharing 85% sequence identity \cite{Liu_2018, Liu_2022}. OSCA homologs possess an exclusive 180-amino-acid cytosolic gating loop. Plant genomes underwent substantial lineage-specific expansions of this family, ranging from 15 in *Arabidopsis* \cite{Yuan_2014} to 42 in bread wheat \cite{Tong_2021, Kaur_2022}. Phylogenetically, the family comprises four monophyletic clades \cite{Wu_2022, Li_2023}: Clade I mediates hyperosmotic perception at the plasma membrane; Clade II regulates developmental osmotic control; Clade III features low copy-number retention and early drought responsiveness; and Clade IV represents the basal single-exon lineage. Evolutionary genomics indicates that OSCA diversified during the land plant transition, expanding via whole-genome duplications to equip stomata, roots, and vascular tissues with precise osmosensors \cite{Wu_2022, Ke_2022, Yuan_2014}.

Single-particle cryo-EM structures reveal that OSCA channels assemble as symmetrical homodimers perpendicular to the plasma membrane \cite{Zhang_2018, Liu_2018, Liu_2022}. The transmembrane domains of each subunit remain physically separated by a central hydrophobic cavity, with dimerization stabilized exclusively by cytosolic hydrogen bonding networks. Each monomer comprises 11 transmembrane helices (M0–M10) organized into three functional domains: an N-terminal domain (RSN1_TM, PF13967) stabilizing membrane insertion, a central cytosolic domain (PHM7_cyt, PF14703) acting as a biomechanical scaffold, and a C-terminal DUF221 domain (RSN1_7TM, PF02714) forming the ion-conducting pore. Gating operates via a "force-from-lipid" mechanism: membrane stretching pulls a hydrophobic anchor (consensus motif `LGFLGLWG`), levering the gating helix M6 to straighten around a conserved glycine hinge (G528 in AtOSCA1.1) \cite{Zhang_2018, Liu_2022}. This movement rotates bulky aromatic residues away from the pore axis, opening a conductive pathway for hydrated $Ca^{2+}$ influx. Isoforms lacking this cytosolic anchor fail to rescue osmotic $Ca^{2+}$ deficiency, demonstrating the sensory centrality of the cytosolic arm.

## 4.3 Cis-Regulatory Architecture, Expression Dynamics, and Interaction Networks
Promoter regions of OSCA genes harbor diverse cis-regulatory elements mediating environmental responsiveness \cite{Li_2015, Ding_2019, Gu_2018, Zhang_2023, Miao_2022}: ABRE governing bZIP-driven transcription under drought; DRE/CRT regulating rapid ABA-independent activation; MBS and LTR driving responses to physical desiccation and cold stress; and CGTCA/TGACG motifs mediating jasmonate signaling \cite{Li_2023}. Spatiotemporal expression profiles show high transcript abundance in roots, vascular bundles, and pollen \cite{Jie_2022, Li_2022}, alongside marked post-duplication subfunctionalization in paralogous pairs \cite{Yang_2026, Wu_2022, Li_2023}. Furthermore, protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks link OSCA channels to transcriptional regulators and structural components \cite{Zhang_2018, Liu_2022}. For example, in *Arabidopsis*, AtOSCA1.3 is phosphorylated at Ser54 by the immune kinase BIK1 upon flg22 perception, rapidly gating calcium influx to execute stomatal closure during pathogen attack \cite{Thor_2020, Han_2022}, and in wheat, TaOSCA1.4 functions as a QTL regulator coupling turgor signaling to vegetative shoot elongation \cite{Lv_2024}.

# 5. Scientific Propositions: Gaps, Hypotheses, and Research Objectives

## 5.1 Specific Gap and Integrating Hypotheses
While late-stage drought adaptations in *campos rupestres* plants are widely documented, the early molecular mechanisms through which cells perceive water deficit and trigger divergent physiological trajectories remain largely unknown. The congeneric pair *Vellozia tubiflora* (desiccation-tolerant) and *Vellozia peripherica* (evergreen, desiccation-sensitive) represents an ideal comparative model: sharing the same evolutionary history and macroclimate, they display diametrically opposed strategies under acute drought \cite{Alcantara_2015, Vieira_2022}.

**General Hypothesis:** The contrasting ecophysiological responses to water deficit in *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* are driven by multilevel molecular divergence, spanning broad genomic architecture, gene family dynamics, structural features, and transcriptional regulation of primary mechanosensory components.

**Specific Hypotheses:**
1. **Broad Genomic Divergence Hypothesis:** Chromosomal organization, syntenic collinearity, and family expansion/contraction dynamics reflect the evolutionary signatures of their respective ecological strategies.
2. **OSCA Channel Structural Conservation Hypothesis:** Core OSCA domain topology and pore-lining residues remain highly conserved in both species due to purifying selection on basic ion conduction.
3. **OSCA Regulatory and Transcriptional Divergence Hypothesis:** Temporal kinetics and stress responsiveness diverge significantly in promoter cis-element composition, producing early, coordinated activation in *V. tubiflora* versus conservative expression in *V. peripherica*.
4. **Interaction Network Specialization Hypothesis:** OSCA protein-protein interaction networks exhibit differential wiring, preferentially integrating with cell wall integrity and ABA cascades in *V. tubiflora*, and with osmotic homeostasis in *V. peripherica*.

## 5.2 Objectives and Thesis Structure

**General Objective:** To investigate the comparative genomics of *Vellozia tubiflora* and *Vellozia peripherica*, delineating global evolutionary signatures associated with their contrasting drought adaptations, and systematically characterizing the molecular, evolutionary, and regulatory determinants of the OSCA mechanosensitive calcium channel family.

**Specific Objectives:**

**Chapter 1 (Large-Scale Comparative Genomics):**
1. Quantify the completeness, contiguity, and quality of chromosomal assemblies and annotations of *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* using metrics such as BUSCO and RNA-seq alignments.
2. Group the predicted proteomes into orthogroups using OrthoFinder to establish high-confidence orthology and paralogy relationships.
3. Map syntenic collinearity blocks via MCScanX/Circos and categorize duplication mechanisms.
4. Model gene family expansion and contraction dynamics (CAFE) and estimate selection regimes ($Ka/Ks$) and functional divergence (DIVERGE).

**Chapter 2 (Characterization of the OSCA Family):**
1. Identify all OSCA family members in both genomes using Hidden Markov Model profiles for the DUF221 domain (PF02714).
2. Reconstruct phylogenetic relationships with monocot/dicot models, classifying genes into the four conserved clades.
3. Model exon-intron architectures, and membrane domain topologies.
4. Map and compare cis-regulatory promoter elements (-2000 bp) via PlantCARE to assess hormone and stress sensitivity.
5. Predict protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks to identify functional partners in early stress signal transduction.

The thesis progresses logically from the whole-genome macroevolutionary framework (Chapter 1) to the high-resolution characterization of the candidate mechanosensory gene family (Chapter 2), establishing predictive molecular hypotheses for future functional validation.
