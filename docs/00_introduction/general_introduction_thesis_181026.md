# General Introduction and Literature Review

## 1. Plant responses to water limitation: strategies and cellular sensing

Water scarcity is a widespread physical constraint that influences the physiology, ecological distribution, and evolutionary diversification of terrestrial plants. When evaporative demand exceeds environmental supply, the physiological impact is determined by the severity and duration of the deficit, the vulnerability of affected tissues, and their ability to recover after rehydration. To investigate how plants overcome these constraints, a rigorous conceptual framework must be established that distinguishes external environmental conditions from internal tissue hydration, laying the groundwork for evaluating diverse adaptive strategies and early cellular sensing mechanisms (CHAVES et al., 2003; GILBERT; MEDINA, 2016; VOLAIRE, 2018).

Drought, dehydration, osmotic stress, and desiccation are all related but distinct aspects of water scarcity, despite being commonly used interchangeably in broader contexts. Drought is defined as an environmental deficit in water supply within the soil or atmosphere, whereas dehydration is the loss of water from plant tissues. At the cellular level, osmotic stress occurs when water-potential imbalances cause water efflux and disrupt intracellular osmolality and turgor, with desiccation serving as the extreme boundary condition in which tissues lose virtually all free water and suspend active metabolism. This distinction is critical because environmental drought does not directly determine a plant's internal hydration state: an identical soil water deficit can cause vastly different degrees of tissue dehydration in co-occurring species depending on their capacities for water acquisition, stomatal control, and tissue retention (GILBERT; MEDINA, 2016; VOLAIRE, 2018; ALPERT, 2000, 2006). Differentiating between environmental water scarcity and cellular perturbation is thus essential for examining how early sensing mechanisms relate to whole-plant strategies such as dehydration avoidance and desiccation tolerance.

Plants adapt to water scarcity through a range of phenological, physiological, and structural strategies (KOOYERS, 2015; BERGER et al., 2016; VOLAIRE, 2018). At the whole-plant and life-history level, dehydration escape operates by adjusting developmental timing so that active vegetative growth, flowering, and seed maturation are completed before severe environmental drought develops (KOOYERS, 2015; GILLESPIE; VOLAIRE, 2017). Lineages that rely on escape typically have developmental plasticity, accelerated reproductive transitions, or photoperiod-cued phenologies that allow them to bridge unfavorable dry seasons using desiccation-resistant orthodox seeds or dormant underground storage organs (ALPERT, 2000, 2006; BERGER et al., 2016). Dehydration escape prevents tissue-level water deficit by limiting active growth to periods of consistent moisture; however, it limits productivity to favorable windows and provides little physiological buffering when drought occurs unexpectedly during the active growing cycle.

In contrast to escaping drought in time, dehydration avoidance allows plants to withstand environmental water scarcity by maintaining cellular hydration, positive turgor, and metabolic function as moisture availability decreases (VOLAIRE, 2018; VOLAIRE et al., 2026). At whole-plant and cellular scales, avoidance relies on coordinated mechanisms that restrict transpirational water loss while maximizing water acquisition: abscisic acid (ABA)-dependent signaling promotes stomatal closure to conserve internal water pools, while root architecture adjusts to exploit residual soil reserves (CHAVES et al., 2003; VOLAIRE, 2018; MARTÍNEZ-VILALTA et al., 2019). Concurrently, cells adjust their osmotic potential by accumulating compatible solutes, which lowers intracellular osmotic potential and aids in the maintenance of turgor as soil water potential declines. Avoidant tissues also protect against photochemical damage by regulating energy dissipation and antioxidant defenses (GILBERT; MEDINA, 2016). Dehydration avoidance delays tissue water loss through coordinated responses; however, prolonged drought can deplete available water reserves, exposing tissues to hydraulic dysfunction and other injury once their physiological limits are exceeded (VOLAIRE, 2018; VOLAIRE et al., 2026).

Volaire et al. (2026) proposed a temporal framework that unifies these strategies across three successive physiological phases: an initial growth phase. when water is readily available; a phase of drought resistance, in which dehydration avoidance maintains positive turgor and growth as water availability declines; and a phase of drought survival, which begins once growth ceases and cellular defenses are mobilized to prevent tissue death. The duration of each phase is determined by water-use efficiency, accessible reserves, and intrinsic physiological thresholds (VOLAIRE, 2018; VOLAIRE et al., 2026). While avoiding dehydration extends the drought-resistance phase, progressive water exhaustion eventually shifts the physiological imperative from growth and hydration to surviving cellular water loss (VOLAIRE, 2018; VOLAIRE et al., 2026).

When environmental drying reaches the limits of dehydration avoidance, loss of turgor marks the transition from growth and drought resistance to survival, but it is not always fatal (VOLAIRE et al., 2026). Mortality thresholds vary among species and depend on traits such as hydraulic safety, protection of meristems and storage organs, and the capacity to tolerate cellular dehydration. Resurrection plants occupy the extreme end of this mortality continuum because their vegetative tissues can survive near-complete drying and resume metabolism after rehydration (GAFF; OLIVER, 2013; OLIVER et al., 2020). Desiccation tolerance is commonly associated with water contents near or below 0.1 g H₂O g⁻¹ dry mass; at approximately this hydration level, corresponding roughly to water potentials near -100 MPa under specified conditions, active metabolism is expected to cease (ALPERT, 2006). Surviving this transition requires coordinated defenses against mechanical strain during cell shrinkage, macromolecular destabilization and membrane phase changes as hydration shells are lost, and photo-oxidative stress when carbon assimilation ceases (HOEKSTRA et al., 2001; FARRANT; MOORE, 2011).

A foundational hypothesis posits that vegetative desiccation tolerance evolved in part through the evolutionary co-option and rewiring of conserved genetic programs originally selected to confer desiccation tolerance in orthodox seeds (OLIVER et al., 2000; COSTA et al., 2017). Central to this machinery are regulatory networks governed by seed-associated transcription factors such as ABI3 and ABI5, which orchestrate the accumulation of protective Late Embryogenesis Abundant (LEA) proteins, dehydrins, and non-reducing sugars that stabilize lipid bilayers, prevent protein aggregation, and drive intracellular vitrification into a protective glassy matrix (BARTELS; HUSSAIN, 2011; COSTA et al., 2017). Because green vegetative tissues face intense irradiance without metabolic sinks, resurrection plants deploy specialized photoprotective mechanisms, including Early Light-Induced Proteins (ELIPs) and the regulated dismantling of photosynthetic membranes through poikilochlorophylly, ensuring that metabolic reactivation can proceed upon rehydration without catastrophic photo-oxidation (FARRANT; MOORE, 2011; VIEIRA et al., 2022).

Beyond downstream protective adaptations, early perception of cellular water status coordinates plant physiological responses to water limitation. Because osmotic stress and membrane strain emerge as immediate biophysical consequences of water-potential gradients, cellular osmosensory perturbations can occur without requiring severe bulk tissue desiccation (HAMILTON; SCHLEGEL; HASWELL, 2015; MOORE et al., 2008). Before cells can activate physiological defenses, which span from stomatal closure and osmotic adjustment in avoidant tissues to protective vitrification and poikilochlorophylly in desiccation-tolerant species, they must rapidly detect water-potential gradients and turgor loss at the cellular boundary. Under hyperosmotic stress or dehydration, the efflux of water down the water-potential gradient drives protoplast shrinkage and turgor loss, generating mechanical tension and altering curvature within the lipid bilayer of the plasma membrane. Mechanosensitive and osmosensory channels transduce these physical changes into intracellular ion fluxes and signaling cascades (HAMILTON; SCHLEGEL; HASWELL, 2015). In vascular plants, early cellular sensing involves diverse candidate mechanisms, including receptor-like kinases monitoring cell-wall integrity (WOLF; HÉMATY; HÖFTE, 2012; BACETE et al., 2022), mechanosensitive channels of the small conductance-like (MSL) and mid1-complementing activity (MCA) families (NAKAGAWA et al., 2007; MAKSAEV; HASWELL, 2012; BASU; HASWELL, 2017), and calcium-permeable hyperosmolality-gated channels of the OSCA/TMEM63 family (YUAN et al., 2014; HOU et al., 2014; MURTHY et al., 2018). Among these candidates, the OSCA family has emerged as an especially compelling sensory system: whereas other mechanosensitive channels such as MSL often favor anion conductance or respond primarily to hypo-osmotic swelling (MAKSAEV; HASWELL, 2012), OSCA channels directly gate calcium influx in response to hyperosmotic dehydration and bilayer tension (YUAN et al., 2014; HOU et al., 2014; MURTHY et al., 2018). This direct biophysical coupling between cell volume reduction and cytosolic calcium signaling positions OSCA channels as key mediators translating external water deficit into downstream physiological responses. However, whether these early osmosensory mechanisms have diverged between plants that maintain hydration and those that tolerate severe desiccation remains poorly understood, demanding natural comparative systems that contrast these strategies within a shared evolutionary and ecological arena.

## 2. Velloziaceae as a system for studying adaptation to stressful environments

Translating theoretical frameworks of drought resistance and drought survival into evolutionary insights requires empirical models where contrasting water-economy strategies coexist within close phylogenetic proximity (GILBERT; MEDINA, 2016; VOLAIRE, 2018; VOLAIRE et al., 2026). In phylogenetically distant comparisons, such as contrasting herbaceous annual models with distantly related perennial resurrection plants, deep historical divergence introduces extensive confounding variation (PARDO; VANBUREN, 2021; CHÁVEZ MONTES et al., 2022). Lineages separated by hundreds of millions of years diverge across numerous life-history traits, including growth architecture (herbaceous versus arborescent or pseudostemmed woody habits), life-cycle length (ephemeral annuals versus long-lived perennials), reproductive systems (selfing versus obligate outcrossing), and fundamental genomic architectures such as ploidy levels, transposable element loads, and genome size (VANBUREN et al., 2018; PARDO; VANBUREN, 2021). Comparisons among closely related taxa provide a framework for examining genomic differences associated with contrasting physiological responses to water deficit while reducing the confounding effects of deep evolutionary divergence (VANBUREN et al., 2018; CHÁVEZ MONTES et al., 2022; MARKS et al., 2024; SCHUSTER; VANBUREN, 2026).

The family Velloziaceae exemplifies this comparative potential. It is a monocotyledonous family within Pandanales with lineages distributed across South America, Africa, Madagascar, and Asia, comprising the South American genera *Vellozia*, *Barbacenia*, and Andean *Barbaceniopsis*, African and Malagasy *Xerophyta*, and Asian *Acanthochlamys* (MELLO-SILVA et al., 2011; WANGA et al., 2021). Across this geographical radiation, the family combines ecological specialization with pronounced variation in responses to water limitation, encompassing classic vegetative desiccation-tolerant resurrection species in *Xerophyta*, *Vellozia*, and *Barbacenia*, alongside non-desiccation-tolerant, dehydration-avoidant lineages (ALCANTARA et al., 2015; COSTA et al., 2017; ALCANTARA; REE; MELLO-SILVA, 2018). This diversity provides rare opportunities to investigate the evolution of contrasting adaptive strategies within a shared phylogenetic context.

Although Velloziaceae displays a broad Gondwanan distribution, its greatest modern species richness and accelerated diversification are concentrated in the Brazilian *campos rupestres* (ALCANTARA; REE; MELLO-SILVA, 2018). These ancient mountaintop landscapes comprise a complex mosaic of exposed quartzite and sandstone outcrops interspersed with shallow soils and sandy depressions (SILVEIRA et al., 2016). Water availability in this ecosystem is constrained both temporally (by the marked alternation between wet summers and prolonged, severe dry winters) and spatially, as steep microtopographic gradients govern soil depth and water retention: bare rocky substrates have negligible water-holding capacity, whereas adjacent sandy patches retain moisture for longer periods (SILVEIRA et al., 2016). These substrates are also acidic and severely impoverished in plant-available phosphorus, promoting resource-acquisition specializations such as carboxylate-releasing cluster-like roots capable of mining nutrients from bare quartzite (TEODORO et al., 2019). This steep environmental template establishes the *campos rupestres* as an exceptional natural laboratory in which microedaphic partitioning is associated with divergent physiological solutions to water deficit and edaphic stress among closely related Velloziaceae lineages.

In response to this microedaphic mosaic, divergent adaptations to water limitation operate across ecological, whole-plant, and cellular scales. Across these rocky mosaics, closely related lineages with contrasting water-use strategies frequently co-occur in close spatial proximity (ALCANTARA et al., 2015; VIEIRA et al., 2025). Experimental comparisons between *Vellozia nivea* (desiccation-tolerant) and *V. intermedia* (dehydration-avoidant) illustrate the physiological trade-offs of these contrasting strategies: the avoidant congener sustained gas exchange, osmotic adjustment, and vegetative biomass under mild deficit but did not recover after severe experimental water exclusion, whereas the resurrection congener exhibited lower baseline growth but survived and recovered after rewatering (TEODORO et al., 2021). In that experiment, the turgor loss point was associated with an irreversible threshold in *V. intermedia*, but hydraulic failure was proposed rather than directly measured. Studies of other Velloziaceae resurrection species document poikilochlorophylly, organelle remodeling, antioxidant responses, and metabolic recovery after rehydration (VIEIRA et al., 2022, 2025); these findings illustrate family-level mechanisms rather than direct measurements in the focal species pair.

The focal species pair of this thesis, *Vellozia tubiflora* and *V. peripherica*, directly embodies this contrast between vegetative desiccation tolerance and dehydration avoidance. These congeneric taxa occur in the Serra da Canastra within the southern *campos rupestres* (Minas Gerais, Brazil) and were sampled under the same regional seasonality in a comparative field dataset (PINTO et al., 2025). Both species share perennial growth forms and the characteristic *Vellozia* pseudostem organization of persistent leaf sheaths and adventitious roots (MELLO-SILVA, 2005; ALCANTARA et al., 2015). Their shared regional climate, broadly similar growth habits, and shared generic placement reduce some of the confounding variation inherent in comparisons between distantly related model organisms and resurrection plants.

Despite sharing a genus and a macroscopic habit, seasonal field measurements demonstrate contrasting leaf-water responses in *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* (PINTO et al., 2025). During the late dry season, the desiccation-tolerant *V. tubiflora* reached approximately 15% leaf moisture, whereas the non-desiccation-tolerant, dehydration-avoidant *V. peripherica* maintained approximately 94-97% leaf moisture across the sampled seasons (PINTO et al., 2025). These field data establish contrasting water relations in the congeners. They do not, however, directly measure thylakoid dismantling, chlorophyll degradation, anhydrobiosis, or the timing of post-rehydration recovery in either focal species; those mechanisms are therefore treated here as hypotheses informed by studies of other Velloziaceae rather than as demonstrated traits of this pair.

Dissecting the genomic architecture underlying these divergent strategies requires high-continuity, chromosome-level reference assemblies. In *Vellozia*, anchoring nuclear assemblies into pseudochromosomes matching the stable diploid karyotype of the genus (2n = 18, x = 9; BRAZ et al., 2025) provides a consistent comparative baseline. Establishing phased, chromosome-scale resources allows lineage-specific differences to be examined within their chromosomal context, including conserved regions and structural rearrangements. This comparative system establishes an explicit evolutionary framework to investigate the genomic basis of water limitation, providing the foundation to evaluate both chromosome-scale structural evolution and early environmental perception across contrasting water-use economies.

## 3. Evolutionary genomics of water limitation: from reference assemblies to candidate signaling pathways

Comparative genomics provides a powerful framework to investigate how plant adaptations to water limitation are encoded and maintained across evolutionary timescales. Indeed, broad genomic surveys across vascular plants demonstrate that many features once considered unique hallmarks of vegetative desiccation tolerance actually represent ancestral, conserved components of general drought and osmotic stress responses (VANBUREN et al., 2018; SCHUSTER; VANBUREN, 2026). Against this shared baseline, studies in resurrection plants reveal recurrent, yet lineage-dependent, specializations for surviving extreme drying. These include the tandem proliferation of *ELIP* genes across independently evolved lineages, the duplication of protective chaperone modules, and the evolutionary rewiring of seed-associated regulatory networks in vegetative tissues (COSTA et al., 2017; VANBUREN et al., 2019; MARKS et al., 2024). Together, these precedents motivate integrative analyses of copy-number dynamics, gene order, sequence evolution, and regulatory context across contrasting water-use strategies, without assuming that every lineage relies on an identical molecular blueprint.

Identifying such genomic signatures and distinguishing them from technical artifacts depends on high-continuity reference assemblies that resolve repetitive and heterozygous regions. Fragmented or incompletely phased assemblies can distort gene models and copy-number inference, including by separating haplotypic alleles or collapsing highly similar loci (VURUPUTOOR et al., 2023). Pacific Biosciences High-Fidelity (PacBio HiFi) sequencing combines multikilobase reads with high consensus accuracy and improves the resolution of repetitive regions and haplotypes (WENGER et al., 2019). When integrated with chromosome conformation capture (Hi-C) and haplotype-aware assembly graphs, HiFi data can support phased, chromosome-scale assemblies (CHENG et al., 2021). For the desiccation-tolerant resurrection species *Vellozia tubiflora*, establishing a chromosome-level, haplotype-resolved reference genome through this HiFi-based strategy provides a structural foundation for investigating vegetative desiccation tolerance. Coupled with high-continuity genomic resources for its dehydration-avoidant congener *V. peripherica*, the *V. tubiflora* reference provides a basis for comparing genomic features associated with contrasting water-use strategies while reducing the influence of assembly artifacts.

Within Velloziaceae, existing reference resources provide important foundations but do not supply a phased, chromosome-scale comparison for the focal Neotropical congeners. Costa et al. (2017) assembled the octoploid genome of the African resurrection plant now treated as *Xerophyta schlechteri* from approximately 77× Illumina and 58× PacBio coverage. The 295.5-Mb haploid representation comprised 1,811 contigs and 896 scaffolds, with contig and scaffold N50 values of 1.11 and 1.67 Mb, respectively, and included a further 125 Mb of assembled haplotypes (COSTA et al., 2017). The genome was originally published under the name *Xerophyta viscosa*; the use of *X. schlechteri* here reflects the taxonomic reassignment of the Buffelskloof population studied in this research lineage (BEHNKE et al., 2013; RADERMACHER et al., 2019). The genome of the East Asian relict *Acanthochlamys bracteata* subsequently achieved chromosome-scale scaffolding (GAO et al., 2021), but its evolutionary distance from the South American taxa limits its value for fine-scale comparisons of recently diverged genomic features (GAO et al., 2021; WANGA et al., 2021). These resources therefore complement, rather than replace, phased chromosome-scale assemblies for *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica*, which are needed to compare allelic variation, tandem arrays, and structural organization in the focal congeneric system.

While high-continuity nuclear assemblies establish the essential structural blueprint of the cell, plant adaptation to water deficit operates across an integrated cytonuclear system. Cellular bioenergetics is fundamentally governed by semi-autonomous endosymbiotic organelles: chloroplasts and mitochondria. During favorable periods, these organelles drive energy transduction; under water limitation, however, impaired electron transport chains can contribute substantially to the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) (HOEKSTRA et al., 2001; CHAVES et al., 2003; FARRANT; MOORE, 2011). Under soil moisture deficit, stomatal closure restricts internal CO2 availability, causing photosynthetic electron carriers to become over-reduced and transferring excess excitation energy to molecular oxygen, which generates singlet oxygen, superoxide radicals, and hydrogen peroxide (CHAVES et al., 2003; FARRANT; MOORE, 2011). In resurrection lineages of Velloziaceae, foliar survival relies directly on mitigating this organellar oxidative damage, notably through the coordinated dismantling of thylakoid membranes and chlorophyll degradation during drying, followed by rapid ultrastructural recovery upon re-watering (VIEIRA et al., 2022). In the dehydration-avoidant *V. intermedia*, sustained hydration and gas exchange under moderate deficit contrast with the desiccation-and-recovery response of its tolerant congener, although organellar integrity was not directly measured (TEODORO et al., 2021). Coordinating these metabolic defenses requires continuous communication between genomic compartments, as all major energy-transducing multi-subunit complexes represent chimeric structures composed of both nuclear- and organelle-encoded peptides (SLOAN et al., 2018).

Investigating these bioenergetic adaptations across evolutionary timescales requires reconstructing complete organellar genomes, which follow structural and evolutionary trajectories distinct from the nuclear genome. In angiosperms, and specifically within Velloziaceae, chloroplast genomes maintain conserved quadripartite architectures composed of large and small single-copy regions separated by inverted repeats, while exhibiting lineage-specific boundary shifts and other structural variation (WANGA et al., 2021). Mitochondrial genomes in land plants display even greater structural dynamism. Unlike animal mitogenomes, plant mitochondrial genomes combine low nucleotide substitution rates in coding regions with extensive repetitive sequences and frequent recombination (SLOAN, 2013). This repeat-mediated recombination generates dynamic multipartite conformations, subgenomic circular isoforms, and branching molecules whose physical configurations cannot be represented by a static circular map (SLOAN, 2013). Long-read sequencing provides a means of resolving complex repeat architectures in chloroplast and mitochondrial genome assemblies for both *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica*. These resources provide a comparative sequence and structural framework for investigating organellar evolution and intracellular gene transfers, and for developing hypotheses about cytonuclear co-adaptation between lineages with contrasting water-use economies (SLOAN et al., 2018).

Complementing the organellar resources, comparative analysis of the nuclear genomes of *V. tubiflora* and *V. peripherica* provides a framework for examining genomic differences in the context of the contrasting water-use strategies described for these regional congeners (PINTO et al., 2025). Genomic attributes identified in an isolated taxon may reflect phylogenetic history and general adaptations to nutrient-poor quartzite substrates as well as its water-use strategy.

Establishing an annotated, chromosome-scale nuclear genome for *V. peripherica* completes this pairwise comparative framework. Methodological parity is essential here. Generating and annotating both nuclear genomes with comparable sequencing and bioinformatic procedures helps reduce the risk of technical artifacts being interpreted as biological divergence (VURUPUTOOR et al., 2023). Across their shared diploid karyotype (2n = 18), macro-synteny and chromosomal collinearity can establish whether the genomes remain broadly collinear or differ through large structural rearrangements. Physical synteny also anchors duplicated genes in their chromosomal neighborhoods and supports their classification into tandem, proximal, segmental, or whole-genome-associated duplication patterns (WANG et al., 2012). Evaluating nonsynonymous-to-synonymous substitution rate ratios ($ω = dN/dS$) across shared orthologs tests whether positive selection or relaxed functional constraint accompanied ecological divergence in *campos rupestres* (YANG; BIELAWSKI, 2000).

At the gene-family level, this comparative framework supports the hypothesis that the evolutionary divergence between vegetative desiccation tolerance and dehydration avoidance may stem not only from downstream protective machinery, but also from differences in early cellular stress perception and signaling. While downstream protective mechanisms, including vitrification, LEA protein accumulation, and poikilochlorophylly, have been extensively characterized across resurrection lineages, whether upstream mechanosensory and osmosensory channels have diversified in copy number, structural features, or selective constraints between resurrection and avoidant congeners remains an open evolutionary question. Among candidate gene families mediating osmotic and mechanical sensing, the OSCA family represents a compelling system to test whether contrasting water-use strategies in *Vellozia* are accompanied by lineage-specific diversification in cellular sensory repertoires.

### The OSCA gene family as a target for comparative evolutionary analysis

Investigating how this mechanosensory machinery diversified across ecologically contrasting lineages requires grounding its evolutionary trajectory in its resolved biophysical and structural properties. High-resolution cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) structures of OSCA1.1, OSCA1.2, and OSCA3.1 revealed a conserved homodimeric architecture related to the eukaryotic TMEM16 superfamily, with each subunit containing 11 transmembrane helices and an independent ion-conduction pathway (JOJOA-CRUZ et al., 2018; ZHANG et al., 2018; LIU; WANG; SUN, 2018). The structures identified hydrophobic pore constrictions and extended intracellular helices whose movements have been proposed to couple membrane tension to pore opening; electrophysiological and mutational analyses support mechanosensitivity, but the detailed gating transitions remain model-based (JOJOA-CRUZ et al., 2018; LIU; WANG; SUN, 2018; ZHANG et al., 2018). Functional assays show that several OSCA proteins generate mechanically activated, non-selective cation currents, while particular family members mediate calcium-permeable responses to hyperosmotic stimuli (YUAN et al., 2014; MURTHY et al., 2018). The resulting changes in cytosolic calcium can feed into stimulus-specific signaling pathways. For example, BIK1 phosphorylates and activates OSCA1.3 during immune signaling in guard cells; this mechanism is specific to stomatal immunity and was not required for ABA-induced stomatal closure (THOR et al., 2020).

This resolved biophysical architecture establishes the OSCA family as a tractable molecular system for examining how early environmental perception evolves across plant lineages. Phylogenetic analyses indicate that the OSCA family originated before land plants and diversified during plant evolution; Wu et al. (2022) consequently hypothesized that OSCA-mediated water-stress sensing acquired particular importance during terrestrialization. Across green plants, chlorophyte algae possess ancestral OSCA channels, whereas four major phylogenetic clades are recovered across land plants, including angiosperms (WU et al., 2022; LIU et al., 2022). Early-diverging land plants such as bryophytes and lycophytes show slower OSCA sequence evolution than seed plants, in which lineage-specific expansions also occurred (WU et al., 2022). The coincidence between these evolutionary patterns and the emergence of leaves, roots, stomata, and vascular transport motivates hypotheses about the contribution of osmosensing to terrestrial water balance but does not by itself demonstrate a causal relationship (WU et al., 2022).

Subsequent genome-wide investigations across diverse angiosperms have revealed that the OSCA family has expanded dynamically through whole-genome, segmental, and tandem gene duplications while retaining four major phylogenetic clades. Systematic surveys in monocots, such as rice (*Oryza sativa*, harboring 11 members; LI et al., 2015; ZHAI et al., 2020) and maize (*Zea mays*, with 12 members; DING et al., 2019; CAO et al., 2020), alongside eudicots such as wild tomato (*Solanum habrochaites*, with 11 members; MIAO et al., 2022) and the paleopolyploid soybean (*Glycine max*, with 20 members; LIU et al., 2022), demonstrate lineage-specific variation in family size. Syntenic and collinearity analyses indicate that segmental or whole-genome duplication accounts for many retained paralogous pairs, whereas tandem duplication has amplified particular lineages in some taxa (DING et al., 2019; LIU et al., 2022). Promoter analyses also identify candidate stress-responsive *cis*-elements, including ABRE, MBS, and DRE motifs; together with tissue- and treatment-dependent expression patterns, these observations generate hypotheses about regulatory diversification that require functional validation (DING et al., 2019; CAO et al., 2020; LIU et al., 2022).

Functional studies indicate substantial diversity within the OSCA family, although they do not yet establish a simple division of labor among all four clades. AtOSCA1.1 mediates hyperosmotic calcium responses in *Arabidopsis*, and heterologous expression of the rice Clade 1 protein OsOSCA1.4 restores calcium signaling, root-growth responses, stomatal movement, and leaf water-loss phenotypes in *osca1* plants exposed to hyperosmotic or salt treatments (YUAN et al., 2014; ZHAI et al., 2020). In soybean, five tested Clade 1 proteins restored the acute hyperosmolality-induced calcium response of *osca1*, whereas tested proteins from other clades did not; nevertheless, two Clade 3 proteins restored drought survival without restoring that acute calcium phenotype (LIU et al., 2022). Electrophysiological comparisons likewise reveal differences in activation thresholds, conductance, and kinetics among OSCA paralogs, including non-selective cation conductance in tested Clade 1 proteins (ZHANG et al., 2018; MURTHY et al., 2018). OSCA1.3 provides an additional example of stimulus specificity: BIK1-dependent phosphorylation activates this channel in guard-cell immune signaling, but the same proteins are dispensable for ABA-induced closure (THOR et al., 2020). These findings support functional diversification among paralogs while leaving the physiological roles of many family members unresolved.

Despite these mechanistic insights, the evolutionary literature on OSCA channels remains constrained by a pronounced taxonomic and ecological bias. Nearly all functional and genomic characterizations have focused on annual model organisms or cultivated mesic crops, leaving the evolutionary dynamics of mechanosensation in wild plants adapted to extreme water limitation largely unexplored. In particular, it remains unknown whether mechanosensory channel repertoires have co-evolved with contrasting whole-plant adaptations to seasonal drought. Lineages adapted to survive complete vegetative drying through anhydrobiosis, for example, might possess distinct OSCA copy numbers, structural variants affecting channel gating, or altered activation thresholds during severe cell dehydration; conversely, dehydration-avoidant relatives that rely on maintaining positive turgor might experience different selective constraints on OSCA paralogs involved in early osmotic responses. Comparative genomic analyses can identify candidate differences in OSCA repertoires and sequence evolution, generating hypotheses for subsequent functional investigation. Examining these differences requires comparative systems that contrast desiccation tolerance with dehydration avoidance within a shared phylogenetic framework, supported by high-continuity genomic assemblies.

## 4. Computational literacy, university outreach, and reproducible practices in biological research

Modern genomic research, exemplified by the chromosome-scale assemblies and evolutionary analyses developed throughout this thesis, has firmly established biology as an inherently data-intensive science. Generating, processing, and interpreting high-throughput sequencing data requires computational literacy, documented workflows, and consistent data management to support the verifiability and reproducibility of scientific findings (WILSON et al., 2017). However, life science curricula have historically prioritized wet-lab training, creating a persistent gap between the exponential growth of biological datasets and the formal computational competencies accessible to bioscientists (BARONE; WILLIAMS; MICKLOS, 2017; ZUVANOV et al., 2021).

This computational training gap is being reshaped by the rapid emergence of generative artificial intelligence and large language models (LLMs). Automated coding assistants and multi-agent AI systems can generate analytical scripts and coordinate bioinformatic tools into multistep workflows, although their outputs still require expert validation (JIANG et al., 2025; BRANDA et al., 2026). However, this rapid technological shift transforms rather than diminishes the definition of bioinformatics expertise (GOH et al., 2026). While constructing a bioinformatic pipeline has become mechanically accessible, evaluating whether its biological assumptions are valid, its parameters are calibrated, and its outputs reflect genuine biological signal rather than computational artifacts demands advanced critical discernment (GOH et al., 2026). As bioinformatics transitions from manual data processing toward theory-driven, critically validated biological synthesis (ZHANG, 2026), foundational computational literacy becomes even more indispensable. This context reinforces the relevance of foundational programming education, which is the focus of the outreach initiative examined in this thesis.

Addressing this training gap and cultivating the methodological skepticism required for modern computational biology requires dedicated institutional mechanisms. In Brazilian public higher education, the integration of research, teaching, and university outreach is a constitutional principle of indivisibility (BRASIL, 1988, art. 207) and a core guideline of national higher education policy (FORPROEX, 2012). Within this framework, advanced academic formation extends beyond conducting specialized research in isolation; it includes the institutional responsibility to democratize technical competencies and return scientific knowledge to the broader community. University outreach acts as a vital conduit for this commitment, providing an effective mechanism to disseminate computational skills and reproducible analytical practices to early-career researchers and students across diverse institutions.

Educational outreach initiatives specifically designed for the life sciences illustrate this integrative role. The *Brazilian Python Workshop for Biological Data*, for example, was established to address this national gap in computational training by engaging undergraduate and graduate students in hands-on programming with authentic biological datasets (ZUVANOV et al., 2021). Building upon the foundations of this initial edition, the initiative was subsequently continued and refined over successive offerings, expanding its reach and pedagogical strategies to strengthen computational autonomy and analytical reproducibility among emerging bioscientists (CRESTANA et al., 2026). By connecting foundational programming logic directly to biological data analysis, such sustained outreach programs provide an adaptable framework for computational training across the life sciences. Within this thesis, the workshop is examined through its design, implementation, and participant outcomes.

Beyond educational outreach, this commitment to computational transparency and reproducible science directly governs the analytical workflows developed throughout this thesis. The analytical workflows for the comparative phylogenomics of Velloziaceae and the evolutionary characterization of the OSCA mechanosensory channel family are maintained in version-controlled GitHub repositories, but these resources are not yet publicly available. Providing complete pipelines, analytical scripts, and execution environments supports reproducibility (WILSON et al., 2017) and FAIR-aligned stewardship of the associated scientific data (WILKINSON et al., 2016). Together, the genomic research workflows and the educational outreach initiative articulate two complementary dimensions of contemporary academic practice: implementing reproducible computational analyses in plant evolutionary genomics while expanding computational literacy and open science practices within the broader scientific community.

## Scope and objectives of the doctoral thesis

### General objective

To investigate the genomic and evolutionary basis of contrasting strategies for coping with water limitation in Velloziaceae through the comparative analysis of available genomic resources, with particular emphasis on the evolution and diversification of the OSCA gene family, while advancing computational training, reproducible practices, and university outreach for biological data analysis.

### Specific objectives

1. To compare the nuclear genomes of Vellozia tubiflora and V. peripherica, examining patterns of gene family expansion and contraction, and coding-sequence divergence, identifying genomic differences potentially associated with desiccation tolerance and dehydration avoidance.

2. Comparative analysis of the OSCA gene family to identify and characterize OSCA genes in both species, examining their phylogenetic relationships, gene structures, conserved domains, and chromosomal distribution.

3. To investigate the evolution and diversification of the OSCA family through analyses of duplication patterns, interspecific synteny, and coding-sequence divergence.

4. To characterize predicted promoter cis-regulatory elements and tissue expression profiles of OSCA genes using available RNA-seq data, identifying candidates for future functional investigation of responses to water limitation.

5. Evaluation of the design, implementation, and participant outcomes of the *Brazilian Python Workshop for Biological Data* as a university outreach initiative for computational training in the life sciences.

## Thesis organization

This thesis is organized into five chapters, encompassing collaborative genomic resource development, comparative analyses, and computational training. Collaborators generated the nuclear and organellar genome assemblies, which provide the foundation for the comparative investigations developed in this thesis. The corresponding chapter specifies the candidate’s contributions to each collaborative study.

Chapter 1 presents the chromosome-scale, haplotype-resolved reference genome of the desiccation-tolerant species Vellozia tubiflora. This collaborative work provides the genomic resource and biological context for the subsequent comparative analyses.

Chapter 2 presents the chloroplast and mitochondrial genome resources for V. tubiflora and V. peripherica and examines their gene content, structural organization, and evolutionary differences through comparative analyses.

Chapter 3 investigates genome-wide similarities and differences between Vellozia tubiflora and V. peripherica, examining patterns of gene family expansion and contraction. These analyses provide a broad comparative perspective on genomic features potentially associated with desiccation tolerance and dehydration avoidance, establishing the context for the focused investigation of the OSCA gene family in Chapter 4.

Chapter 4 focuses on the comparative characterization of the OSCA gene family in both species. It examines phylogenetic relationships, gene structures, conserved domains, chromosomal distribution, duplication patterns, and coding-sequence divergence. Promoter analyses and available tissue expression data provide complementary evidence for generating hypotheses about the diversification of this family.

Chapter 5 examines the design, implementation, and participant outcomes of the Brazilian Python Workshop for Biological Data, addressing introductory programming education and computational training for bioscientists.

The general discussion integrates the comparative genomic findings, considers their implications and limitations for understanding contrasting responses to water limitation, and discusses the contribution of computational training to biological research.
