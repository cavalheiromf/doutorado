ABSTRACT
The Braziliancampos rupestres, a biodiversity hotspot, experiences long droughts. Plants in this region have developed various strategies to handle water scarcity, particularly those from the Velloziaceaefamily.WhileV. niveatolerates dehydration bydesiccating their leaves,V. intermediacan remain evergreen,even after four months without rain.This leads us to the question whether they also sense water deficit differently. To address this question, we have decided to study the OSCA gene family, and its protein interaction networks in both species.TheOSCA gene family encodestotransmembrane proteins responsible for the [Ca2+]iincrease in response to osmotic stress in plants.The studycomprisedan examination of gene expression patternsin different plant tissues andin response to variations in water availability. In addition, weinvestigate thecis-regulatory elementscompositionpresented in the promoter regionand therelationshipof protein-proteininteraction networksof OSCA genesin both species.The analysis revealed tissue-specific and responsive expressions of OSCA genes to varying water conditions. Examining OSCA gene promoter regions revealed significant differences in drought-responsive elements within the orthologous OSCA2 and OSCA3 genes. In addition, our investigation of protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks revealed distinct connectivity patterns between these species, indicating the possibility of stress-driven specialization of OSCA orthologues inV. niveaandV. intermedia.We have already identifiedT-DNA insertional mutants for theArabidopsisOSCAgene family.OSCA2 and OSCA3Arabidopsismutantsas well asArabidopsisoverexpressing theVelloziaorthologswill besubjected todrought and heatstressesto elucidatethe specific functions of theseOSCA genesin plants.

Os Campos Rupestres brasileiros, umhotspotdebiodiversidade, sofre com períodos prolongados de seca.As plantas nessa região desenvolveram várias estratégias para lidar com a escassez de água, especialmente aquelas da família Velloziaceae. Enquanto aVellozia niveatolera a desidratação dessecando suas folhas,Vellozia intermediapermanece sempre-verde, mesmo após quatro meses sem chuva. Isso nos leva à pergunta se elas também percebem a falta de água de maneira diferente. Para abordar essa pergunta, decidimos estudar a família de genes OSCA e suas redes de interação de proteínas em ambas as espécies. A família de genes OSCA codifica proteínas transmembrana responsáveis pelo aumento de [Ca2+]iem resposta ao estresse osmótico nas plantas. O estudo compreendeu uma análise dos padrões de expressão gênica em diferentes tecidos vegetais e em resposta a variações na disponibilidade de água. Além disso, investigamos a composição dos elementos cis-regulatórios presentes na região promotora e a relação das redes de interação de proteína-proteína dos genes OSCA em ambas as espécies. A análise revelou expressões específicas de tecido e responsivas dos genes OSCA em diferentes condições de disponibilidade de água. A análise das regiões promotoras dos genes OSCA revelou diferenças significativas nos elementos responsivos à seca nos genes ortólogos OSCA2 e OSCA3. Além disso, nossa investigação das redes de interação de proteína-proteína (PPI) revelou padrões distintos de conectividade entre essas espécies, indicando a possibilidade de especialização impulsionada pelo estresse dos ortólogos OSCA emV. niveaeV. intermedia. Já identificamos mutantes insercionais de T-DNA para a família de genes OSCA emArabidopsis. MutantesdeArabidopsisparaOSCA2 e OSCA3, bem comoconstruções emArabidopsisque superexpressam os ortólogos deVellozia, serão submetidos a estresses por seca e calor para elucidar as funções específicas desses genes OSCA nas plantas.

INTRODUCTION
Although the mechanisms of drought response are wellknownand have beenextensively studied, the precise nature of the signals that enable plants to detect changes in water availability needs to be elucidated(Lamers, Meer, van der e Testerink, 2020). According to a recent review(Gorgueset al., 2022), the authors describe three classes of perception mechanisms that may detect osmotic signals:The initial mechanism consists of membrane sensors responsible in detecting local osmotic imbalances. The second mechanism involves disruptions in the integrity of the cell wall. The third mechanism relies on alterations in membrane tension.
Regarding to membrane sensors in plants, histidine kinases, such as AtHK1, are implicated in salt stress, drought, and ABA signaling(Tranet al., 2007). By phosphorylating histidine residues that regulate osmotic-responsive gene expression, the AtHK1 kinase pathway in Arabidopsis functions as a positive regulator of stress conditions(Osakabeet al., 2013). However, AtHK1's function as the primary osmosensor in plants has been questioned since loss-of-function alleles lack hyperosmotic signaling-related phenotypes(Kumar, Jane e Verslues, 2013).
Anothermechanismrelated tostress perceptionisdisruption to the integrity of the cell wall. Transmembrane receptor-like kinases (RLKs), such as Catharanthus roseus RLK1-Like kinases (CrRLK), Wall Associated Kinases (WAK), Lectin Receptor-Kinase (LRK), Proline-rich Extensin-like Receptor Kinases (PERKs), and formins, function as sensors of the cellwall integrity(Wolf, Hématy e Höfte, 2012). Several kinases, including THESEUS (THE) CrRLK, have dual roles in maintaining cellular mechanics and regulating the accumulation of abscisic acid (ABA) in response to osmotic stress(Baceteet al., 2022).Another CrRLK that contributes to cell wall sensing is FERONIA (FER).FER induces a late calcium influx in the cell under salt stress, which is required to sustain cell wall reinforcement(Fenget al., 2018).In FER loss-of-functionexperiments, the absence of FER increases salt sensitivity while decreasing osmotic stress and it has been suggested that FER would detect the influence of sodium ions on pectin filament organization rather than changes in turgor pressure(Chenet al., 2016; Fenget al., 2018).
Another type of sensor capable of detecting osmotic stress in plantsarethemechanosensitive ion channels, especially the ones which are responsible for Ca2+intake(Basu e Haswell, 2017; Hartmannet al., 2021; Kurusuet al., 2013). Calcium cations are commonly implicated as rapid secondary messengers for different plant responses(WHITE, 2003). Plants exhibit a rapid elevation in the concentration of cytosolic Ca2+ions upon exposure to mechanical stimuli, such as wind, touch, or osmotic changes(Hartmannet al., 2021; Ketehouliet al., 2022). This phenomenon produces a localized response, while also potentially inducing distant responses by propagating electrical signals (Gorgueset al., 2022). One example of mechanosensitive calcium channel is the MscS-like (MSL) proteins. The MSL10 gene codifies a true mechanosensitive channel that modulates cytoplasmic calcium increases, ROS accumulation, transcriptional regulation, and programmed cell death in response to hypo-osmotic shock and has preference for anions rather than cations(Maksaev e Haswell, 2012). Other mechanosensitive channels, including MCA1 and its paralog MCA2, have been discovered in Arabidopsis(Nakagawaet al., 2007). However, research indicates that mca1 and mca2 mutants exhibited no change in calcium influx in response to osmotic stimulation,suggestingthat these channels may not play a significant role in osmosensing(Yamanakaet al., 2010), but new studies are essential to fully investigated their response. Piezo channels are another protein pointed as mechanosensory. However, these channels are less studied compared to other mechanosensitive channels in plants, but studies may indicate their involvement in stress response and developmental processes(Kauret al., 2023).
Recent research on the hyperosmolality-gated calcium-permeable channels (OSCA) gene family shed light on the critical role these proteins play in a plant's ability to detect changes in osmolality, including drought(Caoet al., 2020; Yuanet al., 2014; Zhaiet al., 2020, 2021). OSCA channels are mechanosensitive ion channels that are distributed in four clades andgenerallyconsist of two transmembrane domains and one cytosolic domain, with one of the transmembrane domains permeable to calcium ions(Houet al., 2014; Yuanet al., 2014; Zhanget al., 2018). These channels are activated by osmolality-induced variations in membrane pressure, resulting in an increase in cytosolic free calcium concentration (YUAN et al., 2014). This increase induces numerous physiological and biochemical responses in plants, some of which have beenmentionedpreviously.
Despite the comprehensive study of the OSCA gene family in numerous plant species, it is notable that the complete elucidation of the functional attributes related to osmotic stress, particularly in the context of drought response, is limited to a small number of OSCA gene family members.(Dinget al., 2019; Keet al., 2022; Liet al., 2015; Liuet al., 2022; Miaoet al., 2022; Yanget al., 2022, 2019; Yinet al., 2021). The overexpression of ZmOSCA2.4 in Arabidopsis conferred enhanced drought tolerance, whereas others may play crucial roles in response to salt and ABA treatments. In rice, Liet al. (2015) found that the OSCA gene family respondsdifferentlyto drought stress, with some members upregulating and others downregulating. According to the findings, the OSCA gene familyare involved in osmotic related signaling pathways and play crucial roles in rice's responses to diverse abiotic stresses, including drought.(Li et al., 2015).
Therefore,the investigation of OSCA channels, their function in perception, and their key interactors is a promising avenue for the development of strategies to improving crop performance underwater deficitconditions. Additional research is required to fully comprehend the molecular mechanisms of OSCA channels and their regulation in response to droughtstress.
Our research team has focused on the physiological and molecularresponsesofdroughtin endemic Brazilian species found incamposrupestres, anecoregionknown for its stony outcrops and distinct water regime(Oliveiraet al., 2016). Two plant species that are uniquely adapted to this environment,Vellozia niveaandVellozia intermedia, are exclusive to thecamposrupestresand are members of the family Velloziaceae(Alcantaraet al., 2015; Alcantara, Ree e Mello-Silva, 2018). The unique phenotypes with whichV. niveaandV. intermediarespond to drought is one of the most distinguishing characteristics between the two species.V.niveais desiccant-tolerant and entirely desiccates both its root system and leaves when exposed to dehydration. In contrast, the evergreen speciesV.intermediademonstrates a unique survival strategy by maintaining a high relative water content and sustaining its photosynthetic capacity during periods of water scarcity.((Alcantaraet al., 2015; Alcantara, Ree e Mello-Silva, 2018).
Due to its adaptations to environments with limited water resources, theVelloziaspecies is an ideal model for studying the perception and response of plants to drought.the study of OSCA ion channel molecular processes and their interactions with signaling proteins and transcription factors may shed insight on theperceptionstrategies ofV. intermediaandV. niveawateravailability.In this regard, the genome-wide analysis of the OSCA gene family is the initial step in identifying the top candidates that regulate water stressperception.
Wewillpresenta comparative genomic study, including the identification and distribution of OSCAs in thegenome ofV. niveaandV. intermedia,their expression patterns in different tissues and under distinct water availability conditions, and the identification of significantinteractors usingprotein-protein interaction networks.By examining the OSCA gene family in Velloziaceae species, this research seeks to contribute to the discovery of other genes that might be involved in water scarcity perception and contribute to elucidate the role of different OSCA proteins as drought sensors.

MATERIAL AND METHODS
Identification of OSCA gene family inV. intermediaandV. niveaand OSCA domains
In order to identify the OSCA protein in these genomes, we used the conserved PFAM accession number 02714 (RSN1_7TM) from the Pfam databasev. 35.0to construct HMM profilesusing the “hmmbuild” functionand query theV. intermediaandV. niveausing “hmmsearch”in ourproteindatabases.Next, we evaluated every non-redundant sequence using the InterProScan database, which meant that in occurrences of alternative splicing, we selected the longest transcript to represent the predicted protein.
Sequencealignment andphylogeneticanalysis
The MAFFT toolv.7(Katoh, Rozewicki e Yamada, 2018)was utilized to align the full-length amino acid sequences of all OSCA proteins fromV. intermedia,V. nivea,Arabidopsisthaliana,Sorghum bicolor,Oryza sativa, andZea maysusing the default setting parameters. Subsequently, the alignment files were analyzed using IQ-Treev.1.6.11(Nguyenet al., 2015),which determined the appropriate amino acid substitution model and constructed a phylogenetic tree using a bootstrap test, set with1000 replications. The construction of the phylogenetic tree was visualized using the iTOL(Letunic e Bork, 2021).
Chromosome location,interspecies synteny analysisand selective pressure
The location of the OSCA genes identified was obtained from the GFF annotation file of each species, and a visualization of their positions on the chromosomes was created using theTBtoolsv.1.122(Chenet al., 2020).To study the relationships of OSCA genes that are orthologous betweenV. intermediaandVniv, we employed theMCScan- Python version (https://github.com/tanghaibao/jcvi/) to analyze collinear blocks, using the default parameters, and then visualized the results using available scripts.The ratio of nonsynonymous (Ka) to synonymous (Ks) substitutions in OSCA CDS sequences, with exception of VnOSCA2.3was computed. Initially, codon alignment was executed using PAL2NAL(Suyama, Torrents e Bork, 2006), followed by the determination of the Ka/Ks ratio through PAML(Yang, 2007).
Gene promoter region analysis
In the annotation files for both species, we used the BEDTools toolbox v.2.30.0(Quinlan e Hall, 2010)with the "flank" parameter to obtain gene promoter regions coordinates and “getfasta” to extract 2kb length sequence of each promoter. Following that, we examined these promoter sequences using the PlantCARE database(Lescotet al., 2002). To avoid counting multiple times, we concatenated overlapping elements of the same kind with “merge” parameter. The elements were then classified according to their functions, with categories such as "Light responsive", "Growth and development", "Anaerobic responsive", "Auxin responsive", "Gibberellin responsive", "Wounding/pathogen responsive", "Cold responsive", "Ethylene responsive", "Stress responsive", "ABA responsive", "Drought responsive", "Jasmonic acid responsive", "Salicylic acid responsive.", “Specific binding site”, “Other”, and “Core element/enhancer promoter”(Quet al., 2019).
Experimental design for gene expression analysis
One population each ofV. intermediaandV. niveawere assessed for this study(coordinates 20°29'07.2"S, 46°30'49.8"W, 1002 m elevation, and 20°29'15.8"S, 46°32'05.3"W, 950 m elevation, respectively)that occupycamposrupestresareas located approximately 2.3 km apart on a privateproperty adjacent to the Serra da Canastra National Park, in the municipality of Delfinópolis, Minas Geraisstate. All individuals utilized in this study were retrieved from these populations (Gerhardt et al., inpreparation).
To analyze the expression patterns of OSCA genes inV. niveaandV. intermediatissues, three replicates of each material type were examined. The analysis included a variety of plant tissues, including roots, stems, leaves, and flowers, from both plant species. In addition, seedlings from both species were collected, planted, and evaluated at specific time intervals: 25 and 30 days after planting (DAP) forV. nivea, and 30, 45, and 52 DAP forV. intermedia. This research also evaluated the seedlings produced from these seeds. To preserve sample integrity, all tissue samples, including seeds and seedlings, were immediately thawed with liquid nitrogen, and then stored at -80°C for subsequent analysis.
For the field experiment under distinct water regimes, five individuals each ofV. intermediaandV. niveaexhibiting similar sizes were selected in theirCamposRupestresareas and tagged on 26 September 2017 for sampling aiming at RNA-seq analyses acrossmultiple seasons. Blades of two mature leaves and approximately 5 to 10-cm deep roots were sampledfrom these same individuals on 26 September 2017, 6 October 2017, 18 April 2018 and 23 July 2018,corresponding tolatedry (LD),earlyrainy (ER),laterainy (LR), andearlydry (ED) seasons,respectively. Thus, 40 samples were collected for each species (five individuals, two tissue types, fourseasons). The LD sampling was made at the end of an approximately four-month period lacking anysignificant precipitation. For RNA extractions, leaf blade and root samples were immediately frozen inplastic vials on dry ice, transported to the laboratory, and stored at -80°C.
RNA extraction, sequencing, and quantification
Total RNA was extracted fromsamplesusing a CTAB protocol(Chang, Puryear e Cairney, 1993).For atlas samples, RNA sequencing libraries were generated via poly-A selection and Illumina HiSeq2500 sequencing, yielding 30Mpaired-end reads per sample. For field experiment samples, RNA sequencing libraries were constructed by depletion and Illumina NovaSeq 6000 sequencing was performed, yielding 50Mpaired-end reads per sample.The samples were aligned to the annotated genome of their respective species using the STAR alignerv2.7.10(Dobinet al., 2013), following ENCONDE’s guidelines for best practices of eukaryotic RNA sequencing(Hitzet al., 2016), including the additional“--geneCounts'' quantification option.Each expression count matrix dataset obtained in the previous step was then normalized to transcripts per million for further analysis (TPM).
Networkand enrichmentanalysis
Theproteomes ofV. intermediaandV. niveawere annotatedusing STRINGdb (v11.5)(Szklarczyket al., 2021).Using the STRINGdb API to retrieve the interaction connections, we constructed a comprehensive protein-protein interaction (PPI) network for each species, with an emphasis on interactions involving OSCA genes. These interactions were transformed into an undirected graph, with proteins representing nodes and known interactions representing edges. To assure robustness, only interactions with a "ppi_score" of at least 0.4 were retained for further analysis.For the enrichment network analysis, we consideredbiological processterms with FDR < 0.05 which appeared at least in 0.1 ratio of the input dataset.To examine the OSCA interaction network in each species, wealsoused BLASTKoala to assess KEGG Orthology (KO) for their interactors(Kanehisaet al., 2016; Kanehisa, Sato e Morishima, 2016).

The OSCA gene familyhasevolved from differentoriginsinV. niveaandV. intermedia
WesearchedV. intermedia(Vint)andV. nivea(Vniv)protein datasets searching for PF02714 domain, which corresponds to the formation of calcium permeable channel (Maityet al., 2019). According to ourfindings, weobservedthatVinthas 16 genes encoding 18 proteins, whileVnivhas 15 genes encoding 16 proteins(Supplem. Table 1 and 2, respectively), as well as theywere grouped intofour distinct clades, as previously observed in other plant species(Dinget al., 2019; Liet al., 2015; Yanget al., 2019; Yinet al., 2021; Yuanet al., 2014)(Fig. 1A).The OSCA genes annotated in our datasethave five genes encoding OSCA1, seven genes encoding OSCA2, and two genes encoding OSCA3, for both species, andVnivhas a single gene encoding OSCA4, whereasVintshows one more member of OSCA4.
The OSCA genes in both species are syntenic and are found in collinear blocks on the chromosomes (Fig. 1B).OSCA genes are distributed similarly across the chromosomes of both species, with the genes located on chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8. However, there are some variances in the number of genes present on each chromosome (Fig. 1C).For instance, both specieshave two genes on chromosome 1.In contrast,Vnivhas four genes on chromosome 2, whereasVinthas five genes on the same chromosome.Vinthas three genes on chromosome 3,whileVnivhas four genes. Similarly, thenumberof genes on chromosomes 4, 6, and 8are different(Fig. 1C).
Although the number of OSCA1 and OSCA2 genes in both species is similar, their evolutionary origins are distinct, as shown by the results of phylogenetic tree analysis, synteny analysis, and gene distribution on their chromosomes. The nomenclature strategy used in this work was in line with how OSCAs are categorized in other monocots, and we placed a strong emphasis on the genetic similarity between the two species under our study. After the gene name, letter notations have been assigned to the genes that were duplicated after the species divergence.

OSCAorthologuesbetweenV. niveaandV. intermediaaresubjected topurifying selection
We estimated the ratio of nonsynonymous (Ka) to synonymous (Ks) substitutions in OSCA's CDS sequencestocomprehend the evolutionary constraints working on the OSCA gene family in both species. We calculated these metrics for 21 ortholog gene pairs and 9 paralog gene pairs (4 for Vint and 5 for Vniv; Table1). We were unable to establish a Ka/Ks ratio for VnOSCA2.1a-VnOSCA2.1c, perhaps because VnOSCA2.1a has one domain but VnOSCA2.1c has three (Fig. 2), resulting in lower alignment rates. The overall Ka/Ks of ortholog OSCA gene pairs is 1, suggesting that OSCA genes may have undergone substantial purifying and selection pressure throughout evolution (Table1), except for ViOSCA1.3-VnOSCA1.3c, which has a ration near to 1, indicating neutral selection. The Ka/Ks analysis for the paralogues revealed that the Ka/Ks value for Vint was 1, suggesting that most of these genes were exposed to purifying selection and evolved extremely conservatively. However, whereas most Vniv paralogues are subject to purifying selection (Ka/Ks ratios<1), the pair VnOSCA1.3a-VnOSCA1.3c has a Ka/Ks ratio greater than one, indicating positive selection(Table 1).

In order to further investigate the previous findings, we conducted an analysis of gene expression at four different timepoints that corresponded to distinct water availability conditions (LD, ER, LR, and ED). This analysis was performed on the roots and leaves of both Velloziaspecies. (Fig. 4; see topic 5.1.4 in Material and Methods for more details).
The results showed that the expression of the OSCA1.1 orthologous genes differed considerably between the two species.(Fig. 4). The expression ofOSCA1.1 was observed to be more pronounced intheVnivorthologue(35±26TPM)than in Vintorthologues (2±0.4TPMand 13±4TPMfor ViOSCA1.1a and ViOSCA1.1b, respectively), as well as exhibiting a pattern of expression associated with periods of precipitation (Fig. 4). Specifically, the expression of this gene was higher in the roots of Vniv(40±21TPM) as well asin the leaves during rainy periods(57±13TPM). The differences are also consistent with the patterns previously observed, where the ViOSCA1.1 had higher TPM values when compared to its counterparts (Fig. 3).
Despite identifying a positive selection signal in the orthologous pair VnOSCA1.3a-VnOSCA1.3c, our analysis did not reveal substantial transcript abundance in either tissue for both species. Specifically, ViOSCA1.3 exhibited an expression level of 8 ± 5 TPM (Transcripts Per Million) in roots and 2 ± 0.5 TPM in leaves. Interestingly, for VnOSCA1.3c, expression levels were consistently below the detection threshold (< 0 TPM) in both roots and leaves. These findings suggest that while positive selection might have occurred, these particular OSCA1.3counterpartsdo not appear to be highly expressed in the examined tissues for either species.
The expression of OSCA2.2 orthologues was relatively higher for both species in comparison to the other genes (60 ± 30 TPM for Vniv; Fig. 4A; and 72 ± 14 TPM for Vint, Fig. 4B). Regarding to OSCA2.4 orthologue genes, the expression levels of the VnOSCA2.4 gene in Vniv root tissue exhibit higher values during the rainy season compared to the drought season, with values of 94 ± 17 and 27 ± 3 TPM, respectively (Fig. 4A). Additionally, the leaves of both plant species exhibited a comparable and consistent manifestation of ViOSCA2.4a and ViOSCA2.4b genes, with mean values of 32 ± 10 and 33 ± 2 TPM, correspondingly. The mean expression and standard deviations in the relevant tissue samples provide evidence for both conclusions. (Fig. 4B).
A similar pattern was observed for ViOSCA2.5 expression, which was relatively constant in Vint forleaf(49 ± 4 TPM) but exhibited more variability inroottissues (60 ± 14 TPM) (Fig. 4B). In the roots of Vniv, the expression of VnOSCA2.5 was similar to that of VnOSCA2.4, i.e., higher during the rainy season when compared to drought season (59 ± 7 and 21 ± 2 TPM, correspondingly), whereas in the leaves there was an increase at the start of the dry season compared to the other conditions (32 ± 0 and 13 ± 10, respectively) (Fig 4A).

PPI networkanalysisrevealed topology differences and mechanisms of stress response.
Using selected orthologues, we constructedprotein-protein interactions(PPI)networks for both species, facilitating the exploration of connection patterns and their potential implications for gene expression and biological processes.This resulted in distinct network topologies for Vniv and Vint species (Fig. 8).Vniv PPI network (Fig. 8A) comprised 41 nodes (representing individual genes) connected by 100 edges (representing gene interactions). In contrast, the Vint PPI network (Fig. 8B) included 28 nodes and 57 edges. The variation in network size underscores potential differences in complexity and interactions between the two species.
Through an analysis of network enrichment focused on biological processes, the investigation's results revealed significant findings.In both networks, we noticed the presence of categories related to stress responsiveness in both Vniv (Fig. 9A) and Vint (Fig. 9B).TheVint network exhibited two stress-related enrichment categories: the first category, described as abiotic stress, mostly comprised proteins associated with the response to limited water availability, containing a total of 7 proteins. The second group, referred to as biotic stress, consisted of six proteins(Fig. 9B).On the other hand,inVniv networktwo categories were identified. The primary category consisted primarily of eight proteins involved in cellular stress responses,whereas the second category displayed an association with transport processes(Fig.9A). Notably, the OSCA proteins themselves emerged in this transport category, along with a notable presence of ABC transporters (Vniv_016829, Vniv_016830, Vniv_019012, Vniv_019032, Vniv_019033).These differences suggest that both OSCA proteins and ABC transporters play intricate and context-dependent roles in mediating plant responses todifferent environmental stimuli in Vniv.
Analysis of the OSCA2.5 orthologues in the two species revealed an intriguingpattern. VnOSCA2.5 displayed much higher connectivity in the Vniv networkthan its Vint counterparts. The level of connectivity was measured by assessing the observed degree of each gene, which is determined by the number of edges connected to a particular node. VnOSCA2.5 exhibited 14 connections, which is 2.8 times higher than the network's average node connectivity (4.88). ViOSCA2.5, in comparison, only had four connections, which is consistent with the network's average degree (4.07). This disparity suggests that the functional roles of OSCA2.5 may differ between species, with VnOSCA2.5 being significantly more involved in PPI interactions within the Vniv network.
VnOSCA2.4 has a reduced number of connections compared to VnOSCA2.5, totaling tofive connections, which represents the mean of their networkconnectivity. Remarkably, although this disparity, they have a substantial overlap of in terms ofinteractions, with four targets shared between them, with Vniv the only gene exclusive to VnOSCA2.4. The fact that VnOSCA2.4 and VnOSCA2.5 share connections may explain why they have similar expression patterns in different water availability situations in Vniv roots(Fig 3).On the otherhand, inthe Vint network,itsorthologues, ViOSCA2.4a and ViOSCA2.4b, had just two connections each, which represents 50% less of theirmeanconnectivity network, which suggest a peripheric role in their regulatory network.The difference in connectivity between Vint and Vniv networks does not necessarily indicate that ViOSCA2.4a and ViOSCA2.4b have diverged in functionality once they both present purifying selection related to their counterparts in Vniv (Table 1). It is possible that the difference in connectivity between the networks is due to variations in gene expression,other evolutionary factors, or lack database knowledge.This highlights the need forfurther analysis to determine if ViOSCA2.4a and ViOSCA2.4b have indeed diverged in functionality within Vint and Vniv speciesand their biological roles in drought perception and response.

Conclusions andperspectives
The analysis of OSCA genes in Vint and Vniv provided intriguing results concerning their evolutionary origins and protein domain composition. Despite having the same number of OSCA1 and OSCA2 genes, additional analyses, such as phylogenetic tree analysis, synteny analysis, and gene distribution on chromosomes, revealed that these genes originated from distinct evolutionary origins. Additionally, protein domain analysis using InterProScan revealed that the majority of proteins in both Vint and Vniv contained two other distinct domains in addition to the OSCA-related PF02714 domain: PF13967 (RSN1 TM) and PF14703 (PHM7 cyt). In addition, there were variations in the composition patterns of these domains among distinct locations encoding OSCA proteins, indicating potential consequences for their protein structure and function.
When comparing the expression patterns of OSCA genes in distinct tissue categories, Vint and Vniv exhibited distinct expression patterns. In general, OSCA1 genes were expressed at low levels in various tissues, whereas OSCA2 genes, especially OSCA2.2 orthologues, were highly expressed in stem, root, and leaves. OSCA2.4 duplicated genes in Vint resulted in similar expression patterns, although VnOSCA2.4 gene expression increased in root and stem tissues. The expression levels of OSCA3 genes were higher, with OSCA3.2 orthologs exhibiting the highest levels. These patterns suggest shared tissue specificity in both species, with orthologues of OSCA3.2 displaying a conserved expression pattern.
We also assessedgene expression patterns in the roots and leaves of Vint and Vniv under different water availability conditions. OSCA1.1 showed significant differences in expression between the species, with higher expression in Vniv during periods of precipitation. Both species exhibited relatively high expression of OSCA2.2 with consistent patterns across tissues and water regimes. ViOSCA2.5 displayed consistent expression in Vint, while VnOSCA2.5 followed a similar pattern to VnOSCA2.4, being higher in roots during the wet season and increasing in leaves at the beginning of the dry season. ViOSCA2.4 showed consistent expression in both Vint roots and leaves. OSCA3.1 expression increased in the roots of both species, peaking at the end of the wet season in Vniv and at the start of the dry season in Vint. Vint expressed lower levels of OSCA3.1 in leaves compared to Vniv, particularly at the beginning of the drought-prone season. OSCA3.2 exhibited higher expression in both Vniv roots and leaves during the rainy season, while in Vint, root gene expression was lower and remained consistent regardless of water availability,suggesting that OSCA3.2 has different regulation mechanisms under various water regimes. These findings highlight the distinct expression patterns of OSCA genes in Vint and Vniv in response to variations in water availability.
In conclusion, our investigation of OSCA gene promoter regions reveals significant changes in drought-responsive element patterns between V. nivea and V. intermedia orthologues OSCA2 and OSCA3. Light responsiveness, growth and development, anaerobic response, auxin response, gibberellin response, wounding/pathogen response, cold response, ethylene response, stress response, ABA response, drought response, jasmonic acid response, and salicylic acid response were all investigated using 2kb promoter regions for each OSCA gene in both species. Notably,“Light responsive”,“Drought responsive”,“Stress responsive”and“Salicylic acid responsive”elements were discovered in all genes, while“Wounding/pathogen responsive”,“Jasmonic acid responsive”and“Growth and development”elements were detected in more than 90% of the genes. Despite these similarities, the OSCA gene clades showed considerable differences in the distribution of drought-responsive elements in promoter regions, indicating unique transcriptional regulation processes under drought conditions.
Additional investigations into the orthologuegene expression patterns and cis-regulatory regions yielded significant results. OSCA2.2's promoter had unique anaerobic-responsive components, which may have contributed to its adaptation to oxygen-depleted situations. OSCA2.4 had a significant abundance of drought- and ABA-responsive components, indicating its importance in drought response. OSCA2.5 was shown to be more sensitive to ABA, drought, ethylene, and wounding/pathogen components, indicating a function in water stress and defense processes. OSCA3.1 orthologues responded better to ABA and dehydration, but OSCA3.2 responded better to light exposure. Tissue-specific gene expression patterns frequently mirrored variations in regulatory profiles. Notably, OSCA3 paralogues in Vint expressed tissue-specifically,may indicatingsubfunctionalization.
The unique topologies ofthe Vniv and VintPPI networks highlight potential differences in interaction complexity between these two species. Notably, the OSCA2.5 orthologues revealed an intriguing pattern, with VnOSCA2.5 displaying significantly higher connectivity within the Vniv network than its Vint counterparts. This indicates a species-specific function for OSCA2.5 and highlights its prominent role in protein-protein interactions within the Vniv network. OSCA2.4 orthologues shared interactions that could account for similar expression patterns in distinct water availability conditions in Vnivroots, despite their reduced connectivity. In contrast, ViOSCA2.4a and ViOSCA2.4b connections in the Vint network were limited, raising questions about their regulatory function.Intriguingly, OSCA2.2 orthologues had the least gene connection in both species. The expression patterns of these OSCA2.2 orthologs in both species suggest they participate in stress response pathways, which may suggest a unique function that deserves further exploration.
The unexpected association of VnOSCA4.1 with VnOSCA2.4, VnOSCA2.5, and VnOSCA3.2, along with shared interactions involving Vniv_027917, the protein containing LEA4 domain, suggests thatVniv_027917genecould havea significant role in the mechanisms governed by VnOSCA proteins. Moreover, the important connectivity of OSCA3 genes in the networks of both species, which exceeds the average network connectivity byfourfold, demonstrates the central role of these genes. Examining genes associated with OSCA3 nodes revealed connections totypically stress-related proteins, especially related to dehydration in Vniv.Now,oursubsequentstepconsists of evaluatingArabidopsisorthologuemutants andArabidopsisoverexpression constructs to elucidate and validate their functional functions within our network analysis.
Overall, the comprehensive analysis of OSCA genes,cis-regulatory elements, and their associations with environmental conditions provides valuable insights into the molecular landscape of water availability perception and response in Vniv and Vint. These findings contribute to our understanding of the complex regulatory mechanisms involved and offer potential targets for further studies and bioengineering approaches aimed at improving plant responses to water stress.
