# Phylogenetics of Paepalanthus (Eriocaulaceae), a diverse Neotropical monocot lineage

**Authors:** Caroline O. Andrino, Paulo T. Sano, Peter W. Inglis, Nancy Hensold, Fabiane N. Costa, Marcelo F. Simon  
**Year:** 2021  
**Journal:** *Botanical Journal of the Linnean Society*, 195(1), 34–52  
**DOI:** 10.1093/botlinnean/boaa070  

## Abstract

*Paepalanthus* is a species-rich monocot genus predominantly diversified across the ancient Neotropical montane grasslands (*campos rupestres*) of Brazil. The taxonomy and evolutionary history of *Paepalanthus* have been historically challenging due to high morphological heterogeneity and nested genera. 

Using extensive molecular phylogenetic sampling across nuclear and plastid loci, we reconstructed the evolutionary relationships and temporal diversification dynamics of *Paepalanthus* and allied taxa in the Espinhaço Range. Our phylogenomic and divergence-time estimates demonstrate that *Paepalanthus* represents a prominent radiation within the *campos rupestres*, exhibiting both early-diverging relictual lineages preserved on ancient plateaus and explosive recent species radiations driven by fine-scale edaphic specialization on quartzite rock outcrops and white sand patches. These macroevolutionary patterns illustrate that the *campos rupestres* function simultaneously as an evolutionary museum and an active cradle of plant diversification, driven by persistent microclimatic buffering and steep edaphic gradients.
