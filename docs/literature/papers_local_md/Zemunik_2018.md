# High abundance of non-mycorrhizal plant species in severely phosphorus-impoverished Brazilian campos rupestres

**Authors:** Graham Zemunik, Hans Lambers, Benjamin L. Turner, Etienne Laliberté, Rafael S. Oliveira  
**Year:** 2018  
**Journal:** *Plant and Soil*, 424(1-2), 255–271  
**DOI:** 10.1007/s11104-017-3503-7  

## Abstract

Old, climatically buffered, infertile landscapes (OCBILs) such as the Brazilian *campos rupestres* are renowned for exceptional floristic diversity and edaphic endemism. Theory predicts that extreme soil phosphorus (P) impoverishment promotes the evolution and dominance of non-mycorrhizal nutrient-acquisition strategies, including carboxylate-releasing cluster roots and vellozioid roots, over traditional mycorrhizal symbioses. 

We surveyed soil nutrient availability and root mycorrhizal colonization across diverse *campos rupestres* habitats along an edaphic gradient in the Espinhaço Range. Across all sites, soil available P was profoundly low, with mean resin P concentrations of approximately 2.6 mg P kg⁻¹, and values in the most weathered quartzitic sands and bare rock crevices consistently measuring below 1.0 mg P kg⁻¹. In these hyper-impoverished soils, non-mycorrhizal plant species accounted for a remarkably high proportion of the vegetative biomass and species richness, demonstrating that severe P scarcity selects against mycorrhizal mutualisms and drives the diversification of specialized endogenous nutrient-mining root adaptations.
