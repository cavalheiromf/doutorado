Structure of the mechanosensitive OSCA channels
Mingfeng Zhang1,2,3, Dali Wang4, Yunlu Kang1, Jing-Xiang Wu1,2, Fuqiang Yao5, Chengfang Pan5,
Zhiqiang Yan 5*, Chen Song 2,3,4* and Lei Chen 1,2*

Mechanosensitive ion channels convert mechanical stimuli into a flow of ions. These channels are widely distributed from bac-
teria to higher plants and humans, and are involved in many crucial physiological processes. Here we show that two members
of the OSCA protein family in Arabidopsis thaliana, namely AtOSCA1.1 and AtOSCA3.1, belong to a new class of mechanosen-
sitive ion channels. We solve the structure of the AtOSCA1.1 channel at 3.5-Å resolution and AtOSCA3.1 at 4.8-Å resolution
by cryo-electron microscopy. OSCA channels are symmetric dimers that are mediated by cytosolic inter-subunit interactions.
Strikingly, they have structural similarity to the mammalian TMEM16 family proteins. Our structural analysis accompanied with
electrophysiological studies identifies the ion permeation pathway within each subunit and suggests a conformational change
model for activation.
