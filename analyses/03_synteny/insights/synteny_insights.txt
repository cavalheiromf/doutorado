# Comparative Genomics and Synteny Analysis of Vellozia genomes and the OSCA Locus

Genome-wide comparative synteny analysis between Vellozia peripherica and Vellozia tubiflora revealed an exceptionally high degree of macro-collinearity across their nine chromosomes, demonstrating a conserved, one-to-one chromosome-level architectural relationship (e.g., vp_chr1A aligning directly to vt_chr1A) with no major inter-chromosomal translocations since their evolutionary divergence. A total of 297 syntenic blocks were identified globally, highlighting a remarkably stable genomic landscape. At the micro-synteny level, focus on the calcium-permeable channel OSCA gene family identified a perfectly conserved syntenic block on chromosome 7A containing a tandem duplication cluster of OSCA1.3, OSCA1.4, and OSCA1.5 (Vper genes g186390, g186400, and g186410; Vtub genes g166590, g166600, and g166610, respectively). The local region is flanked by 24 highly syntenic orthologous gene pairs, confirming the robust structural preservation of this locus. This perfect micro-collinearity and physical contiguity of the OSCA1.3/1.4/1.5 cluster in both species indicate that this tandem expansion event predated the speciation of V. peripherica and V. tubiflora, and the cluster has been strictly maintained under strong purifying selection, potentially due to its crucial role in environmental sensing and stress adaptation in resurrection plants.
